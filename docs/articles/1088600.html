More Than a Quarter of Links in 'Best Of' Answers Lead to Someone Else's Top List

Compilations accounted for 344 out of 1221 links, which is 28%. The proportion at the answer level is higher because one such link is enough for an answer to be counted.

Based on raw links with duplicates, the figures are 36%, 31%, and 34% — the picture is the same.

In Approximately One in Five Company Compilations, the AI Model Names the Author

Out of 73 'question — company website compilation' pairs, the model names the author company in the text of the answer for 14 of them—approximately one in five, six domains. I searched for the name in the answer text with links and addresses removed to avoid counting a domain that the model simply printed as a source, and duplicates of the same question were collapsed.

The mechanism is simple: a company publishes a rating that includes itself, the AI model finds the page based on a query like 'top 10 companies in the niche,' and retells it along with the author. Such cases were found in marketplaces, fulfillment, 1C franchising, marketing research, and content agencies. This is stable: on one question about content agencies, Alice AI had a compilation from a niche participant's website in the sources for 7 out of 9 repeated answers, and the AI model named the author in six of them.

How I Searched for Compilations: A Regex by Address

I did not open the page titles. A compilation was considered a link whose path contained a list indicator:

import re from urllib.parse import urlparse LIST_PATTERN = re.compile( r"(top|luchsh|reyting|rating|podbork|best|spisok|obzor|kakie|kakoe|kakaya|kak-vybrat|sravnen|vs-)", re.I, ) def is_listicle(url: str) -> bool: return bool(LIST_PATTERN.search(urlparse(url).path)) def share_with_listicle(answers: list[dict]) -> float: with_sources = [a for a in answers if a["sources"]] hit = [a for a in with_sources if any(is_listicle(u) for u in a["sources")] return len(hit) / len(with_sources)

I manually categorized 85 domains with compilations into three groups, assigning ambiguous ones to media and catalogs. The regex makes errors in both directions: it sometimes triggers unnecessarily (e.g., 'obzor' in the address of a single product review) and misses compilations with neutral addresses. Therefore, all proportions here are estimates, not exact boundaries.

You Can Check Your Niche in 3-5 Questions

Ask 3-5 questions like 'best ... in your niche' or 'top 10 companies in ...' in Alice AI or Perplexity; they tend to have more compilations in their sources. Open the sources and look for addresses with words like top, luchshie, reyting, sravnenie. A regex is only needed if you want to calculate the proportion. For each compilation, identify its owner, and then the next steps depend on that.

  1. Media and Catalog. Such ratings are updated: send your company's data or an inclusion request for the next version.

  2. Blog Platform. On the same platform, write your own comparison or analysis with an open methodology, naming several players.

  3. Competitor's Website. It's impossible to get there; asking is useless. The countermeasure is to create your own compilation with open criteria and to be present in the other two groups.

Your own compilation can end up in the sources: more than a third of the found ones are on company websites, and in approximately one in five of them, the AI model names the author. I did not count how often authors of compilations in media and on blog platforms are named. The difference lies in transparency. If the criteria are not stated, and the author predictably comes first, a reader who opens the source will see this.

The question I don't have an answer to is: if an AI model includes both an independent rating and a compilation from a market participant in its sources, what will remain of its top list when there are five such compilations in the niche instead of one?
