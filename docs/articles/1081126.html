Mechanics: How a Neural Network Decides Who to Name

GEO (generative engine optimization) is the work of ensuring that neural networks know about a business and include it in answers to customer queries. The key difference from SEO is the price of a position: in search results, there's a second page, but in a neural network's answer, there isn't; it either names you or it doesn't.

When asked "who to choose," a neural network does three things in order: it searches for recent materials on the topic from reputable sources, selects those that provide a direct answer — with prices, criteria, and specifics — and names the companies that are mentioned most frequently and convincingly in these materials.

The strategy consists of three elements: the right words — content using the same phrasing as customer questions; the right platforms — those that neural networks trust; and data accuracy — the company is easy to identify and verify.

How to Get Into Answers: Six Steps

Step 1. Gather the questions your clients actually ask

Real-life phrasing, including all the "cheap" and "for kids": "how much does a dental implant cost in Krasnodar," "how to choose an accountant for an individual entrepreneur." Sources: correspondence and calls with clients, managers and administrators, Yandex suggestions and Wordstat. A prompt you can copy:

You are my potential client. I am [your niche, city]. Come up with 15 questions you would ask a neural network when choosing [your service/product]: about price, deadlines, risks, selection criteria. Phrase them in conversational language, like a real person, not a marketer.

Result of the step: a list of 10–15 questions, sorted by importance for revenue.

Step 2. Write 3–5 articles on your website that directly answer them

The headline should repeat the client's question. The first three paragraphs should be a direct answer: prices, deadlines, criteria. Then, discuss nuances and caveats, and at the end, a Q&A section for 3–5 clarifying points. Articles with specific figures are cited in neural network answers significantly more often than vague texts, and this is not a one-time task: 3–5 articles are a start, followed by regularity — 2–3 new materials per month and updating old ones quarterly.

An example of the first paragraph that neural networks can easily quote: "On average, an implant with a crown in Krasnodar costs from 35 to 60 thousand rubles. The range depends on the implant brand and the complexity of the case. With us, it starts from 38,000 rubles, and the consultation is free."

Checking a finished article is simple: ask the neural network the question from its headline. If it answers with a quote from your text — the article is working; if the answer has to be dug out — rewrite the beginning, the essence should be in the first paragraphs.

Result of the step: each article answers one specific question — and can be inserted into the answer entirely.

Step 3. Publish articles where neural networks look for them

Crucially, on your own website, and in a strong search position: for Alice, Yandex's top-10 is almost everything. Register your site in Bing Webmaster Tools — ChatGPT, Copilot, and other Western neural networks often search through the Bing index; it's free and takes 10 minutes. Next, external platforms for the market: local business → Yandex Zen, review sites, 2GIS, Yandex Maps; expertise and B2B → vc.ru, Habr, industry media; international audience → Reddit, YouTube, Medium.

The most cited genre is rankings and comparisons, but your own "top 5 best" ranking on your website doesn't work: neural networks trust independent platforms; reviews should be published on industry portals.

Result of the step: 2–3 platforms where you are consistently present — neural networks won't remember one-off publications.

Step 4. Get people talking about you outside your website

Brand mentions on external platforms influence recommendations more than links. Collect reviews on Yandex Maps, 2GIS, and review sites, and respond to them thoughtfully. Get into other people's compilations through expert comments and partnerships — a kitchen manufacturer that provides a comment to a publication for an article "How to choose a custom kitchen" ends up in the source from which the neural network will compile its answer.

Step 5. Organize your company's data

A neural network compiles all mentions into a single profile and recommends those whose profiles align without contradictions. Check: the name, phone number, and address match on the website, in Yandex Business, 2GIS, and social networks down to the last digit; the "About Us" page features the real team; the Yandex Business card is fully filled out. This is more important than it seems: according to Bizrate data, 86% of buyers double-check AI recommendations from other sources before purchasing.

Step 6. Measure results weekly

Once a week, ask 5–7 questions from step one to several neural networks simultaneously — ChatGPT, Alice, Perplexity, DeepSeek: each has its own sources, and the dynamics will vary. Record in a table: date, question, neural network, who was recommended. Prompt for checking:

[Client question from your list.] Name 3–5 specific companies you would recommend and explain why each one.

Who GEO is Primarily For

Maximum effect is in niches where customers ask for advice before purchasing: local services ("best dentist in [city]"), experts and schools ("which course on [topic] to choose"), products ("which is better, X or Y"), B2B ("which CRM to choose for a small business"). Niche markets benefit the most — the more specific the specialization, the more confidently a neural network associates the query with your name.

What Can Go Wrong

Timelines. Initial shifts appear within 2–4 weeks, stable results within 2–3 months of consistent work. It can't be faster: neural networks need to re-index sources and "see" new mentions.

Instability. The composition of sources changes with model updates — the very story of Reddit, whose share in ChatGPT's answers dropped sixfold in one update. Weekly measurement from step 6 is therefore mandatory: the channel is manageable only as long as you measure it.

Errors that turn GEO into spam. Self-promotional "top lists" on your own website, templated texts without substance, inflated reviews. Neural networks filter all of this just as search engines filtered web pages.

Technical glitches that are not visually apparent. If the steps have been followed, but there are no results after 2–3 months, check three things:

  • Open yourwebsite.ru/robots.txt and see if access for neural network bots — GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, YandexBot — is prohibited. A line like "Disallow: /" under the name of such a bot means access is blocked independently.

  • Enter the query site:yourwebsite.ru into Yandex — if there are no articles in the search results, they have not been indexed, and neural networks have nothing to take from. Then you need to investigate in Yandex Webmaster and Bing Webmaster Tools — both services show for free which pages did not get into the index and why.

  • Check the microdata using a free Schema.org validator — if the result is empty, you need markup at least for the organization and FAQ.
