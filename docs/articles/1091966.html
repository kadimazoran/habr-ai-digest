In an era of pervasive automation, genuine human interaction, free from the templated scripts of an "answering machine robot," is becoming a business advantage and a customer retention tool. Service leaders agree: quality service has ceased to be merely a department for handling complaints, transforming into a profit generator.

At the V All-Russian Forum "Contact Center 2026," held in Moscow in late September, experts discussed the transformation of customer service departments. The main market insight is that robots should relieve employees of routine tasks, but only people can retain customers in critical moments. Sincere attention at the moment a problem arises neutralizes negativity more effectively than any promo codes.

Simultaneously, the standard of communication itself is changing—a complete ban on templated phrases is becoming a fundamental element of doing business. When operators communicate exclusively live, using scripts only as a flexible framework for conversation, trust in the brand grows.

The real viability of this strategy is confirmed by the case of the health marketplace "Zdravcity.ru." The company's Head of Customer Service, Yuri Onosovsky, shared the results of implementing an empathetic approach in the platform's unified contact center. To distribute the workload, the company has deployed three support lines. Automation handles basic questions, but complex situations are escalated to the second line for individual handling; if necessary, on-duty professional pharmacists join the conversation. As a result, 82% of customers reach an operator in just 20 seconds, and 95% of all problems are resolved during the first call.

"The market today is increasingly focused on optimization and artificial intelligence. These are excellent tools, and the AI assistant implemented at Zdravcity.ru handles routine tasks perfectly. But our main focus is the human. Machines cannot yet empathize. Our operators are chosen for their sincere attention to people, which is why customers prefer to call to place an order by phone," notes Yuri Onosovsky.

According to the results of the second quarter of 2026, the share of customers who made a repeat order immediately after their complaint was resolved on the line grew to 62.48%. Additionally, operators successfully increase the average order value through helpful advice (e.g., recommending probiotics when purchasing a course of antibiotics) and assist elderly people who are not confident using smartphones in placing large orders.

The expert emphasized that direct dialogue with consumers allows for product development based on their real needs: all feedback analytics are sent to developers and logistics specialists for refining interfaces and business processes.
