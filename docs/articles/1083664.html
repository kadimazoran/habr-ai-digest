Why run other people's benchmarks

A Russian chat can be easily tested by asking it to "write a poem." I needed something else: does it maintain its form when the task is long, in English, with rules and several turns in a row. Western labs have long collected open task sets for this. I ran domestic chats through them - the same questions, the same scoring rules.

First, the model responds like a regular chat, each item is a new dialogue. Then a separate judge reads the completed answers. Here it is Grok 4.6. If the connection is lost and the question doesn't reach the model, I don't count the attempt and repeat it. If the chat returns an empty response or its own server error - it's zero, not "our browser broke."

Who is in the comparison

Alice - a chat at alice.yandex.ru with a Yandex Plus subscription. A person in the country opens the website, so I tested the website.

GigaChat Max and GigaChat Ultra - two Sber tariffs. They cannot be combined into one line: Ultra is stronger on long text and on tasks with tools, Max is cheaper and weaker on the same questions.

Arena-Hard - who writes better on a difficult task

Two students, one difficult exam: code, case study, long instructions. The teacher doesn't give "five out of five," but says whose paper is better. This is how Arena-Hard works: 750 difficult tasks, answers are compared in pairs - win, loss, or draw. A win goes to the one who completed the calculation and fulfilled the points.

Each pair has two columns. The left one shows the win rate of the model named on the left, the right one - on the right. A draw is included in the 750, it's not shown on the graph. 83% for Max against Alice means: out of 750 pairs, the teacher gave the paper to Max in 620 cases, to Alice - in 106, a draw - 24.
