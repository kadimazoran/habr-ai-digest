I decided to check in my free time how many people with AI skills are currently in demand on the market. Everyone feels the same: AI is everywhere, you can't do without it, every second post in the feed is about agents. But what about the job market?

I scraped hh.ru (not without the help of LLM), all active vacancies in 25 categories of the "Information Technology" section in Russia - it turned out to be 34,053. Then I counted how many of them mention language models and related things: LLM, GPT, RAG, agents, prompts, Cursor, LangChain, GigaChat, and a couple of dozen other terms. Separately, how many I analyzed by the full text of the description: 853 vacancies.

How many in total

Well, eight percent. If we count any AI at all - machine learning, computer vision, NLP, classic DS - it's 16.4%. The remaining 84% of vacancies say nothing about AI.

AI is included in the job title for only 0.79% - 268 vacancies out of 34 thousand, one out of one hundred and twenty-seven. That is, for every "AI Engineer", there are about ten vacancies where LLM is simply a requirement for a regular Python developer, analyst, or tester.

In other words, AI is not yet a profession in the labor market, but a skill.

Who needs to know this

The sum across all 25 roles gives 34,053 vacancies and exactly 2,836 with LLM.

Things that were a bit surprising:

Data scientists are the only role where AI has become the norm. 46.1% of vacancies with LLM, and if we count any AI - 79.3%. For everyone else, this is a requirement of a minority of employers.

Team leads, DevOps, and system analysts are ahead of developers. 19.0%, 18.3%, and 15.6% versus 12.9% for programmers. The logic is understandable: the wrapper around the models needs to be designed, deployed, and operated, not just written. Moreover, testers (12.2%) and technical writers (11.8%) are almost on par with developers.

AI is practically not required from CTOs. 2.3% - even though their subordinates are required five to eight times more often. Technical support (1.6%), system administrators, and network engineers (1.8% each) are predictably at the bottom of the list.

However, in terms of absolute volume, developers still win: 728 vacancies out of 2,836 - simply because there are 5,632 of them on the market.

What exactly is required

I expected that under "AI vacancies" would be hidden classic ML: train, fine-tune, label a dataset. But it's almost entirely application of ready-made models.

RAG - 522 vacancies. Prompt engineering - 249. LangChain - 224. Cursor - 192, Claude Code - 184, GitHub Copilot - 148. Fine-tuning and fine-tuning - 173, LoRA and PEFT - 104. Out of 853 analyzed vacancies, fine-tuning is mentioned in about one in five, and almost always - alongside application, not instead of it.

A separate story is AI developer tools. Thirty percent of the analyzed vacancies require them explicitly, and these are more often regular backend and full-stack developers than data scientists. Vibe coding has reached job titles:

  • "Development Engineer (Python, Vibe Coding)" - Ak Bars Digital Technologies

  • "Senior Golang - VibeCode developer" - X5 Tech

  • "Tech Lead for Vibe-coding (AI Engineer / ML Architect)"

  • "Junior AI Developer / Vibe Coder"

And in the requirements:

Your main task is to accelerate feature delivery (Time-to-Market) using Vibe Coding practices

Practical experience using AI tools in development: Cursor, Copilot, Claude Code, Devin, CrewAI, and similar

Apply agent development tools (Claude Code, Cursor) and write skills/instructions for agents

I like the last formulation the most: writing instructions for agents is no longer "knowing how to use it," it's engineering work on someone else's behavior.

Wrapper around the model

A separate layer of requirements is not about the model itself, but about what surrounds it. Tools for agents, MCP servers, orchestration, tracing, evals, guardrails. In the analyzed sample, 517 vacancies talk about agent systems, 167 about MCP, 184 about evaluating the quality and observability of LLM systems.

And the word "harness" has arrived in Russian-language vacancies - without translation and explanation, meaning the audience to whom the text is addressed understands it. If you don't know what it is, I wrote about it recently: show me your harness and the wrapper around the model gives as much as the model itself.

We are building harness systems around LLMs: agents with tools, code review automation, assistants for writing DAGs

Building agents and multi-agent systems: harness (OpenClaw, Hermes), tools, MCP, A2A protocols

The word "harness" itself appears in 32 IT vacancies. For a term that was completely absent in Russian descriptions a year ago, this is quite good.

Grades, format, money

The share grows strictly with the grade: from 3.5% for vacancies with no experience to 13.5% for positions "more than 6 years". AI skills are not expected from juniors (they are not valued anywhere now), but quite often from seniors. Remote work is noticeably "smarter" than office work: 12.5% ​​versus 8.3% in the market. Moscow - 11.8%, million-plus cities - 5-9%.

Regarding money: only about 22% of vacancies show a range, so the sample is small. The median for vacancies with LLM application is approximately 174,000 ₽ per month. The main jump occurs at the transition from 1-3 years (median about 113,000) to 3-6 years (about 226,000). The upper tail is 315,000 at the ninetieth percentile, with individual positions like AI System Architect reaching up to 425,000.

What's the result

The market is not yet looking for a separate profession "AI engineer" - there is one such vacancy out of one hundred and twenty-seven. It is looking for ordinary developers, analysts, and team leads who know how to assemble an AI stack, I don't know how to call all this, vibe coding has become too marginal a term.

It will be interesting to release a similar report in six months, we continue to observe.
