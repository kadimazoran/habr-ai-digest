Controlling a robot using images and text instructions remains one of the most pressing tasks in modern robotics. Vision-Language-Action, or VLA, models are already capable of solving a wide range of tasks. However, most notable results have been achieved on standard manipulators: a single robotic arm on a fixed base, with six or seven degrees of freedom and a two-fingered gripper.

Controlling an anthropomorphic robot requires solving a problem of a fundamentally different level of complexity. The large number of degrees of mobility, anthropomorphic hands, a mobile platform, and the need to coordinate the entire body's movements while maintaining balance significantly complicate the development of a control system.

Sber Robotics is launching the Green Challenge, a competition within AI Journey 2026. Participants will need to create or fine-tune a control model for 'Green,' Sber's first anthropomorphic robot. The model must solve mobile manipulation tasks in a digital simulator.

This is a complex task in the field of Physical AI: from data preparation and multimodal perception to planning and coordinated control of the robot's entire body movements.

What is the Task?

The model controls the robot by receiving three types of input data:

  • RGB images from three cameras: one located in the robot's head, and two attached to the left and right wrists. Each image has a resolution of 448 × 448 pixels.

  • Text instruction: a formulation of the complete scenario task (task), as well as a description of the current atomic subtask (subtask).

  • Robot's proprioceptive state: joint positions and body orientation.

As a result, the model must predict target joint positions, i.e., absolute joint positions. These commands are executed by the Whole-Body Controller (WBC), which coordinates the movements of the entire robot body.

Scenarios are sequences of interconnected actions. For example, the robot needs to raise the correct arm, reach for a shelf, pick up an object, move it, and return its arm to the starting position.

This is not a set of independent skills, but a single sequence of actions. An error early on can disrupt the robot's or object's position and cascade to affect the success of the entire episode.

The Main Challenge: Robustness to Out-of-Distribution States

The first natural solution is to collect robot demonstrations and train a model using behavior cloning. However, this is precisely where the main challenge of the competition arises: robustness to states that differ from those in the training data. In machine learning, such states are called out-of-distribution (OOD) states. The primary reason for OOD is the discrepancy between the states recorded in demonstrations and the states the robot enters during autonomous task execution.

Several factors lead to such discrepancies:

  • New combinations of initial robot and object positions not present in the training dataset.

  • Accumulation of execution errors. The low-level whole-body motion controller may fail to bring the robot to the target position. As a result, the next observation and the robot's actual state will differ from what the model expects.

  • Inaccurate trajectories predicted by the VLA policy. These can lead the robot into states not present in the training dataset.

  • Non-determinism in the simulation of dynamics and contact interactions.

  • Erroneous subsequent actions that the policy takes from an already deviated state.

The fundamental conditions of the task do not change: the robot model, the set of cameras, the observation and action formats, and the behavior of the whole-body motion controller during solution verification in the simulator remain the same.

This leads to a key requirement: the model must not only reproduce demonstrations but also correct actions based on current observations, work with out-of-distribution states, and recover from deviations.

The competition evaluates the entire solution: data preparation, state and action coordination, temporal synchronization, multimodal perception, control robustness, and model execution speed.
