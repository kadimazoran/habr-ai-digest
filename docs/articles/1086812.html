But before we compare, we need to understand one important thing.

“Free Agent” ≠ “Free Models”

These are two completely different things.

For example, an application might cost $0 but use paid Claude, GPT, or Gemini via API. In this case, the agent itself is free, but its use may still cost money.

There is also the opposite case: a service may not charge for inference of selected models and at the same time limit the number of free requests.

Therefore, I will look at four things:

  1. How much the agent itself costs.

  2. Which models are actually available for free.

  3. What the free limit is.

  4. Whether you can connect your own API or local model.

1. OpenCode

OpenCode is an open-source AI coding agent primarily focused on working with projects, terminals, files, and agentic tasks.

OpenCode's main feature is freedom of choice.

You can use different providers, your own API keys, local models, and models from OpenCode Zen.

What's Free

OpenCode itself is free.

Additionally, models with a $0 price tag periodically appear in the OpenCode Zen catalog.

The list of free models changes, but at the time of publication, you could find, for example:

  • MiMo-V2.5 Free

  • Ling 3.0 Flash Fin Free

  • Nemotron 3 Ultra Free

  • Nemotron 3.5 Lightning Free

  • Big Pickle

  • Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor Free

There is one very important nuance here.

OpenCode does not promise a unified free quota for all free models.

This means you cannot honestly state:

“OpenCode provides 500 requests per day for free.”

Different models may have different rate limits, session restrictions, and varying availability.

Moreover, a free model might be temporary and disappear from the list later.

Therefore, it's more accurate to say:

OpenCode provides users with a separate pool of models priced at $0, but specific models and their limitations may change.

OpenCode Go

For users who need more stable limits, there is OpenCode Go.

Price:

  • $5 for the first month

  • then $10/month

Go has usage limits that are not measured simply by the number of requests.

The current scheme indicates:

  • $12 for 5 hours

  • $30 per week

  • $60 per month

This does not mean “N requests.”

The number of actual requests directly depends on the model. You can get significantly more work done on a cheaper model than on an expensive one.

OpenCode Zen

There is also a separate OpenCode Zen with pay-as-you-go pricing.

So, OpenCode can be considered in three ways:

  • the agent itself is free;

  • temporarily free models;

  • paid inference.

2. Freebuff

Freebuff is perhaps the most unusual service on this list.

Its idea is quite simple: to give the user a free AI coding agent and free inference, covering some costs through advertising.

However, it's very easy to make a mistake when describing the free tier because Freebuff has two different modes.

Full Mode

In the full-fledged mode, Freebuff provides:

100 Freebucks every day.

You can use various models with these Freebucks.

Currently, the service shows, for example:

  • GLM 5.3 Flash — up to 20 hours

  • Solar Mini 4 — up to 20 hours

  • MiMo 2.6 Flash — up to 10 hours

  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash — up to 6 hours

  • Muse Spark 1.2 — up to 6 hours

But these values do not add up.

If the service indicates 20 hours for one model and 10 hours for another, it does not mean:

20 + 10 = 30 hours of free AI.

Both figures are calculated based on a single daily reserve of 100 Freebucks.

This means the user chooses where to spend their daily quota.

What Happens to Freebucks

Freebucks:

  • are updated daily;

  • are not carried over;

  • are reset at a specific time;

  • are consumed depending on the selected model.

Additionally, individual models and sessions may have their own limitations.

Limited Mode

This mode is particularly important to include in the article.

Users may encounter Limited mode even where Full mode is unavailable. At the time of publication, this applies to users in countries like the Czech Republic and other regions where the full mode is not enabled.

Limited mode is not the same 100 Freebucks.

In this mode, Freebuff provides:

  • DeepSeek V4 Flash

  • 6 free sessions per day

  • each session — up to 1 hour

This means the maximum free usage is:

up to 6 hours of DeepSeek V4 Flash per day.

This is a completely different type of limitation.

Therefore, the phrase:

“Freebuff provides 100 Freebucks per day for free”

by itself can be incorrect for some users.

A more accurate description:

Full mode: 100 Freebucks per day and several models. Limited mode: DeepSeek V4 Flash and up to 6 one-hour sessions per day.

This is precisely what needs to be considered when comparing Freebuff with other agents.

Why Does Limited Mode Exist?

Freebuff uses it as a global fallback for users who cannot access the full mode.

This means a person can still use the service, but instead of a full pool of options, they get a more limited set of capabilities.

And this is a good example of why the word Free in AI services explains almost nothing on its own.

Paid Access

Freebuff also offers more powerful models that fall under the paid tier.

Among them, the service lists:

  • GPT-6 Luna

  • MiMo 2.6 Pro

  • Gemini 3.8 Flash

The fixed price for the paid tier may be displayed separately within the service itself, so I wouldn't include a figure in the article that isn't publicly available.

How I Would Choose a Free AI Coding Agent

Instead of the question:

“Which one is free?”

it's better to ask four questions.

1. Is there free inference?

If not, only the interface is free.

2. How much exactly is given for free?

You need to look at:

  • requests;

  • credits;

  • tokens;

  • hours;

  • weekly limits;

  • session limits.

3. Can I use my own API?

If yes, the free tier sometimes becomes significantly more useful.

4. Can I use a local model?

For owners of decent hardware, this might be more important than any free subscription.
