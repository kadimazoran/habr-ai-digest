(Potentially for doctors of any specialty)

I saw a reel on a banned social network about a man inspired to create AI by watching his doctor wife, who spends her evenings filling out charts. It made me think about how AI could be applied to a specific doctor, and I'll use the routine of an orthopedic trauma surgeon as an example. I haven't delved into the routine of a trauma surgeon, and I'm proceeding from the assumption that the patient could be covered by mandatory health insurance (OMS), voluntary health insurance (DMS), or be a private patient (under a paid medical services agreement). This is a good starting point, but then the "buts" begin.

AI to Assist with History Taking

The first thing I'd like to remove from a doctor's routine is manual typing ("click-clack" on the keyboard). Hence the need for voice-to-text input. Let's assume both the doctor and the patient agree to the processing of biometric data.

The doctor collects the history, and the patient answers: not in order, randomly (redundant phrasing), getting confused about dates: dates of first medical aid, where it was provided, what was done. An AI assistant can recognize speech, extract key facts from the patient's answers, and arrange them chronologically.

Why is chronology important? It's crucial for all treatment processes, understanding treatment effects, and determining if the patient is indeed an orthopedic trauma patient or belongs to another specialty (neurologist, surgeon, phlebologist).

My Vision

  • Routine is not simple routine. Filling out a chart is not mechanical typing but recording clinical decisions. If AI speeds up documentation but distorts meaning, the savings turn into risk. Therefore, the goal is not to "write faster at any cost," but to offload the mechanical part while maintaining accuracy.

  • Specialty matters. An orthopedic trauma surgeon has their own logic: injury, timelines, stages, rehabilitation, chronology of first aid. A "one-size-fits-all" template easily loses these nuances. Universality should be at the platform level, with clinical prompts customizable for the specialty. I'll note separately: the routine of a trauma surgeon is not covered in this analysis and requires separate consideration.

  • OMS, DMS, and private patients provide different contexts. Funding channels dictate documentation requirements, scope and duration of care, patient flow, audits, and paperwork. Private patients have additional conditions: contracts, consent for paid services, price lists, payment procedures, and closing documents. AI must account for these differences, not apply the same scenario to all patients.

  • Responsibility remains with the doctor. No matter how good the AI is, the signature and responsibility lie with the doctor. Therefore, verification is mandatory, and AI should assist in verification, not create the illusion that verification is unnecessary.

  • Data and privacy. Voice, biometrics, medical information are sensitive data. Consent, minimized storage, and transparent access must be built in from the start, not "added later.".

  • Economics and implementation. Even a useful assistant must fit into the real workflow: speed, integration with the chart, no extra clicks. Otherwise, it becomes another "routine on top of routine.".

Reading Medical Images

An AI assistant can help in reading medical images by suggesting which study to use in a specific situation and interpreting the results:

  • CT for verifying non-displaced fractures

  • MRI for viewing internal joint structures (cartilage, tendons, ligaments)

  • Ultrasound for diagnosing inflammation (or damage) of superficial muscle, tendon, and ligament structures, unlike MRI which looks very deep and structurally

Based on the interpretation of the study results, the AI can suggest a treatment plan and/or a consultation with related specialists.

My Vision

  • Choice of study: a prompt. The AI suggests which study is appropriate in a given situation, with justification, but the decision to order it rests with the doctor.

  • Interpretation with confidence level. The AI shows what it sees and its confidence level; questionable findings are flagged for mandatory review by the doctor.

  • The doctor reads the image, the AI provides a "second look." The AI helps not to miss a finding, but the conclusion and signature belong to the doctor.

  • Link to treatment and related specialists. Suggestions for a treatment plan or consultation with a related specialist must be linked to clinical guidelines and chart data, not be "out of the blue.".

  • Limitations on image quality. If an image is incomplete or of low quality, the AI should state this directly, not try to complete the picture.

Treatment

An AI assistant can help in searching for and selecting drug therapy, as well as additional forms of treatment: physiotherapy, osteopathy, therapeutic exercises, manual therapy, acupuncture. Based on the diagnosis, ICD code, history, and medical chart data, the system suggests treatment options considering clinical guidelines, contraindications, and compatibility. The doctor reviews the suggestions, makes corrections, and approves the final treatment plan.

My Vision

  • AI as a "second look." Its task is not to provide a ready-made prescription, but to show relevant options and what might be easily missed: contraindications, drug incompatibilities, allergies, age-related and renal/hepatic limitations. The decision and signature belong to the doctor.

  • Reliance on clinical guidelines and sources. Suggestions must be linked to current clinical guidelines and verifiable sources, with a reference to the basis—so the doctor can see why an option was proposed.

  • Separation of medication and non-medication components. For medications, the focus is on safety (dosages, compatibility, contraindications). For physiotherapy, osteopathy, therapeutic exercises, manual therapy, and acupuncture, the focus is on appropriateness for the given diagnosis and stage, as well as limitations (when the method is contraindicated).

  • Consistency check with the plan. The AI cross-references proposed treatment with already prescribed treatments, with the patient's history, and with the treatment stage to avoid duplication and contradictions.

  • Transparency and learnability. The doctor sees exactly what the AI suggested, what was accepted, and what was rejected; rejected options are not "forced" again.

  • Draft / Final. Suggestions exist as drafts; only what the doctor explicitly approves enters the chart and the plan.

Filling Out Medical Records

An AI assistant can fill out medical records: transfer data from the patient history, enter treatment details, generate draft entries based on recognized speech and chart data. AI-filled documentation must be reviewed by the doctor for accuracy and compliance, as the doctor bears full responsibility for everything signed.

My Vision

  • The principle of "AI prepares, doctor signs." Any entry created by AI exists as a draft until explicitly confirmed by the doctor. Only the confirmed version enters the chart, and the fact of confirmation is recorded (who, when, what was confirmed).

  • Responsibility is not diluted. The phrase "filled out by AI" should not create the illusion that the responsibility also lies "with the AI." The doctor's signature is the point of responsibility, so verification is mandatory and cannot be a "default checkbox.".

  • Assistance in verification, not replacement of verification. AI can highlight for the doctor what needs re-checking: discrepancies in dates, missed mandatory fields, contradictions between history, treatment, and sick leave.

  • Transparency of data origin. The doctor should see what came from voice input, what came from the chart, and what the AI suggested itself.

  • Action log. All doctor's edits and AI suggestions are saved to protect the doctor in case of disputes and allow for assistant improvement without "freelancing" in legally significant fields.

  • "Draft / Final" separation at the interface level. Clear status of the entry (draft, pending review, signed) to prevent accidental signing of unverified text.

Working with Sick Leave Certificates

  • Opening

  • Extension

  • Closing

  • Violation of regimen

When opening a sick leave certificate

An AI assistant can automatically fill in the patient's personal data, pull employer information, transfer the ICD code and reason for incapacity code from the medical chart, and also automatically calculate and set the dates of incapacity based on the diagnosis and data from the completed chart. The doctor only needs to check the correctness of the entry and sign the document.

When extending a sick leave certificate

A new medical chart entry is required, describing the treatment dynamics. The AI can pull previous chart entries and necessary data from the open sick leave certificate. The doctor enters the basis for extension manually. Dates are set automatically. After checking the correctness of the entry, the document is automatically sent to the medical board. The system determines which specific board member to send it to, depending on their position and availability.

When extended by the medical board

Extension of a sick leave certificate by decision of the medical board is allowed for a maximum of 15 calendar days; the AI assistant takes this limit into account when automatically calculating dates and warns the doctor if the proposed date exceeds the 15-day window. After extension, the medical board's protocol and epicrisis are drawn up: the AI can pre-fill both documents with data from the medical chart (ICD code and reason for incapacity code, treatment dynamics, previously established incapacity periods) and check the consistency of dates and information between the sick leave certificate, protocol, and epicrisis. The doctor reviews and signs the documents, after which both are handed over to the responsible person.

When closing a sick leave certificate

A new entry is made in the medical chart, with recommendations indicating that the patient is cleared for work starting tomorrow. The doctor reviews and signs the document.

When a regimen violation occurs (this is when the patient comes in later than the specified date)

A new entry is made in the medical chart with automatic indication of the regimen violation. The sick leave certificate automatically gets the code for regimen violation. The doctor then acts according to the clinical situation: if the patient is fit for work, they are cleared for work; if not yet, the sick leave is extended. The doctor reviews and signs the document.

My Vision for Sick Leave Certificates

  • Deadline control. The 15-day limit, matching dates across three documents (sick leave, protocol, epicrisis), and completeness of mandatory fields are areas where AI can genuinely reduce error risk.

  • Routing. If the required board member is absent, the document should not get stuck; there needs to be a clear route for a deputy and notification to the doctor.

  • Clinical wording and commission decisions remain human. The AI does not "invent" the basis for extension or the commission's decision but prepares a draft for review.

  • Signature only after explicit confirmation. Automatic sending to the commission and responsible person only after the doctor signs.
