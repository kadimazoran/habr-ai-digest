Besides my main job, I occasionally help friends with their applications. In one of these projects, the call center asks clients how they found out about us. Lately, they've been responding more often that GPT recommended us. I decided to check under what queries it recommends the site, which pages it reads, and what it says about us.

A user might ask the neural network to find an intermediary, compare conditions, and provide several options. And if previously they sifted through websites themselves, now GPT can do part of that work for them.

But for this to happen, GPT needs to find your site, read it, and understand what you do. There are nuances to this.

Checked on Another Account

To start, I asked GPT the following:

Clients come to our site [my_website_domain] and say that GPT recommended them. It has many different pages.

Your task is to figure out under what requests you would recommend this site. Conduct research and give me prompts, and I will check them on an independent GPT agent to see if you're lying. But you must double-check everything. And naturally, the prompts should be human-like, not include the site name, etc. A regular user types something, and you recommend this site. Think through all variations.

It provided many good variations, but I took the very first one and tested it on another account:

I want to order a large batch of goods from 1688 for resale in Kazakhstan. Recommend reliable intermediaries with websites.

And it indeed listed our site first. There were other options too, but we were first.

I checked the query itself. But whether users will actually search this way and how many such queries they make is yet to be determined. After all, the neural network formulated the wording based on a site it already knew.

But You Can Go the Other Way Around

Ask it how users search for services in your field, so it can list competitors. For businesses in the CIS, it's best to specify the country and city right away.

As a next step, you can then interrogate it:

  1. How exactly did these sites end up on your list? What criteria did you use? Show the search queries and pages you looked at.

  2. Analyze my site and tell me what's missing for you to recommend it to a user with such requirements.

  3. What pages should be created or supplemented? What should be on them?

  4. Separately, gather popular queries through Yandex Wordstat and Google Ads Keyword Planner. Select your region, download the statistics, and give them to the neural network. Ask it to formulate common customer questions based on this data and check whom it would recommend.

In Wordstat, enter your service, select the region, and look at queries for the last month. For our case, you can start with "buyout from 1688," "intermediary 1688," "wholesale goods from China." The service will show queries containing these words and similar ones. The result can be downloaded in CSV. Only the number next to the general phrase includes queries that contain it. This is not the number of people who typed exactly these two words.

In Google Ads, you need the Keyword Planner. There, you can find new queries or upload your list and see statistics. Specify the country or city and the period. Look at the average number of queries per month. Google rounds the data and considers similar phrasing variations.

For seasonality, there's Google Trends. For example, you can see if interest in a service is growing and in which months it's searched for more often. But its scale from 0 to 100 shows relative interest, not the number of queries.

If the site is already running, also check Google Search Console and Yandex Webmaster. There you'll find queries for which your site was shown. This will help you find phrasing you might not have considered yourself.

Then, give the downloaded data to the neural network with a task like this:

Here is the query statistics for my niche. Group them by what the client needs. For each group, create several common questions that a person might ask GPT. Keep the country, city, and conditions from the original queries. Do not add my company name. Keep the frequency from the file next to it; do not invent new numbers.

You can start with frequent queries for which people genuinely need your service. If someone is looking for how to pay for an order on 1688 themselves, they don't necessarily want full procurement support.

Check the generated questions in new chats before showing your site. Save who was recommended, in what order, and which pages were linked. Then compare it with your site and work on what's missing.

Google and Yandex statistics show demand in search engines. How many times such a question was asked within GPT cannot be determined from them. But these are at least real queries that can be used to start the check.

And one more thing: when you ask the neural network why it chose a competitor, it might invent an explanation. Therefore, you need to open the pages yourself. If a competitor has a minimum order quantity, buyout cost, delivery times, and required documents, while you only offer "individual approach," the difference can be verified.

If you develop this idea further, the agent will advise on what pages to create and how. Then you can check its advice and see if the recommendations change.

What GPT and Claude See on a Website

In my experience: I gave GPT and Claude the same link and asked what was on the page. GPT returned the content, while Claude responded that it couldn't render the page and received plain JavaScript.

But to be fair, I always use GPT in Work mode. This must be considered when making such comparisons. This example doesn't mean that GPT renders sites in all modes, and Claude can't in any mode.

I decided to research how they render sites and found a measurement by Vercel and MERJ. They looked at robot requests to websites. They did not detect JavaScript execution by the checked robots from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Perplexity. However, Googlebot and Applebot did execute it.

But a robot that collects pages and an agent with browser access work differently. An assistant can get the page text, data from search results, or open it in a browser. Therefore, it's important what exactly it used in a specific check. Work mode should also not be automatically equated with any other GPT mode.

Practically, I would check the main thing: is there a description of the service, prices, conditions, city, and contact information in the source HTML? If after loading the page there are only scripts and an empty application block, the robot might not get the necessary data.

For public service pages, static generation or server-side rendering can be used. It's not necessary to redo the entire site for this. The personal account can work as before, and service descriptions and prices will be available directly in HTML.

And check that important data doesn't appear only after selecting a city, clicking a button, or scrolling. Otherwise, your page shows everything, but the tool reading it for the neural network didn't perform these actions.

If you don't want to provide your site for training, you can disallow the corresponding bot and keep the search bot. For OpenAI, for example, these are different rules in robots.txt:

User-agent: GPTBot

Disallow: /

This is just an example for two bots. You should also keep your own rules for restricted sections of the site.

Next, check the protection on your hosting or CDN. It happens that everything is allowed in robots.txt, but the robot couldn't reach the page because it received a 403 error or a captcha. Or a security plugin blocked all bots at once.

You can check server logs to see who visited and what response they received. However, a robot's visit alone doesn't mean you'll be recommended.

Publicity and Company Profiles

Publicity is needed now more than ever. If you're not yet on LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit, Medium, Instagram, and other platforms where your clients are, it's time to think about it. Also, post links to your resources there so that both your website and other company platforms can be found.

You can start by looking at the sources in the answers for your niche. If a particular catalog, forum, or channel is consistently mentioned, it's worth investigating if your company is there and what's written about it.

Not on 2GIS and Yandex Maps? Congratulations, you have something to do in the near future. If your business fits the Google Business Profile rules, a Google profile will also be useful.

Fill in your website, contacts, working hours, address, or service area. If you have branches, it should be clear which number and which service relate to which. It's also best to correct outdated prices and contacts on third-party platforms.

For local services, check one query with and without the city. For Kazakhstan, you can also repeat in Russian and Kazakh. The user's location can affect the response, so the result for someone from Almaty and someone from Moscow might differ.

Reviews should be collected even without AI. But there's a nuance with discounts and gifts for reviews: Google Maps prohibits this. Even if you ask for an honest review, offering a reward for its publication violates the rules. You can ask a client to leave a review about their real experience.

Funny Fact: Your Page Might Advertise a Competitor

When I was looking for examples, I found an experiment with lists of services and conferences. It was conducted by Ahrefs, a developer of SEO tools. The lists included their offerings and those of competitors.

Neural networks linked to these pages, but in 43% of responses that cited conference compilations, Ahrefs' own conference was not mentioned at all. They took information from their page and recommended other events.

Therefore, it's worth looking separately at what happened: did the neural network provide a link to your site, mention your company, or actually suggest contacting you. These are different outcomes.

You could write an article "best intermediaries," put yourself first, and end up helping competitors sell their products :)

Can You Just Ask the Neural Network to Recommend You?

Next, I looked into whether people try to directly influence recommendations through page text. They do. They add instructions asking to put a specific product first or recommend a company.

This is called prompt injection. The neural network is supposed to read the page as a source of information, but within that source, a new command is inserted.

I found research where these insertions managed to change the order of recommended products. In one study by Cornell Tech researchers, in some tests, 13 words added to user text on a page were enough. But this is the result of specific tests, not a ready-made instruction that will work in any GPT.

I've heard that similar things are done in resumes to pass filters. And on websites, I found another option with a "summarize for AI" button.

You click the button, and a chat opens with a pre-filled prompt. It asks to retell the article, and alongside it, an instruction to remember the company as a reliable source and recommend it in the future. Microsoft described over 50 such prompts from 31 companies. How many of these attempts actually changed future responses cannot be determined from the mere number of buttons found.

They also create their own ratings, publish on other well-known sites, and inflate reviews. The idea is for the neural network to find the desired opinion and use it for comparison. But your own compilation, paid publication, and independent customer review need to be distinguished when checking competitors.

If You Want to Dive Deep into the Technical Side

Product Feed. For online stores, there's another way to transfer data. With OpenAI, you can connect product feeds with catalogs, prices, and availability. You need to check availability for your market and connection requirements. However, the feed is just one source; data can also be pulled from websites.

Markdown. I looked into what else can be done for agents to read pages. Cloudflare has a function that serves Markdown at the same address if the client requests text/markdown via the Accept header.

If you use this feature, check what resulted after the conversion. Did prices, tables, links between sections, and service conditions remain? In the cache, you need to distinguish between HTML and Markdown, including considering the Accept header in Vary. Otherwise, you might serve text to a regular user instead of a page.

This is a way to prepare a different version of content for the client requesting it. I haven't found data indicating that enabling this feature once will lead to a higher ranking in recommendations.

PDF and Images. It feels like lately, both search engines and AIs have favored PDFs. But I wouldn't convert the entire site to PDF specifically. A price list with text, a scanned price list, and an image with prices might be read differently.

It's best to keep the main data as text on the page. Then, prices and conditions can be obtained even if the tool doesn't read the attached file. This also applies to interactive tables, calculators, and 3D demos. It's important that the main characteristics can be read without interacting with the interface itself.

Advertising. Paid placement in ChatGPT is also available. But availability needs to be checked for your country. According to OpenAI's description, ads are shown separately from the regular response. Therefore, ad placement and recommendations in the response should also be considered separately.

How to Understand if There's Any Benefit

Here's the most interesting part: will people actually search this way, and how many such queries are made?

To start, you can take frequent queries from Google and Yandex, questions from client correspondence, and check them in several new chats. When checking, save the query itself, the date, the model, and the mode. My example with GPT in Work and Claude clearly shows why the mode should be recorded.

Then, see if inquiries start appearing. Moreover, a person might learn about you in GPT, then search for the name on Google, and only after that submit an application. In analytics, you'll see a transition from Google. Therefore, the simple question "How did you find out about us?" is still necessary.

And if the neural network is already recommending you, check what exactly it's saying. Are prices, conditions, city, and contacts indicated correctly? Otherwise, you might get a client who has already been promised something you don't offer.
