Where the idea came from

This summer, we held a Security Week. I've already discussed one of the formats from that week – a CTF for those not working in cybersecurity – in this article. It was well-received, but CTFs are ultimately about individual tasks. In the feedback for Security Week, someone mentioned wanting "something more," and I also wanted to create a story that draws you in, where one small detail leads to the next.

So, on July 31st, I made the first commit – 1,544 lines, gray cells, and two little figures. Just 74 days later, on October 12th, at the start of the Security Awareness month, the game was released to all Avito employees.

Between these dates were over 100 hours of work after hours and on weekends. It was my personal initiative, but I did it for work: the game solves the very problem our team exists to address.

Why a game if there's a course

We have a mandatory information security course. It's necessary, and we don't plan to get rid of it. However, a simple course with a test has its limits – it only checks if a person knows the rule. But a "real" attack in a game format will test if they can act correctly under pressure, when their supposed boss calls, when an invoice looks normal.

At the same time, it was important for me to create a format where a person finds themselves in a situation where they are asked to dictate a code, and they have to decide what to do about it themselves. They make a mistake, see the consequences, and learn.

This led to three requirements for the game.

  • Safe to make mistakes. An incorrect version can be corrected without any penalties. If you punish mistakes, people will just start guessing the right answer instead of thinking.

  • An attack is a chain. Rarely does an attack fit into a single email. More often, it's a series of seemingly harmless details leading to serious losses. I wanted this chain to extend through the entire task in the game.

  • Teach action, not just knowledge. After finishing the game, an Avito employee should not just "know about phishing," but know that they need to take a specific action – "report it according to the established algorithm."
