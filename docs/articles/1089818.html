Hello, Habr!

If you've read previous articles, you know Giga Writer: hold down a key, speak, release, and the text appears where the cursor is. The GigaAM speech recognition model from Sber works directly on the device, without the internet.

First it was Mac, then Windows, and I didn't even think about Android. I thought dictation was more relevant on a computer, for typing emails, documents, the phone was beyond my vision.

A message I wasn't expecting

And then Denio1337 writes to me, "I've made Writer for Android. What do you say?"

He sent a ready-made application, recognition by the same model directly on the phone, and text insertion into any field.

I installed it, used it for a couple of days, and realized I was using it seriously. I catch myself writing more on my phone than I thought, and the on-screen keyboard is not the most convenient tool for this, nor are messengers, notes, or email replies.

Then we polished it a bit, redesigned the interface in the Android style, added a wave indicator above the field, created a first-time setup wizard, and added the Brain.

How it works

There are two ways to dictate, choose whichever is more convenient for you.

Floating button. A green circular button on top of applications. Hold it, speak, release, and the text goes where the cursor is while you speak.

Volume button. Hold down the volume down button in any input field and speak, release, and the text is in place. You don't even need to look at the phone: a short vibration will tell you when recording starts and ends, a short press of the volume button still just lowers the volume. The text is entered directly into the field, without a clipboard; what you copied yourself doesn't disappear anywhere, it works in Telegram, WhatsApp, notes, browser, email.

No more copying back and forth

The most frequent feedback I hear is something like: "I used to type text, copy it to a translator or a chat with a neural network, ask it to correct it, copy it back, and paste it." Sound familiar?

Now it's done by voice, right in place. Dictate the text and at the end say:

  • "...Writer, make it shorter";

  • "...Writer, translate to English";

  • "...Writer, make it more polite".

The field will then contain the finished result. If you don't like it, there's a "Revert" button above the field, and it will restore what you dictated.

And with "Edit on the fly" enabled, the neural network automatically polishes every dictation: it removes "uhs," repetitions, and slips of the tongue, and adds punctuation.

The Brain on Android only works via the cloud; I consciously didn't add an offline model like on Mac and Windows. A phone is significantly weaker than a computer, and a 2GB model would take a long time to process each phrase and drain the battery. However, the cloud responds in a second or two. You insert your DeepSeek, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or other service key, and Writer will automatically figure out whose key it is, and everything will be sent. The key is stored encrypted only on the phone. Without the Brain, the text doesn't go anywhere at all; everything is recognized on the phone itself.
