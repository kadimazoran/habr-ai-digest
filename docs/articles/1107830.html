OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet, a new payment feature that allows AI agents to make purchases on behalf of users. The company has partnered with major card issuers like American Express, Mastercard, and Visa.

ChatGPT Wallet enables users to securely store their credit card details and grant AI agents the authority to make online payments without revealing their actual card information. For example, if a user selects a hotel for an upcoming trip, they can instruct their AI agent to complete the booking using this feature.

Here's how ChatGPT Wallet and the payment process work:

  • Users can add their credit cards through a secure form in ChatGPT Wallet settings or provide payment details upon the AI agent's request directly at checkout;

  • After adding a card, users no longer need to enter payment details for every purchase;

  • The AI agent finds the desired product or service and prepares the purchase;

  • Before finalizing the transaction, ChatGPT Wallet displays information about the seller, the list of items, the total amount, and the selected card for payment;

  • The user reviews the details and confirms the transaction using biometric authentication, such as Face ID or fingerprint scan;

  • Upon confirmation, the payment system generates a token containing a virtual card number, security code, and expiration date;

  • The AI agent uses this token to complete the purchase without accessing the user's actual card details or sharing them with third parties.

In this process, ChatGPT Wallet acts solely as an interface; the payment is actually debited from the user's credit card. This allows users to retain all card-related bonuses and privileges, as well as benefit from cardholder protection in disputed situations. Although the agent makes the payment, the user still interacts directly with the seller, who is responsible for pricing, order fulfillment, returns, funds, and customer support. Currently, users must confirm each purchase, but OpenAI plans to enable agents to make purchases autonomously in the future, within pre-set limits.

While ChatGPT Wallet uses payment tokens compatible with existing payment systems, OpenAI acknowledges that some websites may block AI agents or reject legitimate transactions due to fraud prevention systems. For instance, Amazon blocked Meta's* Muse agent from making purchases on Amazon.com on behalf of users. To address such issues, OpenAI recommends that companies configure their websites to recognize authorized AI agents and collaborate with payment providers to reduce unjustified transaction rejections. Additionally, OpenAI is working with fraud prevention specialists (including Forter, Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd) and payment processors like Adyen, Checkout.com, and Stripe.

The ChatGPT Wallet feature is becoming available to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US who are 18 years or older. To use the service, a US-issued credit card and a US billing address are required. Supported cards include those from American Express, Chase, Mastercard, and Visa.

It is important to note that ChatGPT Wallet currently only works within "agents" (dots). The feature is not available in regular ChatGPT chats, the ChatGPT Work version, or separate Codex sessions. Furthermore, virtual cards cannot be added to the wallet at the launch stage.

In June, Visa announced that it was integrating its network with ChatGPT, empowering the chatbot to make purchases and complete transactions independently.

In May, OpenAI itself launched ChatGPT for personal finance management. Users can now link their bank accounts to the chatbot and consult on payments, subscriptions, investments, portfolio performance, and other financial matters.
