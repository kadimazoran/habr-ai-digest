Google is testing short page summaries directly in source cards within AI Overviews. According to Search Engine Roundtable, these summaries appear in cards to the right and below the AI Overviews response: a person reads what the neural network said about the page, and only then decides whether to click. Essentially, this is a new snippet (a short description of the page in search results) that an AI model writes for the website owner. Therefore, it is now worth checking the first screen of the page and the direct answers on it: if the summary doesn't make it clear why to click, there will be no click.

What Google writes about the page in the AI Overviews source card

In the AI Overviews source card, an AI summary, i.e., a retelling of the page compiled by a neural network, may now appear instead of the page description. Brodie Clark, an SEO specialist who noticed the test, described the main difference from the usual snippet: the text in the card does not match the page verbatim. Google retells what it considers most relevant to the query. Clark stated he had not noticed such retellings in AI Overviews citations before. Since the retelling is tailored to the query, one page will likely receive different descriptions for different questions.

Whether Google takes text from the description, from markup, or only from the page, there is no precise answer. However, it is known how Google constructs AI answers in general. In the help section for AI Mode, Google's AI search mode, the company writes that answers are based on found web pages to be more reliable. This grounding of the answer in found pages is called grounding. It also describes query fan-out: AI Mode divides the question into subtopics, searches for each in parallel, and collects the results into one answer. In its May guide to optimizing for generative AI, Google, as reported by Search Engine Land, confirmed that it can extract relevant information from pages in its index. I previously analyzed which pages Google cites for Russian queries: it takes half of its sources from outside the top results.

AI retellings in AI Overviews cards are currently a test and not visible for all queries

The summaries in the source cards are currently a test. Google has not announced an official launch, reports Topvisor Journal, and the feature does not appear for every search. Barry Schwartz from Search Engine Roundtable also saw summaries only for a portion of his test queries. The countries, languages, and devices for the test have not been named in publications about it.

I would not wait for the official launch to work on the page: the AI Overviews block itself is appearing more and more frequently. According to Comscore, in June it was present in 39.4% of desktop Google searches in the US, and in July 2025, it was in 25.8%. The block where summaries are being tested is now seen in two out of five searches.

Which websites are affected by Google's AI Overviews in search results (and is it visible from Russia)

This topic is important for websites that receive a significant portion of their traffic from Google: those with a foreign or English-speaking audience and in niches where customers search specifically on Google. If your customers use Yandex search and Alice AI, there is no urgency.

There is no precise answer as to whether users from Russia see AI Overviews. According to a study by Aral, Li, and Zuo, the coverage of AI Overviews grew from 7 countries in 2024 to 229 in 2025. France, Turkey, China, and Cuba remained without it, and the study abstract does not mention Russia. There is no official information about AI Overviews being shown in Russia, so check if the block appears for your queries.

The easiest way to see what Google writes about your page is directly in the search results: enter your queries and open the source cards in the AI Overviews block. The block cannot be disabled, Google writes. However, the Web filter shows only text links without AI blocks, so it is easy to compare the regular search results with the AI response.

Checklist: what to check on your page to make the AI summary in the source card encourage clicks

It is worth checking what the AI model will extract from the page: the first screen and direct answers under subheadings. The summary in the source card acts as a new snippet written by an AI model; it is unknown where it gets the text from, so you should check the page itself, not just the description. A website that clearly formulates its essence and the benefit of visiting at the very beginning of the page will receive a coherent summary. A website with vague introductory text will have its value diminished by the summary even before the click: a person will read an empty retelling and open a neighboring source.

It is not yet known if the summary can be controlled through search description settings, so influencing the retelling remains through the page text itself. Pages that are found both for the main query and its subtopics are more likely to appear in the sources. According to data cited by Search Engine Land, such pages are 161% more likely to be included in AI Overview citations than pages that only address the main query, based on 173,902 URLs.

  1. Ignore the title and description and read only the first screen of the page. Write in two sentences what it is about, for whom, and what the user will gain by visiting it. If you can't do it, the AI model won't be able to either.

  2. Find the first sentence of the main text. If it's about the company's history or general market talk, and the core information starts in the fourth paragraph, move the core information to the top.

  3. List what is on the page beyond what fits into the summary: price, table, calculator, file, conditions. This is the reason to click. If everything important fits into two sentences and there's nothing to add, the summary will replace the click.

  4. At the beginning of each section under a subheading, place a direct answer in the first sentence, followed by justification. Let the subheading be the reader's question or a thesis understandable without adjacent sections.

  5. If numbers, conditions, and names are currently only in an image or PDF, duplicate them as text on the page.

  6. Check the title, description, and the first paragraph: they should talk about the same thing. If they promise different things, the summary will be a mess.

  7. Paste the first screen into any AI model and ask it to summarize it in two sentences for someone looking for your topic. Google creates its summary with its own model and for a specific query, so the text will be different, but it will show what the reader will take away from the page.

  8. Enter your queries into Google, open AI Overviews, and look at the cards on the right or below. Try several formulations: summaries are not visible for all queries.

This checklist is mine; it is compiled from how Google describes grounding its answers in pages, as there is no data yet on summaries in the cards.

Will clicks to your site decrease if it appears in AI Overviews sources

There is no data yet on clicks specifically from the summaries in the cards, but there is data on the block as a whole. According to Seer Interactive, as reported by Search Engine Land, a citation in AI Overview yields 120% more organic clicks per impression than appearing in search results with this block without a citation. However, queries with a citation still receive 38% fewer clicks per impression than queries without AI Overview.

If we take clicks without AI Overview as 100, pages with a citation would have 62, and pages without it around 28. This is my recalculation from Seer's two figures, showing the order of magnitude. Appearing in the sources is beneficial: more than double the clicks. What's regrettable is that even with a citation, a page loses more than a third of its clicks compared to search results without the AI block.

The summary in the card adds another filter after the citation: the page is already in the sources, but the retelling might not provide a reason to visit. The click will go to the website whose first screen immediately communicates what is there and why one should go there. It would be interesting to know if these summaries match the first screen of the page for those who are already seeing them in their search results.
