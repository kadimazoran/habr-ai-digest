Comparing TabPFN-2.5 and CatBoost on Bank Data and Calculating the Cost of Quality Improvement

When I started the experiment, the hypothesis was simple: the more applications in training, the better the model. This sounds obvious, but the data showed otherwise.

Let's take three training sets, each with exactly 500 default applications. The first has only 2,000 applications (25% defaults), the second has 5,000 (10%), and the third has 50,000 (1%). CatBoost shows almost the same quality on these: ROC AUC 0.683, 0.681, and 0.686. The sample size differs by 25 times, but the result is the same. Only the number of defaults coincided.

The Task: Two Errors with Different Costs

A bank decides whether to issue a loan and can make two types of errors. Type I error: a loan was issued to a client who later defaulted. This is a direct loss for the bank, and it's the primary concern in operations. Type II error: a loan was denied to a client who would have repaid it, meaning the bank lost potential profit. To compare models fairly, I didn't use a 0.5 threshold because the probabilities from models trained on datasets with different default rates are biased. Instead, I fixed the rejection rate: a threshold is found on the validation set that rejects 10% of the riskiest applications, and this threshold is then applied to the test set.
