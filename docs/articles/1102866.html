Ensemble Distillation into a Single Model

Motivation

Our early internal ensemble combined several families of 5-fold models, including ConvNeXt, CoAtNet, and ResNet-GRU. Its inference took about 7 hours, leaving almost no room within the 9-hour limit to combine it with other strong but heavy models, such as the global/local branch with MedSAM guidance. I wanted to compress the knowledge of this ensemble into a single lightweight model.

Method

  • Teacher. Out-of-fold predictions of the early ensemble on all training studies. Each study was predicted only by models that had not seen it during training, so the soft targets did not leak training labels.

  • Targets. A 50/50 mix of probability labels derived from LLM (from radiology reports) and the teacher's out-of-fold probabilities.

  • Model. ConvNeXt-Tiny, pre-trained on ImageNet-22k, with a slice-attention head.

  • Input: 140 mm field of view, resized to 384 px.

  • Windows: 8 windows, sampled per series during training, 16 during inference.

  • Data: trained once on all non-Gold studies. 58 Gold studies were held out.

  • Training. 32 epochs, EMA of weights, pairwise ranking loss added to BCE, and separate learning rates for the backbone and head.

Blending Three Final Models

What We Compared

We blended three models with different inductive biases, after transforming each model's predictions into ranks for each finding. We compared three methods for selecting blend weights:

  1. Fixed Weights. A single set of weights, common to all 12 findings. We tried four variants.

  2. Weights per Finding, Optimized on Gold. For each finding, grid search for weights that maximize AUC on the Gold set.

  3. Shrinkage Towards Equal Weights. For each finding:

    w = a · w_gold + (1 − a) · w_equal, where a = n / (n + k)

    • n is the minimum of the number of positive and negative examples for this finding in the Gold set, so rare findings are pulled more strongly towards equal weights.

    • k = 29 was chosen by repeated splits of the Gold set into two halves: weights were tuned on one half and evaluated on the other.

Observations

  • Choice of fixed weights mattered little. Different fixed weights shifted the private LB by no more than 0.001. Higher weight for the strongest single model improved the public score but not the private.

  • Tuning to the Gold set did not transfer. The better the weights were tuned to the Gold set, the higher the score on Gold and the lower on public. Weights per finding, tuned directly to the Gold set, performed best on Gold and worst on the public LB, and split-half CV already showed they performed worse than fixed weights.

  • Shrinkage was safe but not better. It removed most of the overfitting and matched fixed weights without surpassing them.

  • Gold did not allow ranking of blending strategies. With 58 studies, the Gold set could not reliably distinguish these strategies. Therefore, we used fixed weights and, as in shin0000's part, ultimately trusted the public leaderboard for the final selection.

Combining Global and Local Views with MedSAM

Motivation

I combined a model that captures the broad context of the knee with specialized models that examine smaller, finding-relevant regions. The goal was to preserve local details that might become less apparent when the entire image is downscaled.

I used a frozen, pre-trained MedSAM model to estimate an anatomical anchor for cropping. I did not fine-tune MedSAM, nor did I ask it to segment the pathologies themselves. The final branch consisted of one global model and five specialists for specific findings and achieved 0.964 public / 0.953 private LB on its own.

Six-Model Architecture Diagram

Figure 1. Branches using only the global view bypass MedSAM. Other specialists use local crops, and where indicated, global views as well.

MedSAM-Guided Crops

I normalized slice order and intensities and presented a 160 mm field of view as a 576 × 576 image. For each sagittal or coronal series, MedSAM segmented its middle slice. The top boundary of the largest bone mask component provided an anchor from which I placed crops with fixed relative offsets.

Each local crop was 288 × 288 pixels, approximately 80 mm in width. There were three sagittal positions—center, anterior, and posterior—and two coronal positions, side A and side B. Coordinates were reused for all slices in a series and for all corresponding specialists. An unstable mask included a fixed fallback anchor, and out-of-frame regions were padded with zeros.

Sides A/B were not assumed to consistently correspond to medial/lateral. The sagittal anterior/posterior position names similarly describe heuristics in image coordinates rather than individually verified anatomical orientation in each case.

Real Crop Examples

Figure 2. Five local crop positions and the target findings of the specialist models that use them. For each position, the original image and the extracted crop are shown. Crop positions are reused by different models; findings are the models' targets, not diagnostic labels of these example images.

Model Inputs and Target Findings

The system consisted of one global model predicting all 12 findings and five specialists covering distinct groups of findings, as shown above. Some specialists combined local crops with global views, while the bone specialist used only global views.

Model and Training

All six models used independently initialized ConvNeXt-Tiny backbones pre-trained on ImageNet, residual bidirectional GRUs for slice sequence, and finding-specific attention. I trained on all 4,349 non-Gold studies. Gold58 was excluded from gradient updates but used for model selection.

During training, I sampled 16 slices per series; during inference, I used all actual slices of each admitted series.

Per-Finding Blending and Gold Set Development

I blended the predictions of the global model and the corresponding specialist with a separate weight for each finding:

prediction = (1 - w) × global_prediction + w × specialist_prediction

Instead of using equal blending for each finding, I tuned the coefficients on the Gold set labeled by physicians. For the release trained on all data, I kept the coefficients chosen for an earlier Fold0 branch, without re-optimizing them.

The competition evaluates predictions against physician-assigned finding labels, not a unique label perfectly reflecting underlying medical truth. My intention in referring to Gold was to align the combination of different model views with the reading criteria reflected in those evaluation labels.

This is not a claim that the Gold labels were incorrect, nor proof that the intended alignment was achieved. Gold58 was used for both model selection and weight tuning, so I considered it feedback during development, not independent evidence of CV/test level. With only 58 studies, I could not establish whether the chosen weights generalized or were simply overfitted to this small set. For the final configuration selection, I ultimately applied a "trust the public LB" approach.

BA440-C512-WS1: Training and Inference

This model was part of the second-best (non-winning) submission.

Backbone. CoAtNet-2 (timm coatnet_rmlp_2_rw_384.sw_in12k_ft_in1k) at 512 px with single-slice windows and an attention-MIL head, trained on 5 folds on BA440 labels. Public/private LB 0.960/0.948 (equal average of 5 folds). The recipe is based on the public dreaddevelopment notebook for twelve findings. The body geometry follows the public "Raptor" 0.949 notebook. More complex architectures, such as a 3-branch backbone with independent weights for each plane or adding per-plane attention weights, did not improve the score. Smaller models like ConvNeXT-T or ConvNeXT-S gave us lower scores (around −0.002 on both OOF and LB).

dread_v5_512_d96n Body (built from DICOM; same code runs during training and in the kernel)

  • Each study is turned into 96 rows in 5 slots: sagittal fluid-sensitive — 26, sagittal other — 22, coronal fluid-sensitive — 18, coronal other — 12, axial — 18. Each slot takes one series, chosen by plane and fluid-sensitive flag, with fallback to any series of that plane. A missing slot remains empty and is masked.

  • Slices are sorted by position along the slice normal. Slot slices are taken via linspace over 2–98% of the stack.

  • Pixel processing steps: modality LUT, MONOCHROME1 inversion, windowing to 2/98 percentiles for each series, center crop of 140 mm by each slice's own PixelSpacing, then a single INTER_AREA resize to 512. Stored as uint8.

  • Everything resides in a single memmap of size 4407 × 96 × 512 × 512.

Model

  • Each window is a single slice with 1 input channel. The first convolution is reassembled for 1 channel by summing pre-trained RGB filters. Normalization uses the mean from ImageNet means and std.

  • The backbone embeds each window independently. This is followed by LayerNorm, then per-class window attention (Linear 256 → tanh → dropout 0.2 → Linear 12, softmax over windows). Each class gets its own pooled feature and its own linear output, so the head outputs 12 logits.

Training (per fold, ~5.3 h on a single RTX 5090)

  • 5 folds on a fixed random split: 3525 train / 882 held out. BA440 soft targets (0, 1) are fed directly into BCEWithLogits with pos_weight by prevalence; they are not binarized.

  • At each step, K = 12 random windows are sampled per study. A step covers 8 studies (2 × 4 with gradient accumulation of 2). Higher values of K, up to 32, improve the score for small models but have no effect or a slight negative effect for the CoAtNet model.

  • Optimizer and schedule: AdamW with LR 3e-5 for backbone and 1e-3 for head, wd 0.02, OneCycle with 15% warm-up, 24 epochs, bf16, grad clip 3.0, gradient checkpointing, intensity augmentation ±10%, seed 42.

  • Auxiliary loss: SIGReg (isotropic Gaussian regularizer from LeJEPA), weight 0.02, applied via a 256-dim MLP projector on window embeddings. The projector is discarded after training. The effect of this auxiliary loss is unstable across different model architectures: it seems to improve the score for ConvNeXT-T, whereas for the CoAtNet model, its effect is neutral.

  • Final weights are SWA (simple average) of epochs 21–23.

Inference (Kaggle, 2×T4)

Test DICOM goes through the same body builder. Each window from a single slice is used, up to 96 per study (the "kall" setting). Each of the 5 fold SWA models runs in fp16 on chunks of 16 windows, with folds distributed across both GPUs. Sigmoid outputs are averaged with equal weight across folds. No TTA.
