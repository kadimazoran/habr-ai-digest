Google's September spam update ran from September 24 to October 8, lasting almost 14 days for all languages. This is five times longer than any of the three previous spam updates in 2026 (summary from Relevant Audience). If part of a website's pages are written by a neural network, the question is whether they look like mass production. I check this with a short Python script, and I choose the similarity threshold myself: Google doesn't have one.

What Signs of Mass Page Production Can Be Measured by Code

With code, I measure one thing: how similar pages are to each other. I break down text into shingles, which are overlapping chunks of ten consecutive words. The shingles themselves, similarity, and containment are defined in Broder's article "On the resemblance and containment of documents"; the ten-word length was adopted by the authors of "Syntactic Clustering of the Web" (link below). I compare the shingle sets of two pages using two numbers.

Jaccard similarity: the number of common shingles divided by the total number of shingles in both pages. It is symmetrical and suitable for comparing pairs. Containment: the proportion of shingles from page A that are present in page B. It is asymmetrical and detects a short page entirely contained within a longer template.

There is no spam threshold defined by either Google or Broder. The only justified threshold I found is 0.5: with this, the authors of "Syntactic Clustering of the Web" clustered 30 million AltaVista pages. This is a threshold used by researchers. Google's scale for near-duplicates is only mentioned in a patent for SimHash, and it pertains to website crawling, not spam.

Python Script to Find Nearly Identical Pages

import re from itertools import combinations def shingles(text, w=10): words = re.findall(r"\w+", text.lower()) return {" ".join(words[i:i + w]) for i in range(len(words) - w + 1)} def jaccard(a, b): return len(a & b) / max(len(a | b), 1) def containment(a, b): return len(a & b) / max(len(a), 1) sh = {url: shingles(text) for url, text in pages.items()} # pages: {address: main text} for (u1, a), (u2, b) in combinations(sh.items(), 2): print(f"{u1:18} {u2:18} jaccard={jaccard(a, b):.2f} " f"in={containment(a, b):.2f} out={containment(b, a):.2f}")

The example is synthetic: three delivery pages generated from the same template with different cities (one long sentence added for Omsk) and an article about returning goods.

/delivery/kazan    /delivery/perm     jaccard=0.69 in=0.82 out=0.82 /delivery/kazan    /delivery/omsk     jaccard=0.46 in=0.82 out=0.52 /delivery/kazan    /blog/returns      jaccard=0.00 in=0.00 out=0.00 /delivery/perm     /delivery/omsk     jaccard=0.46 in=0.82 out=0.52 /delivery/perm     /blog/returns      jaccard=0.00 in=0.00 out=0.00 /delivery/omsk     /blog/returns      jaccard=0.00 in=0.00 out=0.00

Kazan and Perm differ by one word, but their Jaccard similarity is 0.69: the city name affects ten consecutive windows. Omsk gets 0.46, below the historical 0.5, even though 82% of the Kazan page is contained within the Omsk page. Therefore, I look for templates based on containment, and use Jaccard for comparing pairs.

How to Choose a Similarity Threshold and What to Do with Found Pages

  1. Run your generated pages through the script and examine the distribution of containment, not a single number. In pages, include only the main text of the page, excluding menus, footers, and breadcrumbs: global blocks create common shingles for all pages and inflate containment. You can extract the main text using libraries like trafilatura. Pairwise comparison grows quadratically: it's sufficient for small sites, but for thousands of pages, it's replaced by MinHash signatures from datasketch.

  2. Set the threshold based on your own site: visually inspect 20-30 pairs around your chosen mark and decide where pages do not convey different content.

  3. For the identified groups, ask about their usefulness, not how they were written: what does the reader learn on the Omsk page that is not on the Kazan page. If there's no answer, merge with a neighboring page or add more content.
