In 2022, our team had a specific task: to prepare a local replacement for the global CRM so that employees could continue their usual work without interruption. The system was already part of daily processes. It was used to plan visits to healthcare professionals, record interaction results, and manage communications.

The main driver for localization was the risk of the global solution being disconnected. But there were other reasons too: the desire for more flexibility in system development, simplification of certain processes, and consolidation of several existing local solutions into a single platform.

At the time, no one was thinking about a digital assistant, artificial intelligence, automatic visit planning, or migrating other corporate systems into the CRM. The primary goal was to maintain business continuity and gain control over the platform's future development.

Several years later, it became clear that the outcome was far more ambitious than initially conceived.

Why was the new CRM named NOVA?

Almost immediately after the project started, the question arose: what to name the new system?

Within the team, an unofficial version of the name's origin emerged, inspired by a comic book superhero. His ability to handle vast amounts of information, quickly navigate complex situations, and integrate different system elements resonated with the future CRM's tasks.

The new platform was also intended to consolidate data, processes, and interaction channels, helping employees find information faster and make decisions.

Over time, the name NOVA became established within the company and gradually replaced the initial designation "local CRM.".

The Platform Choice That Defined the Project's Development

When selecting a local solution, the team evaluated not only current functionality. The criteria included implementation timelines, cost, technology level, support capabilities, and development prospects. With limited time, it was particularly important to choose a platform that guaranteed not only a quick launch but also stable development.

After evaluating several options, the team settled on a platform that most fully met the project's requirements and had the necessary capabilities for further development. An external technology partner was engaged for its implementation.

Over time, the advantages of the chosen solution became even more apparent. The platform's flexibility allowed the team to go beyond the initial tasks and implement functionality that was not present in the previous global CRM.

The First Project Beyond the Global CRM

One of NOVA's new development directions became working with information about the key stages of a disease's progression: from identification and diagnosis to the selection of therapeutic approaches. This perspective helps to better understand the specifics of medical practice at different stages and consider them when planning professional interactions with healthcare professionals.

The development of this area became one of the first examples of how NOVA went beyond the functionality of the previous global CRM. It was then that it became clear that the platform could not only replicate existing scenarios but also serve as a foundation for new solutions.

How Did NOVA Grow 10x in a Few Months?

Throughout 2023, alongside CRM development, the team prepared documentation and developed a mobile application practically from scratch. This was one of the key tasks, as most employees primarily use the CRM on mobile devices.

In the fourth quarter of 2023, the first working version (MVP) was launched for 120 employees from two pilot teams. Based on the testing results, feedback was collected, identified shortcomings were fixed, and necessary changes were made. The next stage was employee training, and by the end of the year, about 1200 people had mastered working with the new CRM.

At the beginning of 2024, users completely abandoned the global system and began working exclusively in NOVA. By July 2024, the transition for all employees who had previously used the global solution was completed. For approximately 200 medical advisors and drug experts, specialized functionality was additionally developed, focusing on scientific topics.

Why Was Synchronization the Most Difficult Task?

After the launch, the main difficulties arose not in business processes or even interfaces.

The mobile application required complex integration between the device, the mobile app, the system's backend, and the web version of the CRM. Any non-standard situation could affect the data exchange process. The team encountered scenarios rarely described in documentation: changes in date on a tablet, app being unloaded from device memory, operation with unstable connection, and other peculiarities of real-world usage.

Therefore, a significant part of the first quarter of 2024 was dedicated to improving the stability of the mobile application. The experience gained helped refine approaches to testing and documentation, as well as pay more attention to real user scenarios.

How NOVA Integrated Part of the Work Processes?

As the system evolved, new integrations began to appear.

One of the first was integration with the call center. Employees of a partner company gained the ability to work directly in the CRM through a specialized set of functions. This significantly simplified the process of setting goals and tracking results.

The next step was integration with a provider of publicly available data on healthcare professionals (HCPs) and their workplaces. This allowed for the decommissioning of a separate data exchange system and the migration of its functionality directly into the CRM. With the HCP's consent for personal data processing, system users gained the ability to promptly request the provider to update publicly available data.

As NOVA developed, it was integrated with other corporate services and communication tools. This enabled employees to access necessary information within a single system, without switching between multiple applications.

The consolidation of previously disparate processes also simplified preparation for interactions with healthcare professionals and the execution of daily tasks.

From Printed Materials to Personalized Digital Content

Another area of NOVA's development was the digitalization of interactions with healthcare professionals.

NOVA introduced QR codes that simplified registration on the corporate web portal and feedback collection after visits. This allowed for the replacement of some printed materials with digital ones. Concurrently, the team developed content management tools. Employees can now find and select materials faster, tailored to specific professional tasks and discussion topics with healthcare professionals.

Another direction involved working on AI tools for interactive presentations. The team is exploring their application for evaluating the use of digital materials and improving professional communication formats. Before implementation, such solutions undergo necessary internal reviews and approvals.

AIRIS and Artificial Intelligence in Daily Work

By 2026, the volume of data within the system had become so large that the main task shifted from accumulation to analysis.

As early as the year NOVA was launched, it included a digital assistant named AIRIS. Initially conceived as a CRM assistant, it gradually evolved into a unified access point for company knowledge. AIRIS is implemented as a chatbot and is available in both the mobile and web versions of the system. It helps find information not only about the CRM but also about other company systems and processes.

AI has also begun to be used by the development teams themselves. Analysts and testers are already using models to prepare documentation and test reports, and in the future, similar tools are planned for use in software code development.

Among the promising directions for NOVA's development is the "AI Overview" tool. This tool is being developed to help employees navigate their available work information faster and get a brief summary before their next interaction with a healthcare professional.

It is expected that AI will help reduce the time spent searching for and studying necessary information, allowing the employee to focus on preparing for the upcoming visit.

When Does CRM Start Planning an Employee's Work?

The next stage of the platform's development involved intelligent visit planning.

Today, NOVA helps employees plan work visits and track task completion. In the future, the team plans to develop intelligent visit planning. It is expected that the system will be able to suggest a schedule considering contact frequency, work tasks, employee vacations, visit geography, and even traffic conditions. If necessary, the user can independently modify the proposed route or schedule.

Event Automation and Digital Compliance

One of the largest development areas has been event automation.

In 2026, a dedicated team is transferring functions previously available in another corporate system to the CRM. This will reduce the number of tools used and simplify event preparation and support.

Concurrently, the team is exploring the use of AI for document processing. Promising scenarios include preliminary checks of presentations for compliance with corporate requirements and information extraction from event documents. These tools are designed to reduce manual work and simplify the preparation of materials for approval. However, final decisions remain with the responsible employees.

Another area relates to managing consent for personal data processing, which is essential for interacting with healthcare professionals. NOVA supports the processes of obtaining and revoking consent, and also helps employees verify the completeness of necessary information when performing work tasks. This reduces manual operations and supports prescribed control procedures.

Developing a Proprietary Technology Platform

In 2025, the team completed another important milestone: the NOVA ecosystem was migrated to the company's local technological infrastructure. This reduced dependence on external solutions, increased system resilience, and created additional opportunities for its further development.

Over its operational period, NOVA has received recognition from the professional IT community: in 2024, the project was awarded for the best BPM project, and in 2025, it received the "Project of the Year" award. Concurrently, the team continues to develop the CRM and mobile application in collaboration with technology partners.

By 2026, two in-house development and CRM enhancement teams have been formed within the company. Another team, as mentioned above, focuses on event automation. The current work is directed not only at expanding NOVA's functionality but also at improving development processes to make new features available to users faster. Among the immediate goals is the transition from monthly releases to releasing updates every two weeks.

Instead of a Conclusion

Looking back at the project from 2022, its main objective was to reduce risks and ensure business stability.

Looking at it today, it's clear that NOVA integrates tools for employees' daily work, a mobile application, and various digital services. Furthermore, the platform's development is not limited to expanding functionality. In-house teams have been formed within the company that continue to improve the system, considering user needs and business objectives.

The project, which began as the localization of a critical system, has gradually transformed into a digital platform around which new processes, new products, and new interaction scenarios with the professional medical community are now being built. This, perhaps, is the main outcome of the last four years.
