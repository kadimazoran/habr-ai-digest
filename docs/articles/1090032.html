From January 2027, brands in the US will be able to pay for a piece of the explanation itself that the Alexa for Shopping shopping assistant gives to the buyer (Amazon Ads). This means that part of the visibility in neural networks will become purchasable, and in measurement, paid answers will have to be counted separately from organic ones. And the brief that the brand writes for Amazon, I would publish openly immediately: you can't feed it to ChatGPT or Alice AI, they only take what they found through search.

The format is called Branded Conversations, it was announced on September 29 at the unBoxed conference. The brand writes a brief about its products, and the assistant relies on it when explaining how one model differs from another. Until now, brands paid for a place next to the AI's answer.

What Amazon sells to brands in Alexa for Shopping

Alexa for Shopping is the former Rufus, Amazon's shopping assistant. In May, it was merged with Alexa+ and renamed (WeRSM). There are now two advertising formats within the assistant.

Sponsored Prompts. A paid prompt question below the assistant's answer, similar in mechanics to a sponsored position in search. It was first mentioned a year ago as sponsored questions in Rufus.

Branded Conversations. The brand fills out a brief in free form: descriptions, characteristics, usage scenarios, what products are bought together (AdExchanger). An example from Amazon's announcement: a cosmetic brand describes which cleansing is suitable for oily skin, which for sensitive, what to use after it. The buyer asks to assemble a daily skincare routine, and the assistant builds the answer based on this brief. Such a conversation is marked as sponsored, and the buyer is informed that the brand helped shape the answer.

What the brand cannot do: choose which reviews the assistant will show. If the brief contains falsehoods, Amazon promises to provide feedback in the advertising account. Whether the brief is checked before or after being shown to buyers is not stated in the announcement.

Muthu Muthukrishnan, Amazon's Vice President of Sponsored Products and Brands, formulated the task as follows: the brand should remain in the conversation, not just appear at its beginning.

The brief for Alexa and the text for ChatGPT are compiled from the same material

Alexa for Shopping operates within a single store. The catalog, reviews, and brief are stored by Amazon, so data can be fed directly to the assistant.

ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Alice AI are structured differently. When web search is enabled for them, they find pages, read them, and retell them. There is no place to feed them a brief. The brand appears in the answer if there is clear text about it on the pages that the model found and deemed reliable.

WeRSM describes what a good brief for this format should be: context, claims that can be defended, and wording that the assistant can retell without distortion. In my opinion, the same properties are needed for a page that an open model cites: a comparison with competitors and the conditions under which the product is suitable and under which it is not, with verifiable figures.

Practical conclusion: the brief that the brand prepares for Amazon should be published openly immediately. On its own website as a separate page, on industry platforms as an analysis. Then the same material can work both in paid channels and in free ones.

If you don't have Amazon, there's no one to write a brief for, but the page is the same: on your own website, compare the product with two or three competitors, stating clearly who it is suitable for and who it is not, and a similar analysis on an industry platform that neural networks cite in your niche.

How to measure visibility in neural networks when part of the answers are paid

I described the measurement scheme in a separate article. In short: a fixed list of questions where the brand is not named, mandatory web search, and saving the sources of the answer. Paid answers add one rule to this: if the answer is marked as sponsored, this label is written to the log in a separate field sponsored.
