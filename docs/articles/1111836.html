I decided to dust off the job dataset, perhaps later I'll also unpack the resume dataset, but for now, I'll stick to job postings. So, I decided to look at how things are going with neural networks and their frequency in job postings on job search platforms like hh. It seems that AI is already breaking into the technology market (as a video game enthusiast, I'm looking forward to this) and the labor market by leaps and bounds. The latter aligns more with my professional path.

So, for the material below, I took about 13 job postings over 13 months - from October 2025 to October 2026. It so happened that there are about 7 million records in total, and then it's just a matter of technique - I counted how often and for whom AI, neural networks, ChatGPT, prompts, and other new jargon are required in these vacancies. In general, what turned out, turned out.

AI Bank

Let's start with the biggest whale that has settled in these vacancies - Sber. In general, I had about 43 thousand unique vacancies in my sample where AI is required from the candidate, and Sber posted 13 thousand of them. In total, this is about 8% of all vacancies per month that I analyze (43/~538).

In January 2026, exactly half of such vacancies in the country were from Sber. Now it's 27%, from which I conclude that the rest of the market is also gradually getting into this AI.

Well, Sber gets here because they have such a template for posting vacancies and the wording:

ready to use artificial intelligence in daily work (training at the company's expense)

is present in 89% of its vacancies. Here's where it's found:

  • sales manager - 92%;

  • call center operator - 95%;

  • debt collection specialist - 95%;

  • driver-cash collector - 87%;

  • security guard - 93.5%.

In short, the future has arrived: security guards with service weapons and GigaChat. And therefore, we will look at the numbers without Sber from now on.

Who needs neural networks

What is immediately visible:

1. AI first entered the text domain. Copywriters and SMM account for about a third of vacancies, which is logical: text generation is what neural networks do best and what everyone has known about for a long time. The job of an SMM specialist simply implies that you write posts with a neural network in one form or another.

2. Then everyone who creates content for sales. This includes marketers, designers, marketplace managers, here it's 15-17%. In designer vacancies, it sounds like "knowledge of working with AI for image generation," and for marketplace managers, it will be "using AI with information verification," and so on.

3. Analysts and testers. Business analyst - 17.7%, system analyst - 15.4%, tester - 12.9%. For testers, this is the fastest growth over the year: from 3.7% to 15.8%.

4. Secretary and programmer. They equally need AI, about 12% of such vacancies. Little surprises here, as a secretary nowadays is also a personal assistant and a business assistant. The requirements there are:

proficient in Google Calendar, Google Docs, task trackers, experience with ChatGPT, Claude, Canva

proficient in AI: ChatGPT, Claude…

Neural networks and work experience

Now let's look at neural networks in conjunction with experience.

A senior with 6+ years of experience is 4.5 times more likely to have AI work skills listed in requirements than a candidate with no experience. Outside of IT, there is no such effect: it's from 1% to 2% at any experience level.
