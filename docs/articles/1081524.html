On September 11, Cognition announced the availability of Fusion in Devin Desktop and CLI. Previously, the system worked in the cloud-based Devin. Now, the combination of a leading model and an executor can be used in the desktop application and terminal. The company recommends the Claude Fable 5.1 and SWE-2 combination.

Recall that Devin is a tool where you can assign it tasks related to studying a project, changing code, and checks. Fusion, in turn, organizes the joint work of two models. One, for example, creates a plan and accepts the result, the other is responsible for implementation. It's like a regular development team, but without dailies.

The lead agent communicates with the user, clarifies ambiguous requirements, and determines what work to delegate. The executor studies files, writes code and tests, fixes bugs, and returns the result for review. Both have their own tools and separate context, which is preserved throughout the session. Cognition described this approach in the initial announcement of Fusion.

According to Cognition, simple routing based on the initial request is often insufficient, as the real complexity of the task is revealed only after studying the repository. Switching models may also require re-processing the context. In Fusion, each agent maintains its own cacheable context, and the lead agent decides which actions to perform itself and which to delegate to the assistant. The company discusses these limitations in more detail in an architectural breakdown.

In the graph shown, the Fable 5.1 + SWE-2 combination scores 61.7 points compared to 62.2 for Claude Code with Fable 5.1, with a 36% cost reduction. For Astra + SWE-2, 58.9 points are indicated compared to 61.6 for Codex with Astra, and a 39% cost reduction.

The savings are accompanied by various quality changes. For example, for Astra on Terminal‑Bench 4, the cost decreases by 40%, but the result drops from 55.6 to 50.0. These figures relate to specific tests and configurations from the Cognition report; results cannot be mapped to any project.

The company also has an observation about the cost of the models themselves. In a July experiment, Fable 5 was twice as expensive as Opus 4.8 per token, but with the same assistant, it turned out to be cheaper in terms of work results: $1.86 versus $2.04 on average per run. At the same time, the result was higher — 60.7 versus 54.6.

Cognition attributes this to the behavior of the lead agent. Fable delegated work earlier and repeated the executor's actions less. On average, it required 11.5 lead agent steps compared to 26.5 for Opus. In this experiment, the more expensive model compensated for the price with fewer requests and less processed context.

In the new announcement, the company also emphasizes that the harness needs to be configured for a specific pair of models: choosing the level of detail in the task, the permissibility of executor objections, and the scope of delegated code research.
