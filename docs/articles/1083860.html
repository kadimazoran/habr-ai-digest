LLM vs OCR

One could use OCR technologies for onboarding new employees. Such a system reads text from marked fields and uploads it to a form, eliminating manual data entry. However, we looked at the task more broadly from the outset. As a result, we digitized not only document processing but the entire onboarding process using our own web application, Sminex Start. It guides a new colleague through the onboarding process from the moment they receive their personal link on their first day at the company.

Unlike classic OCR, an LLM model doesn't just 'read' data—it 'understands' it. It determines the document type and the approach to work with it, identifying which fields the system needs, such as full name, passport number, and issue date. This provides universality and flexibility—if you upload a foreign passport or a non-standard diploma format, it works just as accurately as with typical formats. And this was the biggest challenge. We went through many iterations of fine-tuning and adapting the model and document processing logic; the initial universal scenario proved insufficient. As a result, the solution learned to extract necessary HR data from significantly different documents. Moreover, the technology can recognize handwritten text, and the interface supports multiple languages, including those of CIS countries.

How We 'Taught' the Model

Initially, we took the simplest approach: one neural network, separate instructions for each document type, but written freely—'look and return everything you see.' We fed the entire document photo to the neural network; it read the text from the image itself and parsed it into fields. But the accuracy was insufficient, so we looked for other solutions.

Among other things, we conducted an experiment with automated prompt optimization for recognizing passports and diplomas. We created a set of examples: we uploaded document photos and manually specified the reference structured result in JSON format—that is, what specific data the system should extract from the image and in what format to return it. Then, an initial prompt was given, after which a separate AI agent iteratively modified and improved it, running the model on a test set each time and comparing the resulting JSON with the reference answers. The AI effectively became a development tool—one AI helped automatically configure instructions for another AI, reducing manual trial-and-error and allowing for faster improvement in recognition stability. We had high expectations for this approach: we wanted to achieve maximum correspondence in the chain 'passport or diploma photo → understanding its content → strictly structured JSON' for any document type, regardless of format, year of issue, and so on. But in reality, it turned out that such a scenario could not be made universal.

Then we tried simply replacing the neural network with another one—that didn't help either; we reverted to the previous one. At this stage, it became clear that the issue wasn't the choice of neural network but that we were explaining the task poorly to it. We changed our approach:

  • We rewrote the instructions into a strict template (a fixed list of fields, formats, closed value lists, prohibition of guessing) and simultaneously divided the work into two stages—first, the document is read by a separate text recognition service (which only extracts letters from the image), and then the neural network receives the ready text and parses it into the template fields. We also implemented a rule: which document follows which route is set by configuration—separately for each type. If the main route fails, a backup route is automatically activated, and the document doesn't get stuck.

  • We specifically tested fault tolerance: we artificially 'crashed' some service responses and ensured that the document didn't get stuck but was rerouted to a backup. In parallel, we continued to refine the instructions for specific documents—for example, we specifically strengthened the rules where there were many fields with a list of allowed values.

A universal 'any document by one rule' chain didn't work out—but we did get a reliable solution for each type: its own template with rules, its own processing route, and a backup option in case of failure.

Security is the Foundation, Not a Feature

Security is a priority overall, and personal data management is regulated by law. Therefore, protecting personal data was one of our basic architectural requirements from the product's development stage. All processing occurs exclusively within the company's internal network. The AI model used is deployed locally and does not access external AI services; documents are not sent to public clouds. Sminex Start also does not store them: the application performs its function of recognizing necessary information and transmits it to corporate systems, primarily 1C:ZUP, but is not used as an additional archive.

The employee photographs their documents themselves, without letting them out of their hands. This is where the human factor comes into play: many people positively assessed not only the technological aspect of the process but also the ability not to part with their passport, even for a short period.

Another important aspect is compliance with departmental requirements when processing a candidate's personal data. One of the most challenging tasks here was recognizing and correctly exporting the registration address, usually called 'propiska,' from the stamp in the passport. The 1C:ZUP platform requires a strict link between the address and FIAS (Federal Information Address System). If you transfer a string 'as is' to 1C, for example, with abbreviations, spelling errors, or outdated names of renamed streets, the system will not be able to automatically match it with its address classifier. This will block the processing of an HR order or lead to rejection of reporting by the SFR and FNS.

To solve this problem, we designed an algorithmic module for normalizing, cleaning, and enriching addresses, which works through an integration REST gateway with the reference FIAS database. As a result, the handwritten string from the passport is transformed into a validated object for 1C—a clean address, eliminating the possibility of incomplete or incorrect data.

The algorithm includes four stages:

  • Tokenization and initial filtering. The recognized OCR string is cleaned of recognition noise—random special characters, double spaces. The string is broken down into individual tokens, i.e., words.

  • Semantic geocoding and matching. The web service encapsulates the cleaned string into a JSON package and sends a synchronous POST request to the normalization engine. The engine uses fuzzy search algorithms to match the noisy text with reference records in the state registry.

  • Data enrichment and GUID extraction. If the FIAS match is successful, it returns not just the corrected text but unique global object identifiers—FIAS ID (GUID) and KLADR ID for each parameter: region, district, city, street, house. This ensures unambiguous object identification in space, regardless of future changes to its name.

  • Decomposition for the 1C:ZUP object model. The obtained reference response is parsed by the web service into strict atomic fields corresponding to the internal structure of the XML/JSON packages required by 1C:ZUP. The address is broken down by levels and object types, for example, 'ul.' (street) type separately, 'Lenina' (name) separately.

What the Employee Sees and How It Works Under the Hood

We explain and show how a new employee's onboarding process works. On the first day, each person receives an individual link, logs in, and goes through the onboarding process online step-by-step: photographs documents, selects required fields, and fills in checkboxes. The link and authorization are tied to a separate management accounting system where a mechanism for approving the new employee's start date has been created.

  • Online Authorization, Live Support. Everything is intuitively clear and transparent: the user selects a language and on the next screen sees which employee is assisting with their onboarding. The human factor also plays a role here—the new colleague understands that support is nearby. If something goes wrong, they can click 'I need help.' This triggers integration with the corporate messenger. The specific HR department employee receives a notification about who needs to come to which meeting room.

  • Document Upload. This is the main part, and it's simple for the employee: take a photo or attach a ready one. All the interesting stuff happens internally. The content is recognized by a local vision-language model from the Qwen family. It's further adapted for our scenarios of working with HR documents. The model analyzes the structure, understands the purpose of fields, and extracts precisely the data required for onboarding from various document forms. Thanks to integration with 1C:ZUP, the recognized data, confirmed by the employee, is transferred directly to the HR system, so HR specialists don't need to re-enter information from forms and documents manually. At the same time, the new colleague has the opportunity to check the accuracy of the recognized data and make corrections if necessary.

  • Bank Details for Salary. The user enters their account details, and the information is immediately sent to the payroll department. This function also optimizes the accounting department's work—data for paying new employees' salaries is promptly received automatically.

  • Familiarization with Required Local Regulations. We built in flexibility from the start. The onboarding scenario is personalized depending on the specific employee: position, legal entity they are joining. The system determines the required onboarding parameters, including relevant local normative acts that must be read and signed. Previously, these were provided in print; now, the user can read everything online from their smartphone screen. The fact of familiarization with the local regulations is then recorded in the electronic document management system using an electronic digital signature.

  • Photo for Access Badge. At this stage, the employee takes a photo; attaching a pre-existing one is not allowed. Integration with the corporate system and the access control system is activated. The photograph is immediately transferred to the access system for subsequent entry into the office.

  • 'Tell Us About Yourself' Section. On our corporate portal, there is a 'New Employees' section. It displays colleagues' photos and information they write about themselves: some talk about their work experience and education, others share skills and hobbies. We are currently analyzing the user experience—if we are convinced that it is convenient for employees to talk about themselves during onboarding, we will set up integration with the corporate portal.

Employee Journey First, Then Code

It was an excellent decision to involve colleagues from several departments in the development of Sminex Start at the initial stage. We didn't start from how to make the technical part easier. Specialists from HR administration, recruitment, and onboarding departments described the user onboarding scenario in detail: what information needs to be obtained from each document type, the sequence of steps, and the requirements for the new employee's interface and UX. Each person added their expertise and perspective on the process, including anticipating common questions that arise at different stages. Then, this logic was translated into the first MVP, which was also refined collaboratively. As a result, we solved two problems: reducing manual labor and increasing new colleagues' satisfaction.

  • Resource Reduction by Decreasing Onboarding Time per Person. Previously, onboarding one person took about 30 minutes. With the introduction of Sminex Start, this was immediately reduced by 10 minutes. The further plan is digital document submission in 15 minutes for each employee. This is a significant annual saving: an average of 300 hours at current workload, and even more with staff growth. Additionally, new colleagues use Sminex Start simultaneously—previously, a specialist processed documents sequentially. Therefore, the overall speed of the onboarding process has actually doubled or even more.

  • Improved User Experience. Employees no longer have to wait for a specialist to become available and process their documents, they don't need to hand over their passports, the onboarding process is transparent and already contains answers to common questions. Early users noted the speed, convenience, and technological sophistication. They also highlighted the intuitive logic and the preservation of entered data when moving to the next stage. A common problem where everything resets upon page refresh—we anticipated this, so even in case of errors, information is not lost, and you can go back a step and continue filling it out.

Sminex Start perfectly reflects our approach: it's a full-fledged product that benefits the business, is convenient for people, and is adaptable to future changes.
