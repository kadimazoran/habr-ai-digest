Sergey Portnov, the owner of the betting company Parimatch, was tricked into investing in a non-existent AI startup: he was "fooled" with the help of a deepfake of an acquaintance. Although Portnov did not name the sum, he indicated that it cost him "very dearly".

"Scamming is no longer just calls from 'bank employees'. Now it can be a friend or relative: a familiar voice, knowledge of personal details... but in reality, it's not them. Even a video call is no longer proof. Deepfakes have become frighteningly high-quality," he said in a LinkedIn post.

Sergey Portnov advises that before transferring money, one should verify the information by calling the interlocutor's known contact number.

"Five minutes of 'paranoia' will be much cheaper than what I lost," he concluded.

In 2024, a user of the X social network under the nickname facelessboy00 shared how he helped a friend pass a job interview at a tech company using a deepfake. He himself is an experienced developer, so he participated in the call and demonstrated the skills, but in real-time, he replaced his face with his friend's face. On the second attempt, he managed to get an offer of $4,000.

In the same year, an employee of the finance department of the engineering company Arup in Hong Kong joined a video call with the CFO and colleagues, but they all turned out to be deepfakes. The employee transferred 200 million Hong Kong dollars (about $25.6 million) to the scammers in fifteen payments.
