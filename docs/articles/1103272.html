What haven't I been: webmaster, web designer, software engineer, senior IT specialist, I can't even remember everything.

Looking back, I realize: I've truly loved programming since childhood, starting with "landing on the Moon" on a B3-21 or MK-52 calculator with their mysterious EGGOG. It was never just code for code's sake; it was the freedom to create, to bring ideas to life.

The best expression of this was Flash with its Action Script, which provided convenient programmatic control over graphics. What beautiful falling and melting snowflakes could be generated with its help!

"Here's a snowflake, it's melting. Here's a group of snowflakes. Here are their movement trajectories. Here's a snowfall." It wasn't just beautiful. It was simply magical!

And now - now this magic is achievable again. It's an incredible feeling of freedom: you can create anything that comes to mind, easily implement all those old ideas that have gathered a bit of dust - no time, no energy, too long and complicated.

I went ahead and made a game that I invented myself.

Technically, it's simple - React, CSS, Laravel, so from a technical standpoint, writing about it isn't interesting. But I want to share the inspiration I've found. I've been working on it since January 2026, it's not ready yet, although you can already play it, and... I'm not getting tired of it!

Let's take dice, squares, and circular movement from Monopoly, transfer it to the world of the first King's Bounty (...you find a map with a passage to Forestria), add knights. This won't be a game for the bourgeoisie; there must be room for valiant battles here! Gemini will do the graphics, Claude will do the code. We'll polish both with a file as needed. After all, wasn't this great graphics once?

But how should the battle be made? Everything must fit on the screen, so I didn't really want to replicate Heroes or King's Bounty. Maybe just roll the dice, and as many as you roll, that's how many are killed? Besides, it would preserve the board game feel... No, it got boring after just two days. Civilization or Warlords II were more interesting, or even AuraLux. Besides, you need to play not with yourself, but at least with a bot...

And here begins the real adventure:
you have to imagine something that doesn't exist yet, something that has never existed before. This is what development is all about - when you don't know what the result will be, there's no clear task.

There's only an idea and a general requirement: it must be interesting.

This means the game's economy needs to change: you can't just have soldiers; you need weaker militia and stronger cavalry. But you aren't born a knight, you become one! We introduce the "Glory" parameter, which will also serve as a level in Munchkin. So, we need zombie monsters (and ruins for them) and wandering hordes to defend against!

And - oh my god, finally, I can rob caravans! I won't be able to chase them like in Borderlands, but if I miss such an opportunity, I'll never forgive myself - so, caravans must exist.

And if so, we need to add a random event mechanism.

"A knight was riding across a river, saw an orc in the river"

Robbing is good! Or not? Well, we shouldn't add high morality to a medieval setting! So it doesn't turn out like in Morrowind, where every Khajiit attacks you and calls the guards.

Instead, let's add karma: if you rob caravans, karma drops; if you free slaves, it rises! That's better. And let karma affect what the player gets - a dragon might not just fly by in the sky but burn down a farm, or a plague, or taxes... Misery and suffering, that's what's needed!

Should we redesign the battle screen so that everything looks like old frescoes? Let's add it to the plans!

I've tried a dozen different mechanics, the battles have become more interesting, the bots smarter. The game comes alive under my hands and changes, asking for modifications! Some simple, others requiring a complete overhaul. So what? Everything is possible now.

Simple: placing obstacles on the battlefield creates more room for human cunning and tactics. I added that a backstab deals critical damage with a higher probability (but where is their back?).

Complex: a Tower Defense mechanic is clearly needed for building defenders. But I don't have any archers! It turns out to be some kind of Plants vs. Zombies... No, that also has strategy. But I have an Archer Tower and an Arbalest Tower... I need to work on this, it could be very interesting. Or maybe make the tactics for battles with fewer than 12 participants like in the first UFO: Enemy Unknown? And allow healing units, and have veterans... Okay, stop. Let's put it aside. Let's put it aside.

For now, the economy needs to be improved; everything revolves too much around gold. A legacy of "Monopoly"! A mountain of gold in some areas, a shortage in others, pay taxes and ride on peacefully... It all feels very, very boring in contrast to "Heroes" or board games like Catan. And there was the wonderful Tradewinds 1/2 on Palm/PocketPC! How cool it was to visit Shangri La after a successful voyage with a cargo of opium!

So, let's add to the game that each building can produce its own type of resource, peasants will mine them, and then peasants can be armed and made into soldiers... We'll trade three sheep for two wheat... No, that's too complicated for a mobile browser game, and I don't want to make a clone. And this fuss with peasants... digging in the dirt isn't a knightly thing to do! Let there be only two types of resources: weapons and food. And no peasants. After all, we managed with just trade once?

Done, very tired, the room smells of burnt tokens. I'm playing - level 12, no army, no weapons, but a mountain of food, and I don't know what to do with it. So, we need a Harvest at the end of the year, where I'll trade one for another (...we will break your starship, and we don't need anything for it... ).

Stop. Year? Everything in my game is measured in turns and rounds. Some complete a round faster, some slower, but time must flow uniformly so that the Harvest is simultaneous for everyone! So, we need to introduce time and, having put on cufflinks and gaiters, count days and kilometers months and years.

How would I have done all this if I didn't have the unquestioning Claude at my disposal? Yes, he sometimes lags and doesn't understand the context, yes, he sometimes repeats the same mistake or forgets not to say "this will cost you 0 gold coins" again. Yes, the pathfinding algorithm needs to be refined after him. But it's simply incredible, it's magic - to do so much "at a single touch."

I would have died, broken down already on changing the battlefield from hexagons to squares and back. Any normal programmer would have sent me to the south-south-east long ago with the request "let's throw out the army squad division mechanic, which we struggled with for the last 3 months and on which half the game was based, I think it's no longer needed"!

But we live in a truly wonderful time for creators, when from idea to reality is just one Claude away. And if I want to rewrite everything in Rust or Flutter, integrate the Unreal Engine instead of HTML/CSS - I understand that it's not just possible, but not too difficult!

I am a developer again, not a coder, not an engineer, and not a webmaster. I can create! I've never written music in a way that took more than a couple of hours. I've never drawn anything for more than a few days in a row; sketches came out, not paintings. But I've been writing this game for 10 months in my free time, and I'm not getting tired of it - on the contrary, the further I go, the more interesting it gets.

This is a delightful time of opportunity. Don't miss it! After all, no one knows what the Joker's next joke will be - kind or evil. Even if you have positive Karma and great Glory.
