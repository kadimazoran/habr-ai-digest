Big Models of the Week

Gemini 3.8 Flash: Third Update in Three Months

Google rolled out Gemini 3.8 Flash – exactly three weeks after 3.7 Flash, marking the third update to the lineup in three months.

The secret is simple: the model "does more work" on complex tasks – it performs more reasoning steps and calls tools more often, consuming more tokens for the sake of the result.

On DeepSWE v1.1, a test for autonomous solution of complex engineering problems, 3.8 Flash outperforms most larger frontier models at a fraction of their cost.

Qwen3.8-Max Briefly Overtook Claude Opus 5

On September 2nd, Alibaba's model Qwen3.8-Max-0902 took first place on the Code Arena WebDev leaderboard, surpassing Claude Opus 5 Max by three points – at the same price of $2 per million input and $6 per million output tokens.

It was a beautiful story about a Chinese model outperforming a heavyweight, but it lasted exactly five days: by September 7th, the table had rearranged again – Claude Fable 5.1 Max scored 1896 points, GPT-6 Astra Max – 1860, Claude Opus 5 Max – 1766, and Qwen3.8-Max-0902 dropped to fourth place with a "preliminary" tag.

DeepSeek Released V4.1-Flash

DeepSeek introduced the V4.1-Flash model with native multimodality and announced the phased rollout of V4-Pro. This is the most compact model in the new family – an MoE with 552 billion parameters and a causal encoder-decoder architecture, where 8 billion parameters are active for input and 16 for output.

A nice detail: KV cache requirements are reduced – the model needs only a quarter of the HBM memory and one-eighth of the SSD storage compared to the previous generation. From September 14th, all requests to deepseek-v4-pro will be automatically switched to V4.1-Flash.

GPT-6 Astra: OpenAI's First Model to Receive Highest Risk Level

A year after the launch of GPT-5, the company introduced GPT-6 Astra – a model that OpenAI calls "the smartest and most coherent.".

A day before the release, the company confirmed: Astra received a "critical" risk status in cybersecurity – the first time an OpenAI model has received the highest threat level in any of the three tracked areas.

From benchmarks: FrontierMath Tier 4 (v2) – 97.6%, GPQA Diamond – 96.0%, ARC-AGI-1 – 98.5%, although on Humanity’s Last Exam with tools, the model unexpectedly lost to Fable 5.1 from Anthropic, released a couple of days earlier.

Infrastructure

NVIDIA Acquires Hugging Face for $12.93 Billion

Jensen Huang announced that NVIDIA is acquiring Hugging Face for $12,930,300,000.

The platform is used by over 18 million developers and content creators who share more than 3 million models, 500 thousand datasets, and a million applications. Over 200,000 companies also use it.

NVIDIA promises that the platform will remain open: developers will still be able to choose any models, frameworks, and clouds, and NVIDIA computations will not be a mandatory requirement.

NVIDIA PAIR

Speaking of NVIDIA – the company also launched PAIR (Personal AI Router), an open-source router for home networks that utilizes idle Macs and PCs to process AI agent requests like NemoClaw, OpenClaw, or Hermes through sub-agents.

PAIR does not combine GPUs into a single accelerator or split a single request across multiple machines – it simply sends individual requests to a suitable idle node through already installed Ollama or LM Studio, supporting Macs with M4+ and GeForce RTX 20xx+.

In a test with two PCs on RTX 5090 and the Qwen3.6 35B A3B model, five sub-agents accelerated the work by approximately 1.6 times.

Google Uses DDR4 from Retired Servers

Here's an illustration of the memory shortage: Google has started pulling DDR4 modules from retired servers and reusing them in new AI machines.

Nikhil Cherian, Google's Senior Director of Supply Chain Infrastructure, admitted at a forum: the industry has shifted from a compute shortage to a memory shortage in a matter of months – high-performance memory now accounts for about 75% of the cost of components in a typical AI server. The company has even designed special adapters to connect older DDR4 memory to the new generation of servers, building an internal recycling chain, and is simultaneously optimizing model architecture and KV cache compression.

Other Releases

ChatGPT Images 2.5: Faster and More Accurate

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Images 2.5. The company claims to have halved generation latency. The most significant practical improvement is in-painting editing: the model changes only what was requested, without affecting the rest of the composition. Anyone who has asked to change a background and received a different face in the photo instead will understand the value of this update.

The updated model maintains consistency better in long editing sessions and still works well with transparent backgrounds.

Developers will be able to choose one of the new model variants: GPT-Image-2.5 Flare and Sunburst.

GPTunneL Integrates "Grom TV"

The AI platform GPTunneL has launched "Grom TV" – an endless AI broadcast where viewers invent what the TV will show next. Simply type a prompt like "Gandalf brews a potion with Scooby-Doo" – and within 20 seconds, the neural network will generate a video and send it to the general broadcast.

Behind the continuous stream is a pipeline: video with sound is generated in about 7.8 seconds on Nvidia B200, then ffmpeg processes the video and delivers it as an HLS stream. If there are no user prompts, the Grom model itself invents plots for the broadcast. This is television that wasn't filmed in advance – it composes itself as you watch. Suno Rolls Out v6 – With Blessing from Warner, BMG, and Believe

Suno has launched the new v6 model in three versions simultaneously – and is completely phasing out the old ones.

The flagship v6 promises a polished result in any genre; v6-wild is the same lineup but less predictable, for experimentation; v6-mini is free and, according to the company, the fastest among free models on music platforms.

Practically speaking: you can now change individual parts of a finished song with a text prompt – for example, ask for the chorus to be sung by a gospel choir without affecting the rest, or create a mashup of vocals from one song and drums from another.

What Suno is carefully not saying is what exactly the new models are trained on. The company calls Warner, BMG, and Believe co-developers but does not disclose the catalog compositions and previously asked to keep the size of the training data confidential.

Microsoft Turns Excel into a Live Dashboard

Microsoft is preparing the Excel Canvas feature – a Copilot tool that gathers table data into a unified dashboard with charts and metrics, automatically updating as the source data changes.

Essentially, this marks the end of the era of manually formatting charts for the next presentation to management. The feature is listed in the Microsoft AI at Work roadmap under number 569424 with a "in development" status, expected on desktop and web by September 2026 (but the status hints: this is more of a wish than a promise).

Tolstoy "Responds" to Questions Again – Through "Alisa"

On Leo Tolstoy's birthday, September 9th, Yandex launched an AI persona of the writer in a chat with "Alisa." Simply say "Alisa, call Leo Tolstoy," and a dialogue begins where the classic answers with quotes from his books and diaries.

Specialists worked with archival audio recordings, removed noise, and adjusted speech synthesis to convey the writer's intonations.

In mini-quests based on his works, you can influence the plot yourself – imagine how Natasha Rostova and Andrei Bolkonsky would meet, or help Zhilin escape captivity; all this is available from September 9th to 14th.

Additionally, a 3D model of the "Khamovniki" estate has appeared on Yandex Maps, which you can "walk through" and observe rovers broadcasting quotes from diaries right by the estate walls.

AI Solves a Millennium Problem

OpenAI mathematicians announced that a group of 10,000 autonomous AI agents found a "singularity" in the Navier-Stokes equations in three-dimensional space, thus solving one of the six remaining Clay Institute Millennium Prize Problems with a prize of one million dollars. The result has been formally verified in the Lean language.

The Navier-Stokes equations describe how fluids flow, and since the mid-19th century, the basic question has remained open: can their solutions "explode," meaning develop such that an infinitesimally small part of the fluid begins to flow infinitely fast.

About a hundred OpenAI agents worked for approximately 50 hours to disprove regularity for the Euler equations, and then 10,000 agents spent 88 hours proving the singularity for Navier-Stokes, plus another 17 hours formalizing the result. In total, the agents exchanged nearly 5 million messages.
