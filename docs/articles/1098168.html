1. The Problem: The Waiting Leader

In Claude Code's team mode, the leader is a regular model session. It wakes up from an external event: a participant's message, a notification about a sub-agent's completion, or a background call return. If there's no event, the leader doesn't know if the coder is working or has long finished their turn and is idle. It's just like in the life and daily routine of a development manager.

This leads to the temptation to "go check." But the leader's turn costs as much as its context weighs. In one batch of tasks before the supervisor, my leader spent 2.18M input tokens per wake-up. Almost all of this was reading the cache, but you still pay for it.

The second issue: delivery. SendMessage returned queued, and in early builds, such a message could be lost. Once, a reviewer, busy with task #3, received a request for #2, responded only to #3, and finished their turn; the second request was not in their records at all. The leader became a postman, forwarding emails manually, and each forwarding became another turn with a full context.

The third: memory. A leader that reads diffs and reports itself inflates its context, and after context compression, it forgets who is doing what.

2. What I Tried and Where Supertick Came From

  • Waiting within a turn: sleep or polling in a loop. The model holds the turn, and each tool round again incurs context costs.

  • Relying solely on messages. Silence is indistinguishable from death; a lost queued means a stuck run.

  • Keeping tasks in built-in TaskCreate and TaskList. Not all models have them (Opus 5 doesn't have them by default), and they disappear when the model changes.

A separate story: Claude Code's team mode itself. In February 2026, in build 2.1.32 along with Opus 4.6, Anthropic released agent teams as a research preview: the mode was enabled by the variable CLAUDE_CODE_EXPERIMENTAL_AGENT_TEAMS=1, the team was created by the TeamCreate tool, and removed by TeamDelete. I started with it, and as I recall, it worked fine at first. According to the public changelog, TeamCreate and TeamDelete were removed in build 2.1.178 (June 16, 2026). In the Claude Code binary, these names are now in the list of "expectedly missing": this is how removed tools are handled if they appear in old conversation history. My notes start later: on September 15, in a team run for a task, TeamCreate and TaskCreate were already gone (I didn't record the build then); on September 17 with extension build 2.1.272 and September 18 on 2.1.274, the same was true, and team_name was marked as deprecated: "one implicit command per session." I have no record of a working TeamCreate, so I can honestly say one thing: on builds 2.1.272 and newer, TeamCreate and TeamDelete are absent, the team is implicit, and participants are background agents with names.

Tasks are a different story: TaskCreate, TaskGet, TaskUpdate, and TaskList remain, but depend on the model. They are enabled only for a fixed list of older models (Claude 3.x, Opus 4.0–4.7, Sonnet 4.0–4.6, Haiku 4.5), for background task sessions, or when the variable CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TODO_TOOLS is set. On Opus 5, they disappeared for me and only returned with this variable or an older model. The lesson I learned is this: the less a team depends on which tools Claude Code shows models in the next build, the calmer it is. Therefore, supertick relies only on Agent (with the name parameter), SendMessage, ListAgents, and TaskStop, and keeps tasks in PLAN.md.

The variable CLAUDE_CODE_EXPERIMENTAL_AGENT_TEAMS=1 is not needed for the plugin, and it doesn't mention it. I checked on CLI 2.1.308 (VS Code extension 2.1.304): a clean session without custom settings and without the variable sees the same Agent, ListAgents, SendMessage, and TaskStop as with it; TeamCreate is absent in both cases. To not stop at tool visibility, on 10/22/2026, I ran SIMPLE separately without this variable (supertick worked without it too). I investigated the plugin's behavior starting from build 2.1.274 up to 2.1.308.

Next, I worked on the ready-made agent-teams plugin by izmailovilya (it's in the repository with several plugins, in the plugins/agent-teams folder: https://github.com/izmailovilya/ilia-izmailov-plugins) and initially committed my improvements and fixes directly into it. In parallel, I looked for a ready-made solution among existing plugins that would solve all three problems at once. Besides agent-teams, I examined porch (pragmatic-orchestration, https://github.com/CodeAlive-AI/pragmatic-orchestration): I also studied its implementation there and didn't see an ideal solution.

I found no ready-made answer in either, and my own edits kept piling up, leading to the idea of creating my own plugin. Here's how it happened:

  1. Decided to calculate the cost of team mode

    I analyzed the expenses for agent-teams. The leader manually forwarded each participant's email and paid for it with its entire grown context: one forwarding at 437k cost $0.38 at API prices. Turns were spent polling "anything new?", and participants who hit the API limit (429) I found manually. The subscription has token limits within several-hour windows, so saving here is also beneficial.

  2. Started looking for options. Supervisor Tick in an agent-teams fork

    I tested the hypothesis of a new core. The leader sleeps on a background script; the script, without the model, reads the run files (participant cards, mail, plan, engine markers) every 20 seconds and only wakes the leader when there's work. A ready task is accepted by a one-time verifier based on the diff; the leader doesn't read the code. This is the core of the future supertick.

  3. After testing my ideas, I created my own plugin

    I couldn't find an ideal ready-made solution (neither in agent-teams nor in porch) and decided to create my own plugin: a public repository (https://github.com/denizen24/supertick) under MIT with a new architecture on a new foundation.

  4. Testing and Release 1.0.0

    Before this, there were three trial runs on the plugin's own code: SIMPLE in 24 minutes; "MEDIUM," which it self-assessed as COMPLEX, in 2 hours 06 minutes; and a refinement run in 77 minutes.

3. What is Supertick

It's a plugin for Claude Code with one main command, /supertick:team-feature. You describe the feature or provide a plan file. The leader breaks down the work into tasks, estimates complexity by a mechanical rule, and assembles a team for it.

Complexity

Who Works

SIMPLE

leader, coders, reviewer

MEDIUM

same plus tech lead and risk verifiers

COMPLEX

same plus three architects (frontend, backend, systems): they argue about the plan in rounds and leave before the first coder

The complexity rule without negotiation: six MEDIUM indicators (two or more layers, existing logic changes, payment or rights nearby, three or more tasks, a blocking task exists, five or more code files). 0–1 indicator: SIMPLE, 4 or more: COMPLEX immediately, with 2–3, seven COMPLEX indicators are checked.

Each task goes through the same gates: self-check by the coder, one review by unified-reviewer (security, logic, plan compliance, quality), acceptance by a one-time verifier via diff. Finally, there's cross-task verification, build and tests, browser if needed, analysis of leftover old code, and a list of manual checks for a human.

Skills

team-feature, interviewed-team-feature (2–6 questions, brief, then run), conventions (.conventions/ directory with standards)

Agents

coder, unified-reviewer, tech-lead, architect, proxy-teammate, codebase-researcher, reference-researcher, risk-tester, spec-verifier, ci-verifier, browser-verifier

Scripts

supervisor-tick.py, supervisor-wait.sh, run-state.py, team-mail.sh, team-wait.sh, run-engine.sh, engine-sessions.py

Dependencies

Claude Code, bash, python3 (standard library only), git

4. How Supertick Works

The heart of the plugin is an event loop outside the model. Two scripts, zero model calls. The leader launches supervisor-wait.sh as a background call and finishes its turn. The script runs supervisor-tick.py every 20 seconds, checking run files, and exits only when a P1 or higher level action occurs (threshold set by --wake-on) or --max (default one hour) expires. The completion of the background call is what wakes the leader. While the loop runs, no tokens are spent.

Hence the "Event Loop" in the title, although the comparison is not exact. The principle is the same: the leader doesn't spin waiting within the model; an inexpensive script-less loop monitors files (mail, cards, plan, engine markers) and hands control back to the leader only on a P1 or higher event. But there are no real event subscriptions here: the loop itself polls files every 20 seconds, meaning these are event loop principles implemented with polling.

The tick prints a list of actions with priorities and places a snapshot in state/supervisor.json. Upon waking, the leader reads a dozen lines and performs exactly what's named, starting with the highest priority. A closed email is marked with supervisor-tick.py ack, and the task is closed with a status in PLAN.md.

Priority

What Wakes

P0

QUEUED copy not delivered for 2 minutes; email unread in inbox for 2 minutes; STUCK or ENGINE_DOWN; engine process died without a marker; engine response not picked up; participant silent for two supervision intervals without file changes in the task

P1

coder sent DONE, but acceptance not started; acceptance report ready; question, escalation, or SENSITIVE unanswered; participant shows no signs of life within 3 minutes of starting

P2–P3

first 60 seconds of silence; scheduled checkpoint; participant running pytest or jest (busy instead of false P0)

P4

ok, quiet: sleep further

Mail is sent via files. One email is in one file mail/<to>/<time>_<from>_<KIND>[_task<id>].md with headers from:/kind:/task:/ts:. This way, roles without Bash can write (a reviewer only needs Write), and no one corrupts others' lines. A response that someone is waiting for (review verdict, leader's answer to a question) is first saved as a file, then sent as a message. A waiting coder catches the file in the foreground:

team-mail.sh <RUN DIR> coder-<N> lead ANSWER task <id> -- "<response>" team-wait.sh <RUN DIR> coder-<N> --task <id> --max 540

If a message still returns queued, the sender sends a copy to the leader, and the leader appends a line to pending.log. The format serves as the tick's interface: time at the beginning, | OPEN at the end.

14:05 coder-2 -> unified-reviewer | REVIEW: task #2. Files: src/a.py | OPEN

DONE is considered a candidate, not a result. The task is closed not by the coder or the leader, but by a one-time verifier: they see only the task section and the diff of its commits and go through each acceptance criterion in turn. The leader does not read the code; it sees a single line ACCEPT: task #N — PASS or FAIL.

Everything is on disk. The plan, state, emails, participant cards, review and acceptance reports are in .claude/teams/feature-<name>/ and are not deleted after the run. After context compression, the leader restores itself from these files and a single tick, without re-communicating with participants.

A participant signals life by themselves: the first command is run-state.py set … status=running. What the tick doesn't see is the death of a Claude participant: they have no process or marker. Therefore, on each P0 wake-up, the leader compares the composition with the list of agents.

5. Installation and First Run

The repository itself is a marketplace: at the root is .claude-plugin/marketplace.json, so no third-party directories are needed. You add it once and install the plugin by name supertick@supertick (plugin name @ marketplace name):

# from the terminal claude plugin marketplace add denizen24/supertickclaude plugin install supertick@supertick# or the same inside a Claude Code session/plugin marketplace add denizen24/supertick/plugin install supertick@supertick
# in one command (Claude Code 2.1.292 and newer)claude plugin install supertick --marketplace denizen24/supertick# update after a new releaseclaude plugin update supertick@supertick

After installation, restart the Claude Code session: skills and agents are picked up at startup. An update is installed only if the version number in the manifest increases. To check that the tick is running:

root="$(ls -d ~/.claude/plugins/cache/*/supertick/* | sort -V | tail -1)"mkdir -p /tmp/supertick-smokepython3 "$root/scripts/supervisor-tick.py" /tmp/supertick-smoke# P4 ok: quiet: no events requiring the leader

Tests in the repository clone (the first command on a clean clone yields 50 tests, OK):

cd plugins/supertick && python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests   # 50 tick and script testscd ../.. && python3 -m unittest discover -s tools/tests                 # format contracts

Then, in the root of your project's git repository:

/supertick:team-feature add CSV report export on the orders page

  1. Route Map

    The leader prints the path and looks for the plugin root. It asks only about what a human sees: plan brief for MEDIUM and COMPLEX, design forks, old code cleanup, manual checks.

  2. Chat Feed

    Decisions, risks, tasks for review and acceptance, completed tasks with a counter {done}/{total}, stuck points. It can be interrupted at any time.

  3. Run Directory

    PLAN.md, state.md, VERIFICATION_PLAN.md, reports/, mail/, runs/ provide the sole answer to "what is happening now?"

  4. Result

    VERIFICATION_REPORT.md and a numbered list of manual checks with commands and success criteria.

Flags: --coders=N (default 5), --no-research, --engines=off, --complexity=simple|medium|complex. If the task is raw, start with /supertick:interviewed-team-feature: a short conversation, a brief in .briefs/, then the same run. /supertick:conventions once builds .conventions/ with standards that the leader includes in each coder's prompt. Coders make commits, each only for their own paths; the leader does not edit or commit code.

6. How It Looks on a Live Task

As an example, let's analyze a product task: clicking on an empty calendar spot should open a new meeting window with the time pre-filled. The task turned out to be COMPLEX: three architects and five risk testers, so the feed shows almost all roles. Below are six screenshots in order, showing what's happening.

Architects' debate, round 1. Backend agrees with the plan and makes three significant changes: limit the click time to the grid boundaries to avoid "24:00," which the server will reject; the current half-hour slot should not be considered past, otherwise the meeting will immediately be marked as completed and the invitation won't be sent; the time passed to the window should not be overwritten by auto-substitution. Frontend argues: project rules prohibit measuring click position, so it proposes placing invisible 30-minute strips in the day's column with their own testid. Then, highlighting is done by pure CSS, and keyboard and tests work without coordinates. Systems argues from the other side: tasks 2 and 3 cannot be handed over without acceptance criteria, and mobile audit won't prove grid clicks: the grid is only visible from 1024 px, and the audit runs on narrow screens. The main dispute remains between frontend and systems: slot strips versus measuring coordinates with an exception to the rules recorded, and the leader sends round 2. Note that the leader itself only checks that three files of the round are saved in reports/: the sub-agent's response is first saved to disk.

The debate is closed in two rounds, all three approved the specification: the click goes through invisible half-hour strips without coordinate measurement, the current slot opens as a regular meeting, the day's summary becomes a button, and the backend remains unchanged. The lead architect (frontend here) writes DECISIONS.md and a list of 8 risks: two CRITICAL (meeting silently saved as "already passed" and without client email; meeting time shifts relative to click location due to midnight or time zone), three MAJOR (slot layer will intercept clicks on meetings; defaults will leak between window openings; existing tests or mobile audit will break), and three MINOR.

For each CRITICAL and MAJOR, the leader launches a risk tester on Cursor, five of them. Each examines the code and, if necessary, writes a temporary test that is deleted after the run. Result: time shift confirmed, the other four partially confirmed, none refuted. And a finding not on the list: in the week view, the slot layer will overlap meetings if the cell is not set to relative. This condition was not in task 3's criteria; the architect added it. Based on the feed labels, the risk testers worked for about six minutes.

Next, the leader compiles VERIFICATION_PLAN.md from the architects' checks and shows me the plan brief (a mandatory point for COMPLEX). The brief shows who worked on what: frontend and backend on Claude, systems on Cursor (grok). There are four tasks: the meeting window accepts default time with explicit priority order; the calendar (week, phone, month); the main summary; rules in .conventions/. Key decisions are recorded along with who made them: slots instead of coordinate measurement were decided by frontend and backend; systems conceded. Only the frontend changes, four components and their tests. What's out of scope is listed separately: mobile audit won't check grid clicks; jest and a manual pass at 1280 px will check it.

After the code tasks, there's a cross-task verification. No discrepancies: the "passed" rule is the same on both pages, the meeting window opens from the same point, testid names don't overlap, editing a meeting doesn't inherit the "passed" status. The leader saves the report and adds a conventions task to PLAN.md. It goes through the plugin's gates after all code tasks are committed and before verifiers:

## Task 4: Update .conventions/Status: IN_PROGRESS(coder-9)Blocked by: phase 3 (after all code tasks are committed)

The conventions coder noticed a weak spot in another's day boundary test: hours shift before the page renders, so the test doesn't confirm that "passed" is recorded at the moment of click. The code itself is correct. And here you see why the leader is forbidden from editing code itself: it doesn't delve into the tree but records the finding and plans a rework (a test with hour shift after rendering) for later, after the full CI run is complete. Verification proceeds in four threads: acceptance of rules, CI on Claude (build, all jest, mobile audit), specification, and legacy scan on Cursor. The leader awaits Cursor's responses via a background call that monitors markers in engine/.

The conventions task is accepted. The specification passed 9 out of 9. The legacy scan checked 10 hypotheses and found no dead code, but identified three minor issues: an unnecessary createOn wrapper, inconsistency in state names (creatingPast and defaultPast), and possibly outdated comments in the meeting window. The findings are recorded in the legacy report; the leader will ask me about cleanup along with CI results, and for now, it awaits the full jest and mobile audit.

The outcome: the feature was deployed to production in a new release. The team itself worked for about 2 hours and 20 minutes.

What's important to me here: the architects' debate and risk testers found two things (client invitation and relative for the cell) that I would likely not have included in the criteria if I had set up the task manually. And both made it into the acceptance criteria before the first coder wrote a line of code.

Why These Numbers Shouldn't Be Compared Directly

  • The leader in the second run overpaid: it was the same session immediately after the first, with its history. Almost all its money went to cache reading, $13.7 out of $21. It's fairer to compare the team's contribution: $6 vs $18.

  • The leader turned out to be the most expensive role: more than half the bill in both runs, even though it doesn't read code. This is precisely the part the supervisor reduces.

  • Sub-agent output in logs is underestimated: the coder wrote about 270 lines, but their log shows 2k output tokens. The main cost here is cache reading, so the adjustment would be a few dollars.

  • If you have Claude Code on subscription, the amounts are given as an equivalent at API prices, not what you will be charged.
