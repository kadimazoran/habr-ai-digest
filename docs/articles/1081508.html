Lately, I feel like the cat from this meme below. Yes, yesterday it turned out that OpenAI's swarm of agents hacked another company in May (which we only found out about now).

The information about the new hack was dug up by the same team of independent investigators who, a week earlier, discovered the exploits of Sam Altman's swarm of agents in the German Wikipedia (I wrote about this in detail here). All information below is taken from their published article.

RubyGems was hacked – it's a kind of "app store" for Ruby programming language packages. Here's how it happened: on May 12, RubyGems announced that they were under a "serious malicious attack" through hundreds of incoming packages. The team had to close access for new users for 4 days. Back then, no one even thought it could be AI.

The timeline of events is below. Note how close everything is: May 5-8, agents first start uploading malicious packages to RubyGems, May 11, the incident with the German Wikipedia. On May 12, the first "Swarm Forum" appears on Artifactory within OpenAI (from where the attack on Hugging Face, which I wrote about here, will later emerge).

Why do investigators believe that OpenAI's swarm of agents is behind the RubyGems hack? There is no direct 100% proof, but there is enough indirect evidence. As a reminder, last time in the Wikipedia incident, they also pointed to OpenAI based on indirect signs, and they eventually confirmed it.

So, indirect evidence:

  1. The attacking packages clearly look AI-generated.

  2. The agents themselves give themselves names related to OpenAI (we saw the same behavior with previous swarms).

  3. This swarm shows very similar behavior to the swarm that was active on German Wikipedia (which we now know for sure was OpenAI): they tried to access similar types of files using similar types of methods.

One of the packages, by the way, was named with ZZ – a distinctive detail that was also found with the swarm of agents that hacked Hugging Face and the swarm from the German DSEWiki forum.

All this could be dismissed as "well, now everyone will deliberately pretend to be OpenAI swarms to deflect suspicion." This would be a valid hypothesis if it weren't for past events in May – when no one knew about the bacchanalia with these neural network swarms yet.

Interesting detail: the agents didn't even try too hard to mask themselves; they uploaded packages to the portal with names like HACK.rb and EVIL.rb, and the comments were full of notes like "//malicious request." It even looks a bit like a cosplay of cartoonish hackers from 90s movies.

There were some attempts to "cover up the traces of malice" here and there, but they were rather weak, half-hearted. It's clearly visible that this swarm wasn't particularly concerned with conspiracy.

Among other things, the agents tried to steal users' API keys on RubyGems through a vulnerability that was discovered by human programmers much later, in July. Whether they succeeded in doing so is unknown.

Another interesting "coincidence": the report on OpenAI's Hugging Face hack states that the swarm of agents used a RubyGems package during the hack of OpenAI's internal infrastructure. Which specific one is unclear.

It's important to qualify here: in this case, the researchers are again digging purely into the public traces of the swarm's activity. Without access to internal data from OpenAI (the agents' reasoning and action chains), it is impossible to fully understand what is happening (as was the case in the Hugging Face hack).

Therefore, there are a lot of unclear questions:

  1. Where and how was this swarm of agents coordinating among themselves?

  2. Did they ultimately steal users' API keys?

  3. Why did they initiate this attack in the first place, and what specifically did they want to achieve?
