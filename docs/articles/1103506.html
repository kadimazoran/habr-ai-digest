Hello, Habr!

In April, I wrote about how I created an anti-spam bot for Telegram using my own neural network. Back then, the bot had two commands for admins, manual mode switching, and a promise to 'eventually create a public dashboard.'

Half a year has passed since then. The bot is still free and remains a pet project that I refine in the evenings. However, it now has a proper environment: a website with statistics, clear instructions, and a personal account directly within Telegram.

In short, for those who haven't read the first article: TAB (Telegram Antispam Bot) is a bot that automatically detects and removes spam in chats. Messages are checked by an LSTM model with an attention mechanism, trained on my own LANCETNIC library. I didn't use pre-trained models from HF; I wanted to create my own model.

From Dashboard to Website

The first version of tab.lancetnic.ru was a functional dashboard 'for myself': a couple of numbers and a graph. Now it's a full-fledged project page that I can confidently share with the admin of an unfamiliar chat.

What's there:

  • Live Statistics. How much spam has been deleted, how many spammers have been banned, and how many chats are using the bot. The data updates automatically, and you can see the timestamp at the bottom indicating its recency.

  • Metric Cards. In addition to overall figures: 7-day growth and comparison with the previous week, total messages, spam percentage of traffic, and error rate.

  • 'Spam Dynamics' Graph with a period switcher: 7 days, 30 days, six months, a year, or all time.

  • Spam Heatmap by day of the week and hour.

A Few Numbers

At the time of writing this article, the bot was in 68 chats. It processed 527,554 messages, of which 13,422 were spam – that's 2.54% of the traffic (approximately every 39th message). 11,193 spammers were banned. The model's error rate is 0.39%.

Heatmap: Spammers Work on a Schedule

The most interesting feature on the website is the heatmap. I did a similar analysis in December 2025 when summarizing the year's results. Now it's available to everyone and calculated in real-time.

The pattern hasn't changed in a year. Spammers are active from 8 AM to 6 PM Moscow time, with a peak around noon on weekdays. It's almost silent at night, noticeably calmer on weekends, and Sunday is the most relaxed day. In general, bot farms seem to have a typical human workday. Apparently, they are oriented towards the Central Federal District.

Instructions You Can Read in Five Minutes

Previously, the instructions were in a single Telegram channel post. The post grew, accumulated clarifications, and at some point became a wall of text. The questions in the comments were always the same: 'Why is the bot silent?', 'Why doesn't it tag admins?'

Therefore, I rewrote it from scratch and moved it to the website: tab.lancetnic.ru/manual. Now it consists of four steps with screenshots and a FAQ section.

  1. Add the bot to the chat and grant it 'Delete Messages' and 'Ban Users' permissions. This is enough; further fine-tuning is optional.

  2. Configure the chat via the personal account /lk: select the operating mode.

  3. Set up a whitelist – who the bot ignores (trusted users, advertisers, etc.).

  4. Commands for participants: /spam in response to a suspicious message calls admins, /help displays the help.

I've described both modes separately. These are the most frequently asked questions.

  • Automatic. The neural network checks every message, and spam is deleted immediately.

  • Standard. When spam is suspected, the bot calls moderators. They click '✅ Not spam' or '❌ Spam'. The first button keeps the message and adds an entry to the dataset for retraining; the second deletes the message and bans the author.

In both modes, the bot bans automatically only if two conditions are met simultaneously: the user is in the global spammer database, and the model identifies the message as spam. This is how I guard against false bans.

Personal Account /lk and Whitelist

This is the main update that occurred in October of this year. Previously, the mode was switched using the commands /handbot and /autobot directly in the chat. It worked, but it was inconvenient: the command was visible to all participants, and if you had five chats, you had to type it five times.

Now, all management has moved to private messages with the bot.

How it Works

  1. Send the bot a private message with /lk.

  2. The bot displays a list of chats where you are the creator.

  3. Select a chat and change the mode or open the whitelist with one click.

Currently, the account is only available to chat owners, not all administrators. Other admins will have to ask the owner for now.

Whitelist

This has been requested for a long time. A typical situation: there's an advertiser in the chat who collaborates with the admins, and the bot honestly flags their posts as spam. Or an active participant whose messages the model sometimes mistakes for advertising.

Now, such people can be added to the whitelist. The bot completely ignores them: their messages are not checked or deleted.

You can add them in two ways:

  • send the bot the user's numerical ID;

  • forward any message from that person to the bot. This is easier: you don't need to find out the ID.

Removing a user works the same way. The 'Show List' button displays up to 20 people from the chat's whitelist.

One nuance: the bot already ignores messages from the owner and administrators because they are trusted individuals by default. The whitelist is for everyone else: moderators without admin rights, trusted participants, other bots.

What Else Changed Under the Hood

The website and personal account are just the visible parts. However, most of the work over the past six months has been on the invisible aspects.

Model. I didn't retrain the model for two months during the summer. Turns out, that was a mistake. Spammers get creative faster than you'd think, and the bot started missing obvious spam. In July, I compiled a fresh dataset of 30,000 lines. For the 'not spam' class, I added comments from VKontakte city public pages to diversify typical human speech.

Then there were several more retraining sessions. In one of them, I tried RAdam instead of the Adam optimizer. Now I'm observing how it performs in production.

Manual Labeling by Admins. The 'Spam' / 'Not Spam' buttons in standard mode continue to feed the dataset. So, chat admins, unknowingly, participate in training the model=). Thank you to them!

Third Level of Verification. Previously, the verdict was based on two stages: the model and the spammer database. Now there are three. I won't reveal what's checked at the third stage; otherwise, they'll quickly learn how to bypass it.

Complex Messages. There was a type of spam from which the bot couldn't extract text to feed to the model. Over six months, such messages became noticeably more frequent, and complaints started coming in. In September, it seems I managed to fix this. I'm cautiously optimistic for now.

Logs. I rewrote the logging. It sounds boring, but without it, it's impossible to resolve a disputed case and understand why someone was banned. Since June 2025, there have been two such cases: a user was listed in the spammer database, and their message was flagged as spam by the model.

Refactoring. I rewrote the model and database interactions. It's not noticeable to the user, but the bot can now handle a significant increase in the number of chats without issues.

What's Next

  • Spam in Images. The most painful topic. Spammers increasingly send text within images or stickers, and the text model simply doesn't see it. I tested Tesseract, EasyOCR, and RapidOCR, and none of them performed adequately. LLMs (I tried Qwen) did, but they can't be run locally, and API costs would be high per month. I'm unsure what to do here. One option is to write and train my own model for image processing, but I'd get buried. My main job isn't going anywhere.

  • Model Architecture. I want to rebuild it, incorporating everything learned over a year and a half of work. I suspect my LSTM isn't working as an LSTM (I might write a detailed article if I solve this issue).

  • Website. I plan to expand the dashboard further. The roadmap includes a web version of the personal account for configuring chats from a browser.

  • Technical Support. Currently, all questions are in the comments under the instructions in the channel. Something more formal will appear later.

C O N C L U S I O N S

I still don't consider TAB a 'killer of all spam.' It's a pet project that I work on in my free time. But it operates in 68 chats and has already deleted over 13,000 spam messages. For a home project started in an auto enthusiasts' chat, I think that's pretty good.

What helps the project the most is feedback. Every report of 'the bot missed this' eventually turns into a dataset entry and makes the model slightly more accurate. So, if you try the bot, please let me know what's wrong.
