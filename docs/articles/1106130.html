Walt Disney has begun testing AI-powered robot assistants to help find parking spaces in its theme parks. The design of these robots is inspired by characters from the animated films "WALL-E" and "Cars."

The robots are being tested in partnership with Field AI. Field AI was founded in 2023 by former employees of Google DeepMind, NASA, Nvidia, Tesla, and other companies.

The robots presented are small four-wheeled vehicles, similar to some autonomous food delivery robots. One of these robots is painted orange with yellow accents, mimicking a high-visibility vest. The robot is equipped with a large screen, movable eyes, and active eyelids, like the characters from "Cars."

On the roof of the autonomous robot, there is a changeable sign indicating to drivers the direction to a parking space. Additionally, the robot is equipped with two small manipulators with red lights that also guide drivers.

Disney has not announced a timeline for the implementation of these robotic parkers and has not guaranteed that they will actually be installed in Disneyland.

While these robots may be less prone to errors than human parking attendants, their initial costs will be high, according to CarScoops. The presence of such robots could increase the cost of admission tickets to theme parks. Currently, the average cost for a family of four to visit Disneyland ranges from $476 to $836.

This spring, facial recognition technology was implemented in Disney's California parks. It appeared at one of the entrances, but visitors are not required to use it.

In the summer, Walt Disney Imagineering and Adobe introduced a generative AI system designed for the design of Disney theme parks. The system is based on custom models using Adobe Firefly Foundry, trained on Walt Disney Imagineering's catalog.
