What was left out last time?

In the article "About University AI Detectors: What They Can and Cannot Do. Unexpected Results of My Own Testing", I concluded that the current versions of DUMET, Antiplagiat, and GPTZero can correctly identify the author of a text, and I proposed a more effective approach for this purpose, based on assessing the proportion of human text instead of AI. However, at that stage of testing, I only used original/primary texts that were either not subjected to any refinement (my own texts and their generated "twins") or texts of unknown origin with detected mixed authorship. Meanwhile, currently, there are fewer and fewer student papers that AI has not participated in writing in one way or another - as a search engine, generator, translator, or editor.

The workload for AI detectors is increasing, and with it, the number of complaints about detector assessments, voiced not only by negligent students but also by excellent ones. It is claimed, in particular, that even entirely human text can be assessed by detectors as having significant AI involvement. Particularly striking is the story of Angelina, a student from Kemerovo University, who "had to rewrite her thesis 13 times" until Antiplagiat assessed it as original. Unfortunately, all such claims are usually unsubstantiated - I would very much like to examine real cases and understand the exact reason for false positive AI assessments.

To satisfy this curiosity, I conducted the next stage of testing DUMET and GPTZero services (unfortunately, the cost of full testing of Antiplagiat exceeds my financial capabilities).

Testing Goal

  • to understand which AI text refinement methods for texts of different origins AI detectors can detect,

  • to assess the possible quantitative "gain" or "loss" in AI share/AI probability when using different refinement methods,

  • to understand if there is a way to trick an AI detector using an AI chat.

Objects. This time, I specifically prepared excerpts from texts of approximately the same size (11-12 thousand characters with spaces), which allows for obtaining detector assessments with high reliability.

5 texts were tested: 1) a text written by me in 2021 for an application for a grant from the Innovation Promotion Foundation, in the field of biotechnology, local treatment facilities (LOS); 2) a text generated by the Claude chat (Anthropic) on the same topic based on my technical specifications, preserving terminology, applied technologies, and volume (LOS); as well as three texts written by a student of the Philological Faculty of a regional university, B., at different times:

3) a term paper from 2024, 4) a term paper from 2025, and 5) a final qualifying work (VKR) from 2026. These three works, provided to me by the student's academic supervisor for this testing, are of particular interest as they are written by the same person, preserving their stylistic and rhythmic features.

AI Text Refinement Methods. Two technologies are most often offered in the open press, allegedly helping to reduce the level of AI assessments, so I focused on them for this stage:

1. AI editing of the source text using specially developed prompts, which remove various signs of AI generation from the text while preserving its content. You can see different prompt variations, for example, here habr.com/ru/companies/kemp_ai/articles/1039690, habr.com/ru/publications/839468, habr.com/ru/companies/cloud_ru/articles/1031552. The basic rules for writing such prompts are obvious: changing the rhythm of presentation (alternating sentences of different lengths), changing syntactic structure and punctuation, replacing standard terms with less common synonymous constructions. I must note that in addition to these principles, different authors of "manuals for combating AI" also suggest intentionally introducing typos and spaces. I found the "universal" prompt offered in the article "Prompt for Generating Text Without AI Traces" particularly interesting, as the article itself had traces of AI - 58% AI according to GPTZero. Cobbler without shoes?

When testing the capabilities of AI editing, I used, in particular, the following prompt (A):

  • Edit the text, preserving its meaning, scientific style, facts, quotes, and references.

  • Make the rhythm of presentation less uniform. Alternate short, medium, and long sentences, sometimes sharply changing their length.

  • Reorder words and sentence parts, use different syntactic constructions. Avoid repetitive beginnings, recurring conjunctions, and overly smooth transitions.

  • Allow for less common, but natural for Russian scientific language, phrasing. Do not artificially introduce rare words or add new statements.

  • Maintain the approximate text volume and headings.

2. AI translation of text. During the testing, translations from Russian to English (RU-EN), from English to Russian (EN-RU, for the text generated in English), and double translations RU-EN-RU were used, with prompts indicating the need for maximum accuracy without changing the content or introducing new meanings. Double translation from Russian to English and back is considered one of the most effective methods for reducing AI scores in the community [ t‑j.ru/can‑you‑trick‑anitplagiat, avtor24.ru/articles/sovety_studentam/kak_projti_antiplagiat_diploma, zaochnik.ru/blog/13-dejstvennyx‑sposobov‑obojti‑antiplagiat‑i-3-prichiny‑etogo‑ne‑delat], however, I found no serious justification for this.

Text translations were performed using the Claude chat, and editing was done using the ChatGPT chatbot. Detection was performed by DUMET (version not specified) and GPTZero (AI Detection Model 4.1m), and individual runs were checked on Antiplagiat (version 1.8.2 dated 06.11.2026). All runs were performed from November 5-9 using current versions of the detectors.

The testing results and my comments are presented below in separate tables for each text type. I remind you that the AI indicators of different detectors have different meanings and do not allow for a correct comparison between them.

Testing Human-Written Texts

The results of checks by university AI detectors on the "refined" human text confirm my conclusion about the greatest resilience of such texts to any form of refinement.

Antiplagiat and GPTZero assess even text refined in various ways as human-written with absolute certainty - 0% AI. However, DUMET unexpectedly showed a false positive result of 51.1% AI share after double translation of the human text into English and back. This fact prompted me to look for the reason for this result, which I managed to find and "remove," after which DUMET showed significantly lower AI values, close to 0%, indicating human authorship.

The reason for false positive AI share assessments obtained by this Russian detector is non-trivial, which compels me to dedicate a separate material to it.

Conclusion 1: Do not use AI services to "improve" your written text - this can lead to false positive AI presence assessments. Let the text remain imperfect, but you won't have problems with university checks!

Testing Machine-Generated Texts

The results of checks by the Russian-language DUMET on the original text generated in Russian, as well as all texts in Russian that were previously subjected to "refinement" (translation of the English source into Russian, double translation of the Russian source), are absolutely adequate, meaning the text continues to be assessed as generated: the decrease in the AI indicator from the maximum is less than 2%. However, DUMET cannot assess the generation of an English text, giving a false negative result of 0% AI. This indicates its still weak competence in detecting English texts.

As for the checks on the English-language GPTZero, it demonstrates complete correctness of conclusions for English texts - 100% AI, and lower correspondence of AI probability to the actual level of generation in Russian texts. However, the detector's conclusion regarding the text's origin remains correct in this case as well. I note here that the greatest decrease in AI probability is observed when editing the generated text using prompt A - almost by half from the original, by 38%.

Thus, none of the "refinement" methods tested here help turn AI generation into human text.

Conclusion 2: Do not try to turn generated text into human text using AI editing and prompts. The decrease in AI share according to DUMET for all investigated refinement methods was less than 3 percent.

Conclusion 3: Each university detector shows the highest sensitivity to texts in its "native" language. Advice for examiners in cases where a student wants to write and defend a thesis in English - check it on GPTZero.

Testing Real/Mixed Texts

The assessments presented above are of particular value because they tested 3 texts written by the same person at different times. In this case, the student's style, vocabulary, and rhythm of presentation should likely be similar across all texts. The table shows how the student's use of AI texts has increased over the last three years - from 0% to 56% according to GPTZero and from 12.5% to 70% according to DUMET.

I do not know if the student used AI services in any capacity for each of these works, but I know from the academic supervisor that she is an excellent student and defended all three works with a "5" (excellent grade). This information made me doubt the reality of the 70% AI assessment made by DUMET when checking the 2026 VKR. And I managed to prove this assessment wrong by finding a simple way to significantly reduce the AI share in this work without changing its content. I will discuss this method in detail later in a material dedicated to studying the reasons for false positive results of Russian AI detectors.

Thus, editing with a prompt aimed at reducing AI and double translation of your text will not help solve the problem, but will only worsen it. For all three texts, "refinement" increased the AI text share detected by DUMET by 5-25%, except for one case where the decrease in AI share was 3%.

Main conclusion: Do not try to make your text more human with the help of a machine. Only honestly completed and independently written works can pass AI detectors without issues.
