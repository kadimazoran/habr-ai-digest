I am developing my own line of small language models, dpp-gpt. Not fine-tuning someone else's model, but everything from scratch: tokenizer, pre-train, SFT, RL, benchmarks. All of this on a single home RTX 4060 graphics card with 8 GB of memory. Version 2.2 Pro with 260 million parameters was recently released. In this article, I will explain its architecture, what worked, what didn't, and what unexpected things surfaced along the way.

To be upfront: this is not a competitor to models trained by large teams on trillions of tokens. On generally accepted English benchmarks, it lags even behind the smallest of them (benchmarks below). It is interesting for other reasons: how much can be squeezed out of a single graphics card, and what can be seen from the inside.

What is this model

Parameters

259M (242M unique: input and output embeddings are shared)

Architecture

Llama-like: RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, GQA

Layers / width / heads / KV-heads

20 / 1024 / 16 / 4

Context

8192 tokens

Vocabulary

16,384, custom BPE, digits as separate tokens

Languages

Russian, English, French

Pre-train: 31 billion tokens

2.2 continues the pre-training of the base version 2.1: to its 11.8 billion tokens, I added about 19 billion more, for a total of approximately 31 billion. The mix:

  • Wikipedia in three languages: 23%;

  • CulturaX (web) in three languages: 13%;

  • Python code: 16%, web code: 5%;

  • Cosmopedia and Zyda-2: 5% each;

  • poems 4%, facts 5%, translations 5%;

  • synthetic text manipulation data (writing by letters, word analysis, caps): 4%;

  • synthetic code generated by deepseek 4 flash: 1%;

  • mathematics: 14%.

The mathematics was generated by a script, with digit-by-digit calculation. Thanks to this, the model can handle large numbers. Example format:

Action 1: 290 * 3
Hundreds: 2 * 3 = 6 (600)
Tens: 9 * 3 = 27 (270)
Units: 0 * 3 = 0 (0)
Total: 600 + 270 + 0 = 870
Answer: 870
