Why do we converge to (almost) fixed points?

The hypothesis of the work is that neural networks are contractive.

A contractive mapping is one that brings any two points closer: if we take and and apply to them, the distance between them is guaranteed to decrease, and by at least a fixed factor. From this, we get convergence: over steps, the distance decreases as , i.e., geometrically, and any two trajectories will eventually "stick together".

Contractivity can be estimated: it is described in the practical notebook for the review. In short - models are contractive even at initialization - i.e., before training.

After training, the contractive properties of the mapping worsen on the training data and become stronger the closer we are to the boundary outside the training data. This is because the training process forces the model not to lose information about the points. Hence, the loss penalizes the model for any compression (it leads to information loss).
