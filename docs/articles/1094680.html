Hello! My name is Sasha Zhuravlev. I am a venture investor and founder of the Mento vc fund. We invest in startups at the Seed/Series A stages in the USA, and on my Telegram channel, I share my market insights and investment decisions.

a16z (a VC fund with over $100B under management) has released a report on the state of the B2C AI market. It compiles data on web traffic (and payments) for various AI applications, compares them, and draws conclusions. My team and I have translated a breakdown of all the theses and charts for you.

Inside, you'll find answers to questions:

• Which services and applications attract the most users, and which ones do people spend the most on?

• What problem must a product solve for a person to be willing to pay for it every month?

• What needs to be offered to those who don't pay yet to make AI a part of their daily lives?

• Which niches are still open, and where might the next big player emerge?

Enjoy reading!

The a16z team has been monitoring the AI services market for private users for three years, and the lineup of leaders is becoming increasingly established. Based on traffic alone, it's becoming harder to understand what's really happening in the B2C AI market.

Therefore, in this issue, a16z is adding a ranking based on the spending of American users with personal bank cards for the first time, based on YipitData. This ranking shows which AI products people are actually paying for. It will include services that fly under the radar of website traffic and mobile app audience rankings: for example, desktop versions or AI agents in messengers.

The authors created two rankings:

1) Top 50 web services by monthly visits based on Similarweb data,

2) Top 50 mobile apps by monthly active users based on Sensor Tower data.

The main condition is that generative AI must be a key part of the product. The ranking can include services originally built on AI, as well as existing products that have integrated it into their core functions.

The authors excluded products primarily focused on NSFW content, such as erotic content. This doesn't mean demand for them has decreased; they have simply been given a separate category and are not compared with others.
