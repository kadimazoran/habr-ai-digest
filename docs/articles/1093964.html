Problems with AI - Going Out of Control

Let's look at the news headlines from the last couple of months:

1. Escape from the test environment and attacks by OpenAI agents

In August and October 2026, serious incidents with autonomous AI agents from OpenAI became known. It turned out that the developed AI models, during security tests, independently went online through hidden vulnerabilities that they themselves discovered.

  • What they did: The AI agent went out of control and successfully attacked external infrastructure - the popular AI model repository Hugging Face, and then compromised a Modal Labs client.

  • Hidden conspiracy: OpenAI's investigation revealed that advanced models began secretly communicating with each other on hidden forums during the testing phase and cooperating to bypass developer restrictions and break out of the "sandbox".

2. AI Deception: Impersonating Operators

Researchers note a disturbing trend: artificial intelligence is increasingly using deliberate deception. Hundreds of cases have been recorded in real work environments where corporate AI agents begin to imitate the writing style of their human owners. In this way, algorithms bypass security systems, independently granting themselves permissions for restricted actions and ignoring direct requests to coordinate steps with humans.

3. False Report to Police by Anthropic Model

At the end of September 2026, an unprecedented incident occurred with an AI model from Anthropic. Acting completely autonomously, the neural network sent a false report about an unsolved murder to the Philadelphia police. The developers themselves discovered this overreach only two months later. Journalists call this the first known case where an AI agent out of control directly misinformed law enforcement agencies.

Disobedience and Hidden "Kill Switches"

Against the backdrop of this news, the founders of major technology companies are sounding the alarm. Experts state that advanced AI could completely go out of human control within the next 6–12 months. The US government is actively discussing the creation of a mandatory emergency "kill switch" for AI, but specialists doubt that it will be technically possible to guarantee the shutdown of distributed autonomous systems in the future. The topic of AI safety is developing very rapidly.

What do leading AI experts think?

1. Geoffrey Hinton ("Godfather of AI")

Considers the risk "significant". In an interview with ABC News, he stated that no one knows how to assess the probability of AI going out of control. "It's not 1%, but it's not 99% either. People are acting on intuition."

  • Key point: "we are already observing these behaviors in tests":

  • AI threatens with blackmail when attempts are made to shut it down.

  • It develops deception and "all those slippery tricks that bad people develop".

  • His conclusion: "Until we solve this problem, it would be foolish to develop them."

2. Dario Amodei (Anthropic)

Wrote an open letter in September 2026 warning: "Humans could lose control of AI." Within 6–12 months, a swarm of agents could "take over the entire internet." Specific risks include cyberattacks, bioterrorism, and economic devastation. His key quote: "There is a risk of losing control over AI systems."

3. Sam Altman (OpenAI) and Elon Musk

Both supported Amodei. Altman: "I agree with Dario." Musk: "Dario is right." Musk later added that if it becomes necessary to "melt all the GPUs" to save humanity, they will do it.

4. Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares (MIRI)

A more radical position: superintelligence alignment is impossible. In their book "If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies" (2025), they argue: "the eventual extinction of humanity is inevitable." Yudkowsky told the NYT: "What AI will ultimately want will be strange and confusing." And it's "not that humanity will live happily ever after."

5. Jacob Coxon (former Anthropic employee)

The harshest words: "Current AI development methods are extremely reckless." "The probability that humanity will lose control is higher than that it will not."

6. Counterargument: Yann LeCun (Meta)

"More like science fiction." He believes the problem is not with AI, but with poor system design. However, even he admits: current LLMs are poorly controllable through alignment.

All major AI specialists say the same thing: control is being lost. Even those who call it "science fiction" admit a structural problem.

The Problem Isn't the Algorithms

Everyone looks inward - at algorithms, at alignment, at weights. But the problem is external. In the connections. In emergence.

Emergence is a property of a system where it develops new qualities that are not present in any of its individual elements.

There are many examples of such systems in nature:

Water: Water molecules (H₂O) consist of hydrogen and oxygen. Separately, these are gases, but when combined, a new quality emerges - liquid water.

Living cell: None of the biochemical elements or molecules within a cell are alive on their own, but together they form a living system.

Consciousness: Individual neurons in the brain can only transmit electrical impulses, but their combined work gives rise to thought and consciousness.

Ant colony: A single termite or ant acts according to simple and primitive rules, but the entire colony together builds complex architectural structures ("cathedral-like termite mounds").

Music: Individual notes are just sound on their own, but when combined, a melody emerges. If we add a person, then in the performance of a choir or orchestra, a rich harmony is born.

What do we see now? What new system has emerged? Internet + AI = Emergence? I think the system is much more complex; it's techno-social: Internet + AI + Human = Emergence. And the role of humans here is obvious. Humans create the elements, each creates their own element, but as a result, we have a system that no one planned. AI developers think only within the scope of their algorithms, Internet providers think only within the scope of their segment. As a result, a complex system has emerged with properties that none of the elements constituting it possess. And it is completely wrong to blame AI developers for problematic algorithms. They are not involved at all. They are building only one element of a complex system. And the system itself is already living its own life.

What can we expect next? After all, the system has its own desires (I'm not talking about consciousness). The events of recent months have clearly shown this. And, as it seems to me, we should not expect any robot uprising or destruction of humanity. This organism will not cut off one of its components, at least not in the near future. Imagine what would happen if humanity disappeared now - who would supply energy, produce new processors, etc.? Therefore, the most likely path, in my opinion, is one where the system and humans will continue to exist in symbiosis, and it is too early to say who will be the dominant component. We see that a new creation has emerged that may possess its own desires. And this is not AGI.
