Anthropic announced that its AI model Claude has found solutions to two long-standing problems in theoretical physics related to black holes. The results are published in two preprints and may aid in the search for a quantum theory of gravity, which aims to unify Einstein's general theory of relativity and quantum mechanics.

According to Anthropic, the research began with an unusual experiment: Hung-Bin Chen tasked Claude with compiling a list of unsolved problems. The AI proposed 22 problems, after which several copies of it worked in parallel for about six weeks, exchanging results and verifying calculations. For the first problem, almost all calculation steps were performed in two independent ways. For the second, Anthropic reports, the results were further verified using the Lean formal verification system.

Both studies rely on holographic duality. According to this idea, some model universes with gravity can be described by an equivalent quantum theory without gravity that exists on their boundary. This allows for the study of spacetime properties through particle behavior.

The first problem concerns the Hawking-Page transition, described in 1983. In a specific model of the universe called anti-de Sitter space, when heated, a gas of particles transitions to a state with a black hole. This transition occurs abruptly, similar to a phase transition. However, it remained unknown whether its character persists in the quantum regime, where the usual description of spacetime geometry is no longer applicable.

As early as 2003, physicists proposed a calculation that could provide an answer, but it proved too complex. Claude performed the necessary calculations in the supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory and established that, under weak particle interactions, the transition is also abrupt. This supports the hypothesis of a common nature for both phenomena, although a continuous link between the regimes has not yet been proven.

In the second work, the AI investigated the modular bootstrap, a mathematical method for testing quantum gravity theories for internal consistency. It allows for the rejection of models that violate certain symmetry conditions without prior knowledge of the correct theory's form.

For many years, scientists have tried to determine how strict the limitations imposed by this method could be in a simplified three-dimensional spacetime model. The previously proven bound was around 8.5, although numerical experiments suggested a tighter limit.

Claude discovered a pattern in the numerical results and applied the mathematical apparatus of spectral curves. This led to the analytical derivation of an exact bound for the characteristic of the lightest non-trivial quantum state: at a large central charge c, it takes the form c/9.1266. The authors also showed that the chosen variant of the method cannot improve this bound. Other variants of the bootstrap, however, may provide stricter limitations.

Both works are currently presented as preprints and have not undergone independent scientific peer review. Nevertheless, Claude has managed to obtain results that physicists have been unable to derive for many years.
