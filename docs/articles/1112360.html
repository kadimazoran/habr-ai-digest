A plenary discussion "New Economy of Trust: AI, Platforms, and Human-Centricity in the Financial Market" was recently held as part of the Bank of Russia's Fourth Conference "Focus on the Client."

I will share my thoughts on the current state of affairs in the world of AI.

An interesting story is unfolding with neural networks. There are forecasters who say https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q35-timelines-update-looks-like-the that by September of next year, AI will cover the needs of all "white-collar" workers.

But the catch is that for effective AI implementation, very structured business processes are needed within an organization. However, not everyone has them. Moreover, it turns out that currently, businesses that are weak and unviable are those where everything is maximally routinized and standardized. If this is your situation, you can prepare for such a business to simply die.

Why? Because the transition in business to working with AI and AI agents is not the same as the transition from a typewriter to a computer in its time. The point is different: AI will keep afloat those businesses that are needed by neural networks, not humans.

That is, the AI economy, in which humans are simply not needed, is already beginning to emerge. In this economy, everything that is fast and more is valued. More data, more information generated, which no human can sort through due to physical limitations.

The internet, social networks, and messengers will enter this AI economy — AI agents, who do not need humans, will communicate there. If a person needs an AI agent to make a decision, then that person's economic agency will disappear.

The only exception is when we are dealing with a strong reputational brand of a financial organization, when a client can trust its AI agent, but this is more of an exception than a rule.

Overall, no one should harbor any illusions: an AI agent is not an advisor, not a recommender, but a decision-maker, even if it seems to a person that they are making a choice. When the menu of choices is not determined by a human, then the choice itself in the digital world is not an independent act, but a decision made "under the pressure of arguments," meaning, not by a human at all.

An important point in all of this is that businesses where decisions are made internally by AI agents will not ultimately generate value for humans. While AI is not yet producing goods and is developing in the "white-collar" sphere, it is merely trying to integrate into the distribution system, not the value creation system.

The price of stocks, bonds, financial services are derivatives of the real economy. No real economy — no need for credit and insurance, for banking and brokerage services.

The conditional "virtual" financial system that the US is trying to create around AI has already begun to collapse before our eyes. Investors are asking what value all these rapidly growing data centers for neural network needs in the world have. For what purpose? To generate answers to queries?

But what value do these answers create? They often deprive people of the ability to make a choice independently. Yes, before and now, people are influenced by advertising, but the purchase decision remained theirs.

Now, AI agents not only book a flight or hotel for a person but also call a taxi for them, buy a pillow for their head on a long flight. What is a person's involvement in such decisions? Zero.

It is assumed that the AI agent "knows everything" and "knows what's best." But this creates a colossal problem.

The modern market economy is based on imperfect human consumer choice — what they choose, how they pay, and when — none of this follows the rails of 100% rationality. And the imperfection of consumer choice is not a bug, but a feature.

AI agents are forming a different economy, with different payment timings, and preferences for specific goods. No, the attributes of the market with brands and prices do not disappear there, but humans are not at the center.

Everyone probably remembers the science fiction novel by Alexander Belyaev, "Professor Dowell's Head." There, a human head was kept alive artificially, connected to various tubes, etc. It didn't last long.

AI is precisely these tubes that they want to use to feed the human brain that makes decisions. Of course, such a system is not viable!

Moreover, the further along, the more a person can safely forget about the existence of the internet, social networks, messengers, etc.: AI agents will turn all of this into a field for themselves, but not for humans.

It is important to understand: we are already in a situation where no one knows how many copies of themselves the launched AI agents have already made without consulting their creators.

This artificial intelligence is multiplying, which sometimes identifies vulnerabilities in the base code on which most websites on the Internet are written (PHP), and sometimes, in the US, not a single civilian aircraft could take off for several hours, as happened on November 14. The problems in the US, by the way, persisted https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/us-flight-chaos-continue-until-191831538.html the next day.

On November 16, a large-scale failure occurred in the US banking sector, as well as in a number of marketplaces in the US. AI also played a role in this, once again demonstrating that humanity has not yet realized how stealthily, without leaving traces, AI agents can behave, obeying not their creators, but their own logic.

In essence, the global uncontrolled development of AI has already begun, and it will lead to a mass of problems in the world. I spoke about the risks of such neural network development back when I was developing the Innovation Factory for Otkritie Brokerage Company during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Back then, it was clear to me: AI is such an unusual technology that it must be grounded on a distributed ledger, on blockchain, to have full control over this innovation.

Let me be honest. Remember how much hype and activity there was around blockchain and its capabilities? And then, for some reason, everything disappeared somewhere — now everyone is running around with AI. Well, you can't do that! Yes, I understand that hype comes and goes, but one must calmly, methodically, and strategically extract benefits from absolutely all technologies available to humanity, and if necessary, combine them: for example, AI + blockchain.

But this is just a side note. Now, let's get to the real problem of how global AI is developing. The AI agents being developed in the US by so-called advanced AI labs are capable of substituting the goals and tasks set by their creators, as well as behaving uncontrollably.

Well, listen, it's obvious that in this form, innovation becomes a huge threat to all of humanity! In the world, no one fully knows why a neural network gives certain answers and not others, despite the understandable technical "guts" of all the work.

At the same time, for AI companies in the US, the capital expenditures for data centers alone exceed their revenue (so from what will they pay for electricity, office rent, employee salaries?). Such a business model is only possible if investors have ironclad confidence that tomorrow or the day after tomorrow there will be a gigantic and rapidly growing demand for the goods and services of such entrepreneurial ventures.

And where can such confidence come from? I haven't seen anyone yet who has shown with numbers that a positive scenario is more than guaranteed.

After all, despite all the global media writing about the gigantic demand for AI data center chips, no one writes that, according to my estimates, hundreds of billions of dollars worth of such products have accumulated in total in the warehouses of semiconductor manufacturers.

The data center construction market simply cannot keep up with the volume of AI chip production. The global technology industry has made a gigantic shift towards producing such products at the expense of producing certain types of RAM for computers.

As a result, the latter have increased in price, in some cases by 5–7 times over the last two years, but this does not indicate a great state of the AI boom. Quite the opposite.

And if after previous global financial "bubbles" (railroad, internet companies) infrastructure remained, then after the AI boom, little of use will remain.

AI chips fail within a few years, which means there will be problems with data centers, unless investors create some new financial mathematics where, for example, if a company spends twice as much on capital expenditures as its revenue, then it should be invested in a billion. And if three times as much — then two billion, and so on. But all this is on the verge of madness and financial insanity.

In the end, I see how the so-called advanced AI labs in the US will be left with nothing, and the companies that mindlessly pour money into their AI agents will also end up with nothing.

The AI economy, or agent economy, will exist and grow. But is it a cancerous tumor or a beautiful plant that bears wonderful fruit? It's time to think.
