What information processes occur in our brain? What goals does it pursue? What data model and algebra can be used to represent its work? Why are we prone to various cognitive biases? Why do we need sleep and can we do without it? How can we use our brain to its full potential?

My name is Mikhail Kocherov, and I want to understand how our brain works. And what does it mean to understand? Is knowledge about brain morphology or its development from conception to death considered understood or not? To each his own. For me, to understand means to be able to reproduce, even in a simplified form. To grasp the key mechanisms and know how to assemble a working system from them that reproduces the intellectual properties of the brain in a holistic way.

Humans, birds, fish, etc. – these are autonomous intelligent agents with brains. They perceive the world to the best of their abilities, adapt to it, and develop optimal behavior based on trial, error, and rare but highly desired rewards. This is nothing other than Reinforcement Learning (RL). Hence the idea: we develop an RL agent for a non-trivial problem and, in the process, encounter the same difficulties and tasks that nature faced when developing the brain. Consequently, by solving these problems, we also understand the brain itself, its structure, and mechanisms.

Such a non-trivial RL problem is seen in the development of an agent that learns to play computer games. The agent must figure out how to perceive the external environment (e.g., a video stream), grasp the internal logic of games, develop a behavior policy for each, and maintain memory to not forget past experiences. The Atari Learning Environment (ALE) [8][9] + Gymnasium [10] is perfectly suited for these experiments.
