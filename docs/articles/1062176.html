Last autumn, we released the MWS AI Vision Bench into the public domain – a benchmark for evaluating multimodal models on Russian-language documents. Since then, models have improved significantly, we've identified several issues in our own annotation, and added a task that we previously lacked.

In the winter, we clarified the wording of some VQA questions and acceptable answers. In the spring, we collected a dataset and a set of Anti-fraud tasks to test models on their ability to detect forged and generated documents. In the summer and early autumn, we ran the latest models again – most recently GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, and Qwen3.8 – and observed where the metrics were hitting a ceiling.

Anti-fraud turned out to be more complex than we initially thought. This is what I want to talk about.

Where the Task Came From

In the incoming stream of documents, we encounter scans that have been edited in a graphics editor. Names, dates, numbers, amounts, signatures are changed. Now, documents entirely recreated by a generative model have been added to these.

Professional anti-fraud systems use specialized models, rules, attribute verification, and external sources to detect these types of fraud. MWS AI Vision Bench does not replace them. We were interested in a more grounded question: if a developer builds document processing on a single universal multimodal LLM, will it notice even an obvious forgery and be able to explain what exactly made it suspicious.

We wanted to get a single score from 0 to 1. It should account for the correct class and the quality of the explanation.

What's in the Dataset

We collected 430 images and divided them into three classes:

  • original — 200 images considered original and unedited in the experiment;

  • edited — 130 manually edited images using graphics editors like Photoshop;

  • ai_gen — 100 documents entirely recreated by a generative model based on a reference.

The Anti-fraud dataset has a separate research-only license. Its terms were formulated with the MWS AI legal department: the dataset can be used for research and model comparison, but not for developing document forgery tools, training forgery generators, or other illegal activities.
