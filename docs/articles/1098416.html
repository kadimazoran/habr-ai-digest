The article should actually be titled "Which SEO factors influence GEO?" This would be more accurate, as there are no separate site optimization factors specifically for neural networks.

There's also the issue of mixing two different tasks. One is getting a neural network to find a page, link to it, and bring a visitor. The other is for it to know about the company and recommend it in its responses. These tasks share a common base, but the mechanics and metrics differ.

I've compiled a table ranking SEO factors by importance for GEO. This is my current view and practical prioritization for October 2026, not published data from ChatGPT, Google, or Alice algorithms. Below, I'll first show the whole picture, and then divide the factors by two promotion scenarios.

All GEO Factors: My Current Assessment

High Influence

Page indexing; content accessibility for search and AI bots; relevance and semantics; Title, H1, and headings; unique expert content with data; brand mentions on external platforms. The first five primarily help in finding and citing pages. The last one is particularly important for company recognition and recommendations.

Medium Influence

Positions in regular search results, backlink profile, content structure, data freshness, and internal linking. They help in various ways: through page discovery, SEO visibility, extracting specific fragments, and maintaining trust in information. However, none of them can be called a guaranteed "neural network ranking factor."

Indirect or Unproven Influence

Schema.org and Core Web Vitals are indirect factors; the standalone effect of llms.txt on AI citation has not been proven by anyone. Tables, FAQ sections, microdata, or good speed scores alone won't make a neural network choose a specific page.

The same SEO factor can be helpful in one scenario and offer little in another. Therefore, I will further analyze page promotion and brand promotion separately.

Part 1. Factors for a Website: Becoming a Source and Getting Traffic

Here, we optimize URLs, not abstract company awareness. For a website, several stages are necessary: the page must be accessible → the neural network or its search must find it → the system must select the document as a source → the user must see the link and want to click. The fourth stage does not automatically follow from the third: even a cited article may not receive a single click. Moreover, being included as a source does not guarantee that the information from your site will be used.

1. Indexing and Accessibility - Without This, Nothing Else Matters

I start with SEO basics: I check if the required URLs are indexed in Google and Yandex, if crawling is allowed by robots.txt, if there are noindex tags, incorrect canonicals, HTTP errors, JavaScript traps, or protective filters from CDN/WAF. I also check if the necessary bots can access the content. The ability to get into the search index and the ability for an AI bot to read directly are different things.

Google explicitly states that for a page to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode links, it must be indexed and eligible for display with a snippet. The company does not announce any separate technical requirements beyond the basic Google Search requirements. In other AI systems, the search route may differ, with the only distinction being that they use the existing search index of a particular search engine.

2. Semantics, Title, and H1 - Answering the Question the Neural Network Is Actually Searching For

A neural network might not search for exactly what the user typed into the chat. It generates additional queries (query fan-out), breaks down complex questions into subtopics, retrieves different sets of pages, and then selects sources. Therefore, for GEO, it's not enough to know your URL's position for the initial keyword phrase.

A study by AirOps and Kevin Indig (April 13, 2026) analyzed 16,851 queries, three runs for each, and 353,799 pages in ChatGPT's search chain. Pages with headings most semantically similar to the user's query were cited in 41.0% of cases, compared to approximately 30% for weakly matching headings. This is a statistical correlation within the studied sample, mind you.

Therefore, we should structure articles and landing pages not around a meaningless broad collection of LSI keywords, but around specific questions. The Title and H1 should clearly define the subject, and the content should provide an answer that the search subsystem can recognize and extract. Nothing new, right?)

3. Expert Content and Structure - The Material Must Be a Useful Source

Research, original calculations, measurable results, comparative tables, definitions, and methodology descriptions provide the factual basis for a page that is worth retelling or citing. Value arises when the material truly answers the query and contains verifiable information.

AirOps also found that with high relevance to the original question, moderate coverage of additional sub-queries could perform better than trying to cover all possible subtopics. This means you don't need to turn every article into an encyclopedia just for GEO. Tables, lists, and short semantic blocks are useful where they help structure facts.

Data freshness is important primarily where information changes rapidly: prices, tariffs, specifications, ratings, news. Updating the publication date without changing the content is a poor strategy.

4. SEO Positions: TOP-10 Helps, But Is Not a Prerequisite

Here, it's necessary to distinguish between two types of ranking. The first is positions for the original query in Google or another search engine. The second is the URL's place among the results that the neural network itself retrieved for its search sub-queries.

AirOps showed a strong correlation between citation and the second type: for the first position in ChatGPT's internal results, the citation rate was 58.4%; for the tenth, it was 14.2%. Ahrefs, after studying 863,000 search results and about 4 million URLs in Google AI Overviews, found that only 37.9% of cited URLs were in the top ten blocks of regular search results for the original query. Another 31.2% were in positions 11-100, and 31.0% were not in the top hundred blocks.
At the same time, remember that Alice's search rarely goes beyond the TOP10.

It turns out that good SEO positions haven't lost their value, but you can achieve results without them. That's why in my table, the position in classic search results is a medium factor, while relevance to neural network queries is high.

5. Backlink Profile and Internal Linking - A Useful SEO Foundation

External links can contribute to content discovery, SEO ranking, and publication visibility. Internal links help bots find documents and understand the relationships between them. However, they cannot be considered a direct button to increase AI citations. In my research, domain authority and the volume of backlinks did not show a positive correlation with neural network citations. My priority in projects: fix internal links for important pages, then work on the content.

6. Schema.org, Core Web Vitals, and llms.txt

Schema.org can help search engines interpret data and generate rich results. However, we have no proven standalone causal effect on AI citation. In AirOps, pages with JSON-LD showed a higher citation rate, but such comparisons do not allow us to assert that the markup itself caused this result.
I have a theory that by expanding the snippet, it provides more words in it, and neural networks, in the first step of analysis, read snippets, and only the best ones get into the selection of source sites. However, this idea requires further verification, which I have not yet completed.

Core Web Vitals are important for website usability and overall SEO in terms of behavioral factors, but a correlation between improving LCP and increased AI citation cannot yet be considered established. Do not confuse user experience metrics with a bot's ability to retrieve the main text: accessibility and a correct HTTP response are important for a different reason.

llms.txt should also not be marketed as a working GEO technique. Google has not announced it as an additional requirement for AI Overviews and AI Mode; there is no convincing confirmation of increased AI citations from creating this file. Numerous experiments have shown this.

Why Neural Networks Cite Some Pages and Not Others

Researchers Mahe Chen and Nick Koudas analyzed 158,099 responses and 468,037 citations. They compared pages within the same websites to separate the characteristics of individual materials from domain familiarity.

It turned out that ChatGPT first found the page through its own search and only then could cite it. Moreover, the neural network formulates search queries itself – they may differ from the user's original question.

The authors built statistical models. How often a URL appeared in ChatGPT's internal search and how high it ranked helped explain 32% of the differences in the number of citations for pages on the same site. Page characteristics – headings, content, FAQ, Schema.org, and others – explained only 2.8%.

Consider the question: "Which billing system should a telecom operator choose?" A neural network might search separately for information on tariffs, features, and limitations of different systems. If your article is not found at all, additional microdata won't make it a source for this answer. However, text relevance remains important: pages whose headings and paragraphs better answered the questions were cited more often.

My conclusion: before adding special "GEO blocks," we need to check what queries the neural network makes, what URLs it finds, and whether our material matches these queries.

Part 2. Factors for a Brand: What to Do to Get Neural Networks to Name and Recommend a Company

Now for a different task. A user might ask: "Which company to choose?" "What services solve this problem?" "Name alternatives to product X." The neural network responds with company and product names. In this case, it's not obligated to list the website of each as a source, let alone direct visitors to it.

Therefore, for a brand, it's more important whether the model has enough information about the company, in what context it appears, what tasks it's associated with, and how appropriate it is to recommend it for a specific query.

1. External Mentions

In December 2025, Ahrefs studied 75,000 brands and found a strong correlation between the number of their mentions online and their presence in ChatGPT, AI Mode, and AI Overviews responses: approximately 0.66–0.71. The correlation with YouTube mentions was even higher – around 0.737. The dependence on traditional metrics like Domain Rating or the number of site pages turned out to be weaker.

Well-known brands are mentioned more often and appear in queries more frequently. Great, isn't it?)

Later, this was confirmed by our own practice and other studies. The main metric will be the number of brand mentions on the internet. Articles, media, social networks, reviews, videos, etc. Simply the presence of the brand and a description of its product, activities, comparisons, and so on.

2. Where and How to Mention a Brand

I usually work not so much for the number of publications, but for the completeness of presence in choice situations. Industry media, specialized reviews, ratings, expert comments, videos, interviews, thematic communities, substantive reviews, and materials from independent authors. We choose the platform format based on where the potential buyer seeks confirmation and where neural networks find relevant data.

It's more important for the brand to be mentioned in conjunction with a specific competence, product category, customer problem, product characteristic, price, geography, or comparison. However, there are no direct universal coefficients for these formats yet.

3. Positioning and Model Knowledge

The model's knowledge about brands can influence the search queries it formulates and the companies it names. In controlled experiments, the name of a familiar brand could change the order of recommendations with identical product characteristics. Also, a brand mention can occur without a link to a specific page in the response.

This means that for brand promotion, stable connections are needed: "name → category → tasks → distinctions → evidence." These are formed not only by the website but also by the external information environment. This is not a reason to promise that ChatGPT can be quickly "retrained" with publications. The effect of search engines indexing information is faster than changing the model's knowledge.

4. Why a Brand Still Needs Its Own Website

The website should confirm the information found about the company externally: what it does, what products it offers, where it operates, the cost of solutions, limitations, case studies, and reviews. If an external article says one thing and a product page says another, it's harder for neural networks to assemble a consistent answer.

Here, correct headings, semantics, and expert content help again, but as part of the overall knowledge base about the brand. Their role differs from the goal of "getting an article into sources": we increase the probability of correct identification and appropriate recommendation of the company.

5. What to Consider as GEO Results for a Brand

I would differentiate:

  • The proportion of verifiable queries where the brand is mentioned;

  • The proportion compared to competitors;

  • The presence of positive and negative biases;

  • The number of queries where our site is the source of information;

  • Clicks to the site from specific neural networks;

  • If you are not doing SEO in parallel, then an increase in search traffic can be counted as a plus, as clicks in search for neural network responses are visible in metrics as "search traffic."

Measurement should be done on a stable set of real questions and for specific neural networks: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Alice, and other systems search and form answers differently.

How I Would Divide the Work on a Project

If the Goal Is Traffic and Website Citation

Start with indexing and bot accessibility → study real audience questions and neural network search sub-queries → precisely align Title, H1, and content with intent → add unique factual material → ensure convenient structure and internal linking → measure sources, clicks, and conversions.

If the Goal Is Brand Mention and Recommendation

Identify choice situations and competitors → check what neural networks know about the company → eliminate discrepancies on official pages → obtain substantive independent mentions in relevant sources → track not only the appearance of the name but also the context, accuracy, and changes relative to competitors.

Conclusion

For a website, the main task is to become a found and selected source, and then get a click. For a brand, it's to become a known and appropriate recommendation in a specific choice situation.

This is my current GEO factors model. As reproducible research and results from my own experiments become available, its priorities may change. And if someone demonstrates the controlled influence of microdata or CWV on citations and recommendations, all else being equal, I will watch with interest.
