2. Why Does a Company CEO's Romance Tank the Stock?

Let's start with the simple. Why do the sexual escapades of one person cause an earthquake in a $4.5 trillion market?

Well, firstly, because this person is a major shareholder. According to Nvidia's SEC filing for shareholders as of March 2026, Huang owns about 870.6 million shares, which is 3.58% of the company. It seems like a small amount. At current prices, this is about $150 billion.

If it comes to divorce, property division will begin. Huang's marriage is registered in California. Community property laws apply there, and by default, all assets acquired during the marriage are divided equally. Half of the community property here is a stake worth tens of billions of dollars, part of which is held in trusts.

And here's the question that every fund holding NVDA shares is asking itself: what will the new owner do with this stake? Hold it? Sell it in parts? Or just dump it all at once out of spite to the unfaithful husband?

A hypothetical 1.5-2% of market capitalization might not seem catastrophic. But for an overvalued stock, where a large portion of positions are opened on margin, the sudden sale of such a volume can cause a 10-15% drop in an instant. And that leads to liquidations. And the situation becomes like in "Pirates of the Caribbean," where everyone sits and looks at each other - who will blink first or draw a gun. And then the liquidations begin.

The pattern is not new: Bezos's divorce in 2019 also kept the market on edge due to his Amazon stake. The difference is that Bezos's story ended there. But here, the twists turned out to be much more interesting.

But a harem in top management is just the beginning. The real issue is Megaspeed.

What is this company. A Singaporean cloud provider that rents out computing power based on Nvidia chips. It was previously called 7Road International and was renamed Megaspeed in March 2023. Alice Huang was its managing director from March to November 2023. According to Bloomberg, four days after the ownership change, Megaspeed became an official Nvidia Cloud Partner.

Scale. The company's assets grew from $33 million to $3 billion in a year, according to Reuters. According to Bloomberg estimates, since 2023, Megaspeed has imported at least $4.6 billion worth of Nvidia hardware – no less than 136,000 GPUs. According to trade data cited by the NYT, the Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix purchased $4.8 billion worth of equipment from June 2024 to February 2026.

6. The AI Mafia Boss: Circular Financing and the Structure of the AI Pyramid

Now for the most important thesis for my material today. I see that many people don't understand the essence of the circular financing that Uncle Huang has set up. Huang essentially reassembled the financial system of the entire Big Tech into one mega-company, where he became the profit center. I emphasize: not Nvidia, but him specifically.

How did this happen?

Big Tech ran out of growth ideas. The money supply is growing, and this money needs to go somewhere. And then AI appears, which may or may not become AGI. There are no other ideas anyway. If you don't invest in AI, the market takes your money away. If you don't use AI, you're not sexy, and the market takes your money away again.

In classic software, the cost of a new user was almost zero. In AI, it's the opposite: each new user consumes scarce computing resources, and the cost increases. A race begins: who can level this curve, and ideally reverse it, so that each new user increases profit margins, not decreases them. And this entire race has hit a wall with Nvidia and its ecosystem, which has been built for over 20 years. All the money flowed into one company.

How the scheme worked. My reconstruction, based solely on my personal opinion:

  • Tech giants took their suitcases of money to Nvidia. When they ran out of free cash, they started borrowing.

  • To be in a priority queue for new chips, you need to buy certain volumes - whether you need them or not.

  • Chips went from being a toy for gamers and miners to an asset on par with real estate. They are seriously accepted as collateral. A device that can fail at any moment.

  • The "5-year" depreciation is a clever average. A chip can die in six months, or it can last 8 years. But if 10% of your fleet fails in the first year, the financial model starts to fall apart, and you've already taken out loans up to the hilt.

  • And only Uncle Huang can save you: buy back capacity at a non-market price, guarantee new loans, or simply lend money.

Huang wasn't just selling chips. He essentially became the central bank of the AI economy, through which trillions flowed. He elevated everything to the state level, where the state takes a share and can no longer avoid patching holes. Through index funds, pensions and union money are drawn into this structure; through data centers - the energy and water supply of entire states. In my assessment, this summer the budget deficit for one month equaled all revenues, and the government debt market was in turmoil as Huang spun up another cycle of his bubble.

And here's the main question. This entire scheme was held in the minds of Huang and a couple of dozen of his top managers. Trillion-dollar companies depended on his favor and his models. Now he's gone, and the team is likely facing a purge. The system he fine-tuned literally every week is now in limbo. And at any moment, huge chunks could start falling off: CoreWeave, Nebius by Arkady Volozh, Oracle, which has turned into a debt-ridden mutant for the sake of data centers (by the way, I really want to write a separate article about Oracle, as I believe that within a few years, due to the risks of bad loans being shifted to its shoulders, the company will face bankruptcy).

Further down the chain: construction and energy in the US, credit markets. And both top AI labs, which exist in their current scale largely because Huang wanted it that way.

Once again, the whole scheme - in simpler terms for the average person:

Nvidia became the world's most valuable company thanks to reports that showed strong demand for chips every quarter and the assumption that the company would earn more and more in the future.

This demand was sustained because big techs signed long-term contracts to purchase chips.

These big techs were financed by money from Nvidia itself. That is, effectively (yes, through intermediaries, although in some cases almost directly), Nvidia itself financed the purchase of its own chips.

This led to an increase in the company's stock market value.

Moreover, the narrative that if you're not in the AI race, your company simply won't survive in the post-AI era in a couple of years was aggressively pushed. Along with the crazy demand for chips, fueled by Huang's money, this forced all other market players to also buy chips and continue signing supply contracts.

A brilliant scheme.
