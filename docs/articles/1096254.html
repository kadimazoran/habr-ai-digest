For almost a year now, AI has been not an experiment for me, but a working tool: real projects, real deadlines, real responsibility for the result. And over this year, I've seen that people who "write code with AI" do it fundamentally differently. Some throw a prompt and take what comes out. Others design a system where AI does the work under their control.

Andrey Karpaty, who coined the term "vibe coding" in February 2025, meant something simple: to whip up a business card website or a utility for oneself, without reading the code, trusting the result. But the word stuck to everything: from crafts to production systems where an engineer is responsible for every line. The difference between these two ways of working is huge, and a name for the second approach seems to be missing.

On top of that, many truly abuse the technology and create quick, unverified solutions targeting responsible niches where they show their weakness, lack of refinement, and data security threats. Such solutions have added a negative connotation to the concept of "vibe coding," and real software engineers have inadvertently been lumped in with beginners and received the "shameful" label of vibe coder.

This situation seemed unfair to me, not because engineers might be offended, but because there's a substitution of concepts, and a misconception on the business side when evaluating the product being created. Businesses in production don't need MVPs and "one-evening" crafts; they need an architecturally strong and reliable product for which the developer is responsible and provides guarantees. Therefore, defining the concepts became a concrete task for me to form a domain and a common language for communication in the IT environment.

I studied how the industry itself began to name professional development with AI, and here's what I found and liked.

Agentic Engineering. Karpaty himself later went beyond his own term and proposed a new one: the engineer is an architect and supervisor. They don't write code by hand; they design a system in which an agent implements, tests, and debugs code under human control. The key here is that the human owns the architecture and correctness of the final product and, most importantly, as with manual coding, bears responsibility for the result. Thus, a vibe coder and an agentic engineer are like a car owner and a car mechanic. The former uses it without understanding how everything works inside, while the latter monitors the oil level, hears when the engine is sputtering, and can independently find and assess the cause of a breakdown and replace a spark plug if necessary.

I also came across a scale from Backslash Security, which presents three levels:

1. Full vibe: accepting the result without verification, the only feedback is the result and a repeated prompt.

2. Guided vibe: collaborative work, light editing, superficial immersion without deep expertise.

3. Structured vibe: the process is managed by a human. Design, review, and tests are mandatory. Vibe has found its niche: prototypes and idea validation. Engineering is at the top of this scale.

An interesting attempt to organize and assign roles based on the level of expertise immersion.

In the end, I decided for myself that "vibe coding" has become a narrower concept. And if you, as an experienced developer, create a product with AI, being immersed in the architecture, if you can explain how the product works internally, what problems exist, understand how to solve them, and at the same time know how to structure work with AI sequentially, for example, through Spec-driven Development and similar methods. Or at least plan the work more deeply than just describing the expected output, then AE (agentic engineering) better describes your work than "vibe coding," in my opinion.

As an engineer with 15 years of design and development experience myself, I am today creating my product SpecaFlow, which provides a convenient, understandable interface for agentic development. Starting from forming the idea, documentation, and ending with building the product in a sequential pipeline flow. Where each stage reports to the developer and helps make decisions and move forward. Developing my projects with full immersion into the depth.
