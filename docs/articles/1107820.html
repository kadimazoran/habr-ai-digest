Fixing bugs with AI is becoming an increasingly common practice in Linux kernel development, said project founder Linus Torvalds. He notes that the recently released version 7.4-rc2 contains more and more fixes prepared with the participation of AI.

Torvalds suggests that this sometimes even helps developers stay ahead of the stream of new bug reports.

He mentioned that a significant portion of recent kernel support work was done with the help of AI, adding that perhaps in the future there's no point in mentioning it separately in new version announcements.

"And yes, a lot of this has been made possible by various AI-assisted fixes. I think I'll stop repeating this – let's just assume it's the case," Torvalds wrote.

He also cited a very positive comment from Alexey Starovoitov, who is responsible for the BPF subsystem: according to him, the rate of bug fixes in BPF now exceeds the rate at which new bugs are reported.

If this trend continues, it could mean that developers have finally started reducing the accumulated list of known bugs, rather than just keeping up with newly discovered issues. However, Torvalds emphasized that it's too early to talk about such progress across the entire kernel.

"I think in most other areas we are still far from that, but this is at least a sign that the situation might be starting to change for the better," he noted.

These comments came in connection with the release of Linux 7.4-rc2. Torvalds described this release as quite substantial – following the first release candidate (rc1), which was the largest in the history of the Linux kernel. Such a significant volume is largely due to automated updates performed using the Coccinelle tool.

It is important to note that Torvalds did not specify which AI tools were used to prepare the fixes, nor did he indicate which specific patches were created with AI.

Earlier, Torvalds commented on AI at the Open Source Summit Europe conference. He noted that AI can be useful for finding and fixing bugs in the Linux kernel. However, the developer qualified that the tool becomes useful when used precisely as a tool.

In the summer, Torvalds wrote a mailing list post confirming the Linux kernel developers' position that they are not against AI, and responding to criticism from some maintainers who are against using LLMs in the project. He stated that the Linux kernel is not "anti-AI" and that AI/LLMs should be viewed solely as a tool to assist developers. Development does not mandate the use of such tools, but he himself is against those who criticize such solutions.
