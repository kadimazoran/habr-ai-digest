Hello, Habr!

On November 20-21, HSE University held a two-day in-person intensive in Moscow called 'Cybersecurity as Strategy.' InfoWatch Group President Natalya Kaspersky and Head of Consulting Ilya Bashkirov spoke at the event. The program was aimed at top managers responsible for information security and focused on building a cyber-resilient organization, covering risk assessment, budgeting, regulatory changes, and new threats.

Natalya Kaspersky presented on 'Modern Protection Technologies and Artificial Intelligence Security,' discussing the risks of generative AI, the 'black box' problem, shadow AI, and industry approaches to defending against new threats.
Natalya Kaspersky identified four categories of AI-related risks. Humanitarian – reduced cognitive skills in people. Second – social. Most often, this is a change in the labor market. Third – technological. Here, the 'black box' problem, unknown training data, leaks, AI backdoors, and the lack of proper auditing are noted.
Fourth – risks associated with autonomous AI agent systems.

The speed of AI threat development is such that neither humans nor AI protection tools can keep up. They are currently weak, fragmented, and quickly become obsolete. To make anything work, you need to combine several different tools – DLP, LLM Firewall, PAM, maybe SIEM, and it is strongly recommended to use isolated sandboxes. The gap is constantly widening, Natalya Kaspersky noted.


Natalya Kaspersky characterized generative AI as the most dangerous technology since nuclear weapons, noting that Russia significantly lags in this area.

Ilya @ilya_bashkirov Bashkirov conducted a separate specialized block of the intensive on 'Legal Aspects of Information Security Strategy and Methods of Managing the Responsibility of Organizations, Counterparties, Executives, and Officials,' consisting of three sections. The first part – 'Personal Data: Leaks and Other Risks' – examined the structure of legislative violation risks and the specifics of control and supervisory activities.

In the second section – 'Responsibility in Information Security' – Ilya provided a detailed analysis of judicial practice regarding the responsibility of managers and specialists in information security departments. The third section, 'Civil Law Issues,' was dedicated to methods of distributing responsibilities in case of cyber incidents in supply chains.

It is important to remember that law is not only a source of obligations. It is also a tool that can be used to protect the interests of an organization, Ilya Bashkirov emphasized.
