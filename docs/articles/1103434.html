If you run models like Flux or Qwen-Image on a home video card, you are almost certainly using quantized weights. And you have almost certainly encountered the advice: "don't use fancy samplers on a quantized model, they accumulate error." We decided to check if this is true, and got a result that turned out to be inconvenient for ourselves.

In short:

  • ComfyUI's actual INT8 kernels add noise that is almost invisible in the final image;

  • this noise is white, meaning it's uncorrelated between steps, and that's why high-order methods amplify it;

  • the choice of sampler and CFG has a much greater impact;

  • our own Flow-WLS, which we proposed in our previous work, lost to Euler with a small number of steps;

  • simple low-order methods (second order, and third order for distilled models) turned out to be the best, and we made them available in the ComfyUI dropdown list.

Where it all began

In our previous work (Flow-WLS, Zenodo), we proposed a multi-step sampler with regularization: it fits a polynomial to the velocity history using weighted least squares and integrates it. The idea was that such smoothing would suppress high-frequency noise caused by weight quantization. It sounded reasonable, and we expected it.

Before writing "suppresses," we recorded hypotheses in the README (initially six, then nine more before an additional series: H1-H15) and the conditions under which they would be considered confirmed. This is an important habit: it prevents fitting the conclusion to the result. Some hypotheses were not confirmed and remained in the text as they were.

What we measured

  • Models: Anima (bf16, plus simulation of FP8/INT8/INT4/NVFP4), Flux3-Klein INT8, Krea 2 INT8 (with ConvRot), Moss-Image INT8.

  • 4 prompts, 2-3 seeds, 8-24 steps, CFG 1-5.

  • Reference: the solution of the same model with a large number of steps.

  • For Flux3, we compared two modes of the same INT8 model: Q (actual INT8 kernels with activation quantization) and D (the same weights unpacked to bf16, without activation quantization). The difference between Q and D purely shows the kernel error.

Metrics: relative latent error (total, by geometry, and by details), SSIM, LPIPS, observed convergence order, error structure by steps, paired confidence intervals relative to Euler.

Result 1: quantization is harmless, and the noise is white

The error of actual INT8 kernels e = v_Q − v_D turned out to be white over time: the correlation between adjacent steps is -0.001, and the accumulated error matches the prediction for white noise. Magnitude: 1.3% of the velocity norm at CFG 1 and 5.7% at CFG 4. The 4.36x increase from CFG 1 to CFG 4 is understandable: the error is multiplied by the guidance, just like any difference in predictions.

This results in a final latent of 0.02 or less. In image space, the distance between the converged solutions of Q and D: LPIPS 0.014 at CFG 1 and 0.029 at CFG 4. They are indistinguishable to the eye.

Here's an honest disclaimer: we compared INT8 kernels with the same weights in bf16. We did not measure the error of the weight rounding itself relative to the unknown bf16 original.

AB-4 almost doubles the noise, AB-2 barely changes it. This matched our measurements: AB-4 was the most sensitive to INT8 in our runs. But its own excessive error (0.5-0.8) is tens of times larger than the effect of quantization (up to 0.02), so quantization is not the main factor here.

Result 3: the main factor depends on CFG

On Anima, we ran CFG 1, 3, and 5 at 8, 12, 16, and 24 steps, with nine methods. The result:

  • AB-2 is better than Euler in all 12 cells (by 0.03-0.12 in latent error, confidence intervals do not include zero).

  • DPM++ 2M outperforms Euler with strong CFG from 16-24 steps.

  • At CFG 1 (distilled Flux3-Klein and Krea 2 models), AB-3 and dynamic selection are best.

  • AB-4 and long windows of Flow-WLS at ≤12 steps were never the best choice.

That is, the statement "Euler is the best" from our first experiment was only true for the set of methods that did not include AB-2. We discovered this when we added it and corrected the conclusions in the README. It's useful to show such things, not hide them.

Why so

We hypothesized, and then measured: the network output is not a smooth function of the step, but a smooth trend plus noise uncorrelated between steps. Its relative magnitude is about 1% of the norm at CFG 1, about 4% at CFG 3, and 6-7% at CFG 5. The CFG term s·(v_c − v_u) is regenerated at each step (relative change between adjacent steps is 0.88-1.12), while the conditional part changes by 0.05-0.13.

We measured by the energy of differences: for white noise, the normalized energy ‖Δᵏv‖²/C(2k,k) is independent of the difference order k, while for a smooth field it drops rapidly. At CFG 3-5, the profile plateaus; at CFG 1, it does not.

Rules with large weights amplify such noise, and they only win while it's small. This is consistent with the CFG results.

We checked something else: perhaps the history filter just needs to be fitted to the data? Fitting a linear filter with constant weights (leave-one-out cross-validation) yielded a gain of no more than 3.4% over the best existing rule. This means a new sampler like Flow-WLS is not needed. A side result: the consistency metric at one step poorly predicts the final rating, while the accumulated trajectory predicts it well (Spearman 0.77 on average).

How to use

Place the folder in ComfyUI/custom_nodes/ and restart ComfyUI:

git clone https://github.com/YakovSava/flow-wls-research

In the regular sampler_name list (KSampler, KSamplerSelect), you will find flow_ab2, flow_ab3, flow_ab4. Additionally, there is a Flow Easy Sampler node with a choice of two options. What to choose:

  • regular model, CFG 3 and above: flow_ab2, at 16+ steps also dpmpp_2m;

  • distilled model, CFG 1: flow_ab3;

  • less than 12 steps: not flow_ab4 and not long Flow-WLS windows.

For those interested: AB-k is implemented as a recurrent expression on the sigma grid without additional model calls:

spec = adams_bashforth_spec(2)x = sample_history(model, x, sigmas, extra_args=extra_args, spec=spec)

Works with flow-prediction models (Anima, Flux3, Krea 2, Moss). Not tested on SDXL-like models.

What we haven't proven

  • LPIPS measures the distance to an image obtained over many steps, not its beauty. There is no blind test on humans.

  • 4 prompts and 4 models, one seed per image, the best was not selected.

  • For Moss-Image, there is no converged reference (the solution changes with the number of steps), so only large effects are reliable there. For Flux3 at CFG 4, the reference converges at 20-34%.

  • For Moss and Krea 2, only the Q mode was measured; the effect of kernels was measured on Flux3.

  • We added AB-2 and DPM++ 2M to some experiments after the initial results; this is noted in the README.

  • AB-2 is not a new method. The contribution of the work is in the measurements and in the negative result regarding our own idea.
