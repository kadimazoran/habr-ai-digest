Ilya Trikoz, co-founder of GPTunneL, vibe-coded a Discord analogue in 24 hours. He didn't write a single line of code himself. He just sent prompts to AI agents and went to sleep. Overnight, 12 agents built the server, infrastructure, and the application itself. By evening, chats, calls, video conferencing, screen sharing, and desktop and web versions of the application were already working. It cost about $12,000 (a million rubles) in AI tokens. GPTunneL estimates that building the product manually would have been 20-50 times more expensive.

If one person can build the Discord messenger in a day, then tomorrow ten people will build ten identical messengers. In a month, there will be a hundred.

AI won't kill anyone. It will simply generate a hellish horde of competitors for the vibe-coders themselves.

Previously, code was a stopper for beginners and an expensive pleasure for companies. You had to know how to code, find developers, spend money and time. Now, this hassle is going extinct. Why on earth should people use yours specifically?

And so it is.

The mere existence of your product, judging by how the AI is forging ahead like an icebreaker, is almost the same as having a website – well done, others have one too, so what.

Code is becoming cheap.

Therefore, everything that AI cannot reach with its tentacles and clone in an evening becomes more expensive: how to hit the right market, where to find a paying customer, how to set up support, how not to crash during scaling, how to manage as a manager, and other business perks.

So, the main struggle for guys who are now building their SaaS products is not at all about building them quickly or in large quantities (machine-gun logic, may it hit at least one), but about making people pay and not letting them go to the next wunderkind with Claude Code.
