The Essence of the Limitation

NVIDIA separates its product lines into gaming GeForce RTX and professional/data center cards (Tesla, A100, H100, etc.) not only by price but also by the throughput of tensor cores for FP16/BF16/TF32 with FP32 accumulation. The hardware is physically the same (or very similar), but on consumer cards, this computation is artificially limited – often by half of the stated peak speed of the tensor cores.

This doesn't matter for games, as this mode is rarely used. However, for local LLMs, fine-tuning, image, and video generation, this is precisely what computes most of the time during training and the prefill stage (processing the input prompt).

What gpu-unlock Does Technically

It's a Linux kernel module that:

  1. Integrates into the initramfs (mkinitcpio/initramfs-tools/dracut) and loads before the standard nvidia driver is attached to the system.

  2. Writes speed selector registers to the card: SS0 = 0x88888888 (full BF16 speed) and a 4-bit SS1 = 0x8 (IMLA4 mode).

  3. Performs a Function-Level Reset (FLR) of the card, then releases it – and the unmodified original NVIDIA driver attaches to the card as if nothing happened.

The approach combines techniques from the older cmpunlocker project (working with V67/PLM registers) with the idea of interception before the driver loads via UEFI. The NVIDIA driver is not patched or replaced; the driver archive is downloaded only to extract the firmware on which the unlock depends. According to the author, there is no dependency on a specific driver version.

Important: this is not a hack or cheating with calculation accuracy – the author claims that the calculation results are bitwise identical before and after, only the speed changes.

As you can see, Pascal (10-series, the GTX 1070 from my previous article) is not on the list at all – these cards do not have a separate, limited tensor-core BF16 mode that can be unlocked (Pascal simply lacks tensor cores in the required form). So, this project is not relevant for the 1070, but it's perfect for owners of the 20/30/40 series.

Real-world workloads (typical increases, based on community reports, not independent benchmarks):

  • LLM Prefill (FP16/BF16, AWQ/GPTQ): +25–55%, time to first token reduced by 25–30%

  • LoRA and full-finetuning (BF16): +25–50%

  • Image generation (FP16/BF16): +30–55%

  • Video generation (Wan 2.2, BF16): +15–20%

  • FlashAttention/SDPA: +50–60%

  • llama.cpp/Ollama with GGUF: 0–15%, more noticeable with long context

  • No change: LLM decode (limited by memory, not computation), INT8/FP8/INT4 models, games, FP32 CUDA

Note the last point – this is a key limitation for local LLMs. If you run models in INT8/INT4 quantization (as most local setups on home hardware do) and are interested in generation speed (decode), not just prefill – the boost might be zero. The gain is maximal with BF16/FP16 weights and specifically during prompt processing or training.
