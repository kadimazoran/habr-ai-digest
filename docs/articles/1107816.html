Mozilla is shutting down Solo, a service for creating and hosting websites based on large language models. All websites and data will be deleted on November 30.

The company launched Solo in December 2023 as part of the Mozilla Innovation project, and in 2024 introduced its first major release. The service was intended for individual entrepreneurs who needed their own website to promote their services. About 54,000 people used it.

Now, a message has appeared on the Soloist.ai homepage: "We have made the difficult decision to close the Solo project. We are grateful for your support in building and developing this product, as well as for all your feedback. Solo will cease operations completely on November 30, 2026."

Users have just over two weeks to find a new platform for their websites. At the same time, according to Mozilla, the first notification was sent out on October 1. According to the FAQ section, users can download a ZIP archive containing an HTML version of the site, contact form data, and subscriber lists, as well as a CSV file with links to images.

Images themselves need to be uploaded separately. Domains purchased through the Solo Domains service will also need to be transferred, in this case to the registrar Name.com. To do this, users need to create an account there and provide a special code, after which Mozilla will carry out the transfer procedure.

Additionally, users are offered alternative services for automated website creation.

Mozilla representatives noted: "Experiments are an important part of Mozilla's mission to improve the internet and give people more choice and control online. Sometimes this means making the decision to shut down a product. The Solo project will officially cease to exist on November 30, 2026. We notified users on October 1, providing a 60-day transition period, and are providing support for exporting site data and transferring domains registered through Solo Domains to other hosting providers."
