1. How Filtering Works

TSPU

"Technical Means for Countering Threats" (TSPU) is DPI (Deep Packet Inspection) equipment installed with telecom operators (Rostelecom, mobile operators, large providers) and managed centrally. The telecom operator does not control the rules itself; the policy is uniform across the country, but applied unevenly: the picture can differ by region, operator, and network type (mobile/wired).

2. Overview of Bypass Methods

2.1 Classic VPNs (OpenVPN, WireGuard, IPsec, L2TP, PPTP)

  • Protocols with recognizable signatures, recognized by DPI.

  • Status: widely blocked in the RF, especially to foreign addresses. Suitable only as transport within an already masked channel or for communication between trusted nodes within the country.

2.2 Shadowsocks

  • Encrypted stream without explicit headers. Older versions are recognized by statistics and active probes.

  • Status: modern variants (SS-2022) work in some places, but "random noise without TLS" is suspicious in itself. Inferior to protocols that masquerade as HTTPS.

2.3 Masquerading as HTTPS: Trojan, VLESS/VMess over TLS

  • Traffic looks like regular TLS on port 443. Requires your own domain with a valid certificate.

  • Without protection against active probing, the server can be "probed".

  • Status: works, but inferior to Reality.

2.4 VLESS + Reality (+ XTLS Vision) — the main working option

A protocol from the Xray project.

  • Reality: the server does not have its "own" fake certificate. For any client without a secret key, it transparently proxies the connection to a real website (dest). The TSPU probe receives a real website with a real certificate – it's impossible to distinguish the VPN from the website.

  • XTLS Vision (flow=xtls-rprx-vision) removes the characteristic "TLS inside TLS" pattern in packet sizes.

  • Fingerprint (fp=chrome) – the client's ClientHello is indistinguishable from the Chrome browser.

Important: choice of masquerading site (SNI/dest):

  • ❌ Large third-party domains (microsoft.com, google.com, cloudflare.com, etc.) – are cut off by the "SNI ↔ IP" check.

  • ⚠️ Unknown third-party sites – work, but depend on third-party infrastructure.

  • ✅ Best option – your own domain with a real website on the same server. The domain's DNS points to the same IP, the certificate is valid, and the real page is served during probing.

2.5 XHTTP, gRPC, WebSocket — transport over HTTP and CDN

  • Traffic goes as regular HTTP requests, can be routed through a CDN (e.g., Cloudflare): TSPU sees a connection to the CDN, not your server.

  • Pros: server IP is hidden, can survive IP blocking.

  • Cons: lower speed, CDNs themselves are subject to throttling and partial blocking in RF; dependence on CDN policy.

2.6 QUIC/UDP Protocols: Hysteria2, TUIC

  • High speed on poor connections.

  • Status in RF: UDP and QUIC are restricted – unstable, not recommended for the main connection.
