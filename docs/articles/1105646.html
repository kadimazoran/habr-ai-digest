JetBrains has updated its IDE splash screens, and a holy war has already begun around them. Tickets are asking to remove the AI images and bring back the old design. Since it's time to choose a side, I'm currently with those who are delighted.

In PyCharm 2026.3, a man in protective goggles is processing an emerald with a laser. Next to him are a laptop, a robotic arm, and a control panel. At the bottom is the inscription powered with air.

In version 2026.3, Air comes bundled with the IDE: you connect agents, assign tasks, monitor sessions, and check changes. I've already tried Air, and I like the logic itself: an agent performs a task, the IDE helps monitor its work and verify the result. And thanks to ACP for the ability to choose an agent separately from the editor. The protocol is legendary.

The Agent Client Protocol was introduced by the Zed team on August 27, 2025, with Gemini CLI as the first reference integration. Via ACP, an agent connects with its harness: the logic for model calls and action execution remains with the agent tool. The IDE sends tasks, shows progress and changes, and allows responses to permission requests. It can also provide files, a terminal, and additional tools; the set of capabilities depends on the integration.

So, you keep your usual agent, and the editor becomes an interface to its work. You choose the entire tool with its working logic, not just a model from a dropdown list.

JetBrains explains the transition to Air by stating that parallel work with agents requires a different interface. You delegate tasks to them, and then you analyze the changes and are responsible for the result. The company is restructuring its IDEs for this type of work. The man at the control panel well illustrates this transition: the robot works, the human manages the process. This is roughly what we've arrived at with agents, only instead of a laser, we have a terminal.

To examine the splash screen without rushing, I extracted the original from the distribution. I also grabbed the images from other IDEs and compared them.

Neural Networks Appeared Here Back in 2020

JetBrains support writes that designers use ML models and refine the images manually. They experiment in EAP, and for releases, they use more hybrid methods. JetBrains shared its early generators long before this release.

In 2020, JetBrains already showcased a neural network tool for abstract images and animations. In it, you could select preferred options, mix parameters, and get new images. A separate model was trained to predict which parameter combinations people would like.

In a review from 2023, JetBrains explained how they used CPPN along with Stable Diffusion. CPPN can be thought of as a function: you input pixel coordinates and a set of parameters, and you get a color. Change the parameters, and the pattern changes; smoothly change them over time, and you get animation. For Stable Diffusion, styles were prepared based on designer references. The image from CPPN was used as a base to maintain the desired palette and composition. This way, they achieved variability while preserving recognizable product colors.

Another user writes that such graphics cheapen the impression of professional software. Yet another asks to bring back the old splash screen or provide an option to choose another. They want to customize the appearance of the tool they use every day.

In threads started back in 2025, the debate was already ongoing. Some criticized AI graphics, while others replied that they barely notice the startup screen and evaluate the IDE based on its code handling.

I'm all for products trying to change their design, even if it causes controversy. Here, it turned out fresh, unusual, and meaningful: the laboratory with a machine at work coincided with the IDE's transition to agents.

But the splash screen greets the user with every IDE launch, and it's they who have to look at it. So, an option to choose the design would be useful here.

However, choosing is currently difficult. On November 6, support clarified that a switch to a simplified splash screen is currently only available in IntelliJ IDEA. For PyCharm and CLion, they suggest hiding the startup screen via Help | Edit Custom VM Options:

-Dnosplash=true

This disables the splash screen entirely. You can't set a different image this way.

While the Robot Works

Over several years, we've gone from a chatbot that answers code questions to agents that can be given entire tasks. The agent reads the project, makes changes, and runs checks on its own. While one is working, you can analyze the results of another. Along with this, what we do during the workday also changes.

You need to formulate a task so that the agent solves the intended problem, understand its changes, and verify that they haven't broken the rest of the project. Several parallel sessions add questions: who changed what, which results can be merged, where intervention is needed. Therefore, IDEs need tools to handle all this, and Air is precisely trying to assemble them.

The image itself is not the main thing here. You can love it, hate it, or disable it. Much more significant is the fact that the familiar work tool is being restructured for development with agents. The question now is how we will assign tasks, review each other's code, and assemble a working product from it. This transition is what makes the man at the control panel relevant.
