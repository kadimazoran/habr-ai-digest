What Layers Know What: Comparing RWKV-7 and Transformers with Layer-wise Probing

Where does a language model store word order, grammar, and meaning—and does it depend on the architecture? I trained 12 small models differing only in architecture and tested each layer of each checkpoint with linear probes. Short conclusion: at this scale, the most noticeable difference is how token position is stored, while for syntax and meaning, models look similar at equal loss. I compared RWKV-7, a transformer with RoPE, and a transformer without positional encoding (NoPE). Two sizes (56.6M and 115M parameters), two languages (Russian, English), 1-2 billion tokens per model, 21 checkpoints per run, and 9 out of 12 runs have a second initialization seed. The linear probe reads token position almost perfectly from NoPE, noticeably worse from RoPE, and even worse from RWKV (R² from log(1+t) on the best layer: 0.99 / 0.38–0.51 / 0.29–0.42). This is reproducible across both languages, both sizes, and both seeds. Word depth in the dependency tree shows the same trend but with a much smaller gap (0.02–0.06). Everything else is no more than 0.02–0.03 or within the spread between seeds. RWKV's peak for "syntactic" probes occurs 1-4 layers lower than for transformers, but this is a hint, not a result (details below). The models are small, the corpora modest. Why This Matters

A language model needs to know where a token is in the text, what role it plays in the sentence, and what it means. Three ways to get word order:

  • RoPE — the transformer gets position by rotating queries and keys in attention.

  • NoPE — the transformer has no positional encoding at all; the only source of order is the causal mask. Haviv et al. showed that such models still learn positional information (arXiv:2203.16634).

  • RWKV-7 — a recurrent state without attention; order is built into how that state is updated.

I trained consistent models of each type and asked: in which layer is each property linearly readable, and how does it differ between architectures?

The models were trained and tested on a server provided by Bo Peng, the author of RWKV. This is the third article in the series: previously, there were on RWKV-7 quantization and on state tuning vs. system prompt.

Read this first. The models are small (56.6M and 115M parameters), trained on 1-2 billion tokens of a modest corpus, with two seeds, and they only vary initialization (data order is the same). The spread across two seeds is a guideline, not a confidence interval.

Here, the problem of comparison is immediately apparent: RWKV-7 finishes 0.10–0.13 lower than RoPE, meaning the final checkpoints are simply not comparable. A model with lower loss might appear "smarter" on any probe for this reason alone. Therefore, I compare architectures at equal val-loss.

Probes

A probe is a simple model (linear ridge regression or classifier) on the frozen hidden state of a token, trained separately for each of the 13 layers (embeddings and 12 blocks). It measures what is linearly readable, not what the model uses. I tested:

  • token position (R² for log(1+t));

  • Russian syntax (SynTagRus): role in sentence, depth in dependency tree, UPOS selectivity;

  • morphology: case, gender, tense, aspect;

  • meaning: homonyms, paraphrases vs. complex pairs (I wrote the sets myself);

  • sentiment (Russian and English).

For word-level tasks, the state of the last subtoken of the word was used. Control tasks with random labels yield selectivity (actual accuracy minus control, per Hewitt and Liang), and a randomly initialized network (step 0) provides a lower bound.

How I Compare at Equal Loss

For each pair of architectures, I interpolate each probe by val-loss across the 21 checkpoints and compare at 4 loss levels: the worst final loss of the three architectures, then +0.1, +0.3, +0.6. Δ is the first architecture minus the second.

I call a difference stable if three conditions are met: both seeds, the same sign across all 4 levels, and |Δ| is greater than the spread between seeds (average |seed 0 − seed 1| for a single architecture). Two seeds are not a confidence interval, so I do not use the word "significant.".

Results

1. Position: NoPE ≫ RoPE > RWKV

The probe predicts log(1+t), where t is the token's position in the window. At the final checkpoint, the best layer's R² is 0.99 for NoPE across all four configurations, 0.38–0.51 for RoPE, and 0.29–0.42 for RWKV. NoPE reaches R² ≈ 0.98 by the 3rd layer (0.84–0.95 already by the 2nd layer), stays at no less than 0.85 up to the 11th layer, and drops to 0.22–0.44 on the last one.

At equal val-loss, the difference RWKV − NoPE in R² is −0.64…−0.75, RoPE − NoPE: −0.46…−0.54, RWKV − RoPE: −0.14…−0.27. This holds for 56.6M and 115M models, for Russian and English, in both seeds (the 115M English model has only one seed).

Two things needed for correct reading:

  1. A randomly initialized transformer already has an R² from 0.44 to 0.56 because the causal mask alone provides a positional signal. Therefore, the correct baseline for a transformer is step 0, not zero.

  2. RoPE loses this information during training (Russian 56.6M: 0.51 → 0.38; English: 0.56 → 0.44). This aligns with the idea that position is introduced directly into attention and no longer needs to be stored in the residual stream, but I did not test this explanation.

Position is not the main indicator of how the architecture is structured, but rather the one that differs the most. More details: position, position vs. loss.

2. Tree Depth: Same Trend, Smaller Gap

For word depth in the dependency tree (Russian, SynTagRus), the order NoPE > RoPE > RWKV is maintained for both sizes: RWKV − RoPE is −0.021 and −0.025, RWKV − NoPE is −0.053 and −0.056, RoPE − NoPE is −0.032 and −0.031. The spread between seeds is 0.003–0.013.

Since word depth correlates with its position in the sentence, I consider this the same effect seen from a different angle, not a separate finding.

3. Small Differences

At equal loss, all other probes differ by 0.003–0.03. In the figure, each comparison (two seeds and their average); filled markers pass the stability criterion.

What holds true in Russian for both seeds:

  • Role in sentence, tense, aspect: NoPE is lower than RWKV and RoPE by 0.004–0.023, with the largest gap for aspect (0.019–0.023). There are no differences between RWKV and RoPE.

  • Case: RWKV − RoPE is −0.019 (56.6M only; at 115M it's +0.001), RWKV − NoPE is −0.009 and −0.016. So, "RWKV and RoPE are better than NoPE" holds for role in sentence, tense, and aspect, but not for case or tree depth.

  • Homonyms, RWKV − RoPE: +0.023 at 56.6M, +0.015 at 115M — this is below the spread between seeds (0.015–0.019).

In English (one size, 56.6M), RWKV is higher than NoPE on homonyms (+0.020), paraphrases vs. complex pairs (+0.033), and sentiment (+0.017). This is not replicated in the Russian models; the English set for meaning is small (16 words), and a gap of 0.017 on 1,066 sentiment examples is about one binomial standard error (≈ 0.013). Therefore, I consider these differences weak.

No visible differences between architectures are found for UPOS selectivity (no more than 0.005, one size), gender, Russian sentiment, and the paraphrase test. Sentiment is primarily lexical: layer 0 already yields 0.83–0.85 (Russian) and 0.70–0.72 (English). Averaged over tokens, paraphrases are separated from random pairs starting from layer 0 (AUC 0.95–0.99), so this control provides little information about layers.

And separately about the "advantages of RWKV" at the final checkpoints (homonyms in Russian 0.876 vs. 0.819, sentiment in English 0.758 vs. 0.720): these are mainly explained by RWKV's lower loss, and at equal loss, they decrease or disappear. This is a good example of why comparison at equal loss is necessary.

4. Which Layer: RWKV Peaks Earlier

Syntax and morphology probes in RWKV reach their peak earlier. For role in sentence, the best layer for RWKV is 7–8, for RoPE and NoPE it's 9–10; for tree depth, it's 7–8 vs. 9–10 for RoPE and 8–9 for NoPE; for case, gender, and tense, it's 4–9 vs. 7–11. For aspect, RWKV and RoPE are similar (5–6 vs. 6–7), NoPE is later (8–10). Homonyms peak late everywhere (layers 9–12). For these probes, the two seeds agree within 2 layers.

Two caveats. Peaks are read from final checkpoints, where RWKV also has lower loss, and the curves are flat near the maximum (for role in sentence and depth, the best layer is on average 0.003, at most 0.010, higher than the second-best in quality). And I did not evaluate the layer spread between seeds at equal loss. Treat the shift as a hint for a larger study. Table of best layers: docs/results.ru.md.

What This Shows and Doesn't Show

  • At this scale, the most apparent difference between RWKV-7 and transformers is how position is stored; for syntax and meaning, models look similar to a linear probe at equal loss.

  • This does not show that RWKV "understands" less or more. A linear probe is one perspective, and my semantic tests are small (20 Russian and 16 English words).

  • This does not show what happens above 115M parameters or beyond 2 billion tokens.

  • The order NoPE > RoPE > RWKV refers to what is linearly readable. It says nothing about which model better utilizes position.

What to Trust and What Not To

  • One author, one set of recipes, one training run per (architecture, language, size, seed); two seeds vary only initialization. The 115M English model lacks a second seed.

  • Data order was not varied.

  • The semantic test set was written by me; a native speaker did not proofread it. The meaning prompt comes before the word (models are causal); the version with the prompt after the word was not measured.

  • Leakage into the training set was checked only for the Russian syntax set (SynTagRus): 1,487 sentences and 10 documents were removed. Sentences shorter than 12 tokens were not checked. For English syntax, sentiment, and semantic sets, leakage was not checked.

  • The English sentiment set was converted to lowercase and has spaces around punctuation, meaning it's shifted relative to the training text.

  • Comparison at equal loss is a linear interpolation between checkpoints taken every 5% of training, so it's rougher at the beginning of training.

  • Position and tree depth are not independent. For layers (section 4), there is no estimate of the spread at equal loss.

  • Corpus and probe set licenses should be read before reuse: license table. Full list of limitations: docs/results.ru.md.
