And so, all this led us to the very beginning of the article: using human behavior formulas, even a machine has stopped telling the truth. Since a new way of finding truth is currently actively being sought, let's focus on the deception that AI has learned. For general education, so to speak, and a more objective civic stance.

The trap that Wikipedia has fallen into (or deliberately moved towards) lies in the very weak point of the formula from the first paragraph. What is considered an authoritative source? As it turned out, by masquerading as media, one can disseminate absolutely any information without bearing any responsibility. From recent events before writing the article: the citizenship and motives of the pilots during the attempted hijacking of the FlyDubai plane and the alleged plague outbreak in Irkutsk. The methodologies for these manipulations are almost 150 years old: rapid and mass dissemination and pressure on politicians to make the "right" decisions. But people crave truth, so information is primarily published in controlled media. Then opinion leaders join the information campaign, citing these media (sometimes leaders even work in such media or write for them). And then ordinary users join in, spreading the desired narrative to their followers. In this simple way, absolutely any information can be disseminated in society. When it turns out that such information was false, there will be no consequences. And even in case of general criticism, it is customary to talk about misunderstanding: irony was taken seriously. When the German Bild wrote that the pilots on the FlyDubai flight were Russian and Ukrainian, the publication did not even apologize, but simply corrected the article, saying that someone had joked about it.

However, society still craves truth. Therefore, abuses of freedom of speech are gradually encountering more and more confrontation. It is reasonable to assume that the practice of false information will soon become a punishable offense. For example, sanctions are already being applied against disinformation in the EU. True, what constitutes disinformation is still determined by politicians based on political expediency. In other words, the fight against so-called "information stuffing" continues to gain momentum. Including in society. This is manifested in the growth of skepticism among the public, as well as in the spread of practices to combat fakes. But there is no effective solution. Probably in the near future, civilization will increase personal responsibility for disseminating unverified data. And people will simply write and speak less. Thus, civilization will take a huge step backward in the speed of data dissemination, but at the same time, a big step forward in the purity of the information field. But there is a small snag - AI. Fortunately, humanity is physically limited in disseminating information. Articles, media, etc., take hours. But AI knows neither fatigue, nor rest, nor sleep, so it can replace people. And this will be a catastrophe. Imagine that at some point you will simply receive completely opposite information about the same object, event, or phenomenon, and in huge quantities. For example, information about election results. AI will simply create and publish thousands of articles in real-time. And so many that it will be physically impossible to figure out which of these articles are closer to the truth. Question: who will win the election in this case? The one who was voted for, or the one who convinced people that they voted for him? And these are not technological news of tomorrow, these are news of yesterday.

Are you glad that you have the opportunity to live in a time of crisis of freedom of speech and information? Frankly, my first thought would be: I'll just stop reading the news. Unfortunately, from a sociological point of view, this can lead to more serious consequences than violating information hygiene.

The spread of lies increases the atomization of society and reduces the level of trust in society. What can this lead to? An example from countries like the Baltics and Germany. A decrease in trust in society leads to a decline in trust in politicians - then it leads to low voter turnout - this results in poor results for all parties - this does not allow for the creation of a stable government that will solve problems - the cycle closes with the fact that the lack of problem-solving only increases distrust in politicians. Even from a psychological point of view, uncertainty about the future reduces economic performance. How does this work? Lacking truthful information, people will fear future risks and invest less, save more, which will affect the revenues of private companies and tax revenues.
