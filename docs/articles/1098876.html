The more, the more AI content on the internet, and this trend is not going anywhere. I'm too lazy to pull up statistics, but I vaguely remember that the amount of neural text exceeded the amount of human text a year ago. We are still quite good at recognizing where AI text is and where it is human, but the emotional reaction to neural creativity differs from area to area.

Right away, so as not to keep you in suspense, I'll state the main principle: you cannot write with a neural network what is EXPECTED to be human, flesh-and-blood authorship.

Attention! Achtung! Next will be a list, because lists are convenient and clear, and not because I made this article with a chatbot.

Reference materials

Application function documentation, some lists and reviews, reports. Trust in the information will only increase if you add a note "generated by a neural network based on code".

Encyclopedic content

If there is a link to a source for every phrase, it becomes not so important to us whether a human or a neural network wrote the text. It's simple here.

Product cards

I absolutely don't care who wrote the description of the vacuum cleaner in the online store. If a neural network can clearly list the characteristics, explain the differences between models, and not lie about the suction power, I'm all for it. But as soon as the phrase "after three months of use, I can confidently say" appears in the description, the situation changes. Now I'm not being sold information, but a fabricated user experience, and that's sad and disgusting.

Branding, selling content

As far as I can tell, on landing pages and in sales texts, neural text is haram, because the generated text looks frankly cheap. I wouldn't even use neural images, but let the marketing specialists correct me.

Soulful content

Suppose you are writing a blog where you review movies. As soon as your personality is reflected in such a blog, you can forget about neural text. Once you say "I like how Tom Cruise plays in this role" and that's it: now this text is perceived as the opinion of a living person. And therefore, the entire blog is perceived as written by a specific living person. The reader comes for emotions and live content, and as soon as they see even the slightest hint that some text was written by an AI, a wave of disgust and disappointment will wash over them.

The only thing worse than this is using AI in personal correspondence. I suggest everyone imagine receiving a message from a girl describing the depth of her feelings and suddenly stumbling upon neural markers.

Personal experience

When I see an article describing an interesting case in production, I will read it to the end, assuming that the case actually took place, and that this particular author drew some conclusions from it. As soon as you catch the slightest whiff of neural odor, the subject of the article itself can be called into question. A neural network is an ideal sophist; it can convincingly prove any contradictory facts and invent stories demonstrating any point of view. That's why we are disgusted to read neural texts; we instinctively feel that we are being lied to, and truth is intrinsically valuable. Questions arise:

  • Author, do you even exist?

  • And is the case you described real, or did the neural network hallucinate it for you?

And no one cares that you wrote the article yourself, with these working hands, for a month, and just asked the neural network to "correct the style and typos".

The detection of a neural marker spoils the entire article, and a spoiled article casts a shadow on the entire resource. We cannot distinguish text written from scratch by an AI from text that an AI has edited. It's better to write clumsily, but so that it's clearly felt that it's YOU.

Comparison of A and B

This follows from the previous points. A person compares rubber and braided hoses for diving and somehow discusses the pros and cons? We assume that he is a seasoned diver and has eaten a lot of dogs on these hoses, we read every word and drink from the source of wisdom. If we come across a typical neural network phrase in the middle of the article like "this is not excessive caution, this is a reasonable distribution of risks" - the entire article is instantly multiplied by zero. The text loses its value, because now we don't know if the author had the described experience, or if similar stories can be obtained by entering a query into Claude.

Comparing frontend frameworks and describing personal impressions? The same story, such a text must be written by hand, because if it is written by a neural network, no one will believe that it was not generated by a simple query.

Fiction

Strangely enough, it's much more complicated here, and probably depends on the reader. It would seem that if the story is engaging, then it shouldn't matter how and by whom it was written. I once even wrote a plugin that called ChatGPT to create a story with words to be studied in a foreign language. I used it for a couple of days and stopped, it just became uninteresting for some reason.

In short

The more the value of the text depends on the reality of someone else's experience, the more it is tied to emotions, the more destructive the suspicion of using AI will be.

If you want to sneak in a couple of neural paragraphs, warn the reader about it. Write something like "I discussed this with Gemini and got the following answer:" - and then carefully quote it. Or write at the very beginning something like "there is no time for a full article now, but I wanted to share my thoughts after the interview - here is a summary from AI." It is naive to be cunning, replacing long dashes with hyphens, "Christmas tree" quotes with inch marks, and deliberately inserting typos is not recommended - the lie will still come out, and there will be even more negativity.

Do not disappoint your audience and do not be lazy.
