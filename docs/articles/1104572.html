To clarify a thought that is simple and clear to many here on Habr, I will give an equally simple and clear explanatory example, and you will understand everything yourself.

Imagine you come to an expensive, well-known restaurant and ask the AI waiter – what wine would go well with the veal kidneys from your menu, Pommard or Nuits-Saint-Georges? And the AI waiter replies - of course, Pinot Noir Nuits-Saint-Georges, vintage 2017, and you happily take a bottle. So here's the question for you - can you distinguish Pinot Noir Nuits-Saint-Georges from Pommard?

And if the waiter says that today Abrau Durso of the Gejukh terroir is best suited for these kidneys - will you take a bottle?

Here they bring you a no-name wine and say it's OH, AH! And what will you understand from the answer if you don't know the answers beforehand and can't distinguish the terroir?

Or you come to buy a Porsche, you are probably a senior with six zeros, after all. And they give you a Niva with a "PORSCHE" badge, what do you do? The same four wheels, engine, steering wheel, etc. And if you don't know what a Porsche is beforehand, then any answer might be acceptable.

You think this is a joke, everything is made up and far-fetched.

But look at your computer and the processor frequency and nanometers of technology - it's over 4 gigahertz and something around 2 nanometers. And you believe sacredly that it is so. Only any competent, competent electronics engineer will say - it's CMOS TTL and there can be no gigahertz, no way. And the processor clock speed is 133 or 166 MHz (Megahertz!) and at 2 nanometers CMOS does not exist, there is already different physics.

Therefore, when asking AI a question, you need to be prepared to understand the answer, and since AI is just an approximation, including terrible, meaningless, and ruthless marketing, then everything will be mixed up in the answer.
