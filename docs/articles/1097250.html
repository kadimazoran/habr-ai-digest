A breakdown of the implementation of corporate Claude in the company "Pro-Leader" over five weeks – from mid-September to October 21, 2026. With figures, with two erroneous conclusions that had to be reversed, and with two incidents in live systems.

Company

Full-cycle production and installation of fences and gates: own workshop – cutting, welding, painting; own installation teams, automation, pile foundations. About 30 people, 7 teams, turnover of about 160 million ₽ per year, about 4,200 leads, season from April to December. Channels: Avito, Yandex Direct, 2GIS, website calculator, messengers. Three managers and a head of sales. The owner works from Kazakhstan.

Starting Point (Point A)

Data was scattered across five disconnected systems: amoCRM, Avito, 1C for two sole proprietorships, a Google Sheet for installations, and the website. Answering a simple question required manual table merging.

No one saw the real profit: more than half of the expense invoices in 1C were left as drafts, and the expenses of the two sole proprietorships didn't reconcile. Website prices had accumulated historically – it was unknown which fence brought profit and which was sold at a loss. No one listened to calls. The owner himself downloaded deferred leads, wrote to clients, and set tasks for follow-up. The goal of "15 teams and a site in St. Petersburg" existed without calculating what it would entail.

And one more thing, which became clear during the very first session: the owner did not have the keys to the infrastructure. The website and domain were managed by a third-party contractor, there was no corporate email – work processes were handled via personal Gmail. A year earlier, a manager left with access, and passwords had to be changed manually.

First Week: Framework, Not a Chatbot

The first three sessions were spent not on AI, but on the foundation. This is an important part: without it, everything else is technically impossible.

Google Workspace was deployed on a corporate domain, with the owner's account as the super administrator. Corporate email was migrated from an external service to Workspace without changing addresses. SSO login via SAML was configured and tested. Claude Team was subscribed. The work environment was cleaned up and unified to a single browser. The Claude browser agent, GitHub, and Google Drive were connected. A project was created with sections "Strategy," "Security," "Legal," "Operations."

The essence of this stage in one phrase: access is granted and revoked with a single button in the admin console, and the data remains with the company, not with the person who left.

It was then discovered that there was no ready-made connector for amoCRM for this task, so one was written: a custom integration with a long-term token, a repository, a VPS for $6 per month, Docker, and SSL on a company subdomain. The same applied to Avito and 1C. Claude works directly with the data, without exports.

Second Week: A Figure That Turned Out to Be Incorrect

On September 22, the first financial assessment based on incomplete data showed a loss of about 1.5 million ₽. The owner saw that expenses were outpacing income, and the feeling of money was created by advance payments for work not yet completed.

This figure was not considered a result. Over 40 exports from 1C and amoCRM over the year were retrieved: profit and loss reports, invoices, cash flow, accounts of both sole proprietorships, piece-rate orders, salaries, and sales funnel. Four independent revenue sources were reconciled – sales, reports, receipts, and purchases – and they matched.

The real picture: 13.7 million ₽ remained with the company from January to September, about 15.5 million over 12 months, after the owner's salary.

The gap between the first estimate and the verified figure is the main practical takeaway from the entire implementation. AI on dirty data provides a convincing but incorrect answer. First, you check where the data is stored, and only then do you trust it.

Security was built-in from day one. Any change in CRM, on Avito, or in prices is shown in a preview and applied after owner confirmation. Claude can only write to clients for deals with a specific tag, there are quiet hours, a limit on messages without a response, and a ban on duplicates. Client phone numbers are hidden in the output, and all actions are logged. Before price changes, a full copy of the calculator settings is saved – over 1,100 fields, rollback in minutes.

Simultaneously, errors in the CRM itself that distorted reporting were corrected. Calls and correspondence were stored with contacts, not deals – which is why the first report showed "99% of leads without a response." The reason for rejection was read from an empty field. Service funnels were included in the metrics. Server upgrades reduced the transcription of one call from 139 to 25–80 seconds.

Prices: 636 Variants and a Tenfold Margin

Since October 7, the website calculator has been calculating prices based on cost. All 636 fence and gate variants are non-negative after all expenses, including the owner's salary. The average price increase for the client is about 2.4%; medium-length fences became slightly cheaper.

How this was achieved. First, insurance: on September 28, a full copy of the prices for five calculators was made – automatic gates, 3D panels, chain-link, picket, corrugated metal, over 1,100 fields, of which 879 are for corrugated metal. Then, an audit: the calculators were analyzed and cross-referenced with deal outcomes in CRM. Then, a cost model: purchases, team rates, delivery, measurement, manager commission, taxes for two sole proprietorships, rent, winter labor costs, and advertising at a rate of 9,652 ₽ per deal were consolidated. The key indicator is the margin per team-day, with a floor of not less than 20,000 ₽. Only after this were changes made in the admin panel, verified with live calculations on the website. Rates and installation minimums were changed; material prices were not touched.

Daily Manager Review

Every night, all calls of the day are transcribed, and chats are analyzed. By 9:00 AM, the head of sales receives a separate document in CRM for each manager: performance indicators, what was done well, what needs improvement, a breakdown of each call and chat, and a ready-made message for the client to schedule a measurement. From October 9 to 19, this was done manually, then automated on a schedule.

Recurring errors that are now visible daily: the client asks "when will you come?" and the manager doesn't offer two specific dates; a discount is offered before the client objects to the price; the price "from 30,000 ₽" in the ad creates an expectation that is shattered in the first call; promises contradict the contract.

Because of the latter, the company's terms and conditions have been compiled into a single document for the first time: measurement based on mileage, warranties, payment by client type, installation queue, cancellations, and returns. Now, both the manager and AI tell the client the same thing.

Estimates deserve special mention. Claude compared a real estimate from CRM for 176,300 ₽ with the calculator, broke down the 6,300 ₽ difference by reasons, and found an undercharge of 1,000 ₽: the installation of 100×100 posts was calculated at the price of 80×80. A note for the manager appeared in the deal before the measurement. It was also discovered that managers sometimes edit PDF estimates manually, and the sum in CRM differs from the one sent to the client.

AI Manager on the Night Shift

Since October 20, the night shift on Avito chats from 9 PM to 9 AM is covered by an AI manager: it consults new clients, calculates prices using the calculator, schedules measurements for the next day at the earliest, and sends a report in the morning. On the very first night, it responded to a client, continuing the manager's conversation without price discrepancies, and correctly remained silent in chats where a client's response was awaited. This is an AI manager working under the supervision of the manager, and the company does not hide this in public communication.

How it was built. First, training on the best manager: 35 calls and correspondence of the department were analyzed. Strong techniques were adopted as is – the order of questions, the "turnkey" price upfront, a clear explanation of payment. Errors that led to lost clients were corrected: the conversation always ends with a measurement date or a callback, specific days are immediately offered for "can you come tomorrow?", and the client learns in advance what is not covered by the warranty.

Then, a test: a dialogue on Avito, where the head of sales played the role of the client. Calculation of gates, three recalculations based on requests – 144,080, 137,180, 135,680 ₽, a cheaper option without automation for 107,180 ₽, handling the objection "competitors are cheaper," a breakdown of costs by item, and an offer on company letterhead.

Then, owner's corrections at each step: first, two to three clarifying questions, then the price; automatic gate frames default to 60×40; scripts for "expensive"; no false promises like "I'll deliver in 15 minutes."

In parallel, Claude, on behalf of the owner, handles correspondence for stalled deals in messengers: maintains the agreed-upon lowest price, compares the value of configurations instead of new discounts, offers specific measurement dates, and sends no more than one reminder every few days. Each message is added as a note to the deal. Out of three deals as of October 21, one was lost – the client had already installed gates from a competitor at a lower price; negotiations are ongoing for the other two.

A prototype voice bot for missed and invalid calls is ready.

Operations, Finance, Strategy

Material procurement. Previously, the order for materials from suppliers was compiled manually based on deals. Now, Claude takes installations for a specific date from the schedule, cross-references the work scope with the deals, and generates a ready-made message for the supplier, marking unclear points – color, thickness, quantity – separately. Company rules have been internalized: 60×60 posts for 3D panels are made in their own workshop and are not included in the order.

Finances. A complete expense report for the two sole proprietorships has been created – 1,954 payments, 22 categories. The cost of each type of fence and gate has been calculated by team-days. A passport for the 1C database has been compiled. The next steps are an audit of counterparties and balances, followed by a profit and loss statement, cash flow statement, and monthly balances.

Strategy 2027. First, the starting point (Point A) with figures confirmed by the owner, then the goal. The result is 10 teams by June 2027, with four priorities, roles, and a 90-day plan. The goal of "15 teams" has been postponed to 2028: the tax structure, long-haul van, and measurement capacity limit growth to approximately 10 teams.

What We Learned

Facts first, then solutions. Every figure was cross-checked from multiple sources and confirmed by the owner. This eliminated three false conclusions: that the bottleneck was welding, that winter was unprofitable, and that the practice of sending estimates immediately needed to be changed – it turned out to be a conscious competitive advantage.

The owner makes corrections – AI recalculates. The most valuable corrections came from someone who knows the production process: calculating orders by team-days, actual meter rates per day in summer and autumn, and purchasing rules. Each correction changed the conclusion, and the calculation was redone.

CRM figures are verified before conclusions are drawn, not after. "99% of leads without a response" turned out to be a consequence of calls being linked to contacts, not deals.

Errors are caught by comparing with the live system. Three times the calculation differed from reality: markups in 1C were distorted by the gate assembly, the model used a target of 5% instead of 2%, and in fences with automatic gates, the calculator used a reduced minimum. All three were found by checking against the website before making a decision.

Changes in live systems – only with a copy and verification. There were two incidents in the website admin panel: 3D panel prices were reset, and the list of thicknesses collapsed. Both were quickly fixed, after which rules were introduced: a master copy before changes, test edits only with consent, and after each save, a reload and verification.

Honesty with the client is a boundary that is not crossed. Claude refused to create misleading materials for clients and offered honest arguments: comparison of configurations, warranties, service. Prices, deadlines, and warranties in correspondence are checked against the company's terms and conditions.

What's Next

Automation of initial contact: response time, tasks for the next contact, follow-up without the owner. Goal: increase conversion to measurement from 16% to 22%. Night bot in messengers for deals with a specific tag. Voice bot for missed calls. Recalculation of fences with automatic gates. Early booking for the 2027 season from the list of rejected leads and past clients. Management reports from 1C. Client bot based on the knowledge base.

To close the case with facts, in three to four weeks, we will analyze the September database: conversion rate "Avito chat → measurement" (base 8%), rejection rate "will contact themselves" (base 28 out of 35), nighttime leads processed by the bot, cost per contact after bid cleanup, contracts and their amount with the same budget (September: 39 contracts, 7.5 million ₽), average check, and margin for closed deals. The projected +3.3 million ₽ will be verified in Spring 2027 using 1C and CRM.

The main conclusion after five weeks is boring and useful. AI in medium-sized businesses doesn't start with a support chat bot. It starts with who holds the keys to the infrastructure, where the data is stored, and whether it can be trusted. And only then does it calculate – but it calculates what no one has calculated for fifteen years.
