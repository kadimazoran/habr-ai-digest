Google's updated video model Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash has become cheaper, learned to maintain details of characters and products between frames, and allows editing a clip with voice commands without re-generating it.

Longer clips, more precise details

The model creates videos from 3 to 15 seconds long, in one-second increments. The previous version's limit was 10 seconds.

According to Google, the preservation of character appearance, clothing, inscriptions, and objects during camera movement has been improved. Images, short video clips, and audio can be combined as references. The company also claims more natural speech and precise lip synchronization.

Edits via dialogue

A finished clip can be sequentially modified with text commands: replace the background or product, adjust lighting, weather, and aspect ratio. This allows for scene refinement without full regeneration from scratch.

Drafts are available in 360p and 720p; the selected result can be upscaled to 1080p or 4K.

What's new

Length. The maximum clip length has increased from 10 to 15 seconds. Duration is set in one-second increments, from 3 to 15.

Price. Generation at 720p costs $0.07 per second, 30% less than Omni 1.1. A full 15-second clip will cost approximately $1.05.

Frame stability. According to Google, faces, clothing, fonts on packaging, and brand colors remain consistent from the first frame to the last, even with camera angle changes. Object distortions during movement appear less frequently. To achieve this, the model accepts references: images of characters, products, or styles, motion fragments up to three seconds, and audio prompts.

Audio. Speech has become more natural, lips synchronize more accurately with words, and the voices of multiple speakers are better distinguished.

Camera. Continuous motion control has been improved: first-person fly-arounds, pull-outs, object rotations. Fewer extraneous cuts are present.

Editing in dialogue. A clip can be edited through several prompts: replace a product or background, change lighting and weather, switch the aspect ratio. There's no need to regenerate the clip; the feature works on full 15-second clips.

What are the limitations?

Fast-paced sports scenes, abrupt script changes, and complex physics are handled less effectively by the model. All videos receive an imperceptible SynthID watermark. There are no independent tests in the announcement; quality claims are made by the company itself.

Price and access

Generation at 720p costs $0.07 per second — 30% cheaper than the previous version. Consequently, a 15-second clip will cost $1.05.

The model has become publicly available through the Gemini API and Google Cloud. In the Gemini app, access is rolling out for Google AI Ultra subscribers.
