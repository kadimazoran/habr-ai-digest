SGNE-CC v2 Patch for DeepSeek-R1-1.5B (T=0): -87% Hangs, 2.7x Shorter Answers, and +11pp on MATH-500

When greedily selecting tokens (T=0, no sampling or repetition penalty), the reasoning model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B often gets stuck in infinite loops of re-checking its own solution within <think> tags, hitting the 32,768 token limit. I ported the open-source norm-preserving weight edit SGNE-CC from the hybrid Qwen3.5-0.8B to this pure transformer without fine-tuning or inference code changes. To stop the self-looping within the weights, it was enough to rotate just 205 mlp.down_proj columns (0.0177% of parameters, 603KB patch), leaving attention layers, gates, and normalizations untouched. Gains in greedy mode (T=0): on MATH-500 accuracy increased from 65% to 76% (+11pp), on programming tasks MBPP from 53% to 68%, on logic BBH from 68% to 78%. Fewer hangs and tokens (across 5 datasets, 1,054 tasks): the share of unfinished reasoning dropped by 87%, and the average answer length decreased 2.7-fold (10.8k -> 4.0k tokens). With standard sampling (T=0.6): accuracy is not lost (73% vs 72%), residual hangs also decrease by 86%, and generation becomes a third shorter. Below the fold: localization of the self-copying loop in attention heads, the geometry of the patch, honest boundaries of the method (HumanEval, T=0.3), and links to the patch and 32K traces. When greedily selecting the next token (T = 0), the reasoning model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (1.78 billion parameters in BF16 format) often gets stuck in repeatedly re-checking already found steps within <think>...</think>, exhausting the budget up to the 32,768 token limit. Even with recommended sampling (T = 0.6, top_p = 0.95), a noticeable portion of unfinished answers remains in mathematics and programming. Can the number of unfinished reasonings be reduced by a small static correction to the weights themselves—without fine-tuning, without a repetition penalty, and without changing the inference code?

This work ports the SGNE-CC method (Spherical Gated Norm-preserving Edit, Column-Constrained), previously tested on the hybrid Qwen3.5-0.8B (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24235218, dna-weight-editing), to a pure transformer of a different family (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) as SGNE-CC v2, reducing the edit size by almost an order of magnitude—from 1,984 columns (802 + 1,182) and one gate row to 205 columns of mlp.down_proj (0.0177% of parameters). The original and corrected models are compared with a budget of up to 32,768 new tokens on five benchmarks (MATH-500, MBPP, HumanEval, ARC-Challenge, BBH Logic, totaling 1,054 tasks) at two temperatures common to both models—T = 0 and T = 0.6 (the corrected model is additionally evaluated at T = 0.3).

1. Patch Geometry: 205 mlp.down_proj Columns

Before applying the patch, we checked where exactly the self-copying loop closes within the model and which matrices could be changed without degrading normal text.

Attention Head Asymmetry. Comparing all 336 heads (28 layers × 12 heads) on 32 looped and 32 normally completed mathematical traces (64 tasks from MATH-500) reveals a sharp localization: all 12 heads with the highest increase in attention to their own repetitions (+29 … +67 pp) are in the first KV Group 0 (H0–H5). In head L23 H04, attention to the problem statement drops from 31% to 0.3% (about 100 times), and in the four leading heads (L23 H04, L19 H03, L19 H05, L14 H03), the proportion of attention to repetitions reaches 67–83%. Meanwhile, in preliminary tests, composite interventions affecting the input gate gate_proj and the output projection reduced the match of the next token on normal text from 99% to 78%, so the attention layers, normalizations, gate_proj, and up_proj were left untouched.

Selection of 205 Columns and Norm-Preserving (Spherical) Rotation. In the SwiGLU block, the contribution of the -th column is multiplied by , so a column whose gate input is negative in normal text and positive in repetition effectively operates only during repetitions. Based on a small set of mathematical traces (6 + 6 for selecting the punctuation group and 8 + 6 for selecting the size; all 26 tasks excluded separately in §2, and 554 tasks from the other four benchmarks were not involved in the selection), two non-overlapping groups were selected:

  1. Line Breaks (135 columns, layers L18–L27): active on tokens with line breaks (including ) during repetition and suppressed within phrases and after </think>.

  2. Punctuation Marks (70 columns, layers L19–L27): active on ., ?, ! within repeating paragraphs without line breaks (average gate input +0.85 vs −1.41 for normal punctuation and −1.81 after </think>).

5.1. Cross-Architecture Transfer: Comparison with Previous Work on Qwen3.5-0.8B

5.2. Method Boundaries and Observed Costs

  • Lost Solutions. In the summary sample of 1,054 tasks at T = 0, there are 166 new correct solutions for every 71 previously correct answers lost. A per-item analysis of these 71 losses shows that in 18 cases the corrected model closed the reasoning earlier than the original, in 38 cases later, and in 15 cases it left it open; a single position of the </think> marker is insufficient for a causal explanation of a specific error.

  • Repeated </think> Markers. After the first closure in the final answer text, SGNE-CC v2 sometimes generates a repeated service marker </think>: 84 cases at T = 0 and 83 at T = 0.6 versus 0 at T = 0 and 3 at T = 0.6 (BBH indices 16 and 22, MBPP index 55) for the original model.

  • Overall Accuracy with Random Token Selection. At T = 0.6, the accuracy difference across 1,054 tasks is statistically insignificant (+92 / −81, ): with sampling, the patch primarily manifests as a length reduction (−33.6%) and removal of residual unfinished answers (29 → 4; outputs hitting the EOS ceiling—30 → 4).

5.3. Conclusion

The spherical rotation of 205 output columns of mlp.down_proj (0.0177% of parameters) without gradient training and without changing the decoder code significantly reduces the number of unfinished reasonings in the pure transformer DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. In greedy selection (T = 0), the number of correct answers increased from 638 to 733 out of 1,054 (+9.0 pp, ), and the number of answers without </think> decreased from 305 to 41 (without EOS: 300 → 38) while reducing the average length from 10.8k to 4.0k tokens. At T = 0.6, the overall accuracy was 72.3% for the base and 73.3% for SGNE-CC v2 (p = 0.4472), with a decrease in average length by 33.6% and a reduction in answers without </think> from 29 to 4.
