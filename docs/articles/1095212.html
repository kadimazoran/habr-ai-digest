It's fashionable now to call a folder of notes in Obsidian a second brain. I was looking through the GitHubs of Andrey Karpathy and others who write about this, and most often the "second brain" there is a bunch of files, interlinked with each other, plus Claude Code connected to them.

I think differently.

There is second memory, and there is a second brain. These are different things, and the difference between them is precisely what makes all this worth building.

Second Memory

Second memory is everything that has passed through your life:

  • correspondence with colleagues, friends, wives, children in all messengers: Telegram, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, etc. - the entire history, as deep as it can be downloaded;

  • emails, all your mail;

  • your notes, articles, scientific materials;

  • transcriptions of meetings and voice recordings.

Second memory is the ability to aggregate all this information.

I have aggregated it for myself, and now it's extremely convenient to live: RAG, retrieval, and embeddings are built on top of all this, and I have search working across all my information.

In terms of volume, it's about 200,000 files and several tens of gigabytes.

But this is still memory. It's not a brain yet.

Why Obsidian is Not a Brain

Obsidian is just a visual representation of files, interlinked with each other. An external graphical shell made for people.

When there are more than a few thousand files, Obsidian on Windows and Mac, especially on weak computers, starts to lag heavily and can take half an hour to load.

And even if you have tens and hundreds of thousands of files and tens of gigabytes of data – it's still just second memory. No matter how many files you add, they don't do anything on their own.

What Else to Put into Memory: AI Dialogues and Coding Sessions

This is a very important point, although for some reason it's often missed.

You need to put not only correspondence with people into your second brain, but also all your dialogues with artificial intelligence. With DeepSeek, with ChatGPT, with Claude, with Mistral - with all the regular LLMs you've interacted with from your phone, voice, or computer.

And separately - all your sessions that you've ever had in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and so on.

Why?

Every time you start doing something, you don't build from absolute zero, but rely on your background: who you are and what you do.

Start learning something new – you rely on your methodology.

Write new lines of code – you rely on your code writing structure.

Therefore, it's important for the AI to know as much about you as possible. And it can only know in one way – by constantly looking at what you have already done, written, and discussed.

How Search Works
