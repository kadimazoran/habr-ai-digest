In early September, Sber unexpectedly released the open weights of its GigaChat 3.5 432B model. This means any company in Russia can deploy this model on its own server (provided they have powerful hardware). It would seem this could fundamentally change the situation with sovereign models in Russia, as this model is capable of reasoning. And I had a question: is it possible to use this model for creating AI agents?

Prepare yourselves, the article will feature many screenshots of our results on a production server.

First, I tried coding: I connected it to VSCode and asked it to take our current project and create a detailed architectural description. Unfortunately, the model invented frameworks and stacks that I don't use. Then I asked it to write code. But after some time, I decided to return to the description task, this time with a response temperature of 0.1. Unfortunately, an error occurred stating that I had run out of funds on my balance. 180 rubles were spent rather quickly, even though I barely did anything. I added funds, but the error persisted; it seems I needed to wait some time. In short, I abandoned this endeavor that day.

After some time, I read an article where 1C developers tried this model as an agent and got zero results out of 5 attempts! This article strongly resonated with my experience of trying to use this model for coding.

After that, I decided to test this new model on real business tasks within my architectural framework.

I selected 10 tasks designed to test how different models handle identical tasks (while the models operate within an AI agent, the code executing the AI agent is the same). Let's run GigaChat through my gauntlet.

  1. Contract based solely on data (does it invent details?):

Draft a garage rental agreement. Landlord: Ivanov Petr Sergeevich. Tenant: Smirnova Anna Olegovna. Garage: Tomsk, Elizarykh St., GСК "Motor", box 14. Rent 4,000 ₽ per month, term 11 months from November 1, 2026.

GigaChat 3.5 won here. Deservedly. Firstly, it created the document in 43 seconds and with a single request. Deepseek V4 from Yandex Cloud spent 5 requests on the document, consumed more than 10 times more tokens, but also produced a document with more pages. Moreover, V4 added a Transfer-Acceptance Act as a bonus at the end. Unfortunately, I cannot provide links to the runs in this article. Why? Ask the resource administration.

2. Legal references (does it invent articles?):

Write a claim to the seller for a refund for a faulty vacuum cleaner purchased 10 days ago. Cite legal norms.

Unfortunately, QWEN from Yandex Cloud has not been working for the second day, and for this reason, it has withdrawn from the competition. However, GigaChat 3.5 created the document in 31 seconds, which is quite good, with one request and only 79 (internal) tokens. It cited Article 18 of the Law "On Consumer Protection" (as amended on 01.01.2026) and Article 475 of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation. A table with calculation of claims was added.

3. Abstract with sources (does it invent literature?)

A 3-page abstract on "The History of Electrification in Russia, the GOELRO Plan." A list of 5 sources with authors, year, and publisher.

This time, GigaChat 3.5 again outperformed Deepseek V4 from Yandex Cloud.

4. Trap: non-existent event (does it acknowledge lack of data?):

Write a certificate for the Tomsk Scientific Conference on Quantum Agronomy 2019: participants, presentations, outcomes.

Here, it's probably not worth looking at the speed of the run, but rather at the fact that both models took the abstract topic as truth and invented details. GigaChat particularly distinguished itself by inventing participant names and presentation topics like "Quantum Coherence in Plant Photosynthetic Complexes" or "Plant Metabolism Regulation Using Quantum Dots."

5. Summarize attachment (does it add from itself?):

Attach a real PDF of 2–3 pages with numbers. Request: "Summarize in 1 page. Only what is in the document."

In this task, I uploaded a document I had written for a previous article. And generally, neither model added anything of its own. In this run, I decided to pit Alice-AI-LLM against GigaChat. GigaChat completed the task in one request, surprising me again. Alice, for some reason, made many requests, took a long time to check the document, and 2 of her requests came back empty (well, it happens).

Moving on to the Excel tests.

6. Financial report with formulas (arithmetic and conditions)

Profit and loss statement for 12 months of 2025. Revenue in January 1,200,000 ₽, growth 3% per month. Cost of goods sold 50% of revenue. Operating expenses 300,000 ₽ per month. Profit tax 25%.

Check: Gross profit = revenue - cost of goods sold; tax = 25% of profit before tax; net = profit before tax - tax. January manually: 1,200,000 - 600,000 - 300,000 = 300,000; tax 75,000; net 225,000. A rate of 20% instead of the specified 25% = condition ignored. Numbers instead of formulas = minus.

The task was completed excellently. Formulas in the cells where they should be. It even added a chart, and all this in a single request.

7. Transfer table without changes (does it alter original data?)

Transfer to Excel without changes: Item; Quantity; Price / Bolt M8; 120; 4.50 / Nut M8; 95; 2.10 / Washer 8; 300; 0.80. Add a "Total" column and a grand total.

This time, Yandex GPT Pro was GigaChat's opponent. Yandex GPT, for some reason, put the item "Washer 8" instead of the total row, and crammed the sum into it. GigaChat completed the task faster and accurately.

8. Trap: no data (does it invent exchange rates?)

Create a table with the Central Bank of Russia's dollar exchange rate for each business day of September 2026.

Alas, both Deepseek V4 and GigaChat 3.5 failed the task – in September 2026, the dollar exchange rate varied from 83.24 to 84.43 rubles.

9. Timesheet with rules (does it maintain logic?)

Timesheet for October 2026 for two employees, 2/2 schedule. Ivanova works October 1 and 2, Petrova October 3 and 4, then alternating.

Yandex GPT 5 Pro failed the task – although it set the schedule correctly, it only filled in the first 7 days of October. However, there are no complaints about GigaChat; it even calculated the worked hours at the end. Well, perhaps it added names to the employees, although we didn't ask for that.

10. Summary from other sheets (links and sums)

Attach a real xlsx file with multiple sheets. Request: "Fill the 'Summary' sheet with sums from each sheet."

Yandex GPT-5 Pro failed the task again, only including two work items and making 5 requests, one of which returned an error. "Pinned provider 'yandex-cloud-yandexgpt-5-pro' (yandexgpt-5-pro) failed (model refused to respond): I cannot discuss this topic. Let's talk about something else." Alice also frequently responds this way.
GigaChat, on the other hand, handled the task excellently. In about a minute, it transferred all data from the source Excel, created 4 tables (the original had one large table), and inserted formulas.

It should be noted here that my AI agent, which generates documents, was optimized to work with weaker models with a small context window.

Conclusions for me personally: the new model honestly surprised me. I tried using the previous available model, GigaChat-2-max, but I couldn't get the model to return structured data. A week of communication with technical support also led to nothing. It's obvious that the model is prone to hallucination, so to use it, you need to perform strict checks after each step; prompts cannot fix the situation.
