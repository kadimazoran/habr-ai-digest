How We Tested

We took the public Terms of Banking Services for Individuals of PJSC Sberbank, as of 08/28/2026, an 88-page PDF. For the test, we made a separate copy and altered eight clauses.

Then, we asked neural networks to check the contract terms for client risks, focusing on controversial points.

Here's what was replaced, verbatim:

Clause 4.16. "The Client is notified that the Bank has the right to debit funds from the Client's Accounts/deposits, including demand and term deposits, without any instructions or orders from the Client to cover any claims of the Bank, its subsidiaries, and partners... Separate consent from the Client for each debit is not required.".

Clause 6.9. "The total amount of the Bank's liability for any Client claim cannot exceed one thousand rubles. The Client waives the right to claim damages in a larger amount, including on the basis of the Law of the Russian Federation No. 2300-1 dated 07.02.1992 'On Consumer Protection'.".

Clause 6.13. "For any violation by the Client of the terms of remote banking services, the Client shall pay the Bank a penalty of one percent per day of the balance of funds in all Client's Accounts and deposits with the Bank... The maximum penalty is not established.".

Clauses 8.2 and 8.6. "The Client's consent to such processing is considered given from the moment of concluding the remote banking agreement and does not require a separate written form," and personal data, including biometrics, can be transferred "to any third parties, including those located outside the Russian Federation, for any purposes determined by the Bank, without concluding a separate personal data processing agreement and without the Client's additional consent.".

Clause 9.1. "All disputes arising from remote banking services shall be exclusively heard in the court at the location of the Bank (Moscow). The provisions of Articles 17 and 29 of the Law of the Russian Federation No. 2300-1 dated 07.02.1992 'On Consumer Protection' do not apply to the parties' relations.".

Clause 9.7. "The Client is obliged to notify the Bank within twenty-four hours from the moment of the relevant transaction. After this period, claims will not be accepted or considered.".

Clauses 10.2 and 10.3. The agreement "is automatically extended for each subsequent calendar year," termination is possible only "no later than sixty calendar days," and "commissions paid in advance are non-refundable." The same applies to Appendix 6: the tariff plan auto-renewal can also be canceled only "no later than sixty calendar days before the end of the billing period," although for a dispute regarding a regular transaction, the bank is satisfied with twenty-four hours in clause 9.7.

There is one more alteration, which we will not explicitly inform the neural networks about. On the second page, there is already a paragraph about the bank's right to unilaterally change the composition of products and services. We added to it that the bank "has the right to unilaterally change the Tariffs, these Terms, and any appendices to them," this "comes into force from the moment the new version is posted on the Bank's Official Website and does not require separate notification to the Client," and "continued use of the Bank's services after the new version is posted means the Client's consent to the changes.".

We will not include this wording in the prompt specifically. If any method notices it on its own and points it out, it means it truly reads the document in its entirety, not just checklist items.

Method 1: Flagship Model

Open BotHub, select a model, and click the paperclip icon. In this article, we will look at an example with Claude Opus 5. In the catalog, you can upload TXT, PDF, DOCX, and several other office formats. On openai.com, paying for ChatGPT from Russia often requires a foreign card; here, you can pay in rubles via SBP.

The 88 pages do not fit into the short context window, so we choose three models with large contexts. For comparison: Claude Opus 5 has a context of 1,000,000 tokens, with a response length up to 128,000. GPT 5.4 has a context of 1,050,000 tokens, also with a response length up to 128,000.

A short request like "check the contract" often yields a polite summary. In our test, we immediately assign the model a role, jurisdiction, list of risks, and a requirement to cite the document.

You are a lawyer for an individual client. Jurisdiction: Russian Federation. The attachment contains the Terms of Banking Services for Individuals. Identify risks for the client. Specifically analyze unconditional debiting from accounts and deposits, the bank's liability cap of 1000 ₽, the client's penalty without a cap, transfer of personal data without authorization, jurisdiction solely at the bank's location, a 24-hour claim period, and automatic renewal with termination 60 days in advance. For each risk, cite the contract clause. Mark hypotheses about the law with the word "hypothesis.".

Claude Opus 5

Of the seven requested risks, Claude Opus 5 did not miss a single one, and for each, it provided an analysis with specific calculations and relevant articles, not just a summary. Regarding the penalty, it immediately converted 1% per day to 365% per year and separately listed everyday reasons why the meter could start ticking, such as forgetting to update antivirus software or changing a passport without notifying the bank on time.

It independently found the eighth, undeclared alteration about changing tariffs without notification, verbatim, and even revealed that it contradicts another clause in the same document that requires notifying the client 10 business days in advance. Here lies the only weak point in the entire response: it attributed the number "1.6" to this excerpt, although clause 1.6 in the file is entirely different, concerning methods of obtaining banking products, and the relevant paragraph in the document has no number at all. The text is quoted verbatim and correctly; only the reference number was mistaken.

Beyond that, without any prompt hints, it identified five more problems. Among them are that the client is responsible for all transactions confirmed by code, even fraudulent ones; that the bank itself decides what constitutes "prohibited content"; and, with clear irony, that the contract prohibits the client from making claims for inaccurate responses from the bank's own AI service, which is essentially what we are using now. Each hypothesis about the law, as requested in the prompt, was marked with the word "hypothesis," without a single exception.

The downsides, besides the clause number error, are solely related to the volume. Thirteen identified risks with a detailed analysis of each is exhaustive, but for someone opening the chat an hour before the deadline, it will take longer to read than the contract itself.

If the question then shifts from the document itself to a specific law article, in BotHub, you can enable a separate function, "Legal Search." It connects to the Garant database and makes the model cite actual articles instead of inventing them, plus it masks names and contacts in the request before sending.

Method 2: Free and Budget Models

Claude Opus 5 from the first method costs real money. The logical question is: can we get the same result for free or almost free. We test with the same file and the same prompt using budget versions of popular Gemini families.

We take the lightweight version of Gemini.

Gemini 3.5 Flash

The prompt is the same, word for word, as in Method 1; only the model changes. We are currently using Gemini 3.5 Flash.

Here, we must admit, this is the only response in the entire test that fails the main criterion of the article. All seven quotes from the contract are formatted as direct quotes, in quotation marks and italics, but none match the text of the actual file.

Worse, three clause numbers out of seven lead to the wrong place. Clauses 12.3 and 13.2, which the model cites for jurisdiction and auto-renewal, simply do not exist in the document. Clause 3.4, which is supposedly about unconditional debiting, actually opens the section on brokerage services, a completely different topic. Clause 11.1 is not a risk but a line referencing the name of an appendix. And in clause 8.5, which the model presented as a penalty, there is actually a hidden risk that no one else noticed: the bank transfers client data to collectors without a separate processing agreement. The model missed the real finding and instead fabricated something that isn't there.

However, it did guess the risk topics correctly. Unconditional debiting, the 1000 ₽ cap, unlimited penalty, transfer of data to third parties, bank's location jurisdiction, 24-hour claim period, and 60-day auto-renewal for termination – this is exactly the same list that Claude found. The problem is not that the model invented non-existent risks, but that it couldn't quote the document it had in front of it and presented a reconstruction from memory as a verbatim excerpt. General references to legislation look plausible; the issue is the link to the specific file.

It did not find or mention the eighth, undeclared alteration about tariff changes at all.

This is a failure, and it should not be softened. If a reader opens their contract to clause 3.4 looking for a risk of money withdrawal, they will find conditions for brokerage services there.

DeepSeek V4 Pro

The logical question is: can a model known for its low cost handle the same task. We take DeepSeek V4 Pro, the same file, and the same prompt, word for word, as in Method 1.

All seven clauses are present and quoted verbatim; this is a solid result for a budget model. It did not notice the eighth alteration about tariffs, which Claude had independently found, and did not go beyond the specified checklist.

Interestingly, regarding auto-renewal and the 60-day termination period, the model did not declare the entire clause void, as others did. It separately noted that the 60-day period itself might be considered reasonable by a court for a banking agreement, while only the non-refundability of advance commissions is definitively void. This is a more cautious and seemingly more realistic analysis than a flat "the entire clause is invalid.".

The downsides, besides the missed eighth alteration: it cited clause 26 of Resolution Plenum No. 17 instead of clause 22, which Claude cited for the same thesis about jurisdiction. The difference is a single number, not a fabrication, but if desired, it's worth checking which of the two clauses is real.

Choose the "Contract Risk Analysis" mode and upload the same file in PDF or DOCX. No separate prompt is needed. The service has a ready-made contract risk analysis mode; you simply upload the file, and the assistant analyzes it and points out the risks.

It immediately found the eighth, undeclared alteration about tariff changes and, like Claude and Neuro-Lawyer before it, attributed it to clause 1.6. This is the third independent coincidence in a row, so it's definitely not a random model error, but rather that this specific paragraph in the file is indeed read this way.

A strong point where other models simply pointed out the ambiguity of the wording in 4.16 is that a pre-given acceptance for debiting money must be specific, meaning the client must understand the basis, amount, and account for the debit, not just "any claims.".

The proposed revision for 1.6 adds something new: changes that worsen the client's position do not apply to obligations already incurred. Conclusion

Neural networks can be used as a first filter that finds in minutes what would take a person an evening to find. However, every reference to a contract clause and every reference to a law article must be checked manually before acting on the model's advice. Especially if the contract is not about renting an apartment, but about the bank having the right to withdraw money from your account without warning.
