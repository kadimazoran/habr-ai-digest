Introduction: From Chaotic Code Generation to Systemic AI-Disrupt PDLC

The beginning was spontaneous, and nothing foreshadowed the current process. The company where I worked experienced certain difficulties and sharply reduced staff, including me. From my last job, I had experience in developing complex systems and libraries written in languages previously unknown to me. Fortunately, the era of AI arrived, and after trying several assistants, I successfully solved these tasks. But this is not about work. Suddenly, I had a lot of free time and unspent enthusiasm. It was a coincidence. Almost on the same day, to save my nerves from the sudden happiness of unemployment, I was playing Jagged Alliance N-th series. Once again, after quickly completing a sector, I was slightly annoyed by some absurdities in the gameplay. I think many fans of the Jagged Alliance or XCOM series have noticed that the series are degenerating into primitive shooters, losing the spirit of the first parts. Every time I read an announcement of such a game, I hope that someone will finally bring back the spirit of the classics and consider the fans' wishes. In general, everything came together - the thought struck me that it's possible not only to reassemble/connect 20+ libraries in C and Go on other people's projects with the help of AI, but there's also a chance to create the game you want. Especially since, from a purely mobile developer, with the help of KMP, I smoothly migrated to desktop on several projects. Turning the idea over in my mind from all sides, I didn't notice any particular drawbacks and proceeded with the implementation.

Developing a large-scale AA-level game alone is a project that is considered inherently impossible in the traditional industry. A full-fledged tactical RPG like Jagged Alliance 2 or Silent Storm requires simultaneous design of dozens of intersecting systems: fair ballistics, anatomical medicine, destructible 3D environments, enemy artificial intelligence, strategic meta-game, hiring economy, and multilingual interface.

In a traditional studio, 15-20 highly specialized engineers and artists work on this. When I started the "PMC Rhino" project, I quickly realized: pinpoint use of coding assistants for generating individual functions does not solve the main problem. Without a systemic restructuring of the Product Development Life Cycle (PDLC), a solo developer inevitably drowns in architectural chaos, data desynchronization, and technical debt.

The key to achieving a real 10x leverage from artificial intelligence is building an AI-Disrupt PDLC. In this model, I act as the Chief Architect, Game Designer, and Strict Filter, while AI tools work as a unified ecosystem: from technical coordinator and asset generator to ballistics table analyst.

The foundation of my project is not chaotic code generation, but a fixed Master Index of technical documentation, including over 100 specifications. In this article, I want to move away from simply listing features and explain how AI has transformed my engineering process at various stages of game creation.

1. From Chaos to SSOT: 100+ Specifications as the Project Foundation

In the classic indie PDLC, a developer often keeps lore and balance in their head or in scattered Excel tables, immediately jumping into writing code. In a complex tactical project, this is guaranteed to lead to an architectural dead end.

My first development cycle began not with an IDE, but with the creation of a large-scale knowledge base. Together with Google Gemini, acting as a technical and narrative coordinator, I created over 100 strict Markdown specifications. They cover everything from Kotlin Multiplatform and Fleks ECS architecture rules to interface design guidelines.
