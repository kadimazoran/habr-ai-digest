In September 2026, the industry is once again debating what constitutes human-level artificial intelligence – artificial general intelligence (AGI). A convenient answer has emerged: AGI will be achieved when AI can conduct AI research and create its successor, i.e., upon achieving recursive self-improvement (RSI).

1. I'll start with definitions to avoid confusion. AGI (artificial general intelligence) describes the breadth of capabilities: a system is considered general when it can perform most economically valuable tasks at a human level. RSI (recursive self-improvement) describes the mechanism of development: a system improves itself or its successor, with each improvement facilitating the next. ASI (artificial superintelligence) describes the level of capabilities: a system surpasses all humans in virtually everything. These three axes:

  • breadth of capabilities,

  • mechanism of development,

  • and level of capabilities

are logically independent. One can imagine a very general system that cannot conduct its own research, and a narrow system that excels at improving one class of algorithms while being useless in all others. When someone says that AGI will be achieved upon reaching RSI, they are conflating all three axes.

It's useful to keep in mind the Google DeepMind framework from the work of Morris et al. (Morris et al., Levels of AGI, 2023), where capabilities are broken down separately by depth (performance) and breadth (generality), with autonomy as a third dimension. Defining AGI through RSI means pre-emptively guaranteeing the right to declare victory when the means are operational, even if the ultimate goal – human-level capabilities in a broad sense – has not yet been achieved.

2. The idea of AGI via RSI is not new. As early as 1965, I. J. Good, in his article "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine," described an intelligence explosion, stating verbatim that an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines, and that the first such machine would be the last invention man need ever make, provided it was docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control. The caveat about control is lost in this quote, and it shouldn't be, as the modern version of the idea sounds drier and more commercially precise.

In October 2025, Sam Altman, both in a live broadcast and later in a post on X, outlined OpenAI's internal goals: an automated research intern by September 2026 and a fully automated researcher by March 2028 (X). In early September 2026, OpenAI announced (Engadget) that the intern goal had been met: the system performs clearly defined research tasks under human guidance, including those that would take a specialist several days. Note the cautious wording: there is no mention of AGI, but the word "intern" and the phrase "under human guidance" are present.

Concurrently, journalists and the labs themselves began to substitute the term. In May 2026, TechCrunch explicitly wrote that RSI had become the new AGI, and that it was just as hard to define. In August 2026, Time described OpenAI's internal system named Astra and linked discussions about AGI specifically to the self-improvement loop: a model that accelerates its own research is called recursive self-improvement. Thus, the conflation arose: since the threshold for AGI is debatable, let's measure it by the ability to conduct AI research. Convenient, but dishonest.

Let's verify four claims from the original note against primary sources:

a) The Anthropic Institute page "When AI builds itself" does indeed describe RSI as a system capable of fully autonomously designing and building its successor, and explicitly states that we are not there yet and that RSI is not inevitable (Anthropic). Furthermore, Anthropic identifies a specific remaining gap: Claude is already comparable to humans in executing a well-posed experiment, but significant gaps remain when it comes to goal selection, i.e., choosing research directions. This confirms the point in the note.

b) The OpenAI Charter defines AGI as highly autonomous systems that surpass humans in most economically valuable work. The wording is confirmed verbatim as a definition concerning the economics of labor, not self-improvement, and it contains no mention of the system needing to build its successor.

c) Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve (May 2025) did improve computational processes, including the training of Gemini itself: it accelerated a key matrix multiplication core by 23%, resulting in an approximate 1% reduction in Gemini's overall training time (Google DeepMind). This is confirmed by the DeepMind primary source. Separately, AlphaEvolve accelerated the FlashAttention core by approximately 32.5 percent, but this pertains to inference and training, not specifically to self-improvement.

d) On September 9, 2026, OpenAI stated in the text "The AI policy window is open" that fully autonomous RSI does not exist today (OpenAI), and distinguished it from accelerating specific research tasks. The literal meaning is: AI already accelerates parts of research, but this is not recursive self-improvement, but rather an indication of the direction of progress. This is confirmed.

The conclusion from this verification is simple: the primary sources themselves are more precise than their summaries. None of the serious authors have written that AGI equals RSI. The equality sign is placed in headlines and tweets.

3. Why is it convenient for labs to equate AGI with RSI?

a) It automates their next competitive advantage. A lab's value lies in its researchers. If the main threshold is defined as an automated researcher, then the race is precisely for the asset that labs already possess and are best at scaling. This is a convenient definition of the peak for those who are already leading in this specific area.

b) The valuations of frontier labs in 2025 and 2026 are enormous. On October 2, 2025, OpenAI completed a secondary share sale of $6.6 billion at a valuation of $500 billion, outstripping SpaceX as the world's most valuable private company, with $10.3 billion authorized. Anthropic closed its Series G round for $30 billion on February 12, 2026, at a post-money valuation of $380 billion, with lead investors GIC and Coatue. xAI closed an increased Series E round for $20 billion on January 6, 2026, at a valuation of approximately $230 billion, with the company itself emphasizing that the round exceeded the target of $15 billion. The promise of a self-improvement cycle justifies such capital expenditures better than the promise of another useful product.

c) OpenAI's contract with Microsoft demonstrated how expensive the definition of AGI is. Under the October 2025 restructuring terms, any AGI declaration by OpenAI is now reviewed by an independent expert panel, Microsoft's intellectual property rights are extended to 2032 and include post-AGI models, and revenue share payments continue until 2030. The definition of AGI has become a legal switch worth tens of billions of dollars, and labs are interested in controlling its wording.

d) The RSI threshold aligns with their own safety thresholds. Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy introduces AI R&D levels 4 and 5: the first is the ability to fully automate the work of a junior researcher, the second is the ability to cause a rapid acceleration in AI development, effectively RSI, and it requires ASL-4 protection. OpenAI's critical threshold for AI Self-Improvement is defined similarly: a superhuman research agent or a five-fold reduction in model generation time, sustained for several months. When safety and the declaration of AGI are tied to the same threshold, it is convenient to call it the main one, because then the story about safety and the story about victory are told in the same terminology.

But positive feedback does not equal unlimited growth. A monotonically increasing sequence can have a finite limit: the sum of the series ½ + ¼ + ⅛ + … increases at each step but never exceeds one – this is the limit of the given function. The question is not whether a loop exists, but what the amplification factor is and where the constraints lie. There are several constraints, and data exists on them.

Computation
Many software intelligence explosion scenarios assume that software improves software without new hardware. However, serious experiments require computational levels comparable to training frontier models, and the growth of computation is constrained by physics, energy, and delivery times. An economic study from 2025 showed that with a certain connection between experiments and ideas, computation becomes a hard bottleneck (arxiv), and software acceleration only lasts until this limit.

Ideas are becoming harder to find
This is a known result from growth economics: to maintain a constant rate of improvement, ever-increasing research effort is required. Even proponents of the singularity scenario at Forethought estimate the returns such that one doubling of cognitive labor yields about 1.2 doublings of output on average, with a wide spread from 0.4 to 3.6. If the parameter is closer to the lower bound, no "explosion" occurs, only a dampening.

Experimentation and Verification
Even an ideal automated researcher must conduct experiments and wait for results. An Epoch AI review directly notes that experiments are not a silver bullet, as a model can be flawed, and benchmarking requires a full training cycle, accessible only to a handful of players.

Physical Construction
Dario Amodei, an optimist, acknowledges in his essay "Machines of Loving Grace" (October 2024) that a "country of geniuses living 24/7 in a data center" will hit physical and practical limits, such as the speed of biological experiments. His formulas for "powerful AI" and "country of geniuses in a datacenter" deliberately avoid the word AGI. A "country of geniuses in a datacenter" is a vast collective of strong researcher agents living within servers. The metaphor is composed of four properties he lists:

  1. "Geniuses." Each copy of the model is smarter than a Nobel laureate in most fields: mathematics, biology, programming, and engineering.

  2. "Country." The resources spent on training the model allow for millions of its copies to run simultaneously. In terms of numbers, this is the population of a small country.

  3. Speed. Each copy thinks and acts 10–100 times faster than a human.

  4. Self-organization. Copies can work on different tasks independently or form teams. Some can be further trained for specific specializations, like in an institute or ministry.

"In a datacenter" means these geniuses have no bodies. They reside on servers and operate via computers: writing code, ordering materials, controlling laboratory equipment, and giving instructions to humans.

Amodei chose this imagery for two reasons. First: the metaphor moves away from the contentious word AGI and describes a concrete picture that can be evaluated [fact]. Second: it immediately shows the limit. Even an entire country of geniuses must wait for biological experiments to conclude and for new hardware to be built [fact].

Human Contribution and Cost
In July 2025, METR conducted a randomized controlled experiment: 16 experienced developers, 246 tasks, data from February to June 2025, using Cursor Pro tools with Claude 3.5 and 3.7 Sonnet. The result was verbatim: allowing the use of AI increased task completion time by 19%, with a confidence interval of approximately +2% to +39%, even though after the experiment, the same developers estimated the acceleration at 20%. One experiment is not definitive, and METR itself notes that this is a measurement from early 2025. But it shows how easily the feeling of acceleration can be confused with actual acceleration, and why feelings are a poor measure.

4. What does the data say about the pace, and how should it be read? There is a trend that is cited most often. METR measures task time horizons: the length of a task in human time that a model solves with 50% probability. This horizon doubled approximately every seven months from 2019 to 2025, and in the 2024-2025 data, the doubling appears closer to four months. Anthropic, on its "When AI builds itself" page, presents this accelerated estimate and adds internal data: over 80 percent of the code committed to Anthropic's codebase as of May 2026 is written by Claude (Anthropic), and a typical engineer in the second quarter of 2026 committed 8 times more code per day than in 2024 (Anthropic).

Lines of code represent quantity, not quality, and Anthropic acknowledges that the 8x figure almost certainly overstates the actual productivity increase. The METR curve is highly sensitive to a few long tasks, and human baseline times are measured for only a small fraction of the longest tasks. However, this is not proof that the machine is independently choosing research directions. It is precisely goal selection that Anthropic identifies as its main remaining gap.

And one more data point against linear extrapolation. Daniel Kokotajlo, the lead author of the "AGI 2027" scenario (April 2025), shifted his median forecast towards 2029 and 2030 by the end of 2025 and into 2026 because progress is proceeding somewhat slower than the scenario predicted. Skeptics like Ege Erdil and Tamay Besiroglu from Epoch AI place the AGI horizon beyond 2045 and do not consider an intelligence explosion plausible, although they do allow for explosive economic growth. When the authors of the most prominent scenario shift their timelines, it signals that extrapolating a single curve should be approached with caution.

5. What would actually count as proof of RSI's existence? To speak of RSI as a fait accompli, rather than marketing hype, requires repeatable growth in research capability over several model generations, measured considering two factors: human contribution and computational cost. Specifically, I would look at the following:

  • Is the model's ability to independently choose research directions growing, rather than just executing set experiments, as this is precisely the gap Anthropic identifies?

  • Does the acceleration persist when you fix the computational budget, rather than increasing it?

  • Is the effect sustained across several consecutive generations, not just one successful release?

  • Is it confirmed by controlled measurements like METR, rather than surveys and subjective impressions?

Until at least some of these conditions are met, it is reasonable to assume that we are seeing an acceleration of individual tasks, exactly as OpenAI itself wrote on September 9, 2026.

6. AGI and RSI are different things, and equating them means confusing the goal with the means. RSI is a mechanism, potentially powerful, but not yet closed; AGI is breadth of capabilities; ASI is level. The conflation benefits labs for entirely earthly reasons: it automates their main asset, justifies capital, shifts the finish line under their control, and aligns with their safety thresholds.

It is likely that within the next year, one of the major players will announce an internal system that meets its own definition of AGI through an automated researcher, and will do so before independently reproducible evidence of sustained RSI emerges.

Practical takeaway: one should look at reproducible measurements considering computational cost, at the systems' ability to choose tasks rather than just execute them, and at whether companies fix their computational budget in their productivity claims.
What to ignore: single tweets equating AGI and RSI, graphs without confidence intervals, metrics like lines of code, productivity estimates based on user feelings.
