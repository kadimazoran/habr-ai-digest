AI Has Transformed the Hiring Market

AI is increasingly used both in development and in job searching.

According to the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, 84% of surveyed developers already use AI tools in the development process or plan to start. Among professional developers, 51% use them daily.

AI is also used in employment. According to a Gartner study, conducted at the end of 2024 among 3290 job seekers, 39% used such tools in their job search, including for resume preparation and completing assessment tasks.

The Russian market shows a similar trend. In September 2026, "Avito Rabota" and "MTS Link" published the results of a survey of 10,000 people: 40% of respondents have used AI at least once to prepare for an interview. Among job seekers aged 18-24, the proportion who used AI was 65%, and among people over 55, it was 26%.

At the same time, the scenarios of use vary: language models help prepare for interviews and edit resumes, but can also be used to get hints during the interview. Therefore, it is important for the employer to evaluate not the fact of using AI itself, but how it affects the assessment of skills.

Can AI Use Be Detected in an Interview?

According to Anton Pelevin, Head of Recruitment at "Kryptonite", the problem of using AI for deception in interviews and test assignments is acute in the Russian IT sector. However, it is impossible to reliably identify the use of a neural network solely by a candidate's behavior. Pauses, eye movements, and well-structured speech prove nothing on their own. A person might be nervous, choosing their words, or preparing for an interview based on career consultant recommendations.

Therefore, instead of trying to guess whether a candidate is using a neural network, it is better to check the content of their answers and their ability to reason about the task independently.

For example, if a candidate proposes a technical solution, the interviewer can ask them to explain why this particular approach was chosen, modify the solution with new input, or provide an example of a similar approach used in their own practice.

If a person struggles to answer, it is a reason to continue testing their knowledge, but not to automatically accuse them of using AI. Professional competencies should be assessed, not assumptions about how exactly the answer was prepared.

The Position of Technical Leads

Experts emphasize different aspects of candidate assessment: transparency of use, engineer's independence, and verification of neural network results.

"The use of AI is gradually becoming the norm. At the same time, the candidate should clarify in advance whether it is permissible to use a neural network at a specific stage of the interview. AI is comparable to a calculator: if a specialist resorts to it even for simple calculations, it may raise questions about basic skills. The same applies to neural networks: it is important to understand when it helps in work and when it hinders the assessment of independent preparation," believes Evgeny Kincharev, Head of Data Processing at "Kryptonite".

"A candidate must be able to demonstrate the skills required for their level without AI assistance. If a middle-level specialist cannot show the required result independently, it is a reason to reconsider the hiring decision. For a senior developer, AI can be a tool to speed up work, but not a substitute for expertise," says Alexander Televnoy, Director of the Infrastructure Department at "Kryptonite".

To test engineering skills, Alexander recommends involving a senior developer and the candidate's future team in the technical stage. Instead of шаблонные questions, it is better to discuss operating principles, alternative solutions, and reasoning processes.

"The ability to work with a neural network is not limited to formulating a query. A true specialist must find errors in the response and, if necessary, propose their own solution," emphasizes Alexander Grechin, Head of the Testing Department.

Alexander also considers it important to discuss the rules for using AI in advance. If this skill is needed for the job, it can be tested at a separate stage: allow the candidate to use a neural network, and then ask them to check its results, find errors, and justify corrections.

The candidate must understand the solution they propose and be responsible for its outcome. At the same time, the rules for using AI should be clear to both parties before the task begins.

Fighting the very fact of AI proliferation is pointless. It is more practical to define the acceptable boundaries of its use in advance, explain them to the participants of the process, and evaluate candidates based on clear criteria.

What Employers Should Do

  1. Define the rules before the selection process begins. The main conditions should be indicated in the invitation to the technical interview or in the instructions for the test assignment. For example: are language models allowed, can one use search engines, documentation, etc.

  2. Evaluate not only the result but also the process. Prepare evaluation criteria in advance: correctness of the solution, understanding of operating principles, ability to find errors, justification of choices, and ability to adapt the solution when conditions change.

  3. Differentiate between types of AI tools and discuss the possibility of their use separately.

  4. Do not consider suspicion as proof of violation.

What Candidates Should Do

  1. Clarify the rules in advance. If the task description says nothing about AI, search engines, or documentation, you should ask the recruiter before the interview.

  2. Use AI for preparation, but do not memorize ready-made answers.

  3. Verify everything the neural network suggests.

The use of AI in itself does not indicate either high qualification of a candidate or its absence. What matters are the skills the employer is testing, the tools allowed, and whether the specialist can justify the results of their work.

Therefore, the goal of an interview is not to guess whether a candidate used a neural network, but to assess their competencies based on clear criteria. AI can be a working tool, but the responsibility for the decision made and its consequences remains with the human.
