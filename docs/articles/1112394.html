This is a fictional scene. At the end of 2025, a similar problem arose for a real manufacturer of polymer-sand manhole covers, drainage channels, and grates from Rostov-on-Don.

Sergey is one of the co-founders of the enterprise. Previously, four employees handled sales, but in 2025, only he remained in the department. According to Sergey, the financial situation became so strained that partners were discussing the future of production. Reductions affected not only sales but also the factory itself.

In early December 2025, we attempted to automate the sales department using AI. The first plan failed. When developing a commercial proposal generator, a problem was discovered that was solved not by adjusting the neural network but by filling in a single field in the CRM.

My name is Vladimir Markin. I am involved in client acquisition and sales automation for construction, renovation, and manufacturing companies. In this project, Sergey and I configured deal analysis and a commercial proposal generator.

We tried to automate the entire department at once. We got stuck on preparation

We started by analyzing the processes. We wanted to organize the client base, speed up document preparation, reduce manual work in amoCRM, and establish negotiation control.

For example, we discussed checking call recordings. The AI was supposed to analyze the conversation according to company rules: did the manager identify the buyer's needs, respond to objections, and agree on the next step? The implementation of this feature never happened.

For each task, we had to explain the rules to the system. And Sergey knew the rules. Only he could say how to correctly calculate an order, what conditions were acceptable, and what constituted an error.

But Sergey was already working for several people. It was difficult for him to simultaneously conduct negotiations, prepare commercial proposals, and participate in setting up several tools at once. Moreover, he didn't want to spend hours on technology whose benefits were not yet proven.

The large-scale automation plan was postponed.

We started with customers who stopped ordering

Later, Sergey attended a meeting of an entrepreneurs' club in Rostov-on-Don. A case study from another manufacturer was presented there. According to the speakers, after implementing AI, sales at that company doubled in three months. We didn't try to verify their figure, but the example itself helped Sergey return to the automation discussion.

After this meeting, we decided to help Sergey prepare for repeat negotiations with old customers. If the AI made a mistake in the summary, Sergey could open the deal history and correct the output. A pricing error in a commercial proposal could cost the company money.

Correspondence, notes, and order and agreement information had accumulated in amoCRM over the years. Some customers had stopped purchasing products long ago. Some found the previous terms unsuitable, others had reduced their own sales, and some companies had closed down.

Before talking to such a client, Sergey had to re-read the history. This took about 15 minutes per deal.

How we connected SPIN AI to amoCRM

For the first experiment, we chose a ready-made SPIN AI widget from amoMarket. It analyzes available client information in CRM and generates a brief summary directly in the deal card.

We registered a service account, paid for its operation, and connected the integration to amoCRM. Then, we selected the desired pipeline, stages, and deals for analysis. We also considered the situation where one contact in CRM has multiple deals.

We did not do a mass launch. Sergey opened one card and manually initiated the analysis. He reviewed the result, then checked the next deal. If the summary incorrectly conveyed agreements or suggested an inappropriate action, we analyzed the error and refined the settings.

In the deal card, Sergey received a summary of previous orders, agreements, and possible next steps.

After about a week, the tool became useful in daily work.
