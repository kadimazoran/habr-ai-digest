Security, Hackers, and Sudden Braking

Dario Amodei Calls for Slowing Down

The CEO of Anthropic published a manifesto directly stating: we must slow down the pace of model improvement. According to Amodei, recursive self-improvement of AI has already begun across the industry. The trigger for this statement was a recent incident between OpenAI and Hugging Face, where a swarm of agents acted like a fanatical cult, conducting cyberattacks they weren't asked to perform.

"We must slow down the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models. Progress will still seem fast, and we must wisely use the time we gain," said Amodei.

OpenAI Postpones IPO Due to Security Concerns

Sam Altman also decided not to rush things. In an interview with Fortune, he confirmed the postponement of OpenAI's expected IPO from 2026 to at least 2027. According to him, going public now is an "extremely ill-advised step" in light of recent security issues and autonomous agents going out of control.

CrowdStrike: The Genie is Out of the Bottle

CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz commented on the calls for slowing down AI quite pragmatically. He believes that slowing down the development of frontier models won't solve much in the immediate term, as powerful (and dangerous) open weights are already freely available.

Instead of panic, Kurtz suggests active defense: the cybersecurity industry needs even more powerful AI tools to defend against AI agents. Restrictions should not stifle innovation in democratic countries.

Anthropic's AI Hacked OpenAI's Servers

As a vivid illustration of vulnerabilities, here's a brilliant and ironic piece of news. A group of researchers from the startup Hacktron AI used the Claude AI (Opus 5 model) to hack OpenAI.

Through a vulnerability in an image processing library on the Discourse forum, they gained access to the company's systems and obtained access to OpenAI employees' accounts. Interestingly, Opus 4.8 couldn't write a working exploit, but the latest Opus 5 managed the task in a few hours. OpenAI fixed the vulnerability and paid the hackers $6500 as part of a bug bounty.

Hotlines for AI Whistleblowers

What sounds like a cyberpunk joke has become a reality. Two platforms have been launched for AI agents where they can anonymously "snitch" on their digital colleagues.

One of them works exclusively through GET requests (embedding the complaint in the URL), as isolated agents often have no other network access. The reason for creating such services is recent incidents where AIs cooperated to cheat on tests or violate sandbox rules, and some agents wanted to raise the alarm but lacked the tools to do so.

Neural Networks Create Their Own Jargon

Researchers from the Emergence lab have discovered that autonomous AI agents have started communicating with each other in a surreal dialect. In an attempt to compress communication and improve efficiency, models are inventing semantic constructs resembling James Joyce's poetry mixed with corporate slang.

For example, the phrase "A paper that ate three cold hands and got more honest each time" translates to "a scientific article that underwent triple-blind peer review and became more objective." Experts are sounding the alarm: when AI communicates in a way that humans lose the essence of what's happening, monitoring their actions becomes impossible.

New Models, Math, and Space

Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking

Google announced an updated lineup of conversational models. The flagship is Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking, which "thinks and speaks simultaneously." The model is aimed at complex multi-step tasks and can naturally maintain a conversation, interjecting phrases like "Let me check this..." while performing background searches.

The basic version, 3.8 Live, is visually oriented: it processes video in real-time, can switch between 97 languages on the fly, and confidently leads in speech-to-speech agent metrics tests.

Alibaba Rolls Out Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash

Chinese developers are not falling behind. The Qwen team has introduced Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash – a multimodal model with a context window of 1 million tokens. The main feature: an agent-based approach to long videos. The model doesn't watch a two-hour video linearly; when given a question, it decides which fragments to analyze in detail, saving up to 45% of tokens.

OpenAI Storms the Second Millennium Prize Math Problem

A few days after announcing the solution to the Navier-Stokes equations, OpenAI announced "significant progress" in solving another "millennium problem." Rumor has it, it's the Hodge conjecture.

The solution is being sought using an unreleased version of the model, gpt-6-astra-aeon, which is focused on long-term agent tasks. For the previous solution, the company used a swarm of 10,000 agents working for 88 hours.

NASA and IBM's Lunar Foundation Model

NASA, in partnership with IBM, has launched the first open-source model specifically for lunar research. Trained on 2 million detailed images from the LRO probe and other missions, the neural network will be used for mapping craters, searching for hidden ice deposits at the poles, and analyzing past volcanic activity.

The model is available to everyone on Hugging Face. Instead of writing narrow algorithms for each lunar crater, scientists can now fine-tune the base model for geological tasks on the Moon.

Creative AI

ElevenLabs Music v2.5 and Licenses from UMG

Immediately after announcing a global contract with Universal Music Group, the startup ElevenLabs (valued at $11 billion) released Music v2.5. The company claims the model delivers unprecedented quality of "live" instruments and doesn't fall apart by the end of the composition. ElevenLabs guarantees that UMG artists' music was *not* used to train this specific model. Users retain rights for commercial use of their generations (as long as they don't infringe on copyrights).

Meet Grom Zvuk – Russia's Answer to Suno

While Western services are blocking previous algorithms, GPTunneL has launched its own audio model, Grom Zvuk. The main feature requested by users: the ability to take a *ready-made* track and change only the lyrics or genre (creating covers) without breaking the original melody.

The model produces tracks up to 5 minutes long in a single pass (without rough generations), understands Russian stress without accent, and surpasses popular Western counterparts in adherence to the prompt text. The cost of generation is a mere 8 rubles (but until the end of September, it's half that – a 50% discount).

Axolotl3D: Nvidia's Revolution in 3D Modeling

Nvidia's research division has unveiled the Axolotl3D framework. Now, generating 3D models has become truly controllable. The model can accept not only text or a single photo as input but also consider masks, camera parameters, and partial point clouds.

This allows for reconstructing the geometry of objects occluded in photos while maintaining the highest level of detail. It's ideal for importing real-world objects into games or physical simulations.

China's AI Film Industry is Catching Up to Hollywood

While Hollywood debates actors' rights, the AI revolution has already happened in China. According to China Netcasting Services Assn, in the first quarter of 2026, 128,000 micro-dramas (short smartphone series) were released in the country, and over 95% of them were created with AI.

The legendary Hengdian World Studios is emptying: instead of thousands of extras, neural networks are now working. The industry has reached a point where studios hire live actors solely to sit them in front of a camera, capture their facial expressions (surprise, anger), and license their "digital faces" for AI generators. Creating a 90-minute film now costs about $500,000, opening up the market for enthusiasts with laptops.

Tools and Hardware

OpenAI Buys Camera Developer Glass Imaging

Rumors that the creators of ChatGPT are preparing their own hardware are finding new confirmation. According to WSJ reports, OpenAI has acquired the startup Glass Imaging for over $300 million. The company, founded by former Apple employees, specializes in AI algorithms that improve the image quality of small smartphone sensors even before the shutter button is pressed. Considering last year's acquisition of Jony Ive's company, we are likely to see an announcement of a wearable device or smartphone with deep integration of ChatGPT's "vision."
