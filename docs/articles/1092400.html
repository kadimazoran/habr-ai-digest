How Video Neural Networks Were Tested: One Prompt, 8 Seconds, 720p

One text prompt for twelve models. 8 seconds, 16:9, and 720p to put them all on equal footing.

720p was chosen deliberately, even though half the models can do more. Comparing 4K to 720p is pointless; the resolution difference would mask the quality difference. All twelve were rendered at the same resolution.

Why eight seconds? Veo 3.1 no longer generates more than that in a single pass, and comparing 8 seconds to 15 seconds is unfair.

We looked at whether the object remained consistent from the first frame to the last and how fire, steam, smoke, and water behaved. We checked if the model did what the prompt asked for and calculated the time and cost.

Sound was evaluated separately. Runway and Luma don't have their own sound, so deducting points from them in the overall score for this is strange.

Grok Imagine 1.5 Preview only works from a starting frame; it cannot be launched with text. The same applies to Runway Gen-4 Turbo. We made an exception for them: the starting frame was generated using their own image models, Grok and Runway Frames, and then video was generated from that image. The generation process remained one, as with all others.

Each model was run once. In reality, this rarely yields a perfect result; multiple attempts are usually needed. This makes it more interesting: can the models handle a single attempt? We didn't give anyone a second try, including Veo 3.1, whose single generation was the weakest in the upper half of the list.

During the test, all models produced results without errors or crashes, and generation took up to 1 hour. Almost all this time was spent waiting, not working: Gemini takes a minute for a video, Wan takes ten.

All twelve generations went through SYNTX.AI. One environment, one account, one token accounting format. Each service counts tokens differently, and consumption cannot be directly compared across platforms. Here, the unit of accounting is the same for the entire test, making the figures comparable.

Test Prompt: One Task for Twelve Models

A street food vendor at a rainy night market tosses noodles in a wok, flames leap up, steam rises into neon light, raindrops hit the awning, camera slowly pushes in from a medium shot to a close-up of his hands, shot on 35mm film, shallow depth of field

Within eight seconds, the frame should show fire, steam, rain, neon lights, close-ups of hands, and camera movement. Models often struggle with hands and noodles, while smoke and steam test whether the model understands physics or just creates pretty blobs.

The prompt was designed as a trap. A boring task wouldn't show the differences; all twelve would produce roughly the same result.

How Scores Were Calculated: Quality 80%, Speed and Cost 10% Each

The final score is composed of three parts: image quality and adherence to the prompt (80%), generation speed (10%), and token cost (10%).

Quality weighs the most because a video is ordered for its visuals. Speed and cost only refine it: waiting six minutes is unpleasant, but not as unpleasant as getting a flawed result in one minute.

Quality was rated visually on a ten-point scale. Speed and cost were calculated based on the generation run.

Speed: 1 minute equals 10 points, 10 minutes equals 1 point, with a linear scale in between.

Cost: 5 tokens equals 10 points, 138.7 tokens equals 1 point. Tokens are not counted as an absolute figure but relative to the range within the list, otherwise, the number is meaningless.

Video Neural Network Ranking: 12 Models on a Single Prompt

The order is based on the scores above: quality 80%, speed 10%, cost 10%. The captions for the videos indicate the model, length, resolution, and generation time, allowing for comparison with the table.

1. Luma Ray 3.14: 9.1

Released January 26, 2026. Native 1080p, and at 720p it's 4 times faster and 3 times cheaper than Ray 3.0.

Image quality and physics are good; the video requires color correction. Cost: 5 tokens. The best result in the list, 27 times cheaper than Seedance 2.5.

The main feature is not needed by everyone. It's the output in EXR format (OpenEXR: an extended dynamic range, HDR format): the finished frame can be sent to a colorist for grading without loss. This is very valuable for a colorist, but it offers nothing to someone making a video for Reels.

No sound. No character reference either, meaning you can't define a character by an image and maintain it across generations.

The only model that won in both calculations. It rendered almost as well as the leader, cost next to nothing, and this advantage works in its favor regardless of score weighting.

2. Seedance 2.5: 8.6

ByteDance released it on July 31, 2026, and its main figure is 30.

Thirty seconds per pass, without cuts. Other models in the list have a ceiling of 8 to 20 seconds. You can upload up to 50 references: characters, locations, audio. If one frame in the finished video is flawed, it's re-rendered separately, not the entire video again.

The best result of the test in terms of image quality. Ten points, nothing to complain about: fire, steam, smoke, hands, texture. Among all models here, it's the only one that did exactly what was asked and required no compromises.

Second place with 6 minutes and 138.7 tokens. The most expensive generation in the test. If you need a series of videos, you'll have to count each one. If it's a single video where money is no object, it has no competitors.

3. FLUX 3 Video: 8.6

FLUX 3 was trained on images, video, and sound together, within a single architecture. Up to 20 seconds, Full HD, 24 frames per second, with sound.

It can start and end a video on specified frames, and it can also extend an already generated video.

Image quality, motion, and physics are good. A point was deducted for one issue: instead of a camera zoom, the model switched to a close-up of the hands. Formally, it did what was asked, but it did it with an edit cut rather than a smooth movement. Only someone comparing the video directly to the prompt would notice this.

80 tokens. Third in consumption after Seedance 2.5 and Veo 3.1. It's separated from second place by thousandths of a point, and this difference favors Seedance, not FLUX. It's worth considering when you need exactly one good video without storyboarding and at a lower cost than Seedance.

4. Kling 3.0: 7.9

Veo generates a frame. Kling generates a scene.

Kuaishou focused on scene composition: 3 to 15 seconds, multiple shots within one generation, with sound and lip-sync included. If the prompt describes three actions, the model breaks them down into shots rather than cramming them into one.

Eight out of ten points and no failures. Three minutes, 48 tokens. Such a video can be part of a series without needing to re-watch each one.

The best model in the list for the ratio of quality and predictability, if you don't need long scenes.

5. Gemini Omni Flash 1.1: 7.6

It's listed as Google Omni Flash 1.1 in the list; officially, it's Gemini Omni Flash. Google showcased the Omni family at I/O 2026.

Its strength isn't image quality. The video can be edited directly in the chat: "remove the car," "make it evening." It accepts text, images, video, and audio as input, meaning the video can be modified without restarting the generation.

One minute generation time and 10.5 tokens. Second place in cost efficiency.

For the price, it performed excellently. The only issue is with the size of the noodles; they fluctuate slightly. The image quality isn't on par with Veo or Seedance, but it costs ten times less.

When weighted by quality, it drops from third to fifth place. This is the price of its compromise: fast and cheap, but not the best in image quality.

6. Grok Imagine 1.5 Preview: 7.3

xAI released the preview on May 30, 2026. The model takes a starting frame and a description of motion. It generates sound, music, and dialogue with lip-sync in one pass. Up to 15 seconds, 480p or 720p.

It cannot be given text, unlike Runway Gen-4 Turbo, so we generated the frame using its own image model, Grok. It handled the starting image well, including the cooking steam. One minute and 44 tokens.

For its price, this is sufficient. There's a caveat: the model receives half the work pre-done, so a direct comparison with text-only generators isn't entirely fair.

7. Veo 3.1: 7.2

Veo 3.1 was released in October 2025. Up to 4K, sound with dialogue, up to four reference images per generation.

Its single generation turned out to be the weakest in the expensive half of the list: image quality is eight points, but the video adhered to the prompt. We categorized it as an unsuccessful attempt. We didn't give anyone a second try, so the ranking reflects the result from the first attempt.

In terms of image quality, it doesn't lag behind the leaders, and with a second attempt, it could have contended for the top spot. However, the ranking is based on one generation per model, and here it's in seventh place, not first. Five minutes of waiting and 119 tokens, slightly less than Seedance 2.5, negate any advantage.

The question isn't whether it can render, but how many takes it takes to get a good shot. It's only a tenth of a point behind Grok Imagine. Formally seventh place, but in reality, they are peers in the table.

Plus, a ceiling of eight seconds per pass. Longer videos are only possible by extending segments, and the seams will need to be checked visually.

8. Hailuo 3.0 (MiniMax H3): 6.3

Its official name is MiniMax H3, but it's commonly called Hailuo 3.0, after the app. Released on July 31, 2026, the same day as Seedance 2.5, so comparisons between them will continue for a long time.

Up to 15 seconds, up to 2K, 24 frames per second, stereo sound. Dialogue with lip-sync in 11 languages.

Image quality and motion are adequate, three minutes and 48 tokens, the same as Kling. Issues with physics: the fire and noodles behave somewhat unnaturally. Not a disaster, but this is where such flaws become apparent.

Two identical results in terms of cost are differentiated by quality. Here it's 6 versus 8, and Kling is higher in both calculations. It's useful for generating content in other languages, which Kling cannot do.

9. Seedance 2.0: 5.6

It can't be called a consolation prize. Seedance 2.0 topped the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, a public ranking of models based on blind comparisons. 1 to 15 seconds, it generates its own sound.

Why use it when Seedance 2.5 exists? For the cost. Three minutes and 30.8 tokens versus six minutes and 138.7 for the new version. A difference of four and a half times.

Image quality is adequate, but motion and physics are questionable. The worst part is that objects disappear. The spatula goes into the wok and doesn't come back.

10. Runway Gen-4.5: 5.5

In 2026, Runway doesn't just sell a model. It sells a workshop around it.

Gen-4.5 was released on December 1, 2025. 720p, 5 or 10 seconds. It has no native sound; sound is added through integration with ElevenLabs or a separate Lip Sync feature (synchronizing lip movements with a recorded track).

However, it includes an editor, upscaling (increasing the resolution of the finished video), and export to ProRes 4444. If the video is intended for further editing, these features are more valuable than extra resolution.

Four minutes and 28 tokens, a modest consumption. The problem lies elsewhere: issues with image quality, motion, and physics. Five points for this. The surrounding workshop is strong, but the model within it is from a previous generation.

11. Runway Gen-4 Turbo: 5.0

Gen-4 was released in spring 2025, and its main feature was a character that remained consistent from frame to frame based on a single reference. This was a breakthrough at the time. Now, almost all the models above can do the same.

Turbo was included in the list for comparison with the previous generation. 28 tokens, the same as Gen-4.5, but twice as fast. Speed is its only advantage.

Image quality is better than Wan's. Physics and motion are weak. Four points. There's almost no point in using it: for the same 28 tokens, Gen-4.5 produces better results.

It's separated from Gen-4.5 by half a point. When weighted by image quality, the gap between them is more noticeable than when weighted by cost.

12. Wan 2.7: 3.2

Alibaba's model. You can specify the first and last frames, assemble a video from a nine-image grid, and edit the finished video with text commands.

Plastic-looking image, three out of ten points. Motion and physics are unnatural. The only model that lost on both quality and speed, and even the cost weighting doesn't save it: with 60% for image quality, it's still last.

Ten minutes for a video that will be unusable anyway. Ten minutes for a video that will be unusable anyway.

What the Test Showed

The weight of the evaluation matters more than the scores themselves. Seedance 2.5, with the best image quality in the test, was sixth with a 60% weight on quality and became second with 80%. Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 dropped from third to fifth place. Arguing about which model is better is pointless until we agree on what we consider important.

The same effect is seen with Veo 3.1. In terms of image quality, it scored eight points: the same as Kling, and one point less than FLUX. However, 119 tokens and five minutes keep it in seventh place, and even with a 60% weight on quality, it doesn't move up. Expensive seconds drag the average down more than quality helps, even when quality is recognized as high.

The concern about the prompt was unfounded. We expected the task to be too difficult and fall apart for most models: hands, noodles, smoke, and a camera zoom simultaneously. It fell apart for five out of twelve: four at the bottom of the table and Veo 3.1's single generation, where the image quality was eight points, which would have been an excellent result for other models. The top of the list (Luma, FLUX, Kling, Gemini, and Grok) handled the fire, steam, smoke, and camera zoom calmly, and Seedance 2.5 passed the test perfectly.

Therefore, the choice between models should not be based on whether they can perform the task at all, but on cost, speed, and scene length. The difference in image quality between first and fifth place exists, but it is less than the 27-fold difference in cost between first and second.

Test Limitations

Here are the aspects the ranking doesn't show and the boundaries its conclusions don't cross.

  • One generation per model. We did not perform second attempts for anyone, including Veo 3.1, which did not achieve its full potential. In real-world work, models are usually run multiple times.

  • Weakness of a single generation. One result says nothing about stability: it's unknown if the model will repeat its success on a second attempt. With a second take, Veo 3.1 could have contended for the top spot, but the ranking is based on a single generation for all.

  • 720p. Half the models can do more, and nothing is said here about 4K, 2K, or 1080p: the resolution was kept the same to prevent differences in it from masking differences in performance. Model behavior at their maximum capabilities was not tested.

  • Eight seconds. The ceiling was chosen based on the shortest model in the list. What models can do in 15-30 seconds is not shown in the test.

  • Sound excluded from scoring. Most models have it, but not all, so it was not included in the overall score. We did not separately compare sound quality and lip-sync.

  • Starting frame generated by us for two models. Grok Imagine 1.5 Preview and Runway Gen-4 Turbo do not accept text, so the frame was generated using their respective image models. This is not a direct comparison with text-based generators: they received half the work pre-done.

  • Image quality assessed visually by one person. Without blind viewing or a second assessor. Scores for quality should be read as an opinion, not a measurement.

  • Not tested: costs and pricing, licenses and rights for commercial use, handling of character references across multiple generations. Tokens and minutes reflect the output from the SYNTX.AI run, not the service pricing.
