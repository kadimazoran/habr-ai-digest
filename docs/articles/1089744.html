What is WASM – and why is it interesting?

WebAssembly (or WASM) is a binary code format that allows programs written in various programming languages, such as C, C++, C#, Rust, or Go, to run in PWA browsers and messenger mini-apps.

Why were we interested in WASM?

  1. Security. WebAssembly helps run recognition directly in the browser or messenger without server involvement, minimizing the risk of personal data leaks.

  2. Cross-platform compatibility. Websites and company mini-apps equipped with our solutions can be accessed from a phone, tablet, or computer.

The WebAssembly technology was first introduced in 2015 by leading developers from Mozilla, Google, Microsoft, and Apple, and its full support in major browsers appeared in 2017.

At the same time, Smart Engines scientists began conducting the first experiments with WASM, and by 2021, we had created the world's first generation of document recognition technologies in the browser.
