How Nvidia Lost Its Founder

On November 12, 2026, Nvidia officially announced Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO and president of the company. He also left the board of directors. The interim CEO became CFO Colette Kress, who has been with Nvidia since 2013. In the seven trading sessions following Jensen Huang's resignation, Nvidia's stock fell by almost 28%. The company's market capitalization decreased by approximately $1.16 trillion.

The reason for the personnel changes was a scandal surrounding Huang's personal relationships. Reports emerged in the public domain about two extramarital affairs, one of which linked the former head of Nvidia with Alice Huang, the head of the Singaporean company Megaspeed International.

The latter company found itself at the center of a US government investigation due to suspicions of possible circumvention of export restrictions when supplying AI chips to China. US intelligence is also examining her possible ties to Chinese special services. However, there is no publicly confirmed conclusion about espionage yet. The situation was complicated by reports of Huang's second affair with an Nvidia employee who held a management position. On November 17, Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones stated that the board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and did not plan to return him to the CEO position.

Why Investors Started Selling Nvidia

First, the risk of dependence on a specific leader. Huang had led Nvidia for over 30 years. He not only represented the company publicly but was also directly involved in shaping its technological and commercial strategy.

Nvidia has built a complex ecosystem around GPUs, CUDA, cloud providers, model developers, and server hardware manufacturers. Therefore, investors assess not only the new CEO's ability to manage finances but also how seamlessly the company can continue to implement its existing strategy.

China and export restrictions also had an impact. The Megaspeed incident once again drew the attention of US authorities to the supply of advanced AI chips to China.

This is a strategically important issue for Nvidia. The company is interested in maintaining access to the largest technology markets; however, US export restrictions create additional risks for its international business. As a result, not only individual commercial relationships but also the possibility of further sales expansion in China are in question.

The SEC investigation also came out at a relevant time. On November 20, Reuters reported that the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is examining potential issues in Nvidia's accounting practices. Specifically, attention was drawn to two areas:

  • Changes in the methodology for reflecting geographical revenue. The company began to focus on the location of customer headquarters instead of billing addresses, after which Singapore disappeared from the geographical revenue breakdown.

  • Complex financing schemes for AI infrastructure. Nvidia invests in individual market participants and supports projects that use its own chips. This raises questions about the actual quality of revenue and the financial risks of such transactions.
