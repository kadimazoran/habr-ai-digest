According to Bloomberg, OpenAI has supported the US Congress's initiative to restrict the supply of AI chips and related manufacturing equipment to China. The company informed lawmakers of its position in the same week that Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO of Nvidia.

Huang had opposed stricter export controls for years. Bloomberg estimates his resignation weakened the positions of Nvidia and opponents of the restrictions in Congress. OpenAI, after months of deliberation, has now joined the proponents of the bills.

The US Congress is considering three bills:

AI OVERWATCH Act. This bill will prohibit the supply of the most powerful AI chips, including Nvidia Blackwell, to China for 18 months. Less powerful chips will only be available under license.

MATCH Act. The US will seek to impose restrictions on the sale and servicing of equipment for Chinese chip manufacturing plants from the Netherlands and Japan. ASML lithography machines could be subject to these restrictions.

Chip Security Act. This bill provides for the implementation of mechanisms to verify the location of exported AI chips. For example, through software that exchanges signals with trusted servers. Data center inspections and equipment owner reports are also possible.

All three bills are planned to be passed by the end of the year.
