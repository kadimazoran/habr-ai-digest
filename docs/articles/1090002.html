We are witnessing a new industrial revolution. And we are not just observing it, but are a part of it, having become so long before it all began.

The AI boom, as it is commonly believed, started in 2022 with the release of ChatGPT based on OpenAI's GPT-3.5 model. In just under four years, a new era of technological progress has arrived, and it is clearly just gaining momentum.

Without our activity, no machine learning model, let alone neural networks, could have seen the light of day. Every click, scroll, message, video preview, or item added to a cart is recorded and provides important data about us. And this is not just about the internet. Imagine a new bakery. If no one opens its page on a map one day, it won't get reviews, photos, or ratings. For a recommendation system, such a place simply doesn't exist. Information only appears when someone takes action.

But if we talk about tracking our online actions or your bakery appearing on the map, all this happens with our consent. We are willing to pay with our data for the convenience and opportunities that services provide us.

The issue takes a completely different turn, for example, when a company wants to create its own language model for commercial purposes, but the developer doesn't have enough data yet. What to do? You could offer users to upload PDF files and other documents and hope to accumulate the necessary amount of information, but then, I fear, you will quickly lose to richer or more influential competitors. A company that has money and wants to earn even more simply goes and pays. And it pays a lot.

We will look at how, in recent years, information has turned into a resource similar to oil or iron. Why companies are willing to pay millions of dollars for access to forums and library archives.

What is information in the context of machine learning and artificial intelligence? It is the data on which a model learns to identify specific patterns and regularities. Thanks to this, upon receiving new, previously unseen data, it can predict the most likely answer, generate text, recognize objects in images, or perform other tasks.

That is, any videos, images, audio, software code, comments on forums and social networks – all this is information needed for AI development, and therefore it is incredibly valuable, like coal in the 18th century.

Forums, by their very nature, are one of the best sources of textual information. They appeared a very long time ago, people have solved and continue to solve various issues on them, and therefore they are ideal for collecting and processing text.

One of the first signs that information ceased to be a free resource was Reddit.

In April 2023, Reddit announced that it was closing free access to its API. The company did not want its content to be used uncontrollably for training large language models. Two months later, the API became paid. Many third-party applications were forced to shut down. The most famous Reddit client for iOS, Apollo, ceased to exist. Its developer admitted that API access would have cost approximately $20 million per year.

As early as the beginning of 2024, Reddit entered into an agreement with Google for approximately $60 million per year for licensing its content for artificial intelligence training. And while preparing for its IPO, the company disclosed that the value of such agreements had already reached over $200 million.

In the spring of 2024, OpenAI announced new partnerships almost every week. First, an agreement was reached with Stack Overflow, which had recently laid off about 28% of its employees due to a drop in site traffic caused by the development of AI.

Then, a partnership between OpenAI and Reddit became known. A few days later, information emerged about the company's deal with News Corp, which owns publications such as The Wall Street Journal, The Times, and New York Post, for over $250 million over five years.

Following this, OpenAI announced agreements with Dotdash Meredith – for at least $16 million, with Axel Springer – for approximately $13 million per year for up to three years, and with the Financial Times – for $5 to $10 million per year.

Publisher Wiley entered into deals with two unnamed companies for $21 million and $23 million respectively, and British Informa received an initial sum of $10 million from Microsoft.

Shutterstock, the largest stock photo and video platform, reported that AI licensing had become one of its fastest-growing segments, generating about $104 million in 2023.

Such deals and agreements continue to be made today. But as early as 2024, a group of researchers from Epoch AI published their forecast that publicly available human-created content, including that sold by companies like Reddit, would begin to sharply decline in quality as a source of information for training models starting in 2026 and would be practically exhausted by 2032. This is happening due to the increasing volume of data that the models themselves can process.

Therefore, the following case becomes incredibly important and may mark the next milestone in the information market for AI.

On May 2, 2026, Spirit Airlines, a very popular American low-cost carrier at one time, ceased operations. As reported by the media, lawyers continue to sell off the company's assets. One of them attracted Google's attention.

The company won a bankruptcy auction for corporate data, paying $10 million. This means Google could become the owner of about 100 million emails and about 500 million Microsoft Teams messages, as well as all marketing materials, personnel information, project management documents, financial databases, audits, and presentations. The company assures that it will not receive any customer information or any personal data.

It is likely that the number of such deals will only grow in the near future, meaning Epoch AI's forecasts are close to reality. IT giants are starting to pay not for content and messages in the public domain, but for corporate data that held value, at least when their former owners were still actively working.

In addition, one cannot overlook the experience of Anthropic, the developer of Claude. In July 2026, a federal court in San Francisco finalized a $1.5 billion settlement in a case that began in 2024 following a class-action lawsuit by publishers and numerous independent authors. They accused Anthropic of copyright infringement due to downloading millions of books without permission and storing their digital copies obtained from torrent trackers.

The company did not admit guilt and emphasized that these specific versions of the books were not used for training models. However, the federal court in San Francisco approved the settlement, under which copyright holders will be paid about $3,000 for each work covered by the agreement. This event became the largest copyright infringement recovery in history and effectively set new boundaries for the use of information in artificial intelligence: training data must be legal.

Therefore, the news that surfaced during the legal proceedings is not surprising: since the beginning of 2024, Anthropic has been implementing a project called "Panama," under which it has been actively buying physical copies of books, scanning them for Claude's training, and then destroying them. Over these years, the company has acquired and permanently destroyed millions of used paper editions, turning them into consumables for model training.

In just a few years, a new market has formed. If previously e-books, articles, photos, and forum posts were just content, now they have become assets for which the largest technology companies are willing to pay tens and hundreds of millions of dollars. And this market has reinvented itself just as quickly: it increasingly turns to physical media and is also beginning to be governed by laws and other regulations.

And the more data artificial intelligence requires, the more expensive the quality and origin of information becomes. The circle has closed: what began as our free digital footprint and ordinary online communication has turned into one of the most valuable resources of our time. And perhaps, when leaving another seemingly insignificant comment on a forum today, it's worth remembering: right now, you are creating an asset for which tech giants are willing to spend hundreds of millions.
