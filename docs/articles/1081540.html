Impression of Vibe-Coding

I've built websites, Telegram bots, and dashboards. At first, I felt like Harry Potter learning forbidden magic, but then I cooled down.

Look, I tell Codex: 'Make me a bot that collects posts and analyzes them with AI.' And it actually does it. In a day or an evening, I have a new feature that I can immediately use in my work.

Useful? Fast? Economical?

Obviously.

No need to study Python for half a year. No need to spend three hours googling why some crap won't connect to the database. No need to hire a freelance programmer who would build me the same bot as Codex, but would ask for ten times more money and time.

There are so many pros that at first, you don't even notice the cons.

But they exist.

Firstly, all key development decisions are on me.

I managed people in tech support and web development for ten years. Control, planning, project problems – that's my domain.

But working with people is easier!

As a department head, I didn't have to know all the intricacies. For example, how to connect a payment gateway to a user account or what architecture to choose for a new client's project. I would call the product manager and the programmer, and we would solve it together.

Yes, I can ask Codex for advice.

But it won't tell me: 'Leha, you've come up with something stupid here, let's do it differently?' Well, it might say so, but you have to ask. However, it's in vain. If I slip up with some nonsense in a new request, the AI will again adapt to my whims.

With a person, I can rely on their experience. A machine, on the other hand, is always a game of Russian roulette, especially if I only have a superficial understanding of the task and let the AI choose how to structure the functionality's internals.

You know, it's like with text generation. If a person is a layman and the neural network produces nonsense, they won't even understand where the AI made a mistake, will post the article to their blog, and will be surprised why they received a flood of negative comments.

Secondly, the vibe-coding agent makes mistakes.

It can hastily assemble authorization, caching, a complex task queue, but forget to check if the 'Back' button works. Or I send it to build some unprecedented nonsense, and it's already known that this change will unnecessarily complicate a piece of working logic, but this genius stays silent as if it ate shit and still does as the token lord commanded. And then I fix, fix, fix...

Only limits save me.

It would seem that five hours of Codex downtime hinders work. But in fact, it's the only salvation that lights up a red light in the vibe-coder's head: 'That's enough, Leha, stop messing around.'

All of this overwhelms me.

As a result, I've come to the conclusion that I don't want to vibe-code large projects.

You need to understand development, know at least the basics. It's important to be able to control your project, otherwise the agent will mess it up. A ton of patience doesn't hurt, as I have to fix the bugs.

Making something small in an evening is a joy. For example, a bot for exporting channel history, website edits, a step counter. But I have no desire to build a viral SaaS project for a million users.
