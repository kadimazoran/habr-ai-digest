We recently discussed how AI helps developers with everyday tasks and why it's important to verify its work. Now, let's explore how the requirements for specialists themselves are changing.

What Skills Are Becoming More Important

Fundamental Knowledge and Project Understanding

It seems to me that knowledge of syntax is becoming less valuable – AI will prompt it. But algorithms, networks, and security need to be understood by oneself to avoid making blind decisions.

The ability to break down a task into parts and critical thinking are important: not just noticing that the generated code is bad, but explaining to the AI or a colleague exactly what is wrong with it.

In system design, it is necessary to see the big picture and understand the subject area. Clients and projects have different requirements and constraints. Without this context, it is difficult to assess whether the proposed architecture is suitable for a specific task.

Continuous learning is essential. It is now more important not so much to learn new languages as to master AI tools and deepen one's own expertise.

Task Formulation and Agent Management

When delegating work to an AI agent, it is necessary to clearly formulate the task, specify constraints, and define methods for verifying the result.

A separate skill is agent management: configuration, working with context, and tool selection. The result can depend on this no less than on the choice of the model.

How Developers' Work Will Change

Starting Will Be Easier, But Growth Depends on Independent Practice

I think boilerplate code, simple CRUD operations, basic tests, and migrations will increasingly be handled by AI.

It will be easier for beginners to get started. But growing into an experienced specialist may become more difficult: without independent practice in debugging and design, these skills may weaken.

Therefore, it is important to learn to work with AI while continuing to understand tasks yourself.

In my opinion, developers who can combine their own expertise with the capabilities of new tools will have an advantage.

Agents Will Become More Autonomous

Most likely, agents will perform more and more tasks independently: from analyzing requirements to delivering a complete solution with tests and checks. Moreover, they will work not only in the development environment but also as part of the infrastructure.

Even now, you can experiment with them in CI/CD and the cloud. Further, such scenarios, I think, will become common practice.

But here I see a problem: generating code is becoming easier, but verifying it qualitatively is not.

There are more changes, and they appear more frequently, as agents do not get tired and can work around the clock. Therefore, the focus will shift to solution design, process organization, and quality control.

I wouldn't say that AI replaces a developer. Rather, it allows one person to do more, but it increases the demands on their independence and engineering mindset.
