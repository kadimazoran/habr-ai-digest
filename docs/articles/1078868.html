Is it legal?

Yes.

Here's what ChatGPT itself says:

"Creating multiple accounts in itself is not prohibited by OpenAI's rules. Abuse of the platform is usually considered a violation: automatic registration of a large number of accounts, using bots for mass account creation, spam, fraud, bypassing blocks, as well as other actions aimed at disrupting the service or gaining an unfair advantage."

If a user manually creates an additional account and uses it for regular communication with ChatGPT, there is no direct prohibition in the rules. However, OpenAI reserves the right to impose restrictions and take measures against actions that the company deems an attempt to abuse the service."

Simply put, you have the right to create multiple accounts, and the company has the right to restrict or block your profile if it deems you have violated the platform's terms of use. Neither Russian nor international law is violated by the mere creation of new accounts.

First Method: Using Multiple Gmail Mailboxes

This is the simplest, most reliable, and effective method. OpenAI trusts Google mail; registering a new ChatGPT profile via Gmail is the easiest option.

Surely many of you already have several ChatGPT profiles linked to different Google addresses.

If you use them frequently, you will quickly hit the limits. The solution is to register new email addresses. Google often requires linking to a phone number, and here we hit the first hurdle – it's difficult to get new phone numbers for each email.

This is where temporary mail comes in handy.

Second Method: Temporary Mailboxes

There are many such services now; they work quickly and without failures. Here, for example, is TempMail: as soon as you visit the site, a unique email is automatically generated for you. It can be copied with one click (there's a special button).

Further steps:

1. Go to chatgpt.com

2. Click "Sign up"

3. Paste the temporary email address

4. Go to temp-mail.org

5. Check "Inbox"

6. Copy the confirmation code from there and complete the ChatGPT registration

The entire operation takes about a minute. It can be repeated as soon as you hit the chat limit.

Two problems arise here.

Firstly, if you frequently create new email addresses on the same hosting, ChatGPT will detect this and put you in a soft ban. I didn't receive any warnings or messages about the profile being blocked, but confirmation codes for registration simply stopped arriving at the temporary email address.

Secondly, some temporary email addresses are apparently already on a blacklist, and you cannot register with them.

In other words, temporary mail is a working but unreliable method. After several attempts and address changes, it might work, or it might not.

Third Method: Gmail Aliases

If temporary mail stops working, you can try registering with a Gmail alias.

A Gmail alias (or pseudonym) is a way to create a new email address that is linked to your existing mailbox. All emails sent to this additional address will be delivered to your main mailbox.

How it works

Gmail allows you to add a plus sign (+) and any word after your username in the address. Services (like ChatGPT) will see this address as completely unique and new, but for Google, it's still your main inbox.

Example

Let's say your main address is petrov@gmail.com.

You can use the address petrov+1@gmail.com to register with ChatGPT.

The system will consider this a completely new user.

At the same time, the confirmation code email will arrive directly in your main inbox petrov@gmail.com.

I'm not sure if this will work indefinitely, but I managed to register three aliases this way and continue working.

Conclusion

In my opinion, OpenAI treats such workarounds with mild disapproval – as a minor, unavoidable evil.

If you start outright abusing it – registering hundreds of addresses per day, generating thousands of images – you will likely be banned, and no additional email addresses will fix it. The company is willing to turn a blind eye to a few extra chats per day.
