California Attorney General Rob Bonta has announced the launch of an investigation into Nvidia to determine if China has been receiving advanced processors or confidential data from the American company, writes Reuters.

The investigation is linked to reports about the close relationship between former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, who is suspected of espionage. By mid-November, Nvidia had dismissed Huang.

"Nvidia's actions and those of its former CEO raise serious concerns," Bonta stated.

The Attorney General's office also intends to ascertain whether the company misled investors and the public regarding risks. Nvidia's global headquarters are located in Santa Clara, California.

Previously, some members of the US Congress urged the US Department of Commerce to investigate whether Megaspeed, a company headed by Alice Huang, had been transferring Nvidia chips to China. Since the publication of the New York Times article about the investigation into Huang late last month, Nvidia's stock has plummeted by 22%.

US intelligence agencies are attempting to establish the extent of Alice Huang's ties to China's Ministry of State Security and to determine what information she may have passed to Beijing. China denies accusations of an espionage operation against Huang.

Nvidia Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones stated that the company does not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO. Media reports indicate that the board is considering Nvidia Vice President Ian Buck, Arm CEO Rene Haas, AMD CEO Lisa Su, and Apple's Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering Johny Srouji as candidates for the position. The company is currently temporarily led by Vice President and CFO Colette Kress.
