On November 17, OpenAI released two GPT-6.2 models simultaneously. Astra was developed for demanding professional work in science, law, and finance. Sol was built to be fast and versatile, for everyday tasks.

Both are now operational in ChatGPT Work and Codex on Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu plans, and the API is open to developers. The models have not been added to the regular ChatGPT chat. More details below the fold.

What's New

The update primarily affects price and quality. The API for Astra has become 20% cheaper compared to GPT-6 Astra, while Sol remains at the price of GPT-6.1 Sol.

In the Agents’ Last Exam test, which evaluates multi-step workflows, Astra scores 5.2 points higher than GPT-6 Astra and uses less than a third of its budget. It outperforms Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 by 5.5 points at approximately 5% of its cost. Only Google's Gemini 4 Carbon can compete with it here.

In the Harvey LAB-AA legal test, which analyzes contract clauses, for example, Astra has more than double the success rate of Fable 5.2, at one-sixth the cost.

Interestingly, the model was trained on the professional habits of lawyers, financiers, and accountants. In presentations, it adopts the layout, colors, and fonts from a template and adjusts font sizes precisely (though this is not particularly surprising). The slide title should convey the main idea, and the body text should focus on key details. Computer control has also been improved. In a demo, Astra assembled a 3D car model, wrote a physics solver in Python, and animated the result in Blender.

Sol lags behind Astra in FrontierCode 2.0 by only 0.5 points, and costs approximately five times less. The test checks if code edits meet the standards of real projects.

Independent Test Results

The Artificial Analysis platform's intelligence index combines nine tests on knowledge, programming, science, and general erudition. Astra scored 62 points, five more than GPT-6 Astra, securing second place. Only Claude Fable 5.2 is ahead. Sol received 57 points, two more than its predecessor, matching GPT-6 Astra at approximately 15% of its task cost.

The Pareto frontier is the line where no model is simultaneously cheaper and better.

In science, Astra confirms the company's claims. It ranks first in Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and second after Fable 5.2 in the physics test CritPt v2, the factual knowledge test AA-Omniscience, and HLE-Diamond. It also leads in 3D design and robotics. In robotics, it has 79 points compared to Sol's 78 and Fable 5.2's 75.

The Vals AI platform calculates costs per task. A task costs $3.18 for Sol, $10.68 for Astra, and $21.28 for GPT-6 Astra. Astra scored 2.6 points higher than its predecessor for half its price. In the Vals index, Astra is seventh, Sol is ninth, with a 1.6-point gap. Astra uses an average of 91,000 output tokens per task, the lowest among the top ten leaders.

The research organization Epoch AI tested Astra before its release. In its ECI index, the model scored 174 points, matching Fable 5.2. In FrontierMath Erdős, a set of unsolved mathematical problems verified in the Lean system, Astra solved 7 out of 68. The previous record was 3 out of 68 by GPT-6.1 Sol. This is approximately one in ten, and for one in seven problems, solutions were previously unknown.

Areas of Weakness

There is almost no progress in programming. In the Coding Agent Index, Astra remains at the GPT-6 Astra level, while Sol dropped three points relative to GPT-6.1 Sol. This is due to a 14-point drop in SWE-Atlas-QnA-AA, where the model more frequently misses important facts in its answers.

Hallucinations are also noticeable. In the AA-Omniscience test, which measures how often a model guesses instead of admitting ignorance, Astra errs in 59% of cases, and Sol in 55%. The questions are unusually difficult, and these figures are lower in everyday use. In real-world tasks evaluated by Epoch AI, Astra was outperformed by Fable 5.2 and showed little improvement over GPT-6 Astra.

Security Considerations

Both models have achieved Critical readiness in cybersecurity according to the Preparedness Framework, similar to GPT-6 Astra. In the internal ExploitBench test, Astra turns known browser vulnerabilities into working exploits in over 50% of cases. These results were obtained with protections disabled. OpenAI is now tightening filters for risky requests, which will also limit legitimate vulnerability research. The company plans to provide access to the models for verified researchers through OpenAI Daybreak.

How to Connect

GPT-6.2 cannot be run on your own servers; the model is only available via API. For developers, the quickest route is through the Selectel AI router.

Open the control panel, create an API key for the AI router, and copy the model alias [alias GPT-6.2 Sol] or [alias GPT-6.2 Astra]. A request via curl looks like this.

curl [base URL of AI router]/chat/completions 
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SELECTEL_API_KEY" 
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" 
  -d '{ 
    "model": "[alias GPT-6.2 Sol]", 
    "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "Hello! Tell me what you can do."}] 
  }'

The API is compatible with OpenAI, so you only need to change the base URL in your existing client. For Astra, replace the alias in the model field.

from openai import OpenAI
client = OpenAI(
    base_url="[base URL of AI router]",
    api_key="SELECTEL_API_KEY",
)
response = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="[alias GPT-6.2 Sol]",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Hello! Tell me what you can do."}]
)
print(response.choices[0].message.content)
