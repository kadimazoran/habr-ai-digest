How much does a typo in keyboard layout really cost, in LLM tokens?

I wrote Zyabra - a local layout switcher: I typed "ghbdtn" instead of "privet", and the program itself deletes and rewrites it correctly. The key thing is that it's completely local - it doesn't write what's typed to disk, doesn't keep a keystroke log, doesn't touch the clipboard, and doesn't open the network at all. It only keeps the current unfinished word and the last replacement in memory, nothing more. I use it every day. Hold on. What if this isn't the first message in the chat?

Then I realized: the test didn't include a system prompt or chat history. But in reality, a message is almost never the first and only one - usually, there's already some conversation in Russian going on. This changes things: without a single hint of the language, the model solves a pure puzzle from scratch, while with context, it has something to latch onto.

With context, it's 6-7 times cheaper and faster than without it, and with almost no failures. The live chat history worked even better than an explicit system prompt.

So how much does Zyabra really save?

An honest, not inflated conclusion: the realistic cost of one accidentally sent message in the wrong layout is not a catastrophic 2000+ tokens and two and a half minutes, but rather 350-500 tokens and 25-30 seconds, when there is already Russian context in the chat. But even this is 4-7 times more expensive than if the message had come through clean immediately (70-90 tokens, 5-7 seconds).

With a couple of dozen such typos in an evening, it adds up not fatally, but quite noticeably - several thousand extra tokens and a couple of extra minutes of waiting, plus the risk that the model won't decipher the short phrase and will just ask again. Zyabra doesn't work miracles for your wallet, but it's not a useless toy either: it eliminates a specific, measurable source of spending - and precisely where it's not visible until you measure it.
