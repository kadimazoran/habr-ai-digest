What You Uploaded

A supply contract between two LLCs might seem like a document without personal data. This is not the case. It contains the full name and position of the signatory, often the full name of the person acting under a power of attorney. For an individual entrepreneur (IE), it includes passport details, registration address, and INN, which Roskomnadzor considers personal data of an individual. The contact details may include the manager's phone number and email. Delivery notes and acceptance certificates list drivers and couriers. A standard transportation contract I reviewed last week contained data for four individuals.

The fact that a director's name is in the Unified State Register of Legal Entities (EGRUL) and is publicly accessible does not negate its status as personal data. This is the regulator's position, and it is consistent: the source's openness does not make the data publicly available in the sense of the law.

Therefore, by pasting a contract into a chat, you are processing the personal data of at least two to four individuals. The question then becomes: on what basis and to where?

Why This Is Not a Data Leak

A data leak, in the legal sense (Parts 12-18), refers to the unlawful transfer of data of thousands of individuals, starting from one thousand subjects for the first tier. One contract, or even a hundred contracts in a month, does not reach this threshold. Anyone who frightens you with "fifteen million for ChatGPT" has either not read the article or is trying to sell you something.

What is actually applicable is less dramatic and more likely.

Part 1. You are entitled to process the signatory's data without their consent for the purpose of contract execution, as per point 5 of Part 1 of Article 6 of the law. However, "checking a contract through a third-party service" is a different purpose. Processing incompatible with the purpose of collection is precisely the wording of Part 1. For a legal entity, this means 150,000-300,000 rubles.

Part 10. Notification to Roskomnadzor that you are processing personal data. Since September 2022, there are almost no exceptions for small businesses, and in my experience talking with small companies, almost none are registered. This incurs an additional 100,000-300,000 rubles and is entirely unrelated to neural networks – it will simply surface along with the neural network issue.

Why This Is a Cross-Border Transfer

Article 12 of Federal Law No. 152. Cross-border transfer is any transfer of personal data to the territory of another state to a foreign person. It is sufficient to provide access; physically "sending a file" is not required. Personal data lawyers have long considered Google Analytics, Notion, Slack, AWS – any foreign service where data is sent – as cross-border transfers (analysis from B-152, a paraphrase, not the law itself). ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek are no different in this regard.

Since March 1, 2023, before such a transfer can occur, a separate notification must be submitted to Roskomnadzor – not the one about commencing processing, but a second one, about the intention to transfer abroad. For countries on the list with "adequate protection" – Belarus, Kazakhstan, Armenia, China, and others on the regulator's list – transfer can occur immediately after submission. The USA and the European Union are not on this list. For them, you must wait ten working days, during which Roskomnadzor has the right to prohibit or restrict the transfer.

There is no separate offense for cross-border transfer without notification in Article 13.11; it is qualified under the same Part 1. However, there is a second problem that notification does not resolve: according to the law, data processing can only be entrusted to another person under a processing agreement. Your LLC has no such agreement with OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, and never will – you have accepted a user agreement written for a different jurisdiction.

Regarding criminal liability – to close the topic immediately. Article 272.1 of the Criminal Code, effective December 11, 2024, Part 4 – cross-border transfer – carries up to eight years imprisonment and a fine of up to two million rubles. Half of the articles online scare people with this, especially employees. However: the elements of the article concern information "obtained through unauthorized access." This applies to database traffickers. A manager who pastes a contract received for work into a chat does not fall under this. Frightening people with eight years for using ChatGPT is dishonest.

There is also no ban on foreign neural networks. The first version of the AI bill included the concept of "cross-border artificial intelligence technologies" with the possibility of restricting them – this was removed from the law 243-FZ adopted on July 26, 2026. It contained nothing about personal data. Roskomnadzor reported on February 16, 2026, that no decisions to restrict ChatGPT had been received by the agency. The responsibility is standard, under 152-FZ, and it does not make it any easier.

What Is Actually Happening in the Courts

Here, I have two pieces of news for you, and neither is what you expect.

First: Million-ruble fines have not yet bankrupted anyone. According to Roskomnadzor, in 2025, there were 118 leaks and 52 million records – compared to 135 leaks and 710 million records the previous year. From January 1, 2025, to August 2026, there were 52 investigations, 40 protocols, and fines totaling 2.6 million rubles. The Deputy Head of the agency, Milos Wagner, also stated that no repeat leaks had been identified during this period – meaning turnover-based fines have not yet been applied.

The first cases under the new "million-ruble" parts that reached the courts ended with warnings. Ukids online school, a data leak of over one hundred thousand individuals – the Moscow Arbitration Court issued a warning on March 5, 2026, instead of a 10-15 million ruble fine: it was a micro-enterprise. "PKR Analytica," 70,000 subjects, hacker attack – the St. Petersburg Arbitration Court on March 10, 2026, issued a warning: a single incident, no harm caused, timely notification. Russian Railways, a database of 17 million employees in the public domain – the appeal on June 10, 2026, completely overturned the fine: Roskomnadzor failed to prove guilt. This is a paraphrase from IT Channel News, citing B-152 lawyers; the decisions themselves are available in the arbitration case database.

So, money is collected not under the high-profile parts, but under Parts 1 and 10 – hundreds of thousands, not millions. Exactly those applicable to neural networks.

Second news: I haven't found a single court case under 152-FZ due to neural networks. Not because everyone is law-abiding. Because it's not visible from the outside. A contract pasted into a chat leaves no trace with the counterparty or Roskomnadzor – until it surfaces elsewhere.

However, I did find one case, and it's recent. On July 27, 2026, the Babushkinsky District Court of Moscow upheld the dismissal of a sales department head at an engineering company – salary 800,000 rubles, six months in the position. She had been uploading reports on deals, money receipts, and financial data by department into DeepSeek. The court's wording: "uploading information constituting commercial secrets into the DeepSeek artificial intelligence system is a disclosure of such information." The claim for a "golden parachute" of five million rubles was denied.

This is a labor law case, not about personal data. But pay attention to the word "disclosure." The court equated sending data to a neural network with transferring it to a third party. Not with "using a tool." With transfer. This means the court already treats the neural network as a counterparty – before we do.

OpenAI vs. New York Times. On May 13, 2025, a federal court ordered OpenAI to retain all ChatGPT logs, including those users deleted. Not because OpenAI wanted to – but because the court, in a separate lawsuit, ruled they were evidence. The obligation was lifted only on October 9. For five months, the "delete chat" button did nothing. Then the court ordered the transfer of 20 million anonymized chats to the plaintiffs. Your data deletion policy is worth exactly as much as the nearest court ruling in a foreign country.

ChatGPT a month earlier – the same story, about 4,500 chats in search results, the feature was removed, calling it a "short-lived experiment."

And regarding default settings: in the free and paid personal versions of ChatGPT, conversations are used for training unless you disable "Improve the model for everyone." Deleted chats are erased within 30 days – "unless the law requires retention." See the paragraph about the New York Times.

One trap for those who will be retelling: Italy's fine of 15 million euros against OpenAI for training on personal data, which is cited everywhere, was overturned by the Rome Court of Appeal on March 19, 2026. It is no longer a valid example.

What This Means

We treat neural networks as assistants: friendly, always available, sitting on our computers. None of this is true. They are on foreign servers, in a foreign jurisdiction, operate under an agreement we haven't read, and store what we input for as long as a foreign court dictates. This describes a counterparty. Specifically, one with whom you have no contract.

Once you accept this, everything else becomes standard counterparty management.

Do not give away unnecessary information. Contracts are not checked for the director's name and INN – these can be redacted before sending. However, "redacted" means truly redacted: replacing Ivanov with "Client_1" and keeping a correspondence table on your disk does not anonymize personal data if the link can be restored. This is also the regulator's position, not mine.

Do not give away the whole document. Five pages of terms regarding deadlines, penalties, and acceptance do not contain personal information. Contact details, signatures, an IE's passport – these do. Separating them takes a minute.

Formalize paperwork. If a foreign service is necessary – two notifications to Roskomnadzor, one about commencing processing and another about cross-border transfer, and a ten-working-day waiting period. And disable training in the settings. This does not make the transfer flawless – there is still no processing agreement – but it moves you from the "didn't know" category to the "did what we could" category.

Keep data within your own infrastructure or with a Russian provider under contract. Open models run on standard hardware, and Russian clouds provide processing agreements. This is not about ideology; it's the only way to have an answer to the question "Where did the document go?"

And implement a commercial secrets regime on paper. The employer won the DeepSeek case because they had a formal regime in place, and the employee had signed an acknowledgment. Without it, the court would have sided with the employee – using a neural network is not a violation in itself.

None of this requires banning neural networks. It requires ceasing to consider them part of your computer. A tool is something that has no interests of its own. A neural network has them: your data is its raw material, and your chats are its evidence in a foreign court. You handle a counterparty with its own interests cautiously. Not because it's malicious. Because it's not yours.
