Imagine a common situation: a forwarder accepts goods at a warehouse, signs an invoice, and photographs the document with a corporate smartphone. Within seconds, the details should appear in the accounting system – without manual data entry, an office scanner, or waiting for the paper to reach accounting.

But there's a condition: the photo must not be sent to an OCR provider, a third-party processing center, or a manual verification operator. Ideally, the image never leaves the mobile device – only the extracted and verified data enters the corporate system.

Is it possible to recognize a multi-page UРD with a complex table on a smartphone, correct perspective, read small print and handwritten notes – all without connecting to external infrastructure? The short answer is yes. However, for a secure primary document recognition system, it's not enough to write "works locally" in a presentation. Primary documents went mobile, manual entry hasn't yet

A paper document doesn't appear where the accountant's computer is located. Invoices are signed at the warehouse, acts at the site, transport documents during cargo handover, and bills are received during meetings with a counterparty. If the entry point remains an office scanner, there's a delay between the economic operation and its reflection in the accounting system.

With a large volume of documents, this delay turns into working hours. The accountant needs to accept the document, determine its type, transfer details and nomenclature, check sums and VAT, correct typos, and then return disputed items to the responsible department. A complex invoice with hundreds of lines can take from tens of minutes to several hours.

The cost of these hours grows with staffing needs. In November 2025, in Moscow alone, employers were looking for over 7 thousand accountants, and Russian recruitment agencies note an approximate 12% increase in vacancies for accountants and auditors. In some regions, demand for administrative and managerial staff, including accountants, increased several times in early 2025 several times.
