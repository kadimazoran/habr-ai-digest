Introduction

Today, AI assistants allow for significantly faster implementation of new software requirements. This is especially noticeable at the level of individual tasks and small projects. However, while direct implementation speeds up several times, other processes—architectural design, requirement negotiation, and verification—do not always speed up proportionally.

This results in an imbalance. The accumulated complexity and uncontrolled system behavior sometimes begin to create problems faster than developers can detect and fix them.

Imagine an airplane equipped with a significantly more powerful engine. The plane flew well before; its design was reliable and handled its tasks effectively. But it was designed for different speeds. Now, as it accelerates, loads increase, individual structural elements can no longer withstand them, and controllability issues arise. The pilot tries to keep the plane steady, but it's no longer about their skill. The speed itself creates conditions where previous stability methods fail. At further acceleration, the plane might simply disintegrate.

We are observing something similar in AI-assisted software development today. We have a much more powerful engine but continue to use the old approaches to design and control. At some point, the project may begin to rapidly and unexpectedly lose controllability and literally fall apart.

This is our sound barrier.

But the sound barrier proved surmountable. To achieve this, aviation engineers had to rethink aircraft design approaches, considering the loads and flight characteristics at new speeds. A similar task now faces us: we need to develop software design and control methods to leverage new acceleration capabilities without losing the stability of the systems being created.

In this article, we will examine several problems in software development and verification under the conditions of using AI assistants. These problems are not new in themselves; they existed long before the advent of modern AI models. However, the acceleration of development has made their manifestations more noticeable and their consequences potentially more serious.

The main question we will try to answer is: what needs to change in our software development approaches so that the acceleration provided by AI models does not lead to a loss of control over the systems being created?
