Not exactly my full twin, but, let's say, the working side – the part about work. But you can already talk to it, almost like me.

It contains almost everything I've written over 10 years about automation, management, career, books, etc. There's almost no fiction there – it's too hard to extract meaning and benefit from it. There's a lot of text – at the time of publication, 3.4 million characters, which is about 8 books (if we count 400,000 per book).

The idea came by chance. I run a book blog, and I decided to automate working with it somehow, so that people wouldn't have to read the whole wall of text when they have a specific goal – for example, to compile a selection for themselves or choose books on a certain topic. There's a table of contents, of course, but it's also long.

That's when the idea came – to put the entire blog into a small file that the reader could feed to their favorite neural network, and it would help make a selection, choose something, etc.

The thought then developed further – why only a book blog? Why not put everything that can help the reader at work into the file? It's objectively clear that no one in their right mind will re-read everything an author has written on a specific topic, especially if they've written a lot.

AI will definitely cope better than any other tools with the task of finding something useful for a specific situation in a large amount of information. I tried to make the neural network search for information from a specific author and respond only in the context of their knowledge – it didn't work, especially when publications are scattered across different sources.

The solution turned out to be simple – spend a couple of days and compile the texts into a file. DeepSeek came up with some structure, chose markdown (said that AI would understand it better), and I spent a couple of days filling this file. Qwen looked at the structure and said that everything needed to be redone, but I won't for now.

I want to understand if there is any point in this (besides the initial goal – helping readers of the book blog). That's why I'm publishing it – I hope you'll find 5-10 minutes, download the file, upload it to some neural network, and try to talk to the digital me. And then write your impressions and opinions in the comments.

I wanted to upload the file directly to Habr, but now you can only upload pictures here. A link to the cloud would look murky, so I chose GitHub – I put it in one of the public repos, it can be downloaded even without a GitHub account.

And please write what you think about this topic. And about your experience communicating with the AI about the file.
