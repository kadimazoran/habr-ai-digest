How Language Models Actually Verify Contractors

In manual API tests with web search capabilities using Gemini 3 Flash and gpt-5-mini (on 31.10.2026 and 01.11.2026), the agents followed a consistent pattern:

  1. First, the model searches for the company name along with its city and Latin transliteration.

  2. It extracts the 9-digit tax identification number (UNP) from the page and then sends a separate query strictly using these digits.

  3. It verifies if the legal entity actually exists and if its services match what is stated on the website.

A company can claim twenty years of experience and any pricing on its own domain, but for a search model, such text remains self-promotion. When there is no independent confirmation via UNP in the search results, AI either dismisses the website or pulls data from old directories, which may still list outdated phone numbers and general activity codes like "wholesale trade of a wide range of goods.".

What Works Instead of Redoing Your Website: A Digital Company Passport

For businesses to avoid being excluded from recommendations in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Alice, and Google, optimizing their own landing page is not enough. The first step for a company is to have an external digital company passport — an open, machine-readable listing in a neutral registry that meets four technical requirements:

  1. Unified link to state registries (UNP or INN). The status of an active legal entity, registration date, and region are pulled from official databases without manual alteration.

  2. Applied services and geography instead of general OKED codes. Instead of vague descriptions, the listings specify concrete services, price ranges, and answers to common customer questions.

  3. Machine-readable output without wrappers. The same listing is provided in server-side HTML, clean .md and .json formats, as well as in Schema.org markup with the taxID field.

  4. Two-way verification of the website link. A simple request from a director to a directory is insufficient; it doesn't prove to the knowledge graph that the domain belongs to that specific legal entity. Only a reciprocal confirmation from the website (a link to the passport or a sameAs property in the Organization markup) reliably closes the loop.

Following the consolidation of Google AI Overviews and AI Mode (19.05.2026) and with the development of the WebMCP specification at the W3C, browser agents are increasingly working directly with structured data rather than parsing visual layouts. In such conditions, a verified digital company passport in an external industry catalog becomes a fundamental requirement for a business to even appear in the shortlists of AI-generated results.
