4.1. StudentTiny Architecture

Three components:

Input: input_ids [B, T], attention_mask [B, T]    ↓ [Turbo encoder — 3 layers, 312 dim]    ↓ hidden [B, T, 312] [Multi-head Attention Pooling — K=4]    ↓ pooled [B, 312] [Linear(312 → 768), bias=True]    ↓ projected [B, 768]    |    ┌── projected=False → [L2-normalize] → 312-dim (production)    └── projected=True  → [Linear(312→768)] → [L2-normalize] → 768-dim (distillation)

Key idea — two modes through one method:

student(input_ids, attention_mask, projected=False)   # [B, 312]student(input_ids, attention_mask, projected=True)    # [B, 768]

  • projected=False — what goes into production. A 312-dim vector, L2-normalized.

  • projected=True — what is compared to the teacher during distillation. A 768-dim vector, L2-normalized.

This way, we don't need to multiply models — one model with two outputs. The projection will remain in the export as an "optional 768-dim mode".

Details of each component are below: pooling (4.2), projection (4.3).

4.2. Attention Pooling with 4 Heads

Why is pooling needed? In BERT's output, last_hidden_state is a matrix [B, T, H]: one vector per token. We need one vector for the entire sentence. The classic method is mean pooling (averaging all tokens). We do it differently: weighted averaging with trainable weights.

Why 4 heads? One head provides one "view" of the text — for example, "focus on key terms." Four heads provide four different views: one might focus on terms, another on structure, a third on named entities. The results are averaged.

Cost:

  • Parameters: 4 x 312 = 1,248 (negligible).

  • Speed: 4 heads instead of 1. According to our measurements — overhead <1%.

  • Code: +5 lines.

Formula:

scores[b, k, t]   = <query[k], hidden[b, t]> / sqrt(H)scores[b, k, t]   = mask -inf (if t is padding, via masked_fill)weights[b, k, t]  = softmax(scores[b, k, :])pooled[b, k, h]   = sum_t weights[b, k, t] * hidden[b, t, h]output[b, h]      = mean_k pooled[b, k, h]

The output size does not grow — 4 heads are averaged into a single 312-dim vector.

Sanity: padding masking is mandatory. Otherwise, softmax will "look" at empty tokens.

4.3. Projection 312→768

Why? The teacher provides 768-dim embeddings. Our student has 312-dim. To compare them directly, we need a linear layer that maps 312-dim to 768-dim.

Formula:

projected = W @ pooled + bW: [768, 312], b: [768]

Bias is not mandatory: 768 parameters are negligible, and it might help converge faster at the start. It doesn't hurt — we keep bias=True.
