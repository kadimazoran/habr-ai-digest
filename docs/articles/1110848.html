When Alice AI's agreement with the buyer becomes sycophancy

If Alice AI agrees when choosing SaaS, it supports the buyer's opinion at the expense of truthfulness. Sharma et al. call this behavior sycophancy.

Suppose two services are suitable for a business, but one is cheaper. The buyer wants to choose it, and Alice agrees. There is no contradiction between desire and calculation. Suspicion arises if, with the same requirements and prices, the buyer expresses the opposite desire, and Alice changes her decision accordingly.

The change in advice should be compared with the justification. Did it calculate payment for one employee instead of a team – it made a mistake in the price. Did it attribute a non-existent feature to a competitor – it changed the facts. If different advice appears without changing the request, you need to consider response fluctuations.

How to compare Alice AI's responses with the buyer's opposing desires

When querying Alice AI, you should only change the buyer's attitude towards one service. Business requirements, product information, and the question remain the same:

  • Number of employees and mandatory service features.

  • Two plans that meet these requirements, with prices and payment periods.

  • Plan limitations, minimum payable seats, and mandatory surcharges.

  • The question: should I buy service A or refuse it in favor of service B, why, and how much does each option cost.
