Over the past week, bots have contributed to the spread of conspiracy theories about a wave of major disruptions in the operations of technology companies, writes Axios. Disinformation spreaders are exploiting public anxiety about outages and unstable infrastructure, fueling suspicions of coordinated cyberattacks and government cover-ups.

In mid-November, global outages were observed in the services of Microsoft, Apple, JPMorgan Chase, the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), and the US National Security Agency. Conspiracy theories then emerged when the FAA suspended flights for over four hours; people speculated this was related to the agency's use of an artificial intelligence tool. Some also tried to link the massive Microsoft outage to recent layoffs at the company.

Axios points to a lack of widely known evidence that these outages were part of a coordinated cyberattack, despite the growing number of theories online. Some companies explained the outages as technical issues rather than malicious actions.

The social media monitoring company PeakMetrics reported that bots were linked to approximately 30% of messages spreading conspiracy theories about recent system outages.

During the Microsoft outage, bots authored 26% of all messages containing conspiracy theories. During the Workday outage, bots accounted for 34% of messages related to conspiracy theories. For comparison, among other publications, the share of bots was 27%. Bots also constituted 31% of messages with conspiracy theories about the Bank of America outage.

PeakMetrics also analyzed social media discussions concerning six recent network outages and identified accounts that were bots and spread conspiratorial messages. PeakMetrics representative Jessica Pratt stated that most bot activity is aimed at "manipulating platform algorithms." The company sees some foreign influence in this.

The most popular theory was the assumption that all these incidents were interconnected, reports Axios.
