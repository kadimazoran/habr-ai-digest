Imagine a metal depot on the outskirts of a regional center. It's been operating since the nineties, all procurement officers in the city know the manager's phone number, and major clients call the director directly. The website was made about ten years ago: a logo, a photo of the warehouse with a crane, an Excel price list from the year before last, and the inscription "prices to be confirmed by phone." When offered to update the site, let alone "do something with AI," the answer is always the same: Why? Everyone already knows us.

For a long time, this was a reasonable position. Clients came by recommendation, procurement officers moved from one construction site to another with their address books, and the website served as a business card for those who were already going to call. Now, the client has an intermediary who decides whose phone numbers to show them. This intermediary is a neural network. And it might know nothing about the metal depot that "everyone knows."

What has changed in a year

A few figures for 2026:

  • According to VTsIOM, 78% of internet users in Russia have used neural networks in the past year, and 58% do so every week. The most common task: finding information;

  • Yandex, in its Q2 report, stated that Alice AI directly answers 42% of search queries, and its answers below the search bar are seen by 49.5 million people per month;

  • According to a Faves Communications survey, 43% of users are usually satisfied with a neural network's answer and do not proceed to websites;

  • Gartner reports that B2B buyers compare suppliers and prepare requests for proposals using AI, and 69% go to a seller primarily to verify what the AI has already told them.

The last figure is the most important for B2B. A client comes to you with a list of three suppliers and approximate prices. If you are not on this list, you simply won't know about the client.

How people currently search for where to buy metal

Regular clients will continue to call the manager as they always have. The question is about new ones. And new clients for a metal depot appear constantly:

  • a contractor from another region starts a new project and knows no one here;

  • the procurement officer at a factory or construction company has changed, and the old address book went with the previous procurement officer;

  • a private individual is building a canopy or fence and doesn't know the difference between a profile pipe and an angle bar.

Previously, they would all open a search engine, scroll through ten links, and call three places. Now, the query often looks like a question to a person: "where to buy rebar 12 mm with cutting and delivery in Yaroslavl and how much does a ton cost." The neural network answers briefly: three to five metal depots, approximate prices, delivery terms, links to sources. Then the person calls those they were recommended.

If you are not in the answer, the neural network hasn't banned you or deemed you a bad supplier. It simply has nothing to quote. Twenty years of reputation, living in people's minds and in phone conversations, do not exist for it.

Where does the neural network get its answers from?

A neural network doesn't know the prices of rebar in your city itself. To answer, it searches the internet and retells what it finds. From this, two conclusions can be drawn.

First: you need a website that can be found through regular search. The agency "Ashmanov and Partners" studied Alice's answers to commercial queries, including construction-related ones. The share of websites not from the top-10 search results in these answers has fallen from about 40% to 10% in six months. Simply put, Alice primarily quotes websites that are already ranked high in regular search results. If you don't have a website or it's on the third page, you won't be in the answer either.

Second: the website must have something to quote. The neural network compiles its answers from text. A price list as an image, "prices on request," and a single "About Us" page only give it the name and phone number. But the client asked about the diameter, steel grade, price per ton, cutting, and delivery. The one who has this information written down will be quoted.

In addition to websites, neural networks draw data from listings on Yandex Maps and 2GIS, from reviews, directories, and aggregators. If the metal depot itself doesn't write anything about itself, the neural network will tell about it what it found elsewhere: an old address, a price from three years ago, a single angry review from a forum.

What should be on a supplier's website

This is not about design or expensive animation. For a neural network, a website serves as a data source, and it should contain answers to the same questions that a client asks a manager over the phone.

What is asked

What to write on the website

What is in stock

Assortment: rebar, pipe, profile pipe, sheet, angle bar, channel; sizes, steel grades, lengths

How much does it cost

Price per ton and per meter, or at least a "from" price, price list update date, volume discounts

Will you deliver to me

Delivery zones and cost, types of trucks, availability of a crane truck, minimum order quantity

What else can you do

Cutting to size, bending, welding, galvanizing; lead times and cost

How quickly

Warehouse addresses, operating hours, same-day dispatch or not

Can I trust you

Certificates and GOST standards, which factories you work with, major clients, reviews

Do you work with us

For whom: individuals, contractors, factories; cash and non-cash payments, contracts, deferred payment

Separately, about questions that are asked not to a manager, but directly to a neural network: "how much does a meter of 12 mm rebar weigh," "what profile pipe to use for a canopy," "what is the difference between rebar A500S and A240." Your managers answer these ten times a day over the phone. If you put these answers on your website, the neural network will quote you even before a person gets to the "where to buy" question.

And a few technical details, without which all of the above will not work:

  • prices and specifications are written in text, not embedded as images or PDFs;

  • the website is open to search engines and loads correctly on a phone;

  • pages have Schema.org markup for the company, products, and prices: this helps machines understand that the number next to "Rebar 12 mm A500S" is the price per ton, not per meter;

  • listings on Yandex Business and 2GIS are filled out and do not contradict the website: the same addresses, phone numbers, and working hours;

  • prices are updated. A neural network quoting a price list from two years ago is more harmful than silence.

How to check yourself in 10 minutes

No analytics services are needed for this. Just do what your new client does.

  1. Write down five questions that most often start phone calls. For example: "where to buy rebar 12 mm in [city] with delivery," "price of profile pipe 40x20 in [city]," "metal depot with cutting near [district]."

  2. Ask them to Alice in Yandex search or GigaChat. These are the services most commonly used in Russia.

  3. Note down who the neural networks name, what prices they provide, and what websites they link to.

  4. If you are not listed, open the websites of those who are. Most likely, it's not the design that's better, but simply that prices and terms are written in text.

  5. If you are listed, check exactly what is said about you. An old price or a closed warehouse in a neural network's answer is more costly than not being mentioned at all.

Repeat this check once a month. Neural network answers change with search results, and the effect of website updates is best seen this way.

Conclusion

For an old-fashioned business, a website has ceased to be a business card for those who already know you. Now it is a source from which neural networks gather answers for those who do not yet know you.

  1. New clients increasingly ask neural networks instead of scrolling through search results.

  2. Neural networks quote websites that are easily found through regular search.

  3. Those whose prices, assortment, and terms are written in text get quoted.

  4. An expensive redesign is not needed for this. Up-to-date data and answers to the questions you are already asked by phone are needed.
