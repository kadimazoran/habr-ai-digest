Fake documents have long ceased to be a one-off tool for fraudsters. Today they are used in schemes of illegal legalization, bank fraud, account hijacking, and the creation of entire fictitious identities. And generative AI has made such attacks more accessible and technologically advanced. Smart Engines and the law firm INTELLECT have completed a large-scale study of this threat and analyzed how fraudulent schemes have changed and what new risks they create for businesses.

Over the week of the report, we discussed how the statistics of fraud using fake documents in Russia have changed, how the schemes that led to real losses for businesses in 2025 are structured, how fraud has evolved, and how AI helps detect fake documents in various service channels.

We reveal all the hottest figures in our digest 👇

Scale of the Problem

🔹 The number of crimes registered by the Ministry of Internal Affairs has increased year-on-year by 338% and reached a seven-year high;

🔹 The minimum estimate of the illegal market for fake identity documents has reached 2.6 billion rubles.

But the scale is only one side of the problem. In the report, we have collected real cases from judicial practice, analyzed the financial consequences for businesses, the legal aspects of document verification, and the technologies that help detect fakes in various service channels.

What documents are faked most often

In 2025, among the detected fakes, the leaders were:

🔹 driver's licenses — 41%;

🔹 passports of CIS countries and residence permits — 18%;

🔹 Russian Federation passports — 16%.

In these conditions, standard checks are not enough for businesses. It is important to assess the authenticity of the document itself — based on multiple independent features simultaneously.

Our anti-fraud platform "Sherlock" analyzes over 600 authenticity features and supports over 5,000 document templates. The system checks security features and NFC chips, detects screen re-recording, copies and printouts, traces of graphic editing and manipulation, generative fakes, and presentation attacks 🔥

Fakes of foreign documents

In 2025, under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation, 10,942 people were convicted, and almost half of those convicted were foreign citizens. 5,408 of them were citizens of CIS countries.

Fake passports of CIS countries were used primarily in schemes for illegal legalization of stay for foreign citizens in Russia: for migration registration, obtaining documents confirming the right to stay and work in the country.

Therefore, businesses need a tool that equally effectively detects signs of forgery in both Russian and foreign documents.

Business Losses

28.6 billion rubles — this is how much Russian banks lost from external fraud in 2025 💸

Fake documents remain one of the main tools for such fraudulent schemes: they are used to issue loans, open accounts, transfer, and launder stolen funds.

But direct financial losses are only part of the consequences. These are supplemented by expenses for investigations and additional checks, as well as regulatory and reputational risks.

Therefore, the task of banks is to stop fraud as early as possible, at the document verification stage.

How fraudulent attacks are changing

1️⃣ Attackers have moved from forging individual documents to creating fictitious identities

Fraudsters use several documents at once for a fictional or someone else's name to pass through several verification stages.

2️⃣ Fraudsters have found a way to bypass bank checks through less secure channels

In one of the cases reviewed, a fake passport was used to obtain a SIM card from a telecom operator, after which the attackers regained access to the victim's internet banking and stole 4.45 million rubles.

3️⃣ Generative AI has expanded the toolkit for identity forgery

To create fake documents, special skills are no longer needed. Now, almost anyone with access to generative AI can do it. Synthetic documents, text and photo replacement, face swap, morphing, and synthetic selfies are used.
