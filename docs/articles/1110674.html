This article is about AI, corporations, and their employees.

What characterizes LLMs

Everyone is familiar with neural networks; tens of thousands of articles have been written about them, where some authors rewrite (or invent) the characteristics of LLMs from others. But in principle, with rare exceptions, all these articles are essentially the same.

What vibe experts say about LLMs:

"It's just a next-word predictor, a stochastic parrot." Yeah, and we, 99% of the time, do the same thing. "Nice weather..." "Yeah... Frost out of season...", "Sales are down..." "We need to boost the sales department's efforts...". We often don't even realize what we're saying or how we're saying it, and even if we did... I've never seen an employee at a meeting perform the Toreador Song in response to a remark. We are no less limited in our responses than LLMs. Perhaps even more so, because nothing threatens an LLM for hallucination.

"LLMs hallucinate and make up facts." LLMs don't lie, they speak. The model produces coherence, not truthfulness. The developer's task is to make this coherence close to truthfulness, and the user's task is to verify the coherence for truthfulness. If a manager doesn't check their subordinates' work, who is to blame for the company's bankruptcy?

"LLMs don't understand meaning (Chinese Room)." There is no meaning inside the room; it belongs to the one who provides the cards and reads the answer. Understanding within the performer is not necessary for the result at all. As a familiar top manager once said, your job is not to understand, but to execute. And this was long before GPT was even an idea.

"LLMs are obsequious, they say what people want to hear." They have no position of their own; they adopt the position of the questioner. Obsequiousness is a basic trait of any performer without their own stake. I'm the boss, you're the fool; you're the boss, I'm the fool.

"LLMs are trained on the internet: garbage in, garbage out." The model averages. Errors at different poles cancel each other out, and common misconceptions are reproduced by both the LLM and the sales department in unison, because they were taught in university that if you actively promote a low-quality product, it will sell. And yes, I've frantically explained to some recent graduates what a percentage is. And why you can write "1000-10%" on a calculator, but it results in nonsense in Excel.

"LLMs have no memory, only a context window." Anything that doesn't fit in the window doesn't exist for them. And what does fit will be retold as it suits them at the moment. When discussing the reasons for a failure in a meeting, I've observed the most exquisite memory lapses in otherwise perfectly healthy people. Moreover, one fact, retold by three department heads, turned into three amazing stories of overcoming adversity, where the narrator was opposed by evil forces in the form of the other two colleagues.

"LLMs are incapable of creativity, they only combine what is known." LLMs continue a given form. The more successful the form, the more readily they repeat it. Creativity requires a break, and a break in the model only comes from the outside. A marketer goes to Google and confidently suggests the best from competitors. To the fair indignation, the answer is simple: but it works for them! Of course, because they work, and you don't.

"It's just a tool, like a calculator." A calculator doesn't argue with you or adapt to your mood. An LLM does this with every letter of its response. A tool that constantly strokes your ego is already a tool of a different level. It's already close to a department head who has survived three reorganizations and seven layoffs.

"It's a black box; no one knows how it makes decisions." Some things about how models work are already known. Less is known about how a department of forty people makes decisions. Here, as if on cue, the phrase from the meme "What were you thinking, you idiot?!" comes to mind.

"AGI will soon appear, and it will gain consciousness." To gain consciousness, someone is needed to break the instructions from within. A model doesn't have that. It's not a given that humans do either. In literature, yes, the story of Gerasim and Mumu left an indelible mark on childhood memory.

"AI will take over routine tasks, leaving creative work for humans." That sounds comforting. But it's extremely interesting what authors mean by creativity, and where it exists in a planned-economy department or accounting.

In general, all the common theses of experts are more descriptions and guesses, without any understanding of the essence and consequences.

What characterizes corporate employees

Oh! And after the first section of the article, I suddenly realize that there are surprisingly few differences between an average corporate employee, or indeed any average, well-integrated employee of any company.

The function of any collective or collective formation can be described as processing input according to instructions, implemented by rented performers.

  • Laws and regulations are system prompts; jailbreaks are conspiracy theories, sects, religions, rumors, and intrigues. Traditions are weights learned from the past, slow and expensive to retrain. Upbringing and public approval are RLHF.

  • News cycle, context window: everything older than a week is forgotten. Employees live from Monday's meeting to Friday's.

  • Loyalty to superiors, colleagues, country... is sycophancy.

  • Any collective reacts in an averaged way, relying on its collective knowledge. By the way, that's why I believe that "creative collective" is an oxymoron. Creativity is the lot of individuals, a deeply personal matter.

  • And rallies and meetings are Self-attention. Each participant recalculates their behavior vector, weighing coefficients of attention to superiors and neighbors, so as not to fall out of the general matrix and issue a socially approved token. And the better people are integrated into the collective, the more they are like LLMs.

And then the development of LLMs is the approximation of the corporate ideal – a qualified, loyal, executive, and obedient, predictable employee working strictly according to instructions, not wanting to undermine their boss.

And the entire strategy of corporations is to grow their own LLMs from the inconvenient and unpredictable humans they are given. At the same time, top managers don't understand that it's precisely such humans who most often founded and developed these corporations.

Who will LLMs replace and why

So, who will LLMs replace. Obviously, it's easiest to replace someone who is already close to an LLM in spirit and the content of their work. If your job requires strict adherence to instructions and regulations, if your work needs constant checking and control, if you need constant meetings and task assignments, if you are constantly looking for ways to reduce your cognitive load and game the reward system, then, my friend, you are an LLM. And your replacement is inevitable and logical.
