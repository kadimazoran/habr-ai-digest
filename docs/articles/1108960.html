In July of this year, OpenAI launched the "ChatGPT for Academic Researchers" program, which provides free access to ChatGPT for 100,000 scientists, mathematicians, and engineers from selected higher education institutions worldwide. The company is now doubling the number of participants in the initiative to 200,000, but they will now receive access not to ChatGPT Pro, but to ChatGPT Plus.

Following its summer launch, the program offered access to OpenAI's most capable language models via a ChatGPT Pro subscription, along with research tools, training, and other features.

Since July, over 20,000 researchers from 33 countries have joined ChatGPT for Academic Researchers. The company studied how over 1,800 researchers used ChatGPT during the pilot period. Specifically, participants used the chatbot for literature reviews, data analysis, code writing, and developing new research ideas.

Surveyed researchers reported that completing the same work with comparable quality without AI would have taken nearly twice as long.

Following the program's expansion, OpenAI explained the replacement of the ChatGPT Pro subscription with the less expensive ChatGPT Plus by stating that participants did not sufficiently utilize the advanced tier's capabilities. The transition to ChatGPT Plus is intended to make the program accessible to a larger number of researchers, according to the company.

OpenAI will open applications for the next round of the program on November 23, 2026, offering 50,000 new grants. Each grant will cover the costs for one researcher and up to two co-authors, potentially providing free ChatGPT Plus access to an additional 150,000 scientists.

During the previous round, OpenAI selected eligible candidates through a lottery. In the upcoming round, new grants will be awarded on a first-come, first-served basis to those who meet the selection criteria. Researchers on the waiting list from the first round will receive an email with instructions before applications open.

In addition to ChatGPT for Academic Researchers, the company offers two other similar programs for the scientific community: ChatGPT Edu for educational institutions and the Rosalind Academic Plan for qualifying biological laboratories at U.S. universities.
