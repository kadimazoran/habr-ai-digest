A Tale of Data.

Imagine it's the 2010s, and we are a large technology company manufacturing various devices. We notice that for some reason, our competitors are capturing more and more market share and pushing us out. At the same time, the quality of their products and their marketing efforts are not superior. Even those we have many advantages over have improved their positions. This is not acceptable...

Therefore, we decide not to fall behind the trend and acquire a small internet service, creating our own platform based on it. From now on, customers can not only buy a product but also register it, receive updates, contact customer support, and participate in product development.

Now we know not only about the most frequent malfunctions and demand levels but also discover a completely new dimension of user emotions. It turns out that the incorrect placement of a button can cause a client to rarely use the product. And the presence of strange colorful backlighting makes the item more desirable. People are attracted not by functionality, but by how it is presented and who uses it. Some types of devices spawn entire subcultures or even become must-haves within communities. The question of need becomes secondary when culture demands it, and society ostracizes those who don't have the right trinket. A product is a marker of belonging, status, views, and lifestyle. That's why even minimal knowledge yields more than one might imagine.

Now we don't have to worry if that fat guy with a diagnosis of "heart failure" and no insurance can afford our beer-coffee machine or if he'll "kick the bucket" before he can pay off the installment plan. If he manages to pay it off, we'll recommend the product on the main page.

The reverse is also true: generalized data leads to an increasingly personalized approach. At some point, information itself becomes a product. Well-chosen recommendations, targeted advertising, bonuses, compliments, and congratulations: all of this attracts people. Competitors and small firms increasingly turn to us for help. Classic Methods of Influence

Before moving on to advanced methods of influence, let's briefly recall the classic ones.

To influence a subject, one must not only identify their weaknesses and fears but also understand the strength of their will. Interestingly, it is not reason, work capacity, conscientiousness, or other qualities that determine a person's level of influence, although they are important. The primary quality is will. A reasonable conformist will always yield to someone who is ready to act to the end, regardless of anything. A mediocre doer will surpass an outstanding analyst. A person who does not break under circumstances, risks their life, and acts recklessly will either die (with a high probability) or achieve their goal. That's why success belongs to adventurers.

The entire society can be divided into categories for which Euler diagrams and funnels are suitable. They are a kind of visualization of the Pareto principle, where most problems are solved with minimal effort. Let's briefly analyze the categories by the level of the volitional component:

Conformists.

Many people are quite tolerant of the violation of their rights, especially if it is enshrined in the cultural code. Usually, when faced with blatant injustice, the average person reacts calmly and cynically, sometimes even with humor, which protects their psyche from excessive strain. What's the point of fighting against what happens every day and against which you are powerless? That's why in most cases, a single remark or verbal threat is enough to correct behavior.

If special services are interested in such a person, they only need to approach them at their workplace. Pressure through management is the fastest way to coerce.

Scammers often choose a dominance tactic for interacting with this profile: an authoritative guise, boasting about their organization, a commanding voice, and threats of consequences.

Subjects.

This level implies the presence of will and a clear social position. They do not take words at face value, even if forced to act against their beliefs. Subjective individuals usually take an active stance, can take responsibility, and argue their opinions. Typically, these are middle managers, businessmen, administrators, and all those for whom excessive conformity would prevent them from being themselves. It is not enough to present them with the fact "these are our rules here." Therefore, influence must be reinforced with material benefits, social rank, or real sanctions.

Yet, they are yielding. If offered a position or an income increase to change their stance, such a person will likely find an internal justification for why they should agree. If their financial support is withdrawn, they will likely give in and make concessions.

Primitive scam methods no longer work here. No matter who the scammer pretends to be, the person will always ask questions and verify information, as mere "authority" is no longer sufficient.

Leaders.

They have clear principles and an understanding of the control system. They are aware of their capabilities, know their rights, and are ready to defend them. As a rule, they act coldly and rationally. Often, adherence to an internal "code" is more important than material status. Moreover, the code itself can be quite specific. Among this category are: opinion leaders, owners of large assets, politicians, 'thieves in law' (Vor v Zakone), informal leaders, and other people with true status. Typically, the profile of such a person is jagged on the MMPI scale with an elevated score for independence/psychopathy.

Attempting to pressure or buy such a person leads to a reverse reaction. Direct bribery can be insulting, and threats can provoke retaliatory actions. Engaging with such a person without a strategy will be a losing decision. However, studying their fears and hidden desires may still allow for leverage. Similarly, a real threat to family, status, or financial situation can force a change in position. Nevertheless, such a person is a serious opponent, and even a temporary retreat does not guarantee the impossibility of revenge. It is the organized actions of this type of person that have a decisive influence during system instability.

Fanatics.

A rare and absolutely unmanageable profile, obsessed with super-valuable ideas. Neither material wealth nor the threat of death are levers of influence on them. Generally accepted norms may be intentionally rejected with philosophical justification. The world is more likely to bend to such a person than they are to bend to the world. This is the case when the subject is ready to go to hell to take as many enemies with them as possible. Combined with high intelligence and special skills, they can pose a threat at a civilizational level. Among them are: revolutionaries, prophets, leaders, national heroes, serial killers.

Overt correction can be fatal in conditions of uncertainty. Such subjects can influence others themselves, rallying around their persona. Attempting to destroy their reputation or eliminate such a person through radical measures may only fuel public interest.

The methods of influence themselves can be cheap or expensive in terms of resource and reputational costs. Persuasion, bribery, threats of reprisal, imprisonment, murder—all have vastly different costs. The consequences of radical decisions can be unpredictable. Even targeted influence may only remain unnoticed by society temporarily, until all the facts merge into a single picture. It is important to understand that truly cruel measures are applied to a very small group of people who have passed through the funnel, and any adequate management requires their minimization. If there are too many prisoners and "heretics" in a country, society quickly realizes that the problem is clearly not with the condemned. And the more absurd the legitimization of силовые меры (forceful measures), the faster the erosion of the management system occurs. In other words, every murdered and coerced person is a wound that shortens the lifespan of the management system.

Therefore, the main principle is: cheap methods of influence have a high priority over expensive ones, and carrots are better than sticks. The consequences of influence must be minimized, regardless of their type. Ideally, the subject should not even know that influence is being exerted on them. Just as one SMS greeting to a client can prevent them from changing their tariff, a simple meeting can prevent a person from changing narratives. For example, having predetermined the rebellious nature of one of the directors, an influence agent should proactively meet with them in an environment pleasant to the subject, evaluate their work, and offer assistance, thereby preserving the management system. The "assistance" itself should be minimized in cost and maximized in duration of effect to reduce administrative resources (primarily focus of attention). The strategy is chosen based on a complex combination of all variables, for which modern models are ideally suited.

It is also important to realize that people's desires are often irrational. And since the main metric is precisely the correction of attitudes and behavior, one must give precisely what the person desires, not what they actually need. An influence agent will not give advice and recommendations, but like a genie, will fulfill the person's will or, even better, their subconscious desires. In other words, if Winnie the Pooh craves honey, he should get honey, even though in reality he needs a good exercise machine.

Advanced Methods of Influence

So, I believe this is the most interesting section, describing specific technologies from the obvious to the almost fantastic.

AI Twin.

If we assume that it is possible to build a personality model, is it possible to build at least a rough model of a mind—a kind of functional analogue capable of thinking and reasoning?

The thing is, a profile is a kind of imprint of the mind, while an AI agent is its dynamic model. Such a technology can not only analyze a personality from open data but also predict their reaction to various things and events.

Studying weak points, stress thresholds, and a person's knowledge on a particular topic can play a decisive role in an argument or during a fraudulent attack. In other words, by training an AI twin on your data, a manipulator gets a punching bag with the ability to practice and test various strategies.

Consider it as your opponent gaining an ally in the form of your digital copy, which tells them about all its weaknesses and fears. Moreover, such an agent can act independently, like a bot: engage in online arguments, exploit weak points, and elicit the desired reaction from a person. For example, a provocateur agent can drive a victim to an emotional outburst and make them express an opinion that facilitates their crossing into illegality.

Gestalt Control.

Let's break down the essence of the technology using the example of social therapy, such as Alcoholics Anonymous, veterans' societies, support groups after loss, and similar communities. You've probably heard of such groups where people gather and share their stories. But few know how it works and why such therapy is truly effective.

Globally, therapy can be divided into 4 stages:

  1. Involvement. The subject joins the group, meets the participants, and listens to their stories. People speak easily and freely, even when discussing traumatic events. Afterward, the facilitator passes the word to the newcomer and, one way or another, persuades them to speak, for example, through awkward silence.

  2. Trust. By sharing information with participants, the subject increasingly begins to trust them. This is a known phenomenon where the act itself for the group generates an attitude towards it. The brain essentially says: if I share with them, then they can be trusted. Now they no longer experience the former discomfort and do not censor information.

  3. Integration. The subject, having spent enough time in the group, becomes part of it. A gestalt forms, and personal barriers weaken. Now they are part of one large organism.

  4. Introjection. Being in a collective with recovered individuals struggling with the same problems, the subject unconsciously adopts their common traits, based on which they gathered. Successful examples and the general atmosphere of recovery now become part of their wholeness. Thus, the person is healed.

This is an example of productive use of gestalt for the benefit of people. It helps to unconsciously transfer not only knowledge but also a lifestyle concept to a person in need. The illness is cured not by medication or suggestion of attitudes, but by changing the thinking itself.

But there is a flip side: various organizations also adopt this technology, be it the state, special services, or scammers, and use it for their selfish purposes. Imagine a person who enters a specific environment of similar people. After some time, they will begin to "resonate" with them on the same wavelength and become part of the collective. This means they will share personal information, including sensitive information, more often. After all, it's no secret that people tend to speak more frankly in groups they consider their own, for example, family.

But the main thing is that this principle works in reverse: it allows instilling (introjecting) the necessary attitudes and behavioral stereotypes into the subject themselves. Thus, through group environments, for example, in universities or the army, necessary dogmas are introduced. It is especially effective if this happens at the stage of personality formation, as it is almost impossible to get rid of them afterward. The earlier they are introduced and the lower the intellectual potential of a particular individual, the more firmly the programs will be established. This is extremely interesting, but mass control is not the topic of this article and may be discussed later.

This technology has already found application on various internet resources, communities, chats, social networks, and hosting sites. People actively share information about themselves, thereby disclosing useful information. But even more dangerous is the reverse: the introduction of attitudes. Almost everyone has an electronic idol whose opinion is important to them and whom they unconsciously imitate. Thus, they unconsciously adopt their traits, becoming a kind of template copy. That's why controlling organizations find it so important to promote their own idol to the forefront and overshadow others. For the same reason, there is a struggle for the information space, including the use of blocking measures. Making access to a favorite blogger or performer difficult forces a person to seek an available analogue.

It is important to realize that it's not about which talking heads are right and which are "servants of the devil." If a person has an idol to imitate, the main question is why they so desperately want to become like them. Is this image an ideal self or an attempt to compensate for shortcomings that may not even exist? Are these efforts and the time spent worth reshaping oneself according to someone else's templates?

Content Control.

What will be described next will sound like science fiction, but it is the most important section of the article. It is necessary to correctly understand what this technology represents, which should not be confused with targeted propaganda. We are talking about a completely new tool designed to manipulate the subconscious.

If you have well-modeled someone's personality, their views, thinking type, and genesis, is it possible to somehow control a person by introducing the right attitudes through culture? Imagine that among the vast variety of content recommended based on affinity, there is a small portion of information that permeates it and is intended to prompt the person towards a target action or thoughts.

And it's not even about the notorious psychology videos that are supposed to reveal something about yourself. Such a model doesn't need to generate gigabytes of data specifically for you, as that would be too wasteful. It's enough to extract a specific video from the bottomless depths of the internet that should lead you to the right thoughts.

It doesn't matter what the content creator did or why: only the operational system matters. The main thing is not the message of the content, but who it ultimately reaches.

Special content is unobtrusive, rarely encountered, perhaps in a proportion of 1/100 of the total time, and perfectly chosen to influence the consciousness of a specific individual. It's not a separate video or a "25th frame," but a special selection of existing items, like dealing cards from a deck. And as in a game, the deal is precisely coordinated with your own "cards." Each such selection should impact points of instability to push an opinion in the desired direction. It's best to do this at a moment of highest fragility, when a person is seeking support, comfort, knowledge, pity, and the like. In other words, a needy person is a vulnerable target. And just as a beggar is happy to believe in a big win (well, what if!), a person in a period of instability is most manageable. Will they listen to a monologue by a character they like?

How Not to Be a Pawn

Throughout history, control and influence have always been exerted over people. However, at each stage, it was far from absolute: a person could always be themselves within communities, family, or at least in the depths of their own mind. Today, the system is undergoing a qualitative transition, where the concept of thoughtcrime is closer to truth than fiction. States, corporations, special services, scammers, and other organizations will inevitably implement these tools to keep up with competitors. In other words, it's about simple rationalism, not paranoia or a thirst for total control. Modern technologies allow scaling and personalizing the control process to an unprecedented level.

Now the system doesn't need general ideas, as it can adapt to everyone's psychological needs. And considering that telemetry in every toaster is no longer science fiction, there is plenty of data for this.

The internet itself has also become a battlefield. Armies of bots, pretending to be humans, attack the few living individuals who dare to raise their heads. Humor and criticism are used as weapons, along with the generation of fakes. But the worst thing is that morally outdated targeted propaganda is being replaced by almost fantastic content control. Its strength lies in invisible influence and selecting the perfect argument that considers the peculiarities of thinking, the set of attitudes, experience, and genesis of personality. And the quality metric of such technology is not objective correctness, but the ability to convince and sway anyone to one's side. This means that human knowledge and thinking (at least for those close to the median) play an ever-diminishing role, and the person themselves increasingly risks becoming a puppet of giant data centers battling for control over their mind.

This is why it is so important to maintain objectivity and try to study facts. Your ability to independently seek information from various sources, as well as to suppress initial reactions (positive or negative) with willpower, will be a key countermeasure against this type of sophisticated manipulation. If you experience strong reactions towards a certain person or event, ask yourself the following questions:

  1. Why do I feel this way? Any emotion you have is a personal problem. It does not relate to the object that causes it, but only to its reflection in your psyche. So, what is this reflection connected to? What problems does it touch upon? The stronger the emotion, the more effort should be made to get to its core.

  2. Who are you facing? Every news item, film, and blog has a bias. Every agency/studio/channel has an owner and their interests. Even if the content distributor is neutral, they themselves have a set of attitudes and stereotypes. Even if a blog is purely entertainment, it exists with the permission of a higher authority. Nothing should be taken at face value, nor should it be rejected thoughtlessly.

  3. Are you seeing the shell or the essence of things? The object and your attitude towards it are not the same thing. If a character in a play irritates you, you should keep in mind that they are an actor playing a role according to a script within someone else's production.

Next, I will briefly list tips that will help you become more resistant to advanced mind manipulation tools. They will complement standard advice on digital hygiene, fact-checking, and verifying information sources.

Don't believe, don't fear, don't ask.

Any control method relies on fears and desires. Do not be tempted or back down under pressure. If someone is trying hard to sway you towards a certain decision, ask why. It may turn out that the decision is much more beneficial to the one imposing it. Perhaps they themselves are prisoners of their work, as the boss has their own boss. Like in hell, a failed demon who couldn't send enough mortals to the cauldron risks going there himself. Often, modern sophisticated measures are the fantasy of local subordinates who are themselves scared to death, as are their bosses, all the way down the hierarchy.

In our example, Vasily realized that it was unreasonable to take the accuser's word for it and surrender to the system's mercy, and decided to hire a lawyer to consider all options. Later, he would learn how much he didn't know and how much he missed in the case, and how advantageous a decision he made when he decided to trust a professional.

Don't rush to laugh.
