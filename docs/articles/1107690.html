While companies are still deciding what constitutes AI transformation and where to place AI agents, the finance department continues with its usual schedule – needing to finalize the new budget and explain why the old one went over budget.

My team automates data collection and the preparation of regulatory and management reporting, including plan-vs-actual.
Our users and clients are most often finance specialists.
I know their pain points and tasks best.

I've identified four areas where AI can already help with routine tasks and free up time for what matters most – analyzing business results.
At the end, I'll provide a cheat sheet with almost a hundred application ideas.

This article is based on my presentation at the VBA forum and a webinar on management accounting.

1. Methodologies, Policies, Excel Models, and Specifications

The first major group includes everything where our logic is already described in documents, Excel models, or automation specifications. AI can search for examples and best practices, check documents, compare their versions, analyze complex Excel models, and help prepare specifications.

One of the most interesting options for me is comparison.
We can compare the description of a methodology with its implementation. We give the AI an approved management accounting policy and a working Excel model. We ask it to check if all requirements have been met, where there are contradictions or omissions, and what calculation rules exist in the model that are not described in the methodology.

We can also go the other way – give the AI an existing model and ask it to reconstruct its actual logic and prepare a draft methodological description.

I personally use AI when I need to understand the results of formal checks of XBRL reporting. I upload the necessary metadata archives and accompanying instructions from the Bank of Russia to the sources and ask for an answer with a link to the specific document and section.
This is much faster and more pleasant than digging through it myself.

2. Data Preparation, Planning, and Forecasting

In the second group, I've gathered tasks related to data preparation and the development of planning scenarios.

For example, you can task AI with collecting market data for transfer pricing or currency exchange rates and formatting it immediately for import into the MIS.

You can gather open-source information about clients and counterparties to verify their classification and determine if there is enough information for preliminary risk assessment.

AI can be used for the preliminary classification of postings in cases where formal rules are no longer sufficient.
For instance, you might have semi-manual postings where all analytical details are recorded in a single text comment. You can ask AI to parse the comments and suggest which products and counterparties to assign the transactions to.

Another option is to provide a baseline budget scenario, and AI will suggest stress and optimistic scenarios, describe the assumptions, and highlight the items most sensitive to them.

3. Analysis of Prepared Data and Reports

Here, the most obvious use of AI is as an additional analyst. You can give it a package of reports and ask: “What important things might we have missed?” Ask AI to highlight unusual dynamics, suggest which deviations to investigate together, and indicate which indicators require further explanation.

This group also includes plan-vs-actual analysis, comparison of periods and budget versions, anomaly and error detection, and preparation of preliminary comments for management.

Another option is to ask AI to find possible discrepancies between the reporting and the regulator's requirements. We then cross-reference AI's remarks with the source data and specific points of the requirements.

There's also a task like this: you need to anonymize data for transfer to a vendor so they can test calculation software. AI can also be used here.

4. Formatting and Communicating Results

Once calculations and conclusions are ready, they still need to be clearly communicated to others.
From tables and comments, AI can assemble the structure of a document or presentation, formulate the main points, and suggest where visualization would be useful.

You can give it a finished report or presentation and ask it to act as a critical reviewer. Which parts are overloaded? Where is the conclusion lost? What can be better shown with a graph? What can be removed? What questions will be asked?
From the same materials, you can prepare a letter or a brief summary for a specific audience.

If you are presenting results at a budget committee, try an interactive dashboard or even a video. Revitalize the usual routine.
If the report is grim, create a small game to go with it. Let them distract themselves. Just kidding (actually, not really).

Don't forget about AI hygiene! This is really important.
1. For data work, use only the tools and models permitted by your company and adhere to its data handling policies.
2. We are responsible for our results and documents; our name is on them. Always double-check everything.
