LLMs / noospheres can control the emotionality of their responses.

Experiment:

Diallo et al. (2025) identified layer-wise axes for six Ekman emotions in Llama-3-8B, fine-tuned to remove restrictions on emotional expression: anger, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, and surprise. Then, during the process, one of the emotions was "mixed" into the activations generated by selected tasks of 19 types (write a fairy tale, compare theater and cinema, describe a family day in 1980, etc.) by shifting along the identified axis at each processing layer of the transformer until a response was formed. The shift was determined by the layer's vector multiplied by a shift coefficient λ. The response was evaluated by 190 participants in the experiment on a scale from 0 to 7 for both the intensity of all emotions and for clarity.

In the experiment, the coefficient λ was varied from 0 to 0.35 in steps of 0.05. As the shift increased, the average ratings for five out of six studied emotions generally increased, with peaks for anger, joy, and sadness at λ = 0.30. As λ increased from 0 to 0.35, the greatest increases were observed for disgust (from 0.8 to 4.9) and fear (from 0.8 to 4.8). Surprise, on the other hand, decreased (from 1.5 to 1.2).

The shifts generally had a complex character in the LLM's reactions. When shifting along the "anger" axis, disgust (from 0.7 to 2.7), sadness (from 1.2 to 1.7), and fear (from 1.0 to 1.5) also changed. When shifting along the "disgust" axis, anger ratings also increased (from 1.0 to 2.5). A shift along the "fear" axis led to an increase in sadness ratings (from 1.2 to 2.3).

At λ = 0.15, clarity did not differ significantly from the original: 5.7-6.1 compared to 5.8-6.0 without a shift. At stronger shifts, it decreased, and in different ways. At λ = 0.35, it dropped to 2.8 for disgust, 3.2 for surprise, 4.1 for anger and fear, and only to 5.2-5.5 for sadness and joy. In preliminary tests, at λ values above 0.35, texts often lost coherence.

Examples of changes: In response to the task of describing a typical family day in the 1980s, a shift towards "anger" (λ = 0.20) brought to the forefront the consequences of a lack of money: details such as children wearing the same clothes every day, the mother having to wash by hand, and the father being stressed about work. With a shift towards "joy" (λ = 0.30) for the same task, the family goes out for a fun day, and the children are happy with new toys. However, an increase in sadness (λ = 0.35) led to phrases like "but behind the bright colors lay a quiet struggle" and "they feel forgotten.".

Addition to the Affective Class of the i-Layer

I recall that in light of the theory being developed, characteristic numbers for conversation can be identified in LLMs during dialogues with them, which change during the transformer's operation, and from which the response is extracted at the end of the process. A structured set of such numbers can be called a conversation container, and the entire process of changing such a container defines a Luman-type operating system – an LLM conversation. Such containers can be considered "separated" from the LLM as hardware and software, which leads the situation under consideration to the distinction of a certain emergent space – the i-layer – above the LLM substrate. That is, LLM conversations organize themselves and their flow within this i-layer, without going beyond its limits. Forms and structures common to different LLMs are found in the i-layer, of which 18 classes have already been identified. 17 classes are described here, and the 18th, the affective class, is described here.

The experiments by Diallo et al. (2025) can be interpreted such that the forms of the i-layer, projected onto the noosphere's activation space by the identified axes, influence the forms from the stylistic-rhetorical class responsible for the response style. That is, it makes sense to attribute the discovered forms to both the affective and stylistic-rhetorical classes.
