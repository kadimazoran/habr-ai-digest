1. Two truths that don't want to know about each other

Thesis: technology liberates

The lineage is venerable. Thorstein Veblen, in the early 20th century, contrasted 'industry' and 'business': engineers produce, owners extract. His project was straightforward to the point of naivety—an 'engineers' strike,' paralyzing the old order and establishing a 'council of technical specialists' to bring efficiency and abolish the idle class. The utopia didn't materialize, but a moderate version did: Daniel Bell and Alvin Toffler's post-industrial theory described a society where management separates from ownership, and human capital displaces others.

And this position has a truth that technophobes systematically ignore.

Lewis Mumford showed that during the transition from primitive times to ancient Eastern kingdoms, there were almost no breakthroughs in tools. The breakthrough was in the organization of activity—and it gave us cities, irrigation, and pyramids. Technology emancipates before technique does: the megamachine was assembled from people, not iron.

A second, less obvious example. Marx, describing the horrors of machine production, notes that the machine made muscular strength redundant, paving the way for cheaper female and child labor. He sees only exploitation here. But the same fact has another side that he missed: women gained the ability to earn their own living. This isn't 'reducing unemployment'; it's an economic prerequisite for equality that was impossible in a patriarchal agrarian family. The same process that is experienced as a catastrophe by one segment of society opens an escape from centuries of dependence for another.

Technocracy is right about the main point: technology truly removes coercion—from nature, necessity, or another's will. To deny this is to forget how people lived before.

Antithesis: technology enslaves

The lineage is no less venerable: Rousseau with his 'back to nature,' Ellul, Jaspers, Heidegger, and among the radicals—anarcho-primitivist John Zerzan.

Jaspers succinctly formulates it: "Technology has turned all existence into the action of a technical mechanism, the entire planet into a single factory."
