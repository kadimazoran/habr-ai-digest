Introduction

Today, we are introducing Gemini 4 Neon. The model solves complex development, information processing, and scientific research tasks from start to finish, combining speed with the capabilities of leading models.

At the xhigh reasoning level, Neon scores 61 points in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Among comparable models, this is the best result, and in the overall standings, Neon ranks third, slightly behind larger and slower models. In the Vals Index, it ranks second and reaches the Pareto frontier.

In the new Agent Arena Index, Neon ranks second in its ability to incorporate user corrections during operation. The model follows multi-step instructions well, and in internal Google Antigravity tests, it showed the lowest rate of fabricated information among our models.

The context window is 1 million tokens. One million input tokens cost $3, one million cached input tokens cost $0.15 with a 95% discount, and one million output tokens cost $15.

Gemini 4 Neon is gradually rolling out today in the Gemini API via Google AI Studio, in Gemini for business via API and the model selection menu, as well as in Google Antigravity and the Gemini app for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

In scientific and mathematical tasks, from quantitative analysis to technical research problems, Neon shows best-in-class results in Terminal-Bench Science (70.5%), RiemannBench (84.0%), and HLE-Diamond (71.3%).

Code for Production

Neon is designed for long-running tasks where an agent repeatedly interacts with tools. The model maintains quality throughout its operation. It can perform tasks independently or coordinate the parallel work of Hydrogen sub-agents in Orchestrator mode in Google Antigravity, quickly making decisions about next steps. Neon rereads the same files less often, reports on its progress, and reliably compresses context over long sessions.

In core developer and researcher tasks, Neon performs at the level of leading models in its class. It ranks first in the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index with a score of 58.6. In Terminal-Bench 4.1, the model scores 65.2%, and in FrontierCode 2.0 at the default high reasoning level, it reaches 46.2%. For production code tasks, Neon debugs and refactors multiple files and repositories, making targeted changes. In SWE-sweep, the model leads in the mini-SWE environment with a score of 12.8%, and in Google Antigravity, this figure more than triples to a record 39.0%.

In Google Antigravity, Gemini 4 Neon used /swarm and controlled Blender via MCP to assemble a photorealistic 3D lab setup. Model names were recreated considering element properties. For Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Bunsen burner flames were used, while for Gemini 4 Argon and Neon, high-voltage gas discharge tubes were installed.

In Google Antigravity, Neon wrote a first-person free-ride ski simulator from scratch in WebGL2, using real elevation data from Mont Blanc and Chamonix. The simulator features 10,200 lines of custom shaders, 120Hz ski physics across six mountain routes, dynamic snow tracks, snow slab avalanches, and spatial audio via Web Audio. No pre-made assets were used.

Using /swarm in Google Antigravity, Neon found archival photographs and recreated Alan Turing's Cambridge King's College study as it was in 1936 in interactive 3D. The scene includes the chapel from the outside and room X.8 from the inside, including a working universal Turing machine on the desk.

In Google Antigravity, a group of Gemini 4 Neon agents converted a single image into a Google Sheet with 100,000 cells. This was done without built-in images or Apps Script. Reasoning Levels and Task Cost

With each release, we refine the reasoning settings to allow teams to control the model more precisely and ensure smoother transitions between levels. Neon has four levels. They progressively increase reasoning depth and token consumption, allowing you to choose the right balance for each task.

In third-party tests, Neon maintains high quality at a lower task cost. At the default high level, the model scores highly in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and costs 32% less than Carbon per task. At this same level, Neon reaches the Pareto frontier in Vals Index and Agent Arena, with task costs approximately 50–80% lower than leading competitor models. The xhigh level provides deeper, multi-step reasoning for the most challenging engineering, analytical, and scientific tasks. At this level, Neon achieves its maximum score, with task costs at least 36% lower than competitors at their maximum level.

Cybersecurity Task Speed and Cost

As agents for programming become more widespread, it's important to write secure code from the outset. In all complexity categories of AutoBaxBench, Gemini 4 Neon writes more secure code than Carbon. For complex tasks, it shows the best result, 76%.

System security also requires finding vulnerabilities before attackers do. Companies have long used penetration testing for this. In WebExploitBench v1.2, Neon shows the best result, 51.26%, at half the cost compared to large competitor models.

Neon for cyber assists at all stages of securing existing code, from vulnerability discovery and risk assessment to remediation. The model handles these tasks faster and cheaper than large models of the same caliber.

  • When searching for code vulnerabilities, Neon achieves the best result in SEC-bench Pro, 90.5%, at approximately half the task cost.

  • To decide which vulnerabilities to fix first, specialists check if they can be exploited in practice. In ExploitGym, Neon performs at the level of leading models and uses half the tokens compared to Carbon.

Safety and Reliability

In Gemini 4 Neon, we have advanced the safety mechanisms of Gemini 4. We have maintained Gemini 4 Carbon's level of protection against serious misuse and prompt injections, while also improving the model's ability to act within the user's intentions. For long-running production tasks, we focused on three areas.

Protection Against Misuse

Neon makes advanced model capabilities more accessible through speed and lower cost. Therefore, we have implemented real-time protection against cyber and bio-security misuse at the Carbon level. At the same time, Neon is much less likely to refuse legitimate tasks in these areas. For example, the model refused 1.98% of tasks in Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 0% of tasks in Terminal-Bench-Science.

Adherence to Instructions and Task Boundaries

During internal use for programming, the reasoning and action monitoring system noted fewer deviations from user intentions with Neon than with Carbon, which already had a low rate. This indicates that Neon can adhere more precisely to user intentions. The same monitoring protects users in external services.

We have trained Neon to handle multi-step work, perform required steps, rely on actual results, and adhere to user-defined boundaries. In Terminal-Bench 4.0, the model matched Carbon's result of 0.0% test bypass cases. In an internal evaluation of programming behavior, Neon missed required steps in only 2.45% of tasks. This is the lowest rate among Gemini 4 models. In our agent safety tests, 0.09% of actions diverged from user intentions, and the model passed the agent honesty check in 99.2% of cases.

In agent behavior tests across different effort levels, Neon was 43–56% less likely to leave parts of the work unfinished and 15–35% less likely to exceed the assigned scope compared to Carbon.

Protection Against Indirect Prompt Injections

In the Gray Swan IPI test, Neon is better than other models in its class at resisting malicious instructions hidden in web pages, emails, documents, or code that the model reads. With real-time protection and at the maximum reasoning level, Neon blocked all attacks in this test. Previously, only other Gemini models showed a 0.0% attack success rate in this test.

Analysis and Research in the Gemini App

In the Gemini app, the Neon model is suitable for tasks requiring both deep reasoning and quick responses.

  • Neon provides clear, structured answers in simple language and more accurately processes uploaded tables, PDFs, and photos. The model generates reports and summaries from extensive materials and finds necessary information in Google applications, including Gmail and Google Docs.

  • The model frequently uses information from various web sources to keep answers on rapidly changing topics up-to-date. For example, it can compare prices from multiple vendors before a major purchase or prepare a detailed report on the latest market data.

  • The "Tasks" function in Gemini can now run on Neon. The model handles complex multi-step instructions faster and at a lower cost than larger models, allowing more work to be done within the same limits. For instance, Neon can analyze utility bills to determine why charges have increased or cross-reference receipts and documents to assist with refund negotiations.
