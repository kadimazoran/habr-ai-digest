Russian businesses may face a new charge for fraud prevention. According to a Forbes article, the government has supported a fee for accessing the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MVD) databases – 50 rubles for each completed information exchange session. For companies with millions of clients, this is not a symbolic amount but a significant operational cost.

This is not the first unpleasant surprise for businesses: in July 2023, the MVD disabled the service for checking invalid passports, which companies had used for over seven years. Now, the focus is on the System of Interdepartmental Electronic Interaction (SMEV), through which banks and other organizations obtain information from state information systems. This article explores existing alternatives and whether businesses can do without requests to the MVD.

What exactly will be charged for?

According to the draft government decree approving the procedure for businesses to request information from the MVD, a fee of fifty rubles will be charged for obtaining information:

  • On the validity of Russian passports;

  • On the registration of citizens at their place of residence and stay;

  • On the validity of a residence permit or temporary residence permit;

  • On the registration and migration records of foreign citizens.

The payment is intended for each completed information exchange session, and the projected revenue for the federal budget is around 120 billion rubles per year.

Client Verification is Mandatory

Federal Law No. 115-FZ obliges financial organizations to identify clients before onboarding: establish personal details, identity document details, and confirm their authenticity. For violating identification requirements, the Bank of Russia can fine a bank up to 0.1% of its own funds, as well as restrict certain banking operations for up to six months.

Telecommunications operators have a similar obligation enshrined in Article 44 of the Law "On Communications": subscriber information must be verified before providing services. Failure to verify or non-compliance with the established procedure for verification carries a fine of up to 500,000 rubles.

It turns out that refusing identification procedures is not an option – but from all of them? And here we come to a problem, very well articulated by Andrey Emelin, Chairman of the National Financial Market Council (NFMC): if the fee is eventually introduced, banks will start saving on non-mandatory, additional document checks.

Against the backdrop of a tenfold increase in fraud with forged documents, this is unacceptable. According to a study by Smart Engines and the law firm INTELLECT, in 2025, the number of new crimes registered by the MVD under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation increased by 338% to 1739 cases – a seven-year high.

This creates a dilemma: significantly increase the costs of verifying client passports or face even greater losses from fraud.

What Should Businesses Do?

Legislation does not always require verifying client passports specifically through MVD databases. Indeed, in some cases, it is directly prescribed by law; however, businesses can conduct a significant portion of document verification independently. This approach allows for a reduction in the number of paid requests without abandoning client identification and fraud counteraction.
