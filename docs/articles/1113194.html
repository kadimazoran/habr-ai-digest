Volvo employees in Sweden have started training Aeon humanoid robots for work at the car manufacturing plant in Ghent, Belgium. The company expects the robots to take over the most monotonous and physically demanding tasks.

The Ghent plant is the first Volvo facility to test Aeon robots, developed by Swedish company Hexagon Robotics. Staff have shown the robots how to precisely install components in two special fixtures. Aeon learns by copying human actions.

Once the robots have mastered this operation, the team will move on to the next stage of training. In particular, Aeon will be taught to work with multiple parts, move around the production floor, and interact with the plant's automated equipment.

Aeon stands about 165 cm tall and weighs 60 kg. The robot's payload capacity does not exceed 10 kg. The robot uses a wheeled base to move around the factory floor at a speed of just over 8 km/h.

In the first half of 2027, Volvo plans to deploy more robots at the Ghent facility for an expanded pilot project. The company wants Aeon to start working on the production line by mid-next year.

A month earlier, Volvo announced plans to lay off 2,500 employees worldwide, including 2,000 workers in Sweden. However, the company does not link these layoffs to the introduction of robots.
