Hello, Habr!

We, Smart Engines and the law firm INTELLECT, have completed our annual – already the fourth in the series – report on fraud involving forged documents. The results indicate a serious deterioration of the situation. 

In 2025, the number of crimes registered by the Ministry of Internal Affairs under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation increased by 338% year-on-year, from 397 to 1,739 cases, reaching a maximum value over the last seven years. The minimum estimate for the volume of the illegal market for forged identity documents reached 2.6 billion rubles.

We will discuss the scale of document fraud, new schemes involving generative AI, and, of course, technologies for detecting forgeries.

Key Observations and Figures 

Let's start with the numbers. We studied statistics from the Ministry of Internal Affairs, data from the Judicial Department of the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation, and court cases from 2019-2025 to understand how the scale of document fraud has changed, which forgeries are most common, and what it costs businesses.

Here's what we found:

  • In 2025, there was a sharp increase in crimes related to document forgery. The number of new crimes registered by the Ministry of Internal Affairs under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation increased by 338% – from 397 to 1,739 cases, reaching a seven-year high.

  • The illegal market for forged identity documents is worth billions of rubles. According to minimum estimates, the volume of this market in 2025 was about 2.6 billion rubles.

  • The real cost of document fraud is significantly higher than the direct damage. The total material damage established by courts in the investigated cases in 2025 is estimated at approximately 1.8 billion rubles. However, an account opened with a forged passport can also be used in drop schemes for transferring and cashing out stolen funds. Even if the bank does not directly lose money, such cases incur additional costs for investigations and checks, and also increase regulatory and reputational risks.

  • Every second of the 10,942 individuals convicted under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation in 2025 was a foreigner. Of these, 5,408 were citizens of CIS countries, and 66 were citizens of other states. 12 convicted individuals had no citizenship at all.

  • The forgery of documents related to migration has noticeably increased. The number of detected forged CIS passports and residence permits grew by 41% year-on-year, and their share among examined forged documents increased from 12% in 2024 to 18% in 2025.

  • Malefactors are moving from forging individual documents to creating an entire fictitious identity. In complex fraud schemes, passports, driver's licenses, SNILS (individual insurance account number), powers of attorney, and other documents under a fictional or someone else's name are used simultaneously, allowing them to maintain a single legend through multiple verification stages.

  • Fraudsters have learned to gain access to other people's bank accounts bypassing the bank's document verification. In one of the examined cases, a forged passport was presented to a mobile operator, where verification procedures were less stringent. After obtaining a SIM card with it and linking it to the victim's bank account, the fraudsters were able to regain access to the internet banking and steal 4.45 million rubles.

  • Generative AI is expanding the toolkit for identity forgery. In addition to classic image editing, fully synthetic documents, generative replacement of text, photos, signatures, and seals, as well as face swap, morphing, and synthetic selfies are used.

  • Modern document fraud undermines trust between citizens and businesses and requires a comprehensive approach to restore it. Malefactors combine stolen personal data, forged documents, synthetic images, and account takeover methods, bypassing individual security mechanisms. In these conditions, businesses need systems that ensure trust at all stages of customer interaction: from verifying document authenticity and identity to protecting personal data from leaks and transfer to third parties.

Statistics of Crimes Involving Forged Documents

In 2025, the number of new crimes registered by the Ministry of Internal Affairs under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation increased by a record 338% compared to 2024 and amounted to 1,739 cases. This is the highest figure in the last seven years.

To assess the dynamics, only the indicator of new crimes registered in each reporting year was considered from the Ministry of Internal Affairs statistics. This approach allows us to exclude the influence of crimes from previous years, including those not previously disclosed, and to compare precisely the number of new registered cases over the same periods.

The number of convicted individuals under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation increased in 2025 by 0.6% compared to 2024 and amounted to 10,942 people. A positive trend has been maintained for the third consecutive year. Over seven years, from 2019 to 2025, the cumulative number of convicted individuals exceeded 69,000 people.

The sharp increase in the number of forgery-related crimes in 2025 can be characterized as a boom, while the number of individuals convicted by courts remained at the same level. This means that the justice system has not yet reacted to the surge in document forgery crimes. Thus, the increase in the number of convicted individuals following the boom in forgeries recorded by the Ministry of Internal Affairs may manifest in the statistics of subsequent years.

Demographic and Other Characteristics of Individuals Convicted for Document Forgery in 2025

A total of 10,942 individuals were convicted under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation. To understand who most often becomes a defendant in such cases, let's examine their main characteristics: citizenship, gender, age, education level, place of residence, and method of committing the crime by the number of participants.

Citizenship 

Out of the total number of convicted individuals, about half (50%) are foreign citizens (5,456 individuals from CIS countries, 66 from other states), and 12 are stateless persons.

Gender

Among all convicted individuals, the absolute majority are men (85.6%). Women convicted of document forgery are an order of magnitude fewer: 1,578 or 14.4%.

Age of convicted individuals at the time of the crime

In 52.2% of cases, individuals forging documents were between 25 and 39 years old. However, criminals both significantly younger and significantly older are encountered. There were even "forgers" among minors: 18 individuals (about 0.2%). 144 individuals (1.3%) were aged 60 and over.

Education level of convicted individuals

48.3% of convicted individuals (5,284 people) had only a general secondary education. Another 10.4% (1,136 people) had no education or only primary/basic general education. 3,418 and 1,104 people had secondary vocational (31.2%) and higher (10.1%) education, respectively.

Registration at place of residence 

The majority of convicted individuals – 60.2% (6,584 people) – were permanent residents of the locality where the crime was committed. Another 38.2% (4,178 people) did not have permanent registration in this locality. 1.3% (138 people) were homeless individuals, and 0.4% (42 people) were refugees and forced migrants.

Method of committing the crime by number of participants

Individuals convicted under Article 327 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation acted individually in 98.1% of cases – there were 10,734 such individuals. The remaining 1.9% (208 people) committed the crime in a group, and about 1% of them (2 people) – as part of an organized criminal group.

Most Common Types of Forged Documents 

Driver's licenses continue to constitute the largest share of forged documents. The top 3 most frequently forged documents in Russia include driver's licenses (41%), CIS passports and residence permits (18%), and Russian passports (16%):

  • Driver's Licenses 
    Driver's licenses have been the most common type of forged document for several years now: in 2025, they accounted for almost every second detected forgery. Court practice shows a typical scenario: forged licenses are ordered remotely, with the manufacturer receiving a photo and personal data, payment is made via transfer, and the finished document is received by mail or delivery service. In cases examined by courts, the cost of such "licenses" ranged, for example, from 30,000 to 80,000 rubles.

  • Russian Passports
    A forged Russian citizen's passport allows fraudsters to act in another person's name and gain access to services and legally significant operations requiring identity verification. It can be used to obtain loans and other financial services, register SIM cards, purchase airline or train tickets, and commit other fraudulent acts. Slightly less often – to enter into an inheritance or to gain access to closed administrative-territorial formations (ZATO).

  • CIS Passports and Residence Permits 
    Forged CIS passports are primarily used in schemes related to the legalization of stay for foreign citizens in Russia: for migration registration, obtaining residence permits, and other documents confirming the right to stay and work in the country. A characteristic feature of detected cases is relatively simple methods of forgery, including replacing or re-pasting a photo in a genuine document.

Financial Scale of Crimes with Forged Documents 

Document forgery leads not only to legal but also significant financial consequences. According to expert estimates, in 2025, the volume of the black market for forged identity documents was about 2.6 billion rubles, the total damage established by courts was 1.8 billion rubles, and the average damage per case was 1.4 million rubles. These figures are calculated based on judicial practice and are estimates, as they only consider cases with established financial amounts and do not reflect the full scale of the problem.

It is important to understand that fraud with forged documents causes businesses not only direct financial damage. Successful identity substitution can trigger a whole chain of illegal actions, the consequences of which manifest months or even years later. For example, an account opened with a forged passport can be used in drop schemes for transferring and cashing out stolen funds. Even if the bank does not lose money directly, it faces additional costs and risks.

The real cost of fraud consists of four components: direct financial losses, regulatory sanctions, operational costs, and reputational damage. A bank may incur losses due to theft of funds or issuance of loans to fraudsters, face regulatory orders and fines for violations in customer identification. Additional resources are required for incident investigation, verification of suspicious transactions, settlement of claims from victims, and improvement of internal controls.

Moreover, the consequences of fraud do not always appear immediately. Financial losses may be discovered months later, regulatory sanctions after an audit by the Bank of Russia, and reputational damage may accumulate as incidents repeat. Therefore, the absence of direct theft does not mean the absence of damage: even a single forged document can lead to lengthy proceedings and significant indirect costs for businesses.

How Forged Documents and Synthetic Identities Are Created

We propose to divide all forged documents into two broad categories: physical, related to the creation or alteration of the material carrier, and digital. The latter, in turn, can be created using classic image editing tools or generative AI technologies. In practice, these methods can be combined within a single forgery.

Physical Document Forgery

Physical forgeries involve direct interference with the material carrier. A fraudster may create a document entirely, illegally personalize a genuine blank form, or alter an already issued document – replace a photo, personal data, individual pages, or security features.

Counterfeit

Complete creation of a forged document imitating the form, seal, materials, and security features

Stolen Blank

Illegal personalization of a genuine unfilled blank

Pseudo / Fantasy Document

A document that visually resembles an official one but does not exist or is not recognized as an official document

Forgery 

Photo replacement, page replacement, overlay, Forgery of individual elements, Interference with the security layer

Digital Document Forgery

Photo Editors 

The most accessible way to create a digital forgery is to alter a document image in a photo editor. This does not require creating a new physical carrier: the fraudster works with a photo or scan of a genuine document, replacing individual data – the owner's photo, full name, date of birth, document number, or other fields.
