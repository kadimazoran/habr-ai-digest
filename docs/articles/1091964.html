The short answer

  • Text in the frame: both rendered Cyrillic and numbers cleanly, but 2.1 performed the task more accurately — 10 points out of 10 versus 9. The only point 2.1 lost was an extra word that wasn't in the prompt.

  • Infographics: a draw in terms of facts. Eight labels out of eight, eight callouts hitting the mark for both. The layout is neater in 2.1, but a title I didn't ask for only appeared in hers.

  • A scene with three characters: 2.1 is cleaner here — not a single extra detail, the attributes are assigned correctly.

  • The most interesting part: the main point of the release, the increase in infographic accuracy from 0.179 to 0.521, is not visible in a single diagram. Both versions rendered it without factual errors.

What is the model and what is claimed for it

Nano Banana 2.1 is an update to Nano Banana 2, announced by Google on October 6, 2026. Both models are from the Flash line: 2.1 runs on Gemini 3.6 Flash, and 2 runs on Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. The flagship Nano Banana Pro remains on Gemini 3 Pro Image. Further discussion is only about the first two: 2.1 was released as a replacement for 2, not as a separate line.

What is stated in the model's card and Google's materials:

  • improved image quality and realism at all supported resolutions;

  • improved rendering of text and infographic layout;

  • consistency of characters and objects with multiple edits, i.e., in several consecutive steps;

  • wide panoramas and aspect ratios up to 8:1;

  • up to 14 reference images in one request, preserving up to four characters and ten objects;

  • access to Google Search when the model depicts real objects;

  • reasoning levels minimal, medium, and high, with medium being the default;

  • mask editing: the user selects an area, and only that area is changed.

Of the entire list, the mask seems the most practical. It flickered in the Gemini interface back in March, but only became a working feature now, and version 2 doesn't have it in this form. The mask was not tested in my test: all three tasks were about generation from scratch.

The table shows two things, and the second is more important than the first. The Flash model surpassed its own Pro version in all categories, which is contrary to the usual order: usually the junior line catches up to the flagship, not overtakes it. And the largest gaps are where it's not about aesthetics, but about discipline — consistency of multiple characters and infographic accuracy.

A pause is needed here. There are no independent runs of these numbers as of October 8, 2026. The Decoder, Notebookcheck, and Unite.AI all retell the same Google table, and Notebookcheck states directly: "Independent tests are not yet available." Everything found from third-party authors is comparisons on a few prompts without methodology and without scoring. Therefore, I will now check not the numbers, but three things that follow from them.

How I compared

  • The prompts are identical verbatim, one run per task for each model. Generations are in SYNTX, where both models are available in the same interface: this way, the same task text is sent to both versions without switching between services and without memory edits.

  • Evaluation is a checklist "yes/no", without scales. Items are binary: the label matches character by character or not, the callout hits the detail or not.

  • The evaluator is one person — me. The comparison was not blind; I knew which model was which, so the report contains no "I like it better," only what can be verified from the pictures.

  • I did not set or check the resolution and reasoning level, nor whether the interface provides them. The conditions for these two parameters are unknown. This is a point for verification, not a fact.

  • Generation time was not measured. There will be nothing about speed here.
