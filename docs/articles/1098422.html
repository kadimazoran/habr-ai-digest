This is MuFoEd. I'll introduce you to it a little later. For now, I'll tell a short story that, I'm sure, is no longer news. I was missing a couple of features in the Windows notepad. And I didn't go looking for a replacement program, as I would have done before. I went to AI, described my woes, and half an hour later I had the program I needed, created from scratch.

But was it from scratch? And is this model so great that it wrote such a complex program so quickly? It would seem so. But, no. It made this program by standing on the "shoulders of giants" - a bunch of created and debugged libraries made by others, I'll emphasize this, people. And learning from an even larger pile of code made by people.

Is this convenient? Absolutely, yes. And this means that a bunch of SaaS projects, a bunch of software will never be sold again. Because for the price of a subscription to this software, you can create an identical solution, but with the specific features you need. Here I will be objected to that AI writes code with bugs, and debugging and maintaining such code will be more expensive. Starting from a certain level of complexity - yes. But with each release of frontier models, this level will be higher and higher. And something simple enough for personal use? Yes. Something niche enough that no one has really done yet, as there is no demand? Yes.

There is a lot of hype now about "We replicated Photoshop," "We rewrote Excel." Why doesn't this work? Not in the sense that it doesn't work as a program. But in the sense that it doesn't work as an idea. Because making a project and making a product are "two big differences." I've read AI evangelist communities where they say, "Finally, we've defeated the cursed corporations, serves them right! I won't pay for Adobe subscription anymore!" And they have their own truth.

But I've also read discussions in designer and artist communities that say something different. They don't deny these programs, but they say that they don't always do 100% of what's needed and how it's needed. That a subscription gives them support, updates. That the main thing here is the pipeline refined over years and buttons that have been in their places for years. Professionals find it easier to overpay but use a familiar and reliable product. So, the niche for complex solutions and products remains. And where the result is simple and deterministic, assembling a product from ready-made bricks for a specific user is quite suitable. This leads to my manifesto:

AI Utility Manifesto

AI is very good at programming according to clear specifications. Let's give it a chance to prove itself. Are you a specialist in some field? Do you have unused tokens left in your subscription window before reset? Do you know the pain points in your field? Are there libraries to rely on, but not all of them, or they are not good enough? Create a good library. Give the AI a specification. Let it write tests. Let it plan. Let it create acceptance conditions. Let another model cross-check this code and conduct an audit. You will be able to check the result yourself.

Be sure to write a demonstrator program. This will help identify all non-obvious aspects. Use it yourself, tailor it to yourself. Don't create a product unless you are a guru in this field - you won't succeed. Because a product is a very complex thing that requires exponentially more effort.

Don't chase after a product. A product means support, updates, bug fixes, reputation, competition, sales, taxes, lawyers, payments, people, teams, sales, returns, marketing, strategies. I could go on for a long time, but do you really need it?

Meet MuFoEd

Having tasted the creation of programs for myself, I decided to undertake a more extensive work that would cover my needs, a kind of "all-in-one" combo. The result is a 150 MB monster, but a cute one. I named it MUlti FOrmat EDitor. That is, MuFoEd. I won't sell you this application, you don't need it. I will sell the idea. I've gathered a document editor and converter (DOCX, PDF, RTF, DjVu) under one roof with support for local AI via ollama. It also includes a simple image editor that can also use local AI and works with PSD format. Plus, a gallery for viewing and batch processing images, with the ability to view 3D files. And a file manager a-la Total Commander.

Currently, this covers 80+% of everything I work with on my computer and that makes sense to include in this combo. Yes, there won't be a browser. There won't be wallets that require enhanced security. There will be a couple more additions like an SQLite client and a viewer for unityasset packages, maybe something else. And these are my needs. You will have yours. Try to create something for yourself. It will pleasantly surprise you. And any additional functionality will take from 5 minutes to an hour, depending on the complexity. By the way, along the way, I found out that there is no native unrar, only a wrapper for a C++ library. Now there is, I haven't released it yet, but I'll get to it in the coming days. Maybe someone needs it.

Conclusion

Very soon, creating your own SaaS for sale will not be realistic. Only a very serious product with a very large team, with exclusive know-how. The same applies to any other utilities and small programs. On the one hand, this kills such markets. On the other hand, it's wonderful, because everyone will be able to create the programs they need and that solve their specific tasks.

MuFoEd is written in Rust, here is its repository https://github.com/rxdevebeo/MuFoEd

Most likely, you don't need it. Although during the process, a library for working with DOCX in Strict format and converting some other formats to it was created, I assume I'm not the first here. I'm not proud of creating such a combo, it's a simple task. But creating the library was serious work. I hope it's useful for someone. Therefore, create libraries. Very carefully and responsibly. Under the most open licenses. People will thank you.

And welcome to the era of new software!
