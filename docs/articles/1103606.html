Nano Banana 2 Lite is a spartanly budget-friendly model. Let's test it on generating assets for a pixel art economic strategy game.

For a handful of cents

Nano Banana 2 Lite is a champion of cheapness, at least within the Banana family – according to some data, one generation costs as little as 1.2 ₽. At the same time, it's a very fast model, taking 3-5 seconds to produce one image, which significantly outperforms even the quite fast Nano Banana Pro.

Moreover, Banana 2 Lite strives to deliver output of the highest quality its neurons can manage. However, generations are only possible at 1K resolution. Let's see how it handles creating assets and sprites for a top-down view pixel art strategy game.

#1 Grassy Plain Tile

The grass texture turned out quite standard and acceptable. If you generate about 30 of these or add more variation manually, they'll probably be enough to cover hectares of the game world where a pixelated wild lawn is needed.

#2 Plowed Field Tile

This texture also turned out quite convincing. Judging by the initial impressions, the Lite version draws no worse than its more advanced sibling models.

#3 Forest Tile

And the forest can serve as a standard texture, where, for example, bandit gangs could be settled for side quests involving restoring constitutional feudal order.

#4 River Tile with Bridge

Two variants of the same location were accidentally generated. The second one looks prettier.

#5 Rocky Hills Tile

The wild rocky landscape from the Lite model is not bad. If we add more variation, we can create a location with mines, where the repeatedly convicted bandits from the forest area can be sent later.

#6 Village Road Tile

The road turned out strangely too transparent, with a turnip stuck to the side. Why Banana Lite decided to do this is a mystery.

#7 Town Hall

Here, the model got a bit confused about the historical context. Initially, the assets were planned for a medieval setting, but it plunged headfirst into some kind of Brabantine Gothic of the early modern period.

#8 Market

"The bird market... it barks and it sings." The medieval market, even in a friendly pixel art guise, turned out too modern, dynamic, and a bit carnival-like. Plus, Banana Lite wasn't supposed to generate tiny NPCs.

#9 Brewery

No complaints here. Except that it inexplicably drew it in cross-section.

#10 Mill

And this is a very beautiful, soulful little model. You can really feel the Nano Banana lineage!

#11 Barracks

The barracks with a training dummy also turned out quite well. When Banana Lite switches to darker colors, it seems like its drawings gain a new dimension of character.

#17 Main Menu

Interestingly, Banana Lite wasn't instructed to enter the game's name – it came up with it itself and proudly drew it over the menu. What's pleasing is that it consistently works well with text, which is likely why it's so popular among designers.

#20 Mini-map and Control Buttons

The mini-map looks like a pretty cool template, except for the rewind button that appeared inexplicably. The owl in the nightcap was drawn by Banana itself, by the way, and it's quite an interesting detail – is the AI developing its own abstract thinking?

Conclusion

In principle, for a more than budget-friendly model, Nano Banana 2 Lite performs commendably. Its generations undoubtedly have flaws and a bit of improvisation. But in places, like the sleepy owl, this improvisation looks good.

Considering the ridiculously low cost of generations, the characteristically high quality of the Nano Banana family, and its lightning-fast speed, the model can certainly be used for creating references and prototypes, or even for generating background/auxiliary assets that won't bear too much load.
