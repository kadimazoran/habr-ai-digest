Everyone is discussing whether AI will take IT jobs. I have no doubt we are only at the beginning of this difficult path. And finding a new job was already a quest with a star.

Seeing how "automation" decimates entire departments, I created an app that acts as a navigator to a safety net and goals. It provides peace of mind through a money management system and autopilot, rather than another calculator and analytics of where money went.

AI automates routine, but companies cut costs on people

AI automates the routine of various IT roles. However, due to business problems (purchasing power/key interest rates), automation does not lead to increased work efficiency; it leads to reduced operating costs.

For some time, I myself worked on increasing employee "efficiency," and the main KPI was the number of "automated" SHE (Safety, Health, Environment) processes.

AI is the future, but the future with it is uncertain

I'm not scared of AI, but I am concerned about one question: how many months of expenses will I have if my salary suddenly disappears?

I had my own experience. With several goals, a couple of trips, and a strategic objective (real estate), I didn't understand how to allocate money so that I would have enough for everything during that time. I didn't understand if I would achieve my goals within the expected timeframe at the current spending rate, and how a particular large purchase would affect them.

But you need to start with the basics - security

Forming a safety net = mandatory monthly expenses * how many months you anticipate looking for a job in your role.

Estimating how many months it will take to find a job is more or less easy, but calculating mandatory expenses in your head is already a problem.

I derived the following categories for myself:

  1. Loans and installments (mortgage, credit, buy-now-pay-later) + Rent (if you rent) - things you can't opt out of

  2. Planned expenses (utilities, groceries, subscriptions and communication, transport, hobbies and health, marketplaces, restaurants and entertainment)

  3. Wallet - for unforeseen purchases and light hedonism.

It's best to take the amounts as averages from your bank statement over 2-3 months, *not the most economical month.*

110,500 * 6 months = 663,000. "6 salaries" from a salary of 250,000 = 1.5 million.

If you plan for 6 months to find a job, the safety net in this example is 663k. Meanwhile, if you calculate "6 salaries" from a salary of 250k, it comes out to 1.5 million. In my opinion, the first figure looks achievable, while the second is more likely to be demotivating and cause negativity!

Why classic expense tracking doesn't help

I've tried various budgeting apps. Almost all are designed the same way: record your expenses, and we'll show you where the money went – which doesn't really bring you closer to security/a safety net.

At the same time, I didn't want to distribute money across a bunch of bank accounts; I wanted to have one account with the maximum interest rate. I didn't keep spreadsheets because it's super boring and very inconvenient to maintain – although Excel is a possible solution.

What I did

Kubysh which:

  • allocates each paycheck into 2 "piles": what can be spent and what will go towards savings for goals

  • between paychecks, it shows: the daily budget, whether there will be enough until the next paycheck, and the spending rate – whether there will be a surplus or deficit by the next salary.

  • calculates the amounts and timelines for achieving all goals and the safety net.

  • forms envelopes within the app. Your money stays in one account with the maximum interest rate and moves you closer to your goal

Instead of a conclusion

Thus, I managed to create a kind of financial navigator that shows the route to my safety net, vacations, and real estate. As a consequence, my anxiety and stress levels have decreased, and life has become more comfortable.
