But I stumbled on the parenthesis: I had heard about these processors for a long time, hadn't looked into them seriously, and didn't have any numbers in front of me. I checked. I delved in. And the deeper I dug, the clearer it became: this processor deserved its own article.

History. In 1992, as state-funded science was visibly collapsing, a group of engineers in Moscow registered NTC "Module" (Unified State Register of Legal Entities, russianelectronics) and embarked on a venture that was unusual for its time — processors for matrix computations. They bet on an idea that was ahead of the industry: not to chase a universal processor, but to build hardware where the operand precision could be reconfigured on the fly — bit by bit, or in full words. In 1998, the year of the default, they patented and assembled the first Russian neuroprocessor ("National Defense") — according to TAdviser's assessment, one of the world's first 64-bit signal processors. Within a year, the NM6403 was already in serial production — by Samsung using a 0.5-micron process (EURASIP), and its core was licensed by Fujitsu (Elektronika NTB): a Russian processor architecture went to Japan, a rare occurrence.

Then came the years when no one in Russia talked about neural networks, and the word "neurochip" meant nothing. "Module" didn't abandon the line: the architecture lived on two legs — the neuromatrix core awaited its hour, while classical signal processing kept the company afloat: radars, imaging (NM6407 datasheet), onboard electronics for aviation and space. Model followed model, the fourth generation of cores replaced the third, and in 2019, the flagship NM6408 was released: 28 nm, 21 cores, serial production (Habr, 2019). By the time "neurochip" became a buzzword in 2023, "Module" had had it in its documents for a quarter of a century.

Hardware. The price list offers three formats on two chips. NM Card is a PCIe slot card: inside is one K1879VM8Ya — 16 neurocores at a gigahertz plus five ARM cores for support, 5 GB of memory; typical power consumption is 10.5 W — a figure from the MLPerf benchmark, meaning it was measured using a generally accepted methodology, not their own (module.ru). NM Quad is a server board with four such chips: 64 neurocores, 20 GB of memory, 2 TFLOPS in FP32, up to 80 W under load; the company claims it has no domestic market analogues (module.ru, 2023 announcement). NM Stick is a compute module based on the junior chip in the form factor of a real USB flash drive: 87 by 34 millimeters, less than a hundred grams (module.ru). All of this runs under the unified NMDL software, and in the NM Desktop workstation, the cards fit alongside domestic "Elbrus" processors — according to the manufacturer's description, it's a machine with no imported components left (module.ru). But the main feature of the hardware isn't the sum of cores: the chip has four independent compute clusters — they can be combined for a single task or partitioned for separate tasks on each. We'll see later how this specific feature helped the Russian Railways project.

In terms of compute class, the NM6408 is on par with the Jetson Nano — the very one that dominated embedded vision and left the Russian market in March 2022 (Russian Railways project). Orin Nano declares 20–40 TOPS INT8 — there is no comparable public metric for the NM6408 at all: only 512 GFLOPS FP32, and they cannot be directly compared. The same applies to Ascend 310: 16 TOPS INT8 at eight watts — there's nothing to calculate who is "more efficient." So, the picture is this: the NM6408 is exactly what its datasheet says. An embedded AI class from 2019, brought to stable serial production.

Who else is trying. Before examining where it's already working, it's worth looking at the rest of the field — to understand the scale. Here's who in Russia in 2025–2026 announced their own AI chips:

  • "Altai-3" — a neuromorphic processor supported by Kaspersky Lab and the Kurchatov Institute: pilot batch in early 2026, serial production promised in 2027 (Habr, February 2026);

  • Sber's photonic chip — an optical compute unit for AI, shown at SPIEF in June 2026 as a working prototype: claimed performance is one billion matrix multiplication operations per second (Zen analysis). To understand the scale: a modern GPU delivers tens of teraops. This is a demonstrator, not a product;

  • FPI's tensor chiplet — Rospatent registered the topology of a 28nm die in May 2026 for accelerating neural networks. The source itself qualifies this: it's a "drawing, not a microchip," and serial production of 28nm in Russia is not expected before 2027 (Elec.ru);

  • "Baikal Electronics" and Yandex — announced work on their own AI accelerators (www1.ru, August 2026).

So far, for each direction, there are only plans, prototypes, and topologies. The only silicon in this table that can be bought and installed in an industrial system is from "Module." All others (Sber, T-Bank, MTS with Rubytech) are currently testing Chinese Huawei Ascend chips on Russian models — and that's a separate story.

Where it's already working. The best case study currently comes from Russian Railways. NIIS is building the "Chizh" complex: four cameras, RTSP streams, frame stitching, object detection on the tracks. Until 2022, it ran on Jetson Nano. After NVIDIA's departure, the question of replacement arose — and they chose NM Vision from "Module" (project description). What followed is an engineering story rarely seen in press releases. The first launch showed: Jetson Nano was about five times faster than NM Mezzo mini — due to TensorRT and FP16, which NeuroMatrix lacks (it has FP32/FP64). The YOLOv4 detector didn't even start — not all layers are supported by the accelerator, so they had to retrain on YOLOv5. And only after that did "Module's" architectural advantage come into play: the chip has four independent compute units, and they were launched in parallel as four separate models. Speed increased threefold — to 30 frames per second on YOLOv5n at 320×320. The project's results: speed matched Jetson Nano, and accuracy was higher.

Medicine: The "ModuleMed" software for recognizing viral pneumonia from X-rays started with 93% accuracy (Connect-WIT, February 2021), and a demonstrator for the neural network complex for Sechenov, Pirogov, and FMBA hospitals showed 98% (module.ru, March 2021). Industry: NM Pilot within Norilsk Nickel's "MAGMA" platform (project description) — monitoring camera status and video streams in mine conditions, certification for mining equipment in July 2026 (CNews). Servers: Fplus included "Vсход" on "Module" accelerators in the Ministry of Industry and Trade's registry and claims a production capacity of 35,000 accelerators per year (Vedomosti/crktuva). For scale: from January to September 2023, there were 16 registry purchases of AI servers for 930 million rubles; a year later, for the same period, there were 30 for 1.7 billion, and in the registry, besides "Vсход," there's essentially only one "Graviton" server.

Why "niche." These chips do one thing: run already trained networks through themselves. The training itself — adjusting weights based on examples — the architecture cannot handle, and tools for it do not exist. They are promised only in the new generation of cores, the timelines for which have already slipped: in 2022, three 5nm chips were announced for 2024–2026 — under a state contract for 7.63 billion rubles (CNews, Kommersant), and in 2025, the same chips were presented as "Aramis," "Porthos," and "Athos" with market entry in 2028–2030 (Technoomsk). For 2025–2026, the Ministry of Industry and Trade has charged "Module" penalties totaling 481.16 million rubles for four contracts; the company is appealing, citing sanctions (CNews, September 2026, russianelectronics). Since May 2024, Rosatom has been discussing the purchase of the design center — the status of negotiations remains unclear (Vedomosti). And to the most uncomfortable question — where is the 28nm die physically manufactured after 2022? — I found no answer in open sources. Experts confirm: producing a modern 12–14nm AI chip in Russia is currently impossible due to the lack of full-cycle foundries (www1.ru).

Niche is a position.

One person in the field is a warrior. Especially when the field is empty.

"Module" won't be part of training clusters, but where data cannot be taken outside the perimeter — critical infrastructure, onboard systems, medicine, mines — it is currently the only domestic neurochip line that is industrially operational.
