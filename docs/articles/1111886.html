The American newspaper Atlantic has outlined three theories for Jensen Huang's return to his positions as CEO, President, and a member of Nvidia's board of directors.

Huang resigned on November 12, 2026, amid a scandal related to the investigation by US intelligence agencies into Alice Huang, the head of the Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed. US intelligence views Alice Huang as a potential Chinese spy. Nvidia's management approved Huang's return on November 23, although Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones insisted that the company "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.".

Theory 1, according to Atlantic, is related to money. After Huang's resignation, Nvidia's stock fell by approximately 40% compared to its October peak, costing the company trillions. However, after the press release about Huang's reinstatement, the manufacturer's securities rose by 33%, and the S&P 500 index climbed by almost another 5%.

Huang was supported by some Nvidia employees, and a petition for his return was signed by over 200,000 people, including Elon Musk, Tim Sweeney, and other top managers. The financial board, which Huang had shaped for 30 years, yielded to financial logic, Atlantic writes.

"Gatsby is returning to the mansion, and the AI party is coming back to life with renewed vigor," the newspaper summarizes the first theory.

Theory 2 describes a convergence of interests. According to The New York Times, US President Donald Trump was notified about Huang's alleged involvement in Chinese espionage about a year ago. In May of this year, the President was informed again before his trip to Beijing. However, Trump still took Huang with him to China.

"For 11 days, while Huang was a private individual under investigation by the US Department of Justice and the US Securities and Exchange Commission, he was the most dangerous witness imaginable. He didn't have to defend his job, he didn't have to report to the board, his personal life was in ruins, and he had every reason to cut his own deal. People in such a position tend to tell investigators everything they know about everyone," Atlantic notes.

The newspaper points out that the situation within Nvidia was no better in terms of motivation. With interim CEO Colette Kress and an independent board of directors, there was nothing to prevent the company from opening its books and cooperating with the authorities.

Huang partially owes his job to Trump's public campaign, Atlantic believes. Semafor reports that the President called into an Nvidia board meeting to persuade management to reinstate Huang. Thus, Huang, Nvidia, and the White House want the same thing, Atlantic writes.

This does not necessarily mean that Huang will exercise his right not to testify against himself at the closed session on December 1st in the US House of Representatives Committee on Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China. The current CEO's refusal to answer questions could be a disaster for his company.

Theory 3: Nobody cares. A few years ago, every action in Huang's story would have caused a major scandal. A CEO resigns due to a relationship with a suspected spy. The President, aware of these suspicions, pushes for the CEO's return to the company, and the board of directors suddenly agrees, despite previously denying such a prospect.

Huang, Trump, the Nvidia board, and investors are betting that investigators will lose interest in the case, and Congress will receive a transcript of the conversation and move on to something else, believes Atlantic author Josh Tyrangiel. He concludes that this is a colossal risk, likely the last and best of the remaining strategies for the parties involved.
