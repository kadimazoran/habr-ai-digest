Reality and fantasy

In science fiction, computers are large stationary mainframes, because when writing a book or a movie script, the authors did not imagine computers any other way, or vice versa, compact self-propelled ones - the brains of approximately or completely anthropomorphic robots. Computers with which the mainframe terminal operator or the robot owner/operator communicates in ordinary human language, either in writing on the terminal or orally, if with a robot. In ordinary, I repeat, human language - with all its problems of ambiguity, understatement, the need to understand context, and in places, accompanying facial expressions and intonation of what is said, which even among people causes misunderstandings more than often, let alone, it would seem, the linear logic of binary computing devices. Yes, based on this possible misunderstanding between computer and human, more than one plot has been built, but there are even more plots based on human misunderstandings. Nevertheless, I want to focus on interaction using human language, not assembly, C with or without pluses, or even BASIC.

Reality, however, has long been more prosaic. The first tube computers were huge in size, power consumption, and cost, which is why they were produced in small batches, and their programming, although necessary in principle, existed somewhere on the periphery of budgets allocated for computerization. Then semiconductors, small, medium, and later large-scale integrated circuits, and later microprocessors - computers gradually became commonplace, first as mainframes, and later as personal ones, whether workstations or home-made gadgets for the entertainment of contemporary enthusiasts of any age and, primarily, teenagers of that time. These computers needed programs - for business, for entertainment, for anything. And so, gradually, programmers became massively in demand - like sorcerers using magical spells in programming languages. More computers - more users. More users - a wider range of required programs. A wider range - more programmers are needed. Programmers became so in demand that from an appendage to a large computer, they became the cornerstone of digital civilization, and in terms of income, they began to compete with doctors, lawyers, and drug dealers.

And suddenly - an Artificial Idiot. Because it doesn't quite reach Intelligence, but it looks just like the real thing. Suddenly, it became possible to speak to a computer in human language, with all its ambiguities. And, the further, the better, the computer is understood. This is what science fiction writers dreamed of, and this is what ordinary computer users lacked, who, without an external programmer, are completely, absolutely helpless, and limited by the software they already have from somewhere. And this ability - to speak to a computer in human language, is rapidly evolving from a stupid toy into a fully functional tool right now. If you are stuck at the level of early chatbots, then your experience is already severely outdated - everything has gone far ahead. What will happen tomorrow?

Of course, generating machine code from a human description is still far from a situation where you say a couple of words, answer clarifying questions if necessary, and get ready-made code in your chosen programming language or a compiled binary file that works and doesn't crash - and I foresee reasonable comments that in reality, it's not as good as it seems to an idealist far from software development, with examples of exactly how it's not so good. Because yes, in reality, it's still not that good, but much less bad than skeptics and haters say. But let's look at the issue more broadly (or wider?), not just at the moment. Today, we are clearly much closer to what science fiction writers wrote about in books and filmed in movies - that a computer helps a person without intermediaries in the form of spell-casting mages, but simply by executing voice commands, reasoning, and independently making decisions within the broad and relatively volatile framework of a prompt, but not the rigid logic of a finite automaton.

Will the profession of programmer become a thing of the past? Even in the distant future, it's unlikely, because for AI to work and develop, someone must develop and maintain it. Well, the chair under the "shit coders" is probably starting to shake literally today - let's not feel too sorry for them. Thanks to the development of AI, millions of non-programmers have written their first programs. They haven't become programmers because of this, they haven't acquired valuable experience and knowledge that would help them master the profession of a software developer, no. But they don't need it - they just need the computer to do what they want for work or entertainment. And they were able to do it (careful, recursion!) with the help of a computer. Because the computer is for the person, not the person for the computer. Reality is slowly converging with fiction. Isn't that wonderful? Isn't that what we were striving for when we opened, forgive the pathos, the computer era?
