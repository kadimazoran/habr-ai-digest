In China's Inner Mongolia, computing power requiring gigawatts of electricity is being planned. Nearby, wind and solar farms, storage, substations, and communication lines are being built. In Memphis, USA, xAI accelerated the launch of Colossus using an existing industrial building and its own gas generation. Both stories share a common engineering challenge: servers can be purchased faster than everything needed for their continuous operation can be prepared.

China is trying to align the placement of computing with the development of the national power system. This is not enough to place a data center next to wind turbines. It is necessary to ensure power during windless hours, dissipate heat, connect accelerators with a high-speed network, and find tasks for which the equipment will operate. It is at these transitions between industries that the true cost of AI is revealed.

Ulanqab is a convenient example of this approach. However, it cannot be taken as representative of all Chinese AI, nor Colossus as representative of all American infrastructure. We will compare specific solutions: what to move to cheap energy, how to obtain reliable power, and how to measure the result.

Data and documents were verified as of September 16, 2026. The indicators below have their own periods: there is no single synchronized report for all sites. Company statements are separated from statistics, and author's calculations are separated from measurements.

What China is Moving West

In February 2022, China launched the "East Data, West Computing" program — "东数西算". The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) outlined eight computing hubs and ten data center clusters. Among the hubs are eastern agglomerations, including Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei and the Yangtze River Delta, as well as Inner Mongolia, Guizhou, Gansu, and Ningxia. This is a distributed system with different roles for territories. NDRC Explanation dated February 17, 2022.

The east hosts large consumers of digital services, but land and power supply limit infrastructure expansion. Western regions offer resources for construction and generation. Hence the idea: part of the work can be done where it is cheaper to provide electricity, and the results can be transmitted via fiber optics.

The original document contains a caveat that is often lost in retellings. It is primarily proposed to move storage, backup, and processing that does not require an immediate response to the west. Services with strict latency requirements should be located closer to demand. NDRC explicitly included part of the AI inference tasks—the operation of an already trained model—in this category. Same document, section on workload placement.

For a developer, the difference is clear from two examples. Nighttime processing of a document archive can be sent to a remote cluster. A voice assistant that needs to respond quickly to a user requires a different service architecture. At the same time, even within one application, both tasks may exist: an interactive response now and recalculating the search index at night.

There is also a second scale of distance. Moving the entire training process to another region is one task. Distributing tightly coupled parts of a single training process across remote regions is significantly more complex. Intermediate data is constantly transferred between accelerators; latency and bandwidth begin to affect the execution time of each step.

Therefore, the "national computing network" does not mean that all Chinese GPUs have become one supercomputer. It allows for the distribution of suitable tasks among sites. Within a site, a high-performance network is still required. The importance of such connections is clearly visible in DeepSeek's technical report on co-designing models and hardware. DeepSeek, Insights into DeepSeek-V3.

What the Scale of Ulanqab Means

Ulanqab is located in Inner Mongolia, an autonomous region of the PRC, which should not be confused with independent Mongolia. In August 2026, "Keji Ribao" reported on over a hundred data center projects and agreements for more than five million standard racks. These two words alone—"agreements" and "standard"—change the meaning of the news. Original publication.

A Chinese standard rack is a unit for calculating power: 2.5 kW. This definition, citing a 2013 document, is provided in the official explanations of the Jiangsu Provincial Industry Department. To assess the real scale, it is more useful to look at the meters. From January to June 2026, Ulanqab's computing industry consumed 3.267 billion kWh. In this half-year of 4344 hours:

This results in approximately 0.75 GW of average load over the half-year. The average load over the half-year cannot be directly divided by the power commissioned by August and called the utilization rate: the dates and measurement boundaries do not coincide. However, this calculation already shows why the story of a "data center for five million racks" is misleading. We are looking at a large industrial cluster at various stages of construction.

One should not automatically convert local "172 thousand P" into the number of A100s or H100s. Petaflops is 10¹⁵ floating-point operations per second, but comparison requires specifying the precision, operating mode, and methodology. For example, for one A100, Nvidia indicates 9.7 teraflops in standard FP64 and 312 teraflops in tensor FP16 without sparsity. The description of operations has changed, but the hardware remains the same. Without a common methodology, a seemingly precise ranking of cities and supercomputers is unreliable. Why Electricity Attracts Computing

A company chooses a location based on the final price and energy availability. In an Economic Observer report dated August 14, 2026, the final price for some of Ulanqab's data centers is stated as approximately 0.358 yuan per kWh. The same publication mentions 0.3234 yuan as the electricity purchase price in August. These figures should not be mixed: the purchase price itself does not describe the entire consumer bill. This is information from a journalistic report, not a universal tariff offer for any future project. Report on Ulanqab's construction and economy.

Data centers came to the city with an already existing energy-intensive industry. According to the same report, their share in the city's electricity consumption was 5.2% in 2025 and 7.3% in January-July 2026. In the latter period, ferroalloys accounted for more than half of the consumption. Therefore, the large electricity consumption of the entire city cannot be entirely attributed to AI. Data with period and industry breakdown.

The sensitivity to tariffs can be easily estimated without assumptions about a specific operator. Let's take a hypothetical site with an average full load of 100 MW. In a normal year, it will consume 876 million kWh. A tariff difference of just 0.1 yuan will result in 87.6 million yuan per year. With a gigawatt of constant load, it's 876 million.

However, electricity is only part of the cost of AI. Purchased accelerators, memory, and network must be amortized regardless of whether the cluster performs useful work or is idle. If the equipment in one location is significantly underutilized, savings on kilowatt-hours may not compensate for downtime costs.

The cost of computing services can be simplified as follows:

equipment depreciation + electricity + network + operation ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── volume of useful work performed

For training, useful work is progress towards the required model quality. For inference, it is servicing requests with specified quality and latency. The same GPU pool can yield different economics depending on the software, request length, and queue organization.

Therefore, increasing power supply is a necessary but not sufficient condition for AI development. A cheap location wins when suitable accelerators arrive, they can be interconnected, and customers are found for them.

How to Combine Wind Turbines with Round-the-Clock Load

The scale of China's energy industry helps understand the idea of placing computing near generation. According to China's National Energy Administration, in 2025, the country added 120 GW of wind and 318 GW of solar capacity. The total installed capacity of wind and solar reached approximately 1840 GW. Their annual generation was about 2.3 trillion kWh. NEA 2025 Results.

It is fundamentally important to distinguish between power and energy here. Power indicates the rate of production or consumption, while energy is the result over time. A 1 GW solar farm does not provide a 1 GW data center with power 24/7. Even if its annual generation, along with other stations, is sufficient, hourly schedules still need to be matched.

Let's consider a hypothetical calculation. IT equipment continuously consumes 1 GW. Let's assume another 15% is required for cooling and other needs—this corresponds to a PUE of 1.15, which we will return to later. The entire site requires 1.15 GW, or approximately 10.1 TWh per year. Assuming an average capacity factor for wind turbines of 35%, about 3.3 GW of wind generation would be needed for this annual volume:

1.15 GW / 0.35 ≈ 3.3 GW

This is an educational estimate, not an energy supply project. It does not account for losses, grid limitations, or energy storage. And even with this amount of wind turbines, another source will be needed during calm periods.

Hence, the Chinese concept of "source-grid-load-storage," or 源网荷储. Its meaning is to design generation and consumption together. The Zhongjin Data site in Ulanqab has a specific example: 200 MW of wind and 100 MW of solar generation, as well as a storage system of 45 MW / 180 MWh. Specialists from the industry association CDCC described this configuration after visiting the project in July 2025. The scheme includes direct supply and connections to the main grid. CDCC Report on Visit.

Both characteristics of the battery are useful here. 45 MW is the power it can deliver, and 180 MWh is the energy reserve. The division gives four hours of operation at nominal power, excluding operational limitations. This is not four hours of autonomy for the entire data center: to conclude this, one needs to know its load. The storage system helps smooth out fluctuations and shift energy between hours, but it does not automatically become a reserve for several windless days.

Coal-fired generation therefore retains a role in the system. According to IEA estimates, in China in 2025, coal-fired power generation decreased by about 1.5%, although the commissioning of new coal capacity approached 80 GW. These figures can move in different directions: there are more new units, but each operates fewer hours on average. IEA, Global Energy Review 2026.

Inner Mongolia directly describes such a transition in official statements: maintaining power security while reducing the use of coal-fired units. For now, this is a policy direction, not a reason to consider coal reserves carbon-free. Fuel consumption is determined by how much and with what efficiency the stations actually generate. Regional Energy Administration, July 2026.

What Has Changed in Green Energy Regulations

The phrase "80% green electricity" can have several different meanings. It can describe the purchase of energy and certificates over a year, a physical direct line from a power plant, or the proportion of consumption that coincided with low-carbon generation by hour. These indicators answer different questions.

China's 2024 data center development plan indeed set a goal: by the end of 2025, the share of green electricity for new data centers in national computing hubs should exceed 80%. The scope is important: the document does not say that every operating AI server in the country is already receiving such energy. It allows for green deals and certificates and separately supports direct supply. NDRC Plan, item II.4.

If a solar farm produced as much energy in a year as a data center consumed, someone still has to provide power at night. The annual balance is useful for accounting for purchases, but it does not reveal the generation mix at a specific hour. The IEA therefore separately assesses the physical energy supply to data centers and warns that it differs from the contractual mix of operator purchases. Methodology in Energy and AI.

At the same time, it would be incorrect to reduce China's changes to just certificate trading. In May 2025, NEA clarified the rules for direct connection of generation to consumers. These include provisions for both standalone projects and projects that maintain a connection to the main grid. Having a dedicated line does not mean complete disconnection from the power system. Clarification of direct supply rules.

In 2026, the scheme was extended to several consumers, including industrial parks. In the June NEA clarification, the general project operator, responsibility for balancing, and hourly tracking of green energy based on meter readings are described. Obligations to pay for grid and system services are also maintained. NEA Clarification dated June 2, 2026.

For computing clusters, this opens up the possibility of coordinating the schedules of multiple sites with local generation. However, regulatory possibility does not yet prove that a specific campus is already ensuring round-the-clock matching of demand and generation. Such a conclusion requires operational data.

There is also a limit to the flexibility of computing itself. A task with a deadline can be put in a queue for a more convenient time. A running user session cannot be arbitrarily stopped until the next gust of wind. Even training, which allows for state saving, incurs costs for stopping and resuming. Flexibility that is beneficial to the power system must be compatible with the economics of computing.

Cold, Liquid Cooling, and Water

Ulanqab benefits from its climate. Local authorities report that for about ten months a year, sites can use natural cold. This advantage relates to dissipating heat externally; it does not eliminate the need for an internal cooling system design. Officially published description of conditions.

In a liquid cooling system, heat is first transferred from the chip to a circulating coolant, then through heat exchangers to an external loop and the environment. The final stage can use dry coolers, water evaporation, or a combination of solutions. Therefore, the phrase "servers are liquid-cooled" by itself does not indicate how much water a site consumes. The US Department of Energy separately describes a closed loop for equipment and a cooling tower that dissipates heat from the system. DOE Guide to Cooling Water Efficiency.

It is also necessary to distinguish between water that circulates in pipes and water that must be constantly replenished to replace losses. Evaporation helps dissipate heat but consumes water. Dry cooling reduces this need; its efficiency depends on the outside air temperature and the permissible coolant temperature.

For energy efficiency, PUE is usually cited:

PUE = total site energy / IT equipment energy

A PUE of 1.2 means that for every unit of energy for IT, there is an additional 0.2 units for other needs. These include cooling and power supply losses. PUE does not measure the usefulness of the computations performed and does not indicate water consumption. Definition of PUE and measurement boundaries.

Suppose identical equipment in two hypothetical data centers consumes 100 MW. With a PUE of 1.4, the total load is 140 MW; with a PUE of 1.15, it is 115 MW. The savings are 25 MW, or about 18% of the first site's total consumption. This is significant, but cold does not eliminate the main load of the servers themselves.

A separate indicator is needed for water, such as WUE: water consumption relative to IT equipment energy. Moreover, it should be specified whether only site needs are considered or also water for electricity production. In a US study of data center energy consumption, PUE and WUE are considered together: the choice of cooling can improve one indicator at the expense of the other. Berkeley Lab, 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report, section 4.

A clear hypothetical example: with a WUE of 0.5 liters per kWh and a constant IT load of 100 MW, this results in 1.2 million liters per day. This is an arithmetic illustration, not an assessment of Ulanqab or Colossus. Without a published water balance, water source, and seasonal measurements, comparing two cities based on climate alone proves little.

Why Dedicated Chips, Networks, and Algorithms are Needed

At the power system level, a data center appears as a load. At the model developer level, within it are several separate limitations: compute units, memory, data exchange, and software. An additional megawatt does not automatically resolve any of the last three.

Huawei CloudMatrix384 is indicative. In a technical paper by Huawei and SiliconFlow, a super-node consisting of 384 Ascend 910C accelerators and 192 Kunpeng processors, connected by the Unified Bus network, is described. The authors consider the joint use of computation, memory, and communications, as well as separate servicing of request processing and response generation stages. Serving Large Language Models on Huawei CloudMatrix384.

This is a more substantive example of China's strategy than a simple competition in the number of GPUs. Developers are trying to increase the efficiency of the entire system, including the software stack. However, the paper is written by project participants, and the results relate to the described configurations and workloads. It cannot be used to infer universal superiority over any Nvidia cluster or the portability of all existing software without modifications.

Another level of optimization is shown by DeepSeek-V3. The developers' report indicates 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours for the described training cycle and an estimated $5.576 million at a hypothetical rental price of $2 per GPU-hour. The authors explicitly exclude prior research and experiments from this sum. Therefore, this is not the cost of creating a company, a data center, or the entire technology. DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report, February 2025 version.

The same report describes training with mixed precision FP8, distribution of computations, and overlapping data exchange with useful work. The goal is to reduce the time expensive equipment waits for data transfer. The region's energy advantage and the algorithm's efficiency combine here: the former reduces the cost of operating equipment, the latter—the amount of resources needed for the result.

One of DeepSeek's specific techniques relates to experts—parts of the model selected to process a token. If the required experts are scattered across many server nodes, data must be sent to all those nodes. The developers limited the routing of a single token to a maximum of four nodes. This reduces exchange between servers, where connections are slower than internal ones. It turns out that the model architecture is changed considering the network structure, not just by adding more GPUs to it. DeepSeek, analysis of node-limited routing, section 4.3.

However, the success of a specific model does not prove that the country now needs fewer data centers. As the cost of a single request decreases, new users, longer responses, and more complex tasks may emerge. Future demand depends on both efficiency and the scope of application; mechanically transferring the savings of one run to the national energy balance is not possible.

It is also not possible to substitute missing information by calculating "P into graphics cards." A system that efficiently serves many independent requests may have different performance on tightly coupled training. To compare computing capabilities, an identical task, result quality, latency, and measured energy consumption are needed.

How xAI Solved the Same Problem

Colossus is useful for comparison precisely as a specific project. xAI located its first site in the former Electrolux factory in Memphis. The utility company MLGW indicated that the industrial site already had significant infrastructure. Thus, speed was ensured, in part, by utilizing previously made investments in the site. In October 2024, Nvidia announced a cluster of 100,000 Hopper GPUs, built in 122 days. The vendor highlighted Spectrum-X—an Ethernet network for data exchange between accelerators. The large site became a computing machine due to its connections and traffic management, not just the sheer number of installed chips. This is a statement from a project participant, not an independent performance audit. Nvidia Publication.

Own Generation as a Way to Save Time

Connecting to the grid requires verifying how a new consumer will affect others. MLGW, in its 2025 document, described the first commissioned grid tranche of 150 MW, further expansion, and an agreement for xAI to reduce load during peak demand. The company also confirmed the site's connection to the city water supply at commercial rates. This reflects the status as of the document date, not a current summary of the entire complex. MLGW Document.

Own gas turbines allowed for expanding power supply without waiting for all grid work to be completed. However, along with generation, the operator took on power plant responsibilities: fuel supply, maintenance, emissions, noise, and permits.

On July 30, 2026, SpaceXAI announced a coordinated schedule for the removal of 69 temporary mobile turbines in Southaven. According to this statement, they are to be replaced by a permanent 1.2 GW station with 41 turbines; the removal of temporary installations is scheduled for completion in July 2027. In the same announcement, the company stated measures for emission control and noise reduction. These figures describe the situation in July and the transition plan; it is not possible to assume all 69 units were operational on any later date. Official SpaceXAI Update.

The environmental aspect of the project became the subject of a lawsuit. In a lawsuit filed on April 14, 2026, the NAACP challenged the operation of the turbines without, according to the plaintiff, the required permits under the Clean Air Act. The lawsuit states the plaintiff's position; it is not equivalent to a judicial determination of violation. For engineering comparison, the conflict itself around the temporary power supply scheme is significant here. Text of the lawsuit.

The xAI model reduces the waiting time for available power but does not eliminate infrastructure limitations. It shifts part of the work and responsibility from the utility company to the owner of the computing complex.

Water Supply Also Needs to Be Built

MLGW's plan includes a wastewater reuse plant with a capacity of up to 13 million US gallons per day, approximately 49 thousand m³. It is intended for several industrial consumers, including xAI and TVA. This design capacity cannot be presented as Colossus's current consumption. Description of the MLGW project.

The company's website states a planned commissioning in 2026. The mere existence of a promise on the website does not confirm that the plant has been commissioned and has switched cooling to recycled water. Such a conclusion requires a separate announcement of commissioning and actual performance data. SpaceXAI's position on water supply.

This issue is important regardless of which city is "greener." As computing power increases, water supply and treatment must keep pace with servers. Large water reserves in the region do not describe the availability of a specific water intake, pipeline capacity, or impact on other users.

Colossus Became a Computing Provider for a Competitor

On May 6, 2026, Anthropic announced an agreement with SpaceX to use the entire computing power of Colossus 1: over 300 MW and 220,000 Nvidia GPUs, access to which the company expected to receive within a month. This is a more reliable basis for discussing the site's purpose than attempting to reconstruct its utilization from indirect estimates. Anthropic Announcement.

However, here too, it is important to read the source precisely: it confirms the agreement and the stated volume, not providing minute-by-minute telemetry of the cluster. Comparing these 300 MW with the half-year average consumption of all of Ulanqab as results of the same measurement would be incorrect.

From an economic perspective, the deal shows a convergence of models. The owner of a complex built around developing its own AI sells computing services to another company. For Chinese computing clusters, attracting external workloads is also a central task: the equipment must be busy with paid work.

What the Comparison Actually Shows

The contrast "China builds state AI, Musk builds private" is too simplistic. Various companies operate within the Chinese cluster, and Colossus uses communal infrastructure and sells resources to external customers. The difference lies primarily in the organization of expansion.

What Indicators Will Determine the Outcome by 2030

Inner Mongolia aims to increase its new energy capacity to 325 GW by 2030 and expand new energy storage systems to approximately 60 GW. This is a regional plan for the entire energy system and economy, not a volume reserved exclusively for AI. Moreover, 60 GW characterizes the capacity of storage systems; without data on energy capacity, the duration of the reserve cannot be calculated from this figure. Plans published by the regional energy administration.

China itself recognizes the danger of construction without demand. The 2024 NDRC plan provides for limiting new construction in cities where data centers have been operating for over a year and the overall equipment fill rate—上架率—remains below 50%. This refers to equipment placement, not GPU computational load. Data Center Development Plan, item II.1.

For the region, the next question is how much value remains locally. Construction creates one set of revenues and jobs, operation—another. Model development, cloud service sales, and customer relationship ownership may be located in other cities. Therefore, Ulanqab's capacities alone do not prove the city's transformation into an AI development center.

A useful report on such a project should allow answering at least five questions:

  1. What is already operational? What capacity has been commissioned, what is installed inside, and how much electricity has actually been consumed during the specified period.

  2. What work is the equipment performing? Training, inference, or standard cloud tasks; what are the utilization and measured performance.

  3. How is continuity ensured? Power sources, backup, available storage energy capacity, and load shedding conditions.

  4. How many resources does the result require? Energy, water, and total costs at specified service quality and latency.

  5. Who is buying the service? Is there sustainable paid demand corresponding to the expansion rate.

The Chinese approach deserves attention precisely for its attempt to link these levels: computing placement, the energy system, industrial policy, and proprietary technologies. Colossus shows how quickly a large cluster can be assembled if the operator takes on some of the energy tasks. Both examples become clearer when, instead of rack records, one examines the entire path from the power plant to the completed request.

The next convincing figure in this story is the cost of useful computational work at known quality, reliability, and resource consumption. It will show what the built gigawatts have turned into.
