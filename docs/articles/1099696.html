Nano Banana 2.1 and ChatGPT Image 2.5 perform a similar task: they create images from text prompts and edit existing pictures. However, after several generations, it becomes clear that the models make mistakes in different ways. One adheres more precisely to the technical brief, while the other better preserves a face or makes the visual more "put together."

Comparing such neural networks based on a single beautiful example is pointless. For product images, the accuracy of object placement is more important; for posters, the text; for advertising shots, similarity to a reference; and for editing, the ability to change exactly one element without redrawing everything else.

How the Comparison Works

To make the comparison of neural networks useful, we look not only at the overall quality of the image. Each test has a specific criterion: the number and placement of objects, the naturalness of a face, the correctness of inscriptions, adherence to composition, or preservation of reference details.

This approach is more important than a subjective "this image is prettier." In real work, an error in one line of a poster or a changed face of a model can make the visual unusable, even if the image looks impressive.

Test #1. Following a Complex Prompt

Prompt: Create a realistic still life on a gray surface: several objects of specified colors and sizes should be located in specific places relative to each other. Do not add extra objects and maintain the exact number of elements.

This is a good test of instruction following: the model must simultaneously understand the number of items, color, position, and overall style.

In one of the published five-round comparisons, both models retained the main elements of the task. Nano Banana 2.1 conveyed some details more accurately but unusually positioned one of the items. ChatGPT Image 2.5 assembled the scene more naturally and visually tidily, without losing the prompt's conditions.

Here, ChatGPT Image 2.5 has the advantage. The model performs better when the user cares not only about formal accuracy but also about the natural composition of the frame.

However, results can change in product photography. In another independent test, Nano Banana 2.1 more accurately fulfilled the requirement to leave free space on the right and place the product specifically on a separate wooden board, while GPT Image turned the board into a regular tabletop.

Practical takeaway: if a condition is critical, state it twice — first in the scene description, then as a separate constraint.

Test #2. Photorealistic Portrait

Prompt: Create a portrait of a person in soft natural lighting without studio retouching: natural skin, minor imperfections, normal facial proportions, minimal stylization.

This tests more than just detail. Modern neural networks easily create technically high-quality faces, but the result often looks too perfect: smooth skin, enlarged eyes, pronounced symmetry, and advertising lighting.

In a five-round Postium test, Nano Banana 2.1's portrait looked more natural: less beautification, more noticeable skin texture, and less of the characteristic generative "glossiness." ChatGPT Image 2.5 created a high-quality frame, but the face turned out more stylized.

For a regular neural photoshoot, this isn't always a downside. If a commercial beauty shot is needed, ChatGPT's more polished result might be more useful. But when requesting a documentary or RAW photo, Nano Banana 2.1 more often directly meets the requirement.

Test #3. Text in an Image

Prompt: Create a minimalist poster with several lines of Russian text. All words, numbers, and punctuation marks must be written accurately. Do not add your own slogans or captions.

Text has long been a weak point for image generators. New versions of both models have significantly improved: Google specifically claims improved typography for Nano Banana 2.1, and OpenAI claims more accurate layouts and infographics in Images 2.5.

In one direct test, both models correctly wrote all the lines. Nano Banana 2.1 created a more strict typographic composition, while ChatGPT Image 2.5 added more visual flair and brought the result closer to an advertising poster.

In another comparison, both models also reproduced English lines on a concert poster without errors. There, the result was effectively a tie in terms of text accuracy.

Therefore, the issue is no longer just about spelling. Nano Banana often creates a more literal layout, while GPT Image actively interprets the design.

Test #4. Cover with Strict Requirements

Prompt: Create a horizontal video cover. Use the face from the reference, keep it recognizable, place a large specified title, and visually show the conflict between a person and AI. Do not change the title text.

This test simultaneously checks composition, text, face handling, and the model's ability to understand the plot's meaning.

In the published comparison, Nano Banana 2.1 changed the title wording itself and created a more cluttered composition. ChatGPT Image 2.5 retained the specified text and more clearly visualized the plot itself: the connection between the character, AI, and the destroyed site was read faster.

Here, ChatGPT Image 2.5 appears stronger specifically as a model for finished marketing visuals. It more often tries to build a hierarchy: main object, emotion, background, and title.

However, this feature should be controlled. In other tests, GPT Image added its own captions, slogans, or decorative elements that were not in the prompt. Therefore, for a production layout, it's useful to write in a separate line: "do not add any text other than the specified one."
