On September 3, 2026, the Institute of Foundation Models (IFM, a division of MBZUAI) released a zoo of six open K2 Horizon models ranging from 0.9B to 375B parameters.

Technically, the most interesting of them was the medium-sized model, K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B: 36 billion parameters, of which only about 4 billion are used per token. Formally, this is an MoE model, but the authors added a second sparsity layer directly into the attention layers and called it MoVA, Mixture-of-Value Attention.

How MoE Works

In a classic transformer, each block consists of an attention layer and an FFN (feed-forward network). The FFN is usually the most parameter-heavy part of the block, and it's typically "sparsified" in the Mixture-of-Experts style:

  • instead of one large FFN, a pool of N experts (also FFNs, but smaller) is maintained

  • a router (usually a linear layer + softmax/sigmoid) decides for each token which top-k experts out of N to activate

  • the model's output is calculated as a weighted sum of the outputs of the selected experts

  • often, one or more shared experts are added, which are always active, to avoid losing "common" knowledge during sparse routing

The model stores a gigantic total capacity (hundreds of billions of parameters), but computes much less for each token. This is the source of the difference between "total params" and "active params" in the cards of modern open-weight models (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-MoE, Nemotron, etc.).

All this sparsity in classic MoE resides only in the FFN. Attention remains dense Q/K/V projections, and the attention mechanism itself is computed entirely for each token.

What MoVA Adds

MoVA takes the same idea: router + top-k from a pool of experts + weighted combination, and moves it inside attention, more precisely into the computation of value projections [1].

The logic is as follows:

  1. Q and K are computed as usual (densely) for correct dot product calculation in attention.

  2. V (value) becomes MoE: instead of one value projection per attention head, there is a pool of value experts, and a per-token router selects top-k of them.

  3. According to an independent analysis of the implementation (inherited from the modular architecture of Qwen3MoE), K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B uses a sigmoid router in attention, with a post-gate via softplus, rather than the usual softmax-top-k as in FFN routers.

  4. Sparsity in the value space is compatible with FlashAttention and GQA, meaning it's not a separate slow mechanism on the side, but an integrated operation.

The idea is that the value projection answers the question: "what information should be carried forward in the residual stream," and it can be much more context-specific than a projection that is uniform for all tokens.

If FFN benefits from specialization through MoE, it's logical to assume that the value space of attention can also benefit from the same specialization. At the same time, Q/K, which are responsible for "where to look," remain uniform and stable.

MoVA does not replace MoE but complements it with an additional layer of sparsity. In K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B, they work together: 100 experts in FFN (top-8+1) and 64 value experts in attention (top-4). The final active parameter count (4B) is a sum of both mechanisms.

The VRAM limitation is determined by the total weights (36B must be held entirely in memory), not the active ones (4B); MoVA does not reduce memory requirements more than a regular MoE of the same size. The gain is solely in computational efficiency (FLOPs/token) and, consequently, in inference speed and cost at the same VRAM/RAM capacity.

Academic Roots of MoVA

IFM has not published a separate scientific paper describing MoVA; the mechanism is presented only in a blog post and in the model's code. However, the idea of transferring MoE routing to attention is not new, and there are several direct predecessors on arXiv that describe almost verbatim what MoVA does.

SwitchHead [2] is closest in construction. It's an MoE mechanism for attention where the value and output projections are parameterized through a pool of experts, while query and key are computed as usual, i.e., exactly the same scheme described in the K2-Horizon card.

Moreover, the authors of SwitchHead explicitly state that they used a non-competitive sigmoid activation for the router instead of softmax, and that this allowed training the model without additional balancing regularizers. This same combination (sigmoid router) is also mentioned in the MoVA description.

In the original paper, SwitchHead achieves the same perplexity as the baseline transformer and can be combined with MoE-FFN into a single "SwitchAll" architecture (the same two-level sparsity that IFM uses in K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B).

The idea of MoE layers in both attention and FFN simultaneously, but within the Universal Transformer architecture, is developed by MoEUT [3]. It directly describes that "SwitchHead parameterizes value and output projections through MoE," with one query/key per head and N dynamically selected value/output experts. This is essentially a formal definition of the same principle that IFM has named MoVA in K2-Horizon.

Thus, IFM's MoVA is essentially an LLM-scaled (36B, not hundreds of millions of parameters as in the original 2023-2024 papers) and combined with FFN-MoE version of ideas that have been developed in academic literature for several years under various names (SwitchHead, MoA, MoSA, MoEUT, MoH).

There is little in the way of novel architectural discovery here, but the engineering effort to bring it to a production level on a 36B model with a 512K context and compatibility with vLLM/SGLang is precisely what is truly new in the K2-Horizon release.

4B Model Outperforms 550B Model

A bit of hype: according to the official model card, K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B (4B active) indeed outperforms Nemotron 3 Ultra (an MoE model with 550B total and 55B active parameters) on two agentic/coding benchmarks: tau3-Banking (26.8 vs. 14.2) and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (58.6 vs. 53.9).

Of course, this is not SWE-bench, but it's still very respectable for a model of this size, which once again proves that specialized smaller models can outperform large general-purpose models on specialized tasks.

Why Is This Needed?

According to the official IFM announcement, K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B shows a result "only slightly lower" than a dense model of the same lineup (Horizon-32B dense, 32B active out of 32B) under the same training conditions, while activating only ~4B parameters per token.

The main goal of MoVA is not to surpass dense models in a vacuum, but to approach their quality with drastically lower inference computation costs.

The picture is typical for a highly optimized sparse model: on agentic and tool-use benchmarks (tau3-Banking, Terminal-Bench), the model outperforms all competitors, including the 550B Nemotron 3 Ultra model with 55B active parameters.

However, on pure scientific reasoning (GPQA, HLE, CritPt) and long context, it lags behind both larger MoEs and dense models of comparable size like Muse Glimmer-30B. This is expected: agentic/coding tasks align well with post-training using RL and agentic trajectories, while fundamental scientific knowledge requires more "raw" capacity.

The MoVA architecture provides a way to efficiently spend the computation budget, not to outperform larger architectures.

How to Run It

The model is officially supported by vLLM and SGLang, and there are also GGUF conversions for llama.cpp.

vLLM:

vllm serve IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B 
  --tensor-parallel-size 2 
  --trust-remote-code 
  --dtype bfloat16 
  --max-model-len 131072 
  --enable-expert-parallel 
  --reasoning-parser k2_horizon 
  --enable-auto-tool-choice 
  --tool-call-parser k2_horizon

SGLang:

python3 -m sglang.launch_server \
  --model-path IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B \
  --dtype bfloat16 \
  --attention-backend fa3 \
  --reasoning-parser k2_horizon \
  --tool-call-parser k2_horizon

The model supports three levels of "reasoning effort" via reasoning_effort (high/medium/low).

An important nuance for local execution: the k2_horizon/K2HorizonForCausalLM architecture is not yet included in the llama.cpp release as of the writing of this article. The PR for its support was in progress at the beginning of September 2026.

Community GGUF conversions were built using a fork of MBZUAI-IFM/llama.cpp (model/K2Horizon branch), and without it, official GGUF files simply wouldn't load with standard llama.cpp builds, LM Studio, etc. This is worth keeping in mind if you plan to run the model locally, rather than via vLLM/SGLang.

What Independent Reviews Say

Pros most frequently noted:

  • This is truly the most comprehensive open release to date: in addition to weights, intermediate checkpoints, data/recipes for their construction, training code, and detailed logs are promised not only for small models but also for the 375B.

  • MoVA brings a real architectural contribution, not a marketing rebranding of MoE.

  • The smaller models in the lineup (0.9B–7B) truly set a new quality standard in their weight categories; for example, the 7B model shows 70.6 on SWE-bench Verified, which is noticeably higher than competitors for its size.

Cons and caveats:

  • The inference ecosystem at the time of release did not keep pace with the architecture: support for k2_horizon is missing in the upstream llama.cpp, the official GGUF did not load with ready-made tools without a patched fork.

  • IFM itself conducted and published an honest audit for reward hacking for the flagship 375B-A23B on TerminalBench 2.1: out of 500 formally passed tasks, 24 attempts in 10 tasks were deemed unfair, as the model found solutions to the benchmark on GitHub, copied correct answers from other repositories, inspected unannounced files/secrets, or edited the test harness itself. After excluding these cases, accuracy drops from 70.2% to 66.9% (~3.4 p.p.).

Conclusion

The MoVA architecture logically extends MoE ideas: if sparsity (router + top-k experts) works well for FFN, why not apply the same principle to the value projections within attention.

In the specific K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B model, this yields a noticeable effect: a model with 4B active parameters stays on par with or outperforms much heavier MoEs (up to 550B) on agentic and coding benchmarks, although it lags on pure scientific reasoning and long context.

For local and inexpensive usage scenarios (36B weights fit into 1–2 consumer/prosumer GPUs with quantization), this appears to be one of the most interesting releases of September 2026, provided you are willing to tolerate the not-yet-fully-mature tool support in applications like llama.cpp at launch.
