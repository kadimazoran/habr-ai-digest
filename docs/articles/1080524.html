OpenSRE is a tool for building AI SRE agents that investigate and resolve production incidents by operating within your infrastructure. Essentially, it's a helper-automator for SRE.

This article is about my first contribution to an external project, what was the most challenging part, and how this tool works.

Disclaimer. OpenSRE is in public alpha. The maintainers explicitly state that they do not guarantee operational safety. We also cannot guarantee that nothing will break for you. This article is an account of an experiment, and if you decide to try it, carefully consider what exactly you are using and with what permissions you are running it. The account from which you run OpenSRE should not have modifying roles.

How I started contributing

I actually wanted to create such a tool myself, but when I found an existing one, I had the idea to integrate it with Yandex Cloud.

This is because OpenSRE has three valuable modes:

  • Infrastructure Assistant. The agent helps understand what is deployed where, how it's connected, and where to look.

  • Co-pilot for Incidents. You are the investigation lead, and the agent suggests hypotheses based on processed data and a list of things to check.

  • Autonomous Triage. The agent launches automatically upon an alert and, by the time you open your laptop, it already answers initial questions: what degraded first, is it our problem or the provider's, what's the blast radius. This information reduces the time to find the cause.

Moreover, OpenSRE already had numerous integrations with cloud providers, monitoring and alerting systems, and messengers. Initially, I considered making a full fork (the license allows it), but the race against the constantly growing upstream killed the idea. So, I decided to create an integration.

Naturally, the first thing I did was share the idea of integrating OpenSRE and Yandex Cloud with my colleagues. We work closely with various client information systems, so we are always looking for new and effective tools. In short, my colleagues gave the go-ahead, and we decided to see what would come of it.

The maintainers communicate on Discord, which is where I went. Initially, they reacted quite dryly to my proposal and suggested creating a 3rd-party integration as a separate plugin.
