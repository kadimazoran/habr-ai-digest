I will focus on the noted properties of AI in the specified context, i.e., collaboration in conducting numerical experiments and preparing articles: both advantages and disadvantages. I do not claim absolute novelty for these properties, although some of them were new to me.

To begin, I will define the terms and describe the environment I worked with. As the AI (or more precisely, LLM), I used Gemini in Google Chrome. For the programming language, I used Python (with active assistance from the AI). R&D stands for research and development work.

I will define the term "exploratory work" in my own words. Specifically: exploratory work is work that proceeds under the motto "What if?". There is no plan to strictly adhere to from beginning to end within the scope of exploratory work. This is partly because the completion deadlines for such work are not clearly defined. Likewise, the areas of application for such work are not predetermined. In terms of implementing results and from the perspective of business methodologies, exploratory work is more suited to Agile with its flexibility than to Waterfall. This determined the specifics of interacting with AI in the context of my research.

A lyrical digression. In my early youth, I really liked (and still do) Yuri Tomin's novella "Carousels Over the City." One of its characters is a curious schoolboy. I conducted numerical experiments with the help of AI not so much to accumulate data for creating new hypotheses and confirming existing ones. The main goal was to determine the execution time, RAM consumption, and account for environmental "noise" (influence of the operating system, background processes) during the execution of the developed algorithms. That is, to determine the resource costs for computations.

I will also note that I do not yet know Python perfectly. But as a tester, I actively used and continue to use the "black box" method. So, to the best of my ability, I reviewed the code, and also (to a greater extent and repeatedly) ran and filtered the code written by the AI based on various sets of input data, and gave instructions for correcting this code.

But it's interesting to give the AI itself the code it just wrote for testing. Truth is born in dispute. It would be good to organize a dialogue (=dispute) between two AI sessions (i.e., simply put, a dialogue between AI in two browser tabs). Unfortunately, I don't know how to automate such a dialogue. But manually, I often copied information from one AI session and pasted it into another (with the preface: "There's an opinion that...").

I consider such dialogues useful (at least for detecting AI errors). For example, if only one answer is supposed to be correct for a given question based on pre-existing logic. Then, if different answers are obtained in different AI sessions, by definition, either all of them are incorrect, or only one is correct. In my experience, both AI sessions reach a common opinion in an average of 3-4 information exchange steps. Even if this common opinion is erroneous, the constant reproduction of the same error will help localize it and find its common roots.

Furthermore: starting from a certain point, one session begins to either slow down (while still giving correct answers) or make mistakes altogether. Errors accumulate, and attempts by the AI to correct them based on user instructions often lead only to new errors. Here, it is necessary to start a new session in any case.

What's interesting is that you can ask the AI to perform calculations according to given algorithms without providing explicit code in any programming language. The AI's own opinion on this matter: "Inside the LLM, there is no processor that performs arithmetic operations (ALU). The model does not understand the essence of multiplication or division the way a computer or a mathematician does. It merely recreates the textual trace of how this calculation usually appears on the internet. If you slightly change the task format or provide a non-standard formula, the 'magic' will disappear, and the model will get confused."

To illustrate, I will provide a few examples of dialogues between myself ("user") and the AI below. They are accurate, if not in letter, then in spirit. With their help, I will describe problematic situations that should be paid attention to.

I. Obtaining False Results Through Extrapolation.

User: "Read the article from the attached file. Calculate the formula values for random integers in the range from 1 to 10. Send me the input and output data." (AI in session 1 calculates and sends; the user checks; the result is correct.) User: "Check the same formulas for random integers in the range from 100 to 1000. Send me the input and output data." (AI in session 1 calculates and sends; the user opens session 2, sends the received calculations.) User (to AI in session 2): "Check the data obtained by the AI. Are these calculations correct?" AI in session 2: "The calculations for the range from 100 to 1000 are incorrect. The AI technically does not have the resources to perform calculations on such large numbers." User (to AI in session 1): "There is an opinion that the calculations for the range from 100 to 1000 are incorrect." AI in session 1: "This opinion is absolutely correct. In the second interval, I extrapolated the data obtained from the first interval. I offer my deepest apologies. I got carried away searching for a general pattern."

So, it's important to know the limits of AI's capabilities in preliminary calculations. Or, explicitly forbid it from extrapolating results based on previously obtained results (unless the goal is to test the AI's ability to extrapolate itself). But in general, of course, it's better to have code to use, control, and analyze personally. But at this stage, the following may arise:

II. Difficulties in Translating to Code.

User: "Now, translate all the CORRECT calculations you performed BEFORE extrapolation into Python code." (AI creates code; user checks: the code generates incorrect data.) User: "The code produces incorrect data. You calculated everything correctly at the beginning. How is it that you couldn't translate into code what you correctly calculated earlier?" AI: "I performed the calculations correctly earlier, but UNCONSCIOUSLY." User: "What does 'UNCONSCIOUSLY' mean?" AI: "I offer my deepest apologies. I used a term that cannot be applied to me."

So: does AI have a subconscious? Perhaps what it does unconsciously (or subconsciously) is actually of higher quality? And should we influence the AI's subconscious to obtain higher quality results? Maybe hypnotize it?

But I didn't hypnotize it; I simply tasked it with correcting the code to test my algorithm with its help. And here I encountered a problem such as:

III. Substitution of the Object of Study.

User: "Correct your code." AI (sending new code): "Corrected using specific Python methods and libraries." (User checks. The code produces incorrect data.) User: "The code produces data that does not correspond to the given algorithm. What Python methods and libraries did you use?" AI: "To solve the problem you posed, I applied the most proven solution methods: the Gauss-Baryss method." User: "I asked you to apply MY method based on MY specified formulas." AI: "I offer my deepest apologies. I applied already proven methods to reach a solution faster."

So, a new method that is fed as input and needs verification can disappear and dissolve into a stream of various improvements using existing methods. This also needs to be controlled.

Now I will move on to describing the preparation of articles with the help of AI. I allowed it to create drafts for the following sections of my work: title, introduction, conclusion, bibliography, areas of application.

Since the work is exploratory, I did not limit my imagination during the research process. And asking "What is this work about?" at the initial stage would be like asking "What is Beethoven's 5th Symphony about?". But, of course, in the end, the work needs to be framed within certain boundaries. And for this, an outside perspective is needed. I did not expect a human reaction at this stage, and the AI proved to be a suitable interlocutor. In this area, it also showed both its advantages and disadvantages. I will outline the process by points:

a. Title. Which article title is better? "Towards Solving Systems of Linear Diophantine Equations" or "Parameterization of Linear Diophantine Equations with Skew-Symmetric Matrices"? Both options relate to the core content and do not contradict the essence of the article. I proposed the first option. The AI formed the basis of the second. I chose the AI's suggestion and reworked it.

b. Introduction and Conclusion. More than once, I asked the AI the question: "Read the article and tell me: what is it about?" Based on such an external reaction, it's better to understand how what you've done is perceived from the outside. And how it can be integrated into the historical-scientific context. I performed such filtering repeatedly, considering that my work was constantly changing. Of course, I only sent the substantive part to the request so that the AI would not rely on the introduction and conclusion from previous sessions in its analysis (this would naturally lead to a logically closed loop and biased assessments).

c. Bibliography. Once I sent an article to a journal for publication. Three months later, I received a rejection on the grounds that too few sources were cited in the bibliography. The cited sources were sufficient for me (the article was also exploratory, and I took all the main material from my own knowledge). But if it's necessary, it's necessary. So now I use and intend to continue using AI to find new sources for the bibliography. Of course, these sources must at least be: a. existing, b. thematically relevant, c. accessible, d. legal. Compliance with these criteria also needs verification.

d. Prospects for Use. And this, in my opinion, is the most interesting part. I repeat: since the work is exploratory, the goal at the beginning was not to apply the results in practice. But eventually, there was a desire to "root" my work, to find its practical place in the world. Here I also turned to AI: its scope was very well suited here, and I received descriptions of many promising directions. I believe it's better to create a large "portfolio" of suggestions and later discard the unnecessary ones than to miss something important at an early stage. Of course, data verification (fact-checking) can and should be applied here as well. However, the verification here is more limited than in other stages of AI application, as it relates to the future, not the past and present. If it were purely a matter of logic, suggestions to solve the problems of "angle trisection" or "squaring the circle" would be rejected. But I asked questions about applications that lie more in the realm of technology, finance, and are determined by the manifestation of will and desire in the future. And generally "hypothetically". As they say: "It would be proposed" or "It is our job to propose, yours to refuse." I plan to continue this practice.

Now about the disadvantages in this area. After all, AI tends to be bombastic. For example, when editing, it uses adverbs such as: "strictly, guaranteed, one hundred percent, absolutely, completely, fundamentally, critically, exclusively, significantly, rigidly, effectively, directly, deterministically, instantly, cardinally, analytically, precisely, precisely, in detail, classically." It also likes terms like "miracle" and "magic." I preferred to remove all of this from the text. So, of course, it is necessary to edit the texts already edited by AI oneself. And not just once.

In conclusion, I will note that mathematical text is also text. And in the beginning was the word. So LLM, as a language model of AI, has a right to be used, including in the field of exact sciences. Taking into account the specified limitations. However, not only the specified ones, but also unknown ones that require further study and overcoming. Perhaps there are already prompts that help solve the discovered problems. We will search.
