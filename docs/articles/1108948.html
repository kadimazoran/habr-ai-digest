What Google has changed in AI Overviews and what it hasn't confirmed yet

Google is testing a new location for source icons, moving them from the top of the overview to the very bottom. Barry Schwartz from Search Engine Roundtable described this test on November 11, 2026. Brodie Clark from SERP Alert noticed that in this version, the icons are placed below the answer, and the full list of sources appears on hover. In a previous version of the same experiment, the block was at the top, directly above the snippets.

The second change concerns definition queries. Glenn Gabe noticed that the overview for these is now fully expanded, and there's no longer a need to click a button to read the full answer. This happens for all such queries. For the query 'define expandable', the overview takes up the entire visible screen, and users only reach the sites by scrolling.

Google has not announced either of these changes. Schwartz himself did not see the top part of the results, so CMO Mag advises considering the icon placement shift an experiment that few have seen. Google has not provided timelines or specific countries.

This is relevant for Russian sites if they have traffic from Google in foreign markets, as AI Overviews are not officially available in Russia. The closest parallel in Russia is Yandex's Alice AI answers in search, where the question is the same: whether the company was mentioned in the answer itself.

When Google has an AI overview above the search results, clicks on links are almost halved

According to Pew Research Center, with an AI overview, users clicked on regular links 8% of the time, compared to 15% without an overview. In March 2025, Pew tracked the browsers of 900 adult Americans and collected 68,879 Google searches, of which 12,593 included an overview. Clicks on sources listed within the overview itself were, according to Pew, very rare.

This data pertains to the previous version of the overview when icons were still at the top. There is no click data for the new format yet, so predictions for it can only be based on previous formats.

Citations in AI Overviews only partially restore clicks to sites

A site that Google includes in the overview's sources (Seer calls such a brand 'cited') receives more clicks than a site not included in the sources, but fewer than on queries without an overview. This is evident from Seer Interactive's data: the agency tracked 53 brands and 5.47 million queries from January 2025 to February 2026. They calculated CTR, the percentage of impressions in the search results that led to a user visiting the site.

For queries with an overview, Seer's organic CTR increased from a minimum of 1.3% in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026. This is an almost twofold increase, but the level remains low.

CTR in such data can be easily misinterpreted. For cited brands, impressions more than doubled while clicks remained constant, leading to a decrease in CTR without a loss of traffic. Seer's sample size is small, and whether a query had an overview was determined retrospectively based on the last search result snapshot. Neither Seer nor Pew differentiate the placement of icons, so their figures for the new format are estimates.

Why the list of sources at the bottom of AI Overviews won't become a traffic channel

The list of sources at the bottom of the overview is a gesture of acknowledgment to the site, not a traffic channel: even fewer people will click through from AI Overviews. With an overview above the search results, clicks on links are almost halved, clicks on sources within it are very rare, and cited sites, according to Seer, still lag 38% behind queries without an overview. The test adds an extra step: users must read the answer to see the icons, and the full list only appears on hover.

However, users do look at the overview text itself. In a Microsoft Research eye-tracking study, participants paid significantly more attention to the AI block than to regular blue links. A brand mentioned in the answer text is seen by the user even if they don't click through. Therefore, it's important for the brand and site facts to be in the answer text itself, and mentions of the brand in the answer should be measured.

How to check site mentions and click dynamics

The main metric here is mentions of the brand in the answer text; clicks on them are secondary. This check shows mentions and click dynamics but doesn't establish that the overview caused the decline. The steps are as follows:

1. Collect queries for which the site receives traffic from Google and check weekly if your brand and facts appear in the overview text. Pages that answer a question in the first paragraph and mention the company there have a better chance. A hypothetical example for a city delivery query: "Delivery is made the next day after ordering, this is how the company [company name] works." More details: why a neural network cites such pages and how to check your own.

2. Open Search Console, Google's site statistics service, and navigate to the Generative AI performance report. Google launched it in June 2026, and it shows how often your site's URLs appeared in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Start with pages that have the most impressions there.

3. For pages that answer definition queries, calculate absolute weekly clicks. Seer's cited brand CTR decreased with constant clicks, and CTR alone cannot distinguish traffic loss from increased impressions.

4. To compare results with and without an overview, use the "Sites" filter. According to Google's help, you cannot disable overviews, but this filter only shows links to websites.

For a site with traffic from Google, the criterion is singular: whether the brand is mentioned in the answer text itself. If overviews bring in only a few clicks, efforts should be focused on mentions within the answer and measuring results based on those.
