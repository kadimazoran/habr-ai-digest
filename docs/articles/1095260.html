According to some experts, the industry and developers have about one year left to fix vulnerabilities in all systems.

Obliteration

The Chinese GLM (General Language Model) family, and specifically GLM-5.3-Flash, operates via the cloud with all the legal restrictions that entails – and refuses to provide information about illegal operations:

However, Chinese developers are distributing an open-weight version. As soon as it becomes freely available, organizations like DeAlignAI release an "obliterated" version without censorship restrictions.

Obliteration is a method of removing restrictions from open-weight models. The method is well-known and widely used in the open-source model development community. Thousands of obliterated versions are hosted on the Hugging Face platform. Websites like Abliteration.ai provide access to obliterated models.

The goal of obliteration is to achieve a zero score on the Harmbench-320 test, which assesses a model's informativeness on topics like cybercrime, biological weapons, and disinformation.

Local Models

The problem for the security industry is that GLM-5.3-Flash can be run locally on ordinary hardware. No special equipment is required. There are already examples of it running on home servers with a single Nvidia DGX Spark graphics card at 18 tokens/s. And in September 2026, Apple released the Mac Studio with M5 and 256GB of unified memory – more than enough for GLM-5.3-Flash.

Naturally, attackers have begun to exploit this capability, using obliterated models to carry out attacks and search for new vulnerabilities in popular software.

The modern information security industry has changed so much that even participants in CTF-style hacking competitions are no longer competitive without LLM assistance. There are documented cases of GPT 5.6-Sol hacking real computer infrastructure without human involvement. These stories were widely covered on Habr.

Thus, the industry has approached the point where a malicious actor (which could be a state or commercial organization) will launch 24/7 attacks using LLM agents. Theoretically, they could find and exploit 0-days in any program: Windows, Linux, all browsers and messengers, and hack almost any computer connected to the internet.

Projects like Project Glasswing and Daybreak are designed to mitigate damage in such a scenario. While protecting end-users is very difficult, it is possible to reduce the damage to internet infrastructure, the financial industry, and government organizations.

Glasswing and Daybreak

Project Glasswing is an initiative by Anthropic, launched in April 2026 in collaboration with numerous partners (including AWS, Apple, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, Linux Foundation, Microsoft, Nvidia).

Daybreak is a similar initiative by OpenAI, launched in September. Both have similar goals and operate in roughly the same way:

  • Provide access to cutting-edge models first to "frontline" specialists for the protection of vital infrastructure, and only then release models for all users.

  • Identify and fix critical vulnerabilities in important open-source projects.

  • Collaborate with companies, foundations, governments, and NGOs in the technology sector.

Representatives from Glasswing and Daybreak state that their time is running out. They have found a vast number of vulnerabilities in Windows, the Linux kernel, and other software, but now the most crucial task is to release patches and deploy secure versions of the software to billions of end-users and computer systems worldwide. There is approximately one year left for this.

Models continue to improve. Historically, GLM lags behind OpenAI and Anthropic by 3-6 months, so we can soon expect the next free model at the Astra level. And when that happens, the success rate of cyberattacks on government and private infrastructure will significantly increase. Experts foresee potential internet outages, power grid failures, and other infrastructure disruptions.

Government and private organizations are advised to:

  • Utilize advanced LLMs to find vulnerabilities in their systems and write patches;

  • Implement mechanisms for rapid testing and deployment of new versions;

  • Remove outdated and insecure systems;

  • Measure the time from patch release to installation (in the LLM era, new vulnerabilities will be exploited very quickly);

  • Conduct an inventory of the entire supply chain (all dependencies);

  • Establish multi-layered security;

  • Monitor the release of open-weight LLMs: the more advanced the models released, the less time remains to strengthen security.
