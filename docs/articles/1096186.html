How to Use These Prompts

For the five comparisons below, we rely on the open Fuser test from October 6, 2026. There, Nano Banana 2 and 2.1 received identical source materials and identical English prompts; images were generated at 2K, with one attempt each without restarts. Prompt 1. Photorealistic Scene with Precise Atmosphere Requirements

The first test checks basic adherence to the description: character, lighting, palette, composition, and absence of extraneous elements.

Prompt

Photorealistic documentary photograph of an elderly fisherman mending a bright orange net on an old wooden pier at dawn.

A foggy harbor in the background. The fisherman's hands are in sharp focus, the background is softly blurred.

Natural light, 50mm lens effect, muted color palette. Do not add text or inscriptions to the scene.

Nano Banana 2.1 maintained the cool, foggy background, showed the fisherman entirely, and left the frame without extraneous text. Nano Banana 2 made the scene warmer and more saturated, and an invented inscription appeared on the boat, which was not in the prompt.

What's important here is not the beauty of the image itself. Both can look convincing, but 2.1 more accurately fulfilled the constraints.

Winner: Nano Banana 2.1.

Prompt 2. Poster with Precise Text

This is a more challenging task for generators: it requires adhering to the layout and writing several lines without typos simultaneously.

Prompt

Create a minimalist concert poster on light paper. The top half features a large dark blue wave.

Place four lines of text:
"NIGHT TIDE"
"Live at Harbour Hall"
"Friday 14 November · Doors 8 PM"
"harbourhall.com/nighttide"

Do not add any other words. Make the first line a large, narrow grotesque font, the second a lighter weight.

This time, there's almost no difference: both versions correctly wrote the specified lines, including the date and address. This means there was no noticeable leap between 2 and 2.1 specifically in spelling on this example.

This is a good example of why one prompt is not enough to conclude "the new version is always better." If Nano Banana 2 already handles a specific task well, the update might not be visible from the first image.

Winner: Tie.

Prompt 3. Product Photography with Space for Ad Text

Here, the prompt specifically includes several minor constraints: direction of light, direction of shadow, and a free third of the frame.

Prompt

Studio photograph of a matte sage-green ceramic coffee funnel on a light oak board.

Soft window light from the left, a light shadow should extend to the right. Clean, almost white background. Leave the right third of the frame free for ad text.

Realistic product photography. No additional items or inscriptions.

Nano Banana 2.1 fulfilled all the main conditions simultaneously: light from the left, shadow to the right, and a free area for text. Nano Banana 2 also left space, but effectively divided the image with a blank light area, and the specified shadow direction was less clear.

For advertising creatives, this is precisely the kind of situation where a slight improvement in prompt adherence saves time. If a designer asks in advance to leave space on the right, they don't need just a "beautiful picture with some free space.".

Winner: Nano Banana 2.1.

Prompt 4. Transferring a Product to Another Location Without Changing the Product Itself

Now the task is more complex: there's a source image of a bottle, and only the surroundings need to be changed.

Prompt

Place this same bottle on a wet, moss-covered stone. The scene is outdoors in the morning, with soft sunlight and a blurred forest stream in the background.

Change only the surroundings and lighting.

Do not change the bottle's shape, amber glass, black dropper cap, or the "LUMA 02" label.

Both versions managed the major features: the bottle's shape, glass color, and label text were preserved. However, both simultaneously violated one minor condition—they changed the smooth cap detail to a ribbed one.

The differences were more visual. 2.1 chose a wider frame and kept the label clean. Nano Banana 2 zoomed in on the object and added water droplets on top of the label.

This is a good test for an important limitation of any image-to-image models: the phrase "don't change anything in the object" doesn't yet guarantee pixel-level identity of minor details.

Winner: Tie.

Prompt 5. Keep the Person and Completely Change the Scene

The final test is one of the most difficult: preserve the appearance of a real character but show them in different clothing, pose, and location.

Prompt

Use the woman from the reference portrait. Show her full-body riding a bicycle on a wet city street at night. Neon signs around, she's wearing a yellow raincoat with the hood down.

Keep the same face, red curly hair, freckles, and round glasses. Photorealism.

Here, 2.1 no longer has an unconditional victory.

Nano Banana 2 preserved the curls and glasses well, but changed the facial expression: the woman developed a wide smile that wasn't in the reference. Some of the signs became unreadable.

Nano Banana 2.1 more accurately constructed a full-body frame, but weakened the resemblance in details: the hair became less curly, the frame thinner, and the freckles almost disappeared. The text on the background signs also did not turn out to be readable.

If we evaluate only Nano Banana 2 and 2.1, the choice here depends on what's more important: individual features or the natural composition of the new scene.

Winner: Tie.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Nano Banana 2.1 really better than Nano Banana 2?

According to official specifications, it is the new recommended version with improved instruction following, quality, text, and character consistency. In the test reviewed, it won two tasks and did not clearly lose to the old version in any, but when transferring appearance, the results remained ambiguous.

Why does the same prompt give different results?

Generation is not deterministic by default. Even within the same model, a repeated run can change the composition, face, lighting, or small objects. Therefore, for a proper comparison, you need not just one beautiful example, but a series of runs with identical settings.

Why does the model ignore part of the prompt?

Often, the problem arises when a single request contains too many equally important instructions simultaneously. Critical constraints are better written separately: "change only the background," "the text must be written verbatim," "do not add logos," "keep the face unchanged.".

Is it always worth enabling high Thinking in Nano Banana 2.1?

No. 2.1 has minimal, medium, and high levels. A higher level can be useful for complex layouts or a large number of constraints, but it usually increases reasoning volume and execution time. For simple drafts, it's logical to start with minimal or the standard medium.

What does Nano Banana 2 have that 2.1 doesn't?

The most noticeable technical difference is the 0.5K generation mode. 2.1's minimum resolution starts at 1K. However, the new version is cheaper at matching resolutions and adds extremely wide and vertical formats.

How to improve results when working with a reference?

Describe separately what is allowed to be changed and what needs to be preserved. Instead of "put her in another city," it's better to list: keep the face, age, hair, and proportions; change only the clothing, background, and pose. If a specific detail of an object is important, name it separately – as the test with the bottle showed, a general command "preserve the object" does not always protect small elements.
