Google DeepMind is giving AI agents the ability to conduct experiments independently to create the next version of the Gemini model.

In the company's podcast, Vice President of Research Koray Kavukcuoglu, who leads Gemini development, calls this part of strategic changes in the lab. According to him, instead of maintaining separate teams for highly specialized projects – such as AlphaFold or nuclear fusion research – DeepMind now relies on a single universal algorithm that is applied in both scientific research and the creation of commercial products. Kavukcuoglu adds that each generation of Gemini is a unique, handcrafted system, not a product with a fixed architecture; meanwhile, parallel teams are already working on its successors.

As Kavukcuoglu notes, AI agents themselves manage part of the computational resources for conducting experiments, although the lab is still far from full recursive self-improvement. For safety reasons, DeepMind does not encourage internal reasoning chains of agents during the training process: this allows their intentions to be preserved in their original, unfiltered form, accessible for verification in natural language before the agents take action. Kavukcuoglu also points out existing limitations. Modern AI can model physical processes with high visual accuracy – for example, fluid flows or object collisions – but it lacks a deep, precise understanding of them. According to the expert, there is no test that could determine when models will overcome these gaps and reach the level of artificial general intelligence, as none of the existing benchmarks cover the very essence of intelligence. He believes that the emergence of AGI will only be possible when everyday useful products gain enough trust from people – so much so that they are willing to delegate decision-making to them, similar to how they trust other people.

Earlier, Google DeepMind opened Institute – a platform for interdisciplinary research dedicated to the safe development of artificial general intelligence. The Institute will address issues of AGI application and its impact on society, including cyber risks, bio-risks, and the potential loss of control over self-learning systems.
