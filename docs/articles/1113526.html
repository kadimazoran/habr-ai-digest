Why One Provider Is a Risk

There was Sauron, who seemed mighty, deceived everyone, gave rings to all, and kept the One Ring of Power for himself to enslave them all. And what happened? As soon as the ring was destroyed, his entire empire crumbled. It's the same with providers; you shouldn't rely solely on one.

Failures themselves can be different. For example, there are transport failures when there's a timeout, a broken connection, or 502/503 from the load balancer. The SDK usually retries them itself.

Then it gets more complex. There's 429 (rate limit)—you've hit a limit, which could be on requests per minute, tokens per minute, or tokens per day. Retrying with backoff helps somewhat, but it's unlikely to help with a daily limit. OpenAI has quota exceeded, Anthropic has a similar mechanism, and in both cases, you just have to wait until midnight. Or pay for a higher tier.

There are also 5xx and 529—problems on the provider's side. Anthropic has a separate 529 code (overloaded_error) when the provider honestly admits it's overloaded and can't handle the request. The SDK retries it with exponential backoff, and the ceiling is configurable. From a fallback perspective, this is the most correct trigger—the provider is definitely at fault, and your request is not.

And then there are such wonderful things as regional restrictions and sudden changes in terms. Your key stops working from your country. Or the tariff plan is redesigned, and RPM drops threefold. The model is completely removed from service (this has happened repeatedly with older versions of GPT and Claude in 2025–2026).

So, you need to understand that Retry is a repeated attempt to the same place, assuming the problem is temporary. Fallback is switching to a backup path, assuming the problem will not resolve itself.

Fault Tolerance Layers

Fault tolerance layers are like a pyramid. At the bottom is the cheapest and almost always sufficient layer, in the form of retry with exponential backoff and jitter. This layer fixes only transient issues; if the model is unavailable for a minute, three attempts at 1/2/4 second intervals will save you. But if not—you'll just keep hammering the API.

The next level up is a different deployment of the same model. If you're working through Azure or self-hosting, you might have two instances of the same model, often even from different providers (GPT on Azure as primary, the same GPT on OpenAI as backup). The response format is identical, and everything transfers almost painlessly.

The next level is a different model from the same provider. For example, switching from Claude Sonnet to Claude Haiku. Cheap, effective, but differences in behavior emerge.

And the top level is a completely different provider. Maximum resilience, maximum risk of losing meaning. This is precisely why the architecture is built in cascades, each level is cheaper and safer than the previous one, and the request should reach the top level as rarely as possible.

It's worth retrying 429 (not forgetting the Retry-After header), 408, 409, 5xx, 529, as well as timeouts and connection errors. But there's no point in retrying 400/422 or 401/403/404. By the way, OpenAI SDK already has retries built-in (max_retries, defaulting to 2–3 attempts with backoff).
