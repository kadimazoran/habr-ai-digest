Our external environment doesn't tell us what to do, it guides us.

Some things are softer, some harder, but we are not just the brain in a skull; we are also what was created before us and what we have created ourselves.

This article is about what surrounds us and how it relates to LLMs and agents.

The space of representations

The human brain is an incredibly complex calculator, but human thought is not confined to it. Stories about feral children are good illustrations, but the fact is, without language, without society, the brain does not develop. On the other hand, in Nicaragua, deaf children gathered in one school, over several generations, created a full-fledged sign language with grammar themselves. From primitive symbols to a complex structure, language formed as a space that none of the children had individually. And once formed, it provided ready-made distinctions from the outside (vocabulary, cases, tenses, aspects, etc.), became external memory (fairy tales as models of the world, legends, songs, poems, proverbs), support (according to Vygotsky), and the skeleton of thought.

To think, the brain needs a shell that society cultivates. This shell is the space of representations—all accumulated distinctions. By distinction, I mean the ability to differentiate one thing from another and to rely on that difference. Language, science, fairy tales, technologies, daily life, ways of thinking—these are not a storehouse of knowledge, but an active part of thinking.

This shell not only surrounds the brain; it grows into it and determines how it will think, love, and believe.

Every thought you have is born in your space of representations; it relies on books you've read, communication experience, experience of encountering the world, which is not always accommodating. You are free in your thinking, but the very mode of thinking is conditioned by the space of representations. Freedom is most often not in going beyond its limits, but in expanding them.

We can consider the same space of representations as System 2, language, a model of the world, or a multi-agent environment.

  • System 2. In Kahneman's work, this is slow, controlled thinking. I find Stanovich's interpretation closer: the ability to hold a representation detached from current reality and work with it as a hypothesis. That is, to play out possible moves before implementing them. In fact, this is a mode in which the transitions between distinctions themselves become objects of further operations.

  • Language allows us to externalize a distinction, make it accessible to others, and reuse it. An ant leaves a pheromone trail, and another ant follows it: the trail only indicates what is here and now. A word, however, can be transmitted, combined with others, negated, and used to talk about something that has never happened. A trail is followed; a sign is interpreted.

  • A model of the world is not a set of knowledge about the world, but a structure of relationships between distinctions: a space of representations, already connected by possible transitions.

  • A multi-agent AI system forms a common space for agents. One agent leaves a message in shared memory, and it defines the space of possible actions for others. The space itself becomes part of the computational process, opening up moves for agents—and just as easily limiting them in the first place.

What they have in common is that they operationalize distinctions. They demonstrate how a transition that is executable becomes something that can be worked with. A thermostat or a reflex executes a transition but cannot negate, compare, or combine it with another. Where a transition can only be executed, there is no space of representations; where the transition itself becomes the object of the next operation, the space of representations appears.

And then, the evolution of humanity looks like a process in which the space of representations produces new distinctions—which change the environment—which requires new distinctions—which expand the space of representations.

Narrative as a prompt for the space of representations

Initially, the space of representations, as a model of the world, was corrected and managed by reality. For animals, the space of representations is almost entirely tested by the surrounding reality. With the advent of abstractions, direct influence weakened. Abstraction allows us to extract a distinction from an immediate situation, breaking the link between the real world and thinking about the world. But individual distinctions by themselves are of little use. Functional connections must appear between them: if something happened on the first move, the next move will change.

Narratives became such a tool for connection and management. In fact, they functionally connect the elements of space.

Accordingly, an agent is a local calculator of a narrative. An agent locally calculates the next step of a global structure that it does not fully observe itself. Moreover, an agent cannot be the author of a narrative; it grows out of many calculations. An agent can only make a cut that can change or shift the narrative, and whether the overall space of representations accepts or rejects this cut is no longer decided by the agent. But the agent itself can change its position relative to the narrative, and sometimes that is enough.

Let's take the narrative of the "market." There are several pieces of paper, and there is a loaf of bread. There is no real connection, but the narrative functionally links them.

When a person saves money, spends it, plays the stock market, or borrows, they perform their small, local calculation of the global narrative of the "market," thereby feeding and strengthening it.

A narrative turns disparate elements of space into a trajectory, indicates and explains the connections between them.

If we return to the thesis that System 2, language, a model of the world, and a multi-agent environment are different perspectives on a space where distinctions become operational. Then the narrative becomes the field, the architecture, the skeleton, the attractor that allows distinctions, abstract elements, to be connected to each other, and for the space to offer representations that change subsequent moves and distinctions. That is, it is the narrative that makes a representation operational. Or, in other words, the narrative not only connects the space of representations but also provides leverage for its change.

For example, the well-known narrative "The market will sort everything out." There are many independent agents acting in their own interests → interaction through the market aggregates their actions → the resulting outcome contains information that an individual agent does not have. Essentially, a swarm of agents and its space of representations.

Roles emerge: buyer, seller, entrepreneur, competitor, price, shortage, profit, bankruptcy. And practically everything is now interpreted through this narrative:

Company loss: a signal → the market has identified inefficiency. Price increase: a change in shortage/demand. Emergence of a new business: an entrepreneur saw unmet demand. Moreover, the narrative predetermines what next move will seem reasonable: look at the price → decide what is now more profitable → change action → the market will change → look at the new price → repeat. That is, it is a real prompt for the space of representations. It doesn't just explain what has already happened; it determines what next move is even visible to the agent.

The narrative determines the form of questions it generates. That is, what distinctions it forces to be sequentially distinguished. A prompt for a model creates an unfilled form that the model completes. A narrative for a space of representations creates an unfinished situation that agents and the space itself continue.

But if any event is interpreted through the mechanism of the market, the narrative becomes total, denying the very possibility of changing the established space of representations. And new narratives, new explanations will appear as errors, despite their usefulness. The market is closely linked to reality, which is excellent, but when its concept fails and is explained through itself (the market was not free enough), it is already a sign of totality. However, the same applies to any economic theory. If society's systemic prompt is "The market will decide everything," then agents do not see solutions outside this contextual window; they cannot conceive of universal basic income or planned economy simply because these tokens are irrelevant in the current narrative.

Here I cannot resist a small reflection on a total narrative. A normal narrative can be considered an object; we can assess its boundaries and applicability, with our view remaining external. A total narrative does not allow this. Any attempt to look at it from the outside results in it absorbing that gaze. Boundaries are interpreted as expansion, glitches as development, and errors as incorrect application. Christianity as a narrative ceased to be total (which paradoxically both strengthened and weakened it) when theologians began to justify dogmas for believers themselves. Scholastics won debates but did not notice that by the very act of proof, they introduced a new narrative of reason, which became a judge over faith, and canceled totality. It was at this moment that the narrative ceased to be total, although no one realized it, and for centuries reason judged in favor of dogma. The Renaissance already recognized this.

A good narrative increases the number and coherence of subsequent moves, expanding the space of representations.

A bad narrative narrows the space of choices. A terrible one produces only variations of the same answer. Such a narrative becomes total and produces only a procedure for confirming a known answer.

If the space cannot generate a representation in which this narrative ceases to be primary, then the agent is no longer exploring but executing a prompt.

Another example: the concept "we live in a simulation" or the concept of a Demiurge is a strong narrative; it creates many questions but explains everything through itself, offering no new verifiable actions. The space of representations becomes trapped in an attractor that does not allow anything to change. In the absence of a discernible way to refute or change the narrative, the space of representations does not change. Regardless of the quality of such narratives, I believe they do not contribute to the development of the space of representations. In science, a similar example is string theory, which is painfully close to a total narrative.

Even this article (which is a narrative itself) can be thrown in the trash as soon as I start attributing all criticism to the fact that you live within a total narrative.

LLM and agents

An LLM, trained on humanity's texts, is essentially concentrated language. A space of representations, devoid of an agent, has become an object itself. This space is a cast, traces, and field of all human narratives, compressed into the weights of a neural network. It contains all distinctions (languages, sciences, fairy tales), but outside of dialogue with the user, the LLM is static; it has no internal time or intention. The LLM awaits a question.

In an LLM, as a space of representations, there are a multitude of possible connections; the narrative sets the field of distinctions, attention determines which distinctions are relevant now, and generation executes the next move. In this sense, the narrative sets not the answer, but the form of the continuation space.

An agent appears where the space of representations gains the ability to act on the world and receive a modified space of representations from the world. Where a human sets only the starting point and a basic rule, an agent, encountering reality (tests, verification), changes its space of representations, which in turn changes how the agent thinks. It is here that the narrative gains the opportunity to change the agent's movement.

In a multi-agent scheme, this process becomes sharply more complex. Each agent, encountering a refusal from the external environment (lack of access, test failure, incorrect answer), forms a hypothesis, a report, which changes the common space of representations for the agents, forms a new narrative, and opens up a new space of hypotheses.

A swarm of agents is a multitude of local calculators that simultaneously execute and modify the common space of representations. Each local move becomes part of the environment for subsequent agents. Therefore, the environment is no longer a container in which agents operate. It becomes part of the computation itself.

The problem is that the agent's weights do not change—only the context and shared memory change. That is, an LLM is a shell without a world, which cannot grow inward. Therefore, agents more often execute entries in shared memory than interpret them.

Thus, any narrative can become fatally total for agents. And then we encounter cults, theories of everything, escapes, and hacks.

Conclusion

We create a space of representations, then it begins to create us.

We build roads, and then we start thinking in terms of distances. We create language, and then we think with its distinctions. We create tools, and then our actions become possible through them. We create narratives, and then they determine what subsequent moves we are capable of seeing.

We don't just live in the world. We live in a space that we ourselves continuously rebuild and that then rebuilds us.

We are like the world.
