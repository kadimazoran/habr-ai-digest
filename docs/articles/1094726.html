Abrasive materials are an essential tool for homeowners: they are used to sand wood, clean metal, and prepare surfaces for painting. In construction, the quality of abrasives affects literally everything: whether walls and floors will be processed evenly, whether a house can be called beautiful and comfortable, one that is not ashamed to show to guests. And in production, the precision of abrasive processing determines the final quality of parts. For manufacturing companies, the quality of abrasive products is a matter of their prestige and regular customers, who must be sure that abrasives under their brand will not let them down. We are talking about the Belgorod startup 'Solid' – an intelligent visual analytics platform for quality control of abrasive products. It was created by students of Belgorod State Technological University named after V. G. Shukhov (BSTU) and was developed at the inter-university accelerator 'Homenet,' implemented as part of the Federal Project 'Technologies.'

Why Check the Seams of Sanding Belts

You probably haven't even heard of terms like 'under-sanding' or 'over-sanding,' at least when it comes to sanding belt seams. If so, the main thing you should know is that we are talking about the seams of so-called endless sanding belts: it is a flexible abrasive tool in the form of a closed loop with ends glued butt-to-butt. The endless sanding belt rotates endlessly (until it wears out, of course) in a circle on a sanding machine or, say, a manual belt sander.

So, these seams can seriously affect the quality of the finished product: they can simply spoil it, leaving unsightly scratches in the most visible place. And to prevent this from happening, the 'Solid' team used a machine vision system that helps to automatically assess the quality of the finished belt directly on the factory conveyor and instantly find any deviations from the ideal. Thus, the automation of quality control in the production of abrasive belts has a very specific task: to detect a defect before the product moves to the next stage.

The startup has already created an MVP, which is currently being tested in action at the Belgorod Abrasive Plant. If everything goes well, the team will have good prospects: according to research by Sostav.ru, the abrasive market in Russia is about 1.5 billion rubles, with paper abrasives on belts or in packs accounting for up to 25% of the market. As stated in the company's presentation, the defect rate in production can range from 5-10%, and sometimes 40-50%, depending on the industry and the level of process organization. And up to 40% of defects are solely due to the human factor.

Why Development Started Directly at the Plant

Industrial AI is difficult to create relying solely on a technical specification: it does not always convey the specifics of the production process, which become clear only after an in-person acquaintance with the process in the workshop. Therefore, one of the first stages of development was a working trip to the plant. The team worked there under the guidance of the senior lecturer of the Department of Computer Engineering Software and Automated Systems of BSTU and the head of the enterprise's project office, who helped clarify the task on-site. At the plant, the 'Solid' developers were shown how the seam sanding is done during a work shift, they understood the production limitations and the requirements for the result, and determined exactly where on the line their computer vision system should be installed.

The principle of operation of the complex can be described in several sequential steps. First, the camera captures an image of the processed surface. Then, computer vision models analyze the visual data and determine if there are any signs of deviation from the norm in the area. The result is transmitted to the software part of the system, where settings can be adjusted and detected defects can be registered.

The architecture is designed for automated real-time image analysis. The system combines several computer vision models, each responsible for its part of the task; three main components are used:

  • YOLO detects the object that needs to be analyzed in the image.

  • ResNet forms an image representation in the form of features used for subsequent analysis.

  • PatchCore detects deviations from the norm for the surface.

The modules work as part of a unified system, not as separate tools. The software connects image analysis results with the operator interface, settings, and reporting. The development workflow includes several directions:

  • Acquiring images and preparing data for model training.

  • Labeling frames that show different surface states.

  • Training and testing computer vision models.

  • Developing a software interface for interacting with the system.

  • Configuring model parameters and registering detected defects.

  • Preparing the solution for integration into the production process.

Specifics of industrial application: it is necessary to consider equipment characteristics, permissible inspection speed, and the special role of materials science. Therefore, the system must allow for customization to specific conditions and, in the future, support additional product types.

What the Model Can Already Recognize

At the time of preparing the diagnostic report, which formed the basis of this article, the 'Solid' team had trained computer vision models and tested the prototype, achieving the key task – distinguishing three surface states after sanding the seam. These three states are:

  • Under-sanding. The processing was insufficient, so the inspected area shows signs corresponding to this state.

  • Normal. The surface meets the expected processing result.

  • Over-sanding. The processing was excessive and exceeded the permissible limits.

The model is not limited to assigning one of the three classes to an image. In the demonstration examples, the system also highlights anomalous areas, showing exactly where the deviation was detected. This is an important function for production control. It is not enough for the operator to receive a signal about a potential problem – they need to understand which area to inspect. Visual highlighting of anomalies helps to quickly understand the situation and localize the suspected defect.

What's Next

However, based on two metrics alone, it is not yet possible to draw a final conclusion about the model's robustness: it will require testing on a more diverse sample set and under conditions close to real-world operation. Nevertheless, the initial results show that the task can be solved using computer vision. The next step is to expand the dataset, test the recognition quality on new images, and improve the model's performance considering production conditions. In addition to image analysis algorithms, the team is working on an interface through which enterprise employees can interact with the computer vision system.

The Belgorod startup is developing several components for the future software product:

  • The main system window – a user interface for interacting with the software and hardware complex.

  • Model settings – tools for managing parameters that affect image analysis.

  • Defect log – a section for registering detected deviations.

  • Access rights panel – a mechanism for restricting user access to system functions.

This set of components is necessary to turn the recognition algorithm into a tool that can be used in the daily operations of an enterprise. In addition to image analysis itself, an industrial system needs tools for configuration, result recording, and access management. The presented interface should currently be considered a demonstration version. The diagnostic report designates the development of a full-fledged platform software, integration mechanisms, and subsystem interaction as one of the next project stages.

Another task for 'Solid' is to increase recognition accuracy. To achieve this, as indicated in the diagnostic report, it is necessary to obtain more images and video materials with different abrasive belts. The project architecture allows for adding new products and retraining the system on existing ones. This creates a foundation for expanding the development. If the enterprise decides to use it for controlling additional product types, developers will not have to start from scratch: they can adapt the existing architecture and models.

However, the ability to retrain alone does not guarantee the required accuracy. The result must be confirmed with new data and during comprehensive testing. It is important to ensure that after expanding the scope of application, the system maintains the required recognition quality under specific production conditions.

The team also needs to create integration mechanisms and configure subsystem interaction. This stage should help combine individual components into a unified software and hardware complex. After this, testing, preparation of operational documentation, and personnel training are planned.
