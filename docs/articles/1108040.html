Hello everyone, my name is Pavel Sokolov, I am the editor-in-chief of the Online Patent magazine. I also write fiction sometimes. And today I want to write about a sore subject: the use of AI in creating artistic (and not so artistic) texts.

Recently, there have been several scandals in the literary community related to AI. The jury of the Goncourt Prize excluded a novel by an author suspected of generating text from the shortlist. Our jury of the Voloshin Competition stripped a laureate of their title, suspected of the same. Before that, similar scandals occurred with varying degrees of literary resonance in various countries. For example, Kudan Rie, a laureate of the Akutagawa Prize in Japan, admitted after receiving the high award that she had used ChatGPT.

Publishers, competition juries, thick journals (and, by the way, until recently, Habr) are drowning in blatant AI-generated nonsense. Self-publishing, however, is flourishing. Top-tier prompt engineers generate dozens and hundreds of books a year. Amazon itself is literally flooded with such products. Some even manage to make money on it.

At the same time, publishers themselves are actively using AI for translation, editing, proofreading, writing reviews, creating covers, illustrations, layouts, etc. There are quite harsh cases when an author honestly wrote the entire book themselves, but they are returned a manuscript, accepted for publication, already with our favorite artifacts. And even the most primitive AI checker identifies their text as generated. And the innocent gets a slap on the wrist from haters. Although it was just a proofreader or a junior editor who decided to save time. Saved, yeah.

The music industry, video production, film, and TV series: AI is used everywhere. There have even been screenwriters' strikes in Hollywood. It turned out that a neural network writes scripts no worse than seasoned veterans with proper editing. And not to mention music!

According to Russian law, only a human can be an author. Even if the entire text is just AI-generated nonsense, the computer operator, also known as the prompt engineer, also known as the author, will bear criminal and administrative responsibility. Almost everyone has to develop a red policy regarding AI on the fly. Habr, for example, introduced a rather strict moderation rule for content suspected of AI generation. Has it become less after that? Yes and no. Blatant nonsense is less. But now the same major players simply clean up the AI text, removing artifacts and watermarks. Such things read better, yes. This is not blatant AI nonsense. This is text significantly reworked by a human. Such masterpieces contain many proverbs, archaic or strange words that a conditional Claude would not have thought of (a bowsprit to your galley and angry philippics on your forehead). But AI was indeed used in their creation.

The head of a company well-known in narrow circles shared the following case at an event in Moscow: he trained his AI agents on his own texts, now they write for him in different styles, and he only edits. It's convenient for him, and clients are delighted. There are tons of orders. He was somewhat silent about AI detectors, but apparently, he has never been caught.

Advocates of pure human intellect demand that everyone who has used AI in any way declare it. For example, like this: "this text was edited with the help of AI" or "the draft for this translation was done by AI." This is ethical and fair to the reader. But few will do so. It is easier to upload the generated text into a program and clean it of artifacts or train the same agents. And you don't have to write anything or prove anything to anyone. The law does not prohibit such practices. Not yet.

When reading information about a particular painting, it usually says something like: "1899, canvas, oil." This is an accepted standard. Meaning, the painting was created, say, by Repin with oil on canvas in 1899. It is strange to draw analogies between generated texts and this approach, but this is exactly what is ideally required of all prompt engineers, forgive me, authors.

Even if a person wrote the entire text themselves and ran it through AI as an editor or typesetter, it is still the use of AI. And for such innocent things, you will be penalized, especially those who created something in the last three to four years and forgot to mention in some note that they dared (burn the scoundrel!!!) to use AI.

IT professionals spit at "vibewriting," translators at "vibetranslating" (when the entire text is translated by AI and sent to the editorial office without editing, and sometimes to print), editorial and magazine staff are fed up with AI-generated nonsense that they are forced to read and run through AI checkers.

The mass use of AI has only been around for a few years, and this technology has already frankly annoyed many. People have developed a discerning eye; a casual reader can identify blatant AI-generated nonsense in a minute. Content creators themselves have become afraid of long dashes, bulleted lists, ternary examples, short sentences, potential artifact words like "identity," long enumerations, like the one you just read, fragmentation into short paragraphs, and much more.

People have become more tolerant of errors and typos, as this is also a sign that the text was created by a human. Some AI-nonsense cleaners even deliberately add them to humanize the text.

There will be no conclusions to my reflections. Neuroethics are still forming. If previously, to somehow harm an undesirable author, they were accused of plagiarism, sexual harassment, witchcraft, immoral behavior, or some other horror, now it is enough to write: "this is AI." And on Habr, there is an extremely convenient downvoting system with karma.

Returning to the literary process, I will add that almost the entire history of literature has been built on mystifications, scandals, idea theft, and persecution; let's recall Romain Gary, who became Émile Ajar and twice received the Goncourt Prize, bypassing all the rules. Those who want to will find a way.

In the meantime, every magazine, every website, every award must determine its own attitude towards AI texts. If it's forbidden, then it's forbidden. If not, then it's allowed. In France, a special prize was recently established for books created solely by AI, by AI with human participation, and by humans but edited by AI. This is honest. It's more honest than giving first, and then revoking both the author and everything they did.

Alas, we can no longer get rid of AI paranoia; we are infected with this virus. Now we see (and not without reason) AI-generated nonsense everywhere. Just as in 2022 we looked for a sixth finger on people in photos, in 2026 we look for and find artifacts in texts. A whole industry of AI detectors has grown around our desire to find out the truth and expose liars. Good luck to us all!
