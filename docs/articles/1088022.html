Catalog Criteria

A catalog is a table that Archicad compiles from the model based on conditions in 'Scheme Settings.' Here, it was calculating wall areas by finishes. Codex read the conditions without changing anything. Simplified, with conditional finish names:

Element Type = Wall                                    AND
Wall Finish Material differs from Building Material      AND
( Finish = Tile A                                      OR
  Finish = Tile B                                      OR
  Finish = Plaster )                                   OR
  Finish = White Paint

The opening parenthesis was on the third line, and the closing one on the fifth. White paint was on the sixth line, after the parenthesis. 'AND' has higher precedence than 'OR,' so the expression reads:

(Wall AND Differs AND (Tile A OR Tile B OR Plaster)) OR White Paint

For the 'White Paint' branch, the 'only walls' condition does not apply. Archicad has to check all elements in the project for this finish, not just walls. Graphisoft explains this directly: an ungrouped 'OR' forces the program to check all project elements (see support article). So, we found not a new bug, but a known trap. Next, we'll discuss how it was discovered in someone else's file and what the fix changed.

5.61 m² We Can't Explain

Three out of four lines matched to the hundredth. White paint decreased by 5.61 m². Logically, these are elements that were included in the table only through the 'OR White Paint' branch, bypassing the wall filter. We didn't determine exactly which ones. Codex started temporarily adding an 'Element Type' field to the table to see them, but didn't complete this check and canceled the dialog without changes.

Moving the parenthesis changes not only the speed but also the selection. You can't silently put the faster version into someone else's estimate and consider the task solved: the numbers in it have changed, and we don't know which of the two sets is correct.

The Question the Client Answered

Claude Code presented this as a separate question to the client: what does she use this table for, and why is there white paint in the model? The answer resolved the problem. For her, white paint is a temporary fill: first, all walls are white, then she assigns them the desired tile or color. She needs to calculate plaster and tile, and these matched in both variants. The fast catalog is suitable for her task, and the discrepancy in white paint doesn't bother her.

This knowledge was not in the model, the log, or Graphisoft's documentation. The neural networks found the line, measured carefully, and didn't force the numbers. The client made the decision about which set was correct.

Transferring to the Working File

In the working project, the client corrected the parenthesis herself based on our suggestion. She opened our catalog copy as a sample, asked in chat where the parenthesis should be, and corrected the condition in her table within minutes. By the next day, she had changed the finishes in the catalog, so yesterday's numbers no longer applied to her file. Codex didn't substitute them but reread the current scheme: the parentheses were correctly placed, and the catalog opened in less than ten seconds.

We also checked the template from which each of her new projects starts. There, both wall finish catalogs were already grouped correctly, and we didn't touch them again. A new project created from the template opened the wall catalog in about twenty seconds, accounting for remote screen delays.

If Your Catalog is Slow

  1. Copy the project and work on the copy. Check the window title to ensure you've opened the correct one.

  2. Don't rush to kill a task just because it shows 'Not Responding.' If the process is using the CPU, record the time and give the operation a reasonable amount of time: this will give you a baseline for comparison. If it still doesn't complete, that's a different diagnostic issue.

  3. Open 'Scheme Settings' and look for chains of identical conditions connected by 'OR.' Check where the parentheses are placed and which element type conditions are outside the group.

  4. Before making changes, rewrite the areas for all table rows.

  5. Change only one thing: the parenthesis position. Don't add or remove finishes simultaneously.

  6. Compare both the time and each area. If the numbers differ, find out from the model's author which variant meets their needs.

  7. Close the tab of a heavy catalog before confirming the settings for its copy.
