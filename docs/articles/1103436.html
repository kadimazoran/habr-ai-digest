I encountered a misunderstanding that testers have become a bottleneck in the software development process. Modern programmers, using subscription coding agents for $20 a month, can produce the volume of code and functionality in a day's work that they previously took a week or even a month to do. But what's the point? Until testers verify what the programmer, along with the LLM, has produced, it won't be released to production or made public for users.

What was the ratio of programmers to testers before? It was considered half a tester per programmer. Ideally, one tester per programmer. And how has everything changed now? If one programmer produces 3–5 times more results, then even minimally, you need 2 testers per programmer instead of half a tester, as was previously thought.

The situation with programmers is also funny: no matter how many teams I've worked in, only 20% of programmers produce 80% of all code, while the other 80% produce only 20%. How is this possible? Usually, to increase the volume of work done, they hire programmers, while costs increase by 2–3 times, and the volume of work done increases by 50% at best. And this is because it's difficult to find productive programmers, and they hire whoever they can just to fill the gap. Then the question arises: why not redirect budgets from inefficient programmers to increase the number of testers? It's easier to add $20–100 to the salaries of programmers and testers for AI and cut at least 30% of programmers. There will even be extra money left over.

And, by the way, I don't really understand why testers don't use coding agents? For example, a tester can have access to the latest codebase and connect via MCP to the ticket system. That is, the tester asks the coding agent to create a ticket according to all the rules and even look at the codebase for clarification when creating it. Screenshots can even be added to the request to the coding agent, and they will also be included in the ticket. Then I see that many people don't want to create and maintain UI autotests. And what if the tester has a coding agent at hand that will write autotests at their request, peeking into the project code? Wouldn't that be great and fast? It's hard and time-consuming for the tester to do it themselves, and it also requires constant maintenance because the product changes, but here it will all be done for them.

I also heard that in one of the largest product companies with an online service, they went even further. Quoting from a correspondence: "Now the agent has entered Figma, checked the screen map. Launched everything itself on the emulator, checked everything. Fixed it, opened a merge request itself, made a description with screenshots, and moved all tasks in Jira to Done." In addition to reducing programmers, they got rid of testers. They say that the person who does the task tests it themselves. But this is a categorically wrong approach. The programmer's job is to prove that it works, and the tester's job is to prove that it doesn't work. As a result, as I was told, the quality of what goes into production has, of course, decreased. But they seem to be tolerating it for now. Maybe they are waiting for a major incident to bring testers back.

Update 1.

So, AI shouldn't do the tester's job. You need to test yourself. AI can only speed up the tester's work when creating tickets or writing UI autotests. Testers have become so important now precisely because they manually check the neuro-slop from programmers. This is the last line of defense, which some companies have decided to remove altogether. Which is fundamentally wrong.
