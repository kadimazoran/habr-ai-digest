"Combat" Task: AI Explained a Physical Mechanism to Me. But That Wasn't the Point

The fact that AI can search literature, including scientific literature, and provide reviews of various things - such as scientific theories, concepts, models, disagreements in known approaches, experimental methods, and so on - is now quite commonplace. In fact, when I asked it to briefly list known approaches to describing phenomenon "X," I expected exactly that - a competent review, including beyond the specific field I had been working in until then. It did this quite competently. It did mess up the references (that's its general problem - scientific references...), but then it corrected itself and provided the right ones.

I asked for more detailed information about one equation - I hadn't seen it before, but I had arrived at the exact same one myself. Not because I'm so great, but because the equation is very simple - if you have the data, it's hard to "miss" it, it comes out naturally. But why it turns out exactly like this - I didn't quite understand. I asked it to explain.

In response, it predictably provided a description of known concepts that use either this equation or very similar ones. However, these concepts were not very applicable to my case - although the authors of these publications didn't always think about this and tried to apply them specifically to my case. Accordingly, I asked, what about this - authors of article B refer to process V, which, however, according to article G and many others, should not, in principle, be observed in this specific case. Who is lying - or is everyone right in their own way?

The AI churned its gears a bit and said that in that case, one should start from another phenomenon that is observed here and which, in principle, can also cause this very effect. I asked: what if this phenomenon is also not observed (reference to source), and the pattern is still the same - then what? The AI thought for a moment again and offered another direction. But that also didn't explain the situation very well... So I kept pointing it to one thing, then another, then a third... and then I asked "point-blank": you yourself see that we observe this very specific dependence in various cases, including those where none of these explanations seem applicable. If the same equation is applicable both when phenomenon "A" is present and when it is absent, and the same applies to "B," and "C," and so on, then perhaps the reason is something else?

And then it gave me an explanation that I found very interesting. I hadn't heard of it before... I asked it for a reference - it gave one. The reference turned out to be "bogus" again (again, references are its problem). I asked for clarification. It led me and itself in circles for a long time. I also searched for references to what it explained - I couldn't find any. In the end, it honestly admitted: it invented this mechanism itself, because it logically follows from the very work it initially referred to, taking into account this and that. And this metaphor (referring to a well-known children's game) can help a lot. I didn't immediately grasp what it meant here - but then I got it. It was right!

One can argue with the logic of the explanation, of course, but that's not the point - the point is that the AI, after being nudged about several contradictions in existing theories and models, was able to derive a new concept that - at least in my opinion - does not contradict anything and reasonably explains the cause. Moreover, this mechanism leads to several verifiable consequences, some of which are consistent with literature data, some with unpublished (!) measurements by my colleagues (by the way, a rather simple model could describe this quantitatively - which my colleagues couldn't do at the time), and some things - as far as I know, have not yet been investigated, and there is definitely something to investigate there.

I discussed this with my colleagues, including those who are experts in this specific topic (they've been working on it for 20 years) - no one could raise any substantive objections. We will most likely conduct experiments soon.

In conclusion: the AI was able to invent a new scientific concept that explains something that none of the previously known concepts could explain. It did this in communication with a biological entity - but this is precisely the kind of communication that is usually qualified in scientific practice as "author's creative contribution." Some might argue - but very high-level specialists who have been in the field for many years did not: this is a truly new scientific explanation, with real advantages and no apparent disadvantages.

AI Derived an Equation for a Function. But, Again, That Wasn't the Point

Another case - and again about what AI, in principle, should be able to do, but not quite in this variant.

I needed a general equation for the temperature dependence of a certain property. Einstein, Debye, and some other people of similar caliber were specifically involved in this issue at one time; for example, Bohr and Planck were nearby, and Maxwell wasn't far either. They proposed equations - not just one, but several. Some of them were very accurate - and I needed extreme accuracy. But these equations have one common drawback: they can "approximate," but they cannot "extrapolate." That is, their equations very accurately describe what has already been measured, but they cannot predict what has not yet been measured. There are other equations - they are not as accurate, but they allow for extrapolation. And I needed both at once: extreme accuracy and extrapolations, even if not so extremely accurate, but acceptable in the absence of data.

These two things, accuracy and the ability to extrapolate, are rarely combined in one model. But, I thought, maybe? Again, since Einstein and Debye were involved in this, perhaps they could have found such a solution? Alas - they couldn't.

And we delved into the analysis. It happened like this. I asked the AI to propose an equation that would describe the same thing as a certain initial equation "A," but with an additional condition "B" - for example, that it would have not 7 parameters, but a maximum of 5, or preferably 4. Or that the fourth derivative would not "chatter" and "jiggle." Or that the asymptote when approaching the limiting value would not be like this, but like this, as follows from these published data (which, again, Einstein and Debye couldn't describe well, but the data were reproduced many times). Or...

We tried a lot. But in the end - it did find an equation that satisfied all my wishes - including the fourth derivative, extreme accuracy, and extrapolations. I, by the way, also found one new equation during this analysis - but it found a better and more general one.

I asked, just in case, if such an equation is already known? And the AI happily replied: YES, Sir! Exactly - the equation by such-and-such author from the work of such-and-such year. But - references, alas, are its problem... This work did not contain such an equation. A similar one - but not the same, and the accuracy is not quite the same... It gave me several more references (including to my own work, confusing, as usual, both its author and content...). I also searched - all misses... The equation turned out to be its own finding.

Here I want to emphasize: I didn't give it any datasets. I didn't provide any numerical data (although we both provided a few references to known works, but the AI didn't analyze the data from them itself). I analyzed the data myself, trying to apply the equation. It could have handled this itself (I've been convinced of this many times in similar matters), but I deliberately didn't do it here - and in general, I didn't want to go beyond its free functionality and communicated with it anonymously through a publicly available web interface. My task was not to "adjust coefficients," but to "derive a new equation that Einstein and Debye couldn't derive." And we, in the end, succeeded - I repeat, using the free version, in a couple of hours of work.
