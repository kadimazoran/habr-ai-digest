❯ From Assembler to Vibe Coding. A Brief History of Development Tools

To appreciate the scale of the situation, it's worth at least briefly tracing how computer game development has evolved in principle.

The first mass-market games from the late 70s to the early 90s were often made in assembler. In the 1970s-1980s, game engines, operating systems, and standard libraries simply did not exist. Writing a game was like hand-assembling a Swiss watch: any error led not to a nice error message screen, but to a dead freeze of the chip.

And you had to be extremely resourceful, especially in the heyday of the NES and Famicom. Shigeru Miyamoto said that, in essence, the entire iconic Super Mario Bros. is built on technical tricks like "clouds and bushes in the game use the same sprite, but with different colors."
