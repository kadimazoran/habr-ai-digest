Gemini Notebook has established itself as one of the best tools for in-depth work with sources: it structures unstructured data, builds mind maps, generates presentations, and creates realistic audio summaries in podcast format, and generates excellent tests which are always useful in my research. It is a tool that Google pays a lot of attention to and is constantly improving.

YouTube channels are now overflowing with videos about the new stage of automation: AI agents are going beyond ordinary dialog windows. One of the common scenarios is delegating research routine to a tandem of Claude Code and Gemini Notebook. The promise looks flawless: you enter one phrase, the agent independently scours the internet, downloads materials, uploads them to a Google notebook, and provides ready-made structured slides in a corporate style. I decided to test this pipeline for robustness in a real work environment (based on Windows 10), to disassemble the solution architecture piece by piece, and to answer the main practical question: where does practical benefit end and meaningless complication on crutches begin?

The promise from the video versus reality

In demonstration videos, everything looks like magic in three steps:

  1. Installed the desktop client and opened the agent environment.

  2. Fed the agent one Skill file.

  3. Received an autonomous pipeline that works without your participation

But when an engineer tries to deploy this construct on a clean machine, the magic quickly dissipates. Under the hood of this connection is not an elegant official API, but a wrapper library notebooklm-py, which tries to emulate the actions of a live person through browser automation.

During the actual environment initialization, the agent encountered a whole bouquet of system inconsistencies:

  1. Errors in the authors' documentation, the very instructions you feed to Claude, the Skill file. The library authors honestly write in the README that it is a community project, has no relation to Google, and may break at any moment.

  2. Had to separately install Chromium via Playwright, manually log in to the Google account, and give the session to the script.

  3. Most of the scripts broke at the launch stage due to a hard dependency on the Linux architecture, which made them absolutely unviable in a Windows environment

https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py

This is where it gets interesting, the reason I'm writing this article. Claude honestly fixed all this himself: he picked up the error, looked into the documentation, corrected it, restarted. From the outside, it's impressive – the agent is indeed capable of pulling itself out of a hole on an unfamiliar OS. But I sat next to it for 15 minutes, watched the running log, and at some point caught myself asking a simple question: Why am I setting up a local server, installing a browser emulator, and monitoring all this, when Gemini Notebook has a clean, fast, and free interface that opens in fifteen seconds?

I am not one for wholesale denial. Complex engineering connections have a right to exist – but only where they justify their own maintenance.

When it's pure over-engineering:

  • One-off task. Uploading two or three articles or a video link to gather тезисы for a presentation. Doing it manually in the browser is faster, cleaner, and safer.

  • Confidential and sensitive data: Passing Google authorization tokens to third-party scripts based on Playwright in unverified local environments is a serious security compromise.

  • Creative and deep research: A human selects sources critically. The agent takes what's on the surface of the search results, often missing non-obvious professional insights.

When the connection really works for you:

  • If it is critically important for you to receive a ready-made digest or a set of slides on a narrow topic every morning by 9:00 AM without a single click, while the computer is on.

  • Batch processing of local archives: When you have hundreds of structured Markdown files or reports on your disk, and dragging them through the browser manually becomes a mechanical torture.

  • End-to-end design standardization: If a strict visual order manifest (monochromatic palette, grotesques, minimal visual noise) is embedded in the text skill, the agent can forcibly make the engine format presentations according to a single brand book.

Behind the facade of automation lies a fundamental problem that is often forgotten in the pursuit of hype: blind trust in algorithms when dealing with meaning. By delegating information gathering and filtering entirely to the machine, we risk receiving impeccably formatted, but absolutely empty or distorted information.

The next obvious step is the "agent button": press it, the service goes to the internet and fetches links. And here's the interesting part. At the same moment, Gemini Notebook will turn into an ordinary Perplexity or ChatGPT. It will lose the only thing that distinguishes it from them today – the fact that you personally collected the knowledge base and you are responsible for it too.)
