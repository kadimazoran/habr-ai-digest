Background and Objectives

Our client operates in the corporate training market. Their areas of expertise include high-stakes negotiations, B2B sales, work with management teams, strategic sessions, and facilitation. The company approached Kokoc Group seeking a new source of traffic and increased brand awareness.

The corporate training segment is characterized by high competition: the market includes training companies, business schools, consulting firms, educational platforms, and experts. Many well-known brands, such as Moscow Business Academy, SKOLKOVO, RSHU, ITC Group, LiCO, EKOPSY Consulting, etc., had significantly higher visibility in AI responses than our client. We used this as our benchmark.

Furthermore, the same task can be phrased differently by various corporate clients. Some may not be looking for a training company at all, but rather a reliable partner for business education or a facilitator for their management team.

Our task was to establish a strong association between the brand and its key areas of expertise, so that neural networks would start considering our client's company when generating recommendations for potential corporate buyers. It was important not just to increase the number of mentions, but to ensure the brand appeared in the relevant semantic context: personnel training for large companies, B2B sales, high-stakes negotiations, development of managers and top executives, and so on.

At the project launch stage, the team was set a KPI: to achieve at least 30% brand visibility in neural network responses within 3 months.

Developing the company's blog was planned as the next phase of the project. The focus was on systematic publications on external platforms with a high level of trust, the parameters of which we had thoroughly researched in advance.

The key goal was to cover not isolated queries, but a holistic set of user scenarios, and to understand in advance which materials, in what format, and on which resources could build the necessary semantic connections around the brand.

Project manager Natalia Natarova and SEO specialist Daria Serbina also worked on the project.

Step 1. Measuring Initial Visibility and Defining Result Criteria

The active work period lasted from June 4 to August 5, 2026. Before that, we conducted a technical audit – the client implemented some recommendations, which laid the groundwork for further GEO/AEO promotion.

Then, we measured brand visibility before starting work using a pre-approved list of prompts, including "who conducts strategic sessions," "best negotiation training," etc. Measurements were taken in Yandex Search with Alice, Google AI Overview, ChatGPT, Perplexity, as well as DeepSeek and Claude.

For each prompt, we recorded whether the brand appeared in the neural network's response. Based on this data, we calculated the overall project visibility and tracked its dynamics during the promotion. The collected data formed the basis for calculating the cumulative project visibility – the proportion of "prompt × system" checks in which the company was present in the neural network's response. This was the main KPI of the project. With 37 prompts and 6 systems, one measurement covered 222 checks.

Step 2. Forming a List of Target Prompts

We selected them based on a combination of criteria: the corporate training niche itself, our client's key services, questions arising from the target audience, search demand, current neural network responses, and the extent to which competitors were represented in them.

We also considered the commercial weight of the user scenario, prioritizing queries with a conditional commercial intent. In total, we obtained 37 target prompts.

Step 3. Semantic Clustering

We divided the selected prompts into thematic clusters:

  • 10 prompts were included in the "Corporate Training and Business Training" cluster. We then prepared 30 publications on this topic.

  • 7 prompts and 21 publications in the "Negotiations" cluster.

  • 6 prompts and 18 publications in the "B2B Sales, KAM, and Complex Deals" cluster.

  • 6 prompts and 18 publications in the "Leadership and Management" cluster.

  • 5 prompts and 15 publications in the "Strategic Sessions and Facilitation" cluster.

  • 3 prompts and 9 publications in the "Business Trainers and Experts" cluster.

Step 4. Creating a Content Matrix and Selecting Platforms

Before publishing articles on external platforms, we created a matrix – for each of the 37 prompts, we developed 3 separate topics for platforms like VC.ru, Dzen, and TenChat. The platforms were chosen based on several criteria: speed and stability of indexing, likelihood of articles ranking high for our target queries, domain trust level, presence of the target audience, availability of desired formats (more on this later), and good chances of materials being picked up by search and generative systems.

Having a matrix and selecting platforms helped us later – we didn't have to adjust the content plan on the fly, as often happens. This significantly contributed to exceeding the target KPI.

Step 5. Developing Article Topics and Structures with the Aim of Appearing in Neural Network Responses

We selected prompts with a recommendation intent: for example, "who to choose," "recommend a company," "what are the best programs," etc. Rating articles, lists, reviews, and materials that analyzed selection criteria fit this intent perfectly – and these are what we wrote.

The materials were designed to cover all 37 prompts and encompass the client's 6 business areas. Importantly, one prompt did not turn into three identical copy-pasted articles. We considered the specifics of each platform – VC.ru, Dzen, and TenChat – and prepared a unique title, structure, and semantic focus for each. Thus, the same user scenario was presented on one platform as a rating of providers, on another as a selection for HR and L&D specialists, and on a third as a guide with tips for choosing contractors.

Naturally, all articles were written with the potential structure of a generative response in mind.

Step 6. Creating Brand Semantic Profiles

A universal company description was not used in our work. We prepared a specific factual profile for each cluster: for instance, in the negotiation cluster, the focus was on complex B2B deals, building negotiation strategy, and managing power dynamics. In the leadership cluster, we wrote about manager development and the LeadSmart methodology. In articles about strategic sessions, we discussed facilitation and guiding parties to a common solution. Each topic was maximally relevant to the user's task.

Step 7. Developing a Rating Methodology

Before writing ratings and selections, we established a general regulation outlining how to select participants, by what criteria to compare companies and individual experts, how to describe them, and how to conduct fact-checking. This ensured that publications remained informative and brand positioning was based on verifiable characteristics.

The evaluation criteria were applied equally to all participants in the selection: all descriptions were taken from open data to avoid distortion. Comparisons between rating participants were also not allowed.

Step 8. Writing and Publishing Materials for External Platforms

The work on the materials was done step-by-step. First, we took a specific query for which an article needed to be written. Then, we generated a topic – a unique one for the platform where it would be published. Next, we created a prompt for an AI agent and received a draft text from it, which was then manually edited by a human editor.

Each material underwent several filters before publication: fact-checking, manual editing (including removing clichés and promotional language), standardizing descriptions of rating participants, adapting for VC, Dzen, and TenChat, and a final proofread before publication. The strategy relied on a hybrid format: the strategy and thematic architecture were developed by an expert, while AI was used to accelerate and scale content production.

After publication, we tracked whether each material was indexed by Yandex, Google, and Bing search engines. If not, we took additional measures to rectify the situation.

In total, over two months from June 4 to August 5, 111 articles were published on external resources. Such a dense release schedule allowed us to widely establish the brand for target prompts in a short time and accelerate the accumulation of content volume, without which visibility in neural network responses does not grow.

What About Leads?

I want to talk separately about leads. In parallel with the brand gaining weight in neural networks, the client received an influx of new leads, with marketing efforts primarily focused on GEO during this period.

Of course, attributing all the increase in inquiries solely to GEO would be a mistake: the user journey in generative search is much more complex than the usual source attribution model. For example, a user asks a neural network who conducts the best negotiation training in Moscow. The response includes our client among other providers. The user remembers the brand, then searches for it on a search engine, visits the website, and submits a request.

In this case, the source of the visit would be recorded as Yandex or Google, and the transition itself would fall into the category of branded search traffic. Although, in reality, the first touchpoint with the brand and the interest in it occurred in the neural network. And even when the neural network provides links to sources, not everyone clicks on them – the acquaintance might end with a separate search for the company name.

From this, we can conclude that the number of direct transitions from neural networks does not convey the full picture of GEO's influence on the sales funnel. The main indicator at this stage is brand visibility in relevant recommendation responses. Simply put, how often a potential buyer sees the company among those from whom they have to choose.

To accurately understand the role of the GEO promotion channel in lead growth, the picture needs to be assembled holistically: we need to observe how branded demand behaves in parallel, what is happening with branded organic traffic, and how the number of inquiries is changing. These are the metrics we will compare with the visibility growth in the next stage.

Conclusions and Project Insights

  1. Success was ensured not by the publication activity itself, but by the quality of the strategy prepared in advance.

  2. The associative link between the brand and the promoted services – corporate training, B2B sales, negotiations, leadership, and strategic sessions – has significantly strengthened. The preparatory architecture played a key role in this: precise prompt selection, coverage of main business areas by clusters, understanding platform specifics, and consistent building of the brand's external information field.

  3. Teamwork deserves special mention: distributed teams acted in concert, and strong project coordination allowed all participants to equally understand the client's goals, maintain focus on the final result, and quickly respond to questions and changes.

  4. External GEO promotion can yield independent and measurable results even before a large-scale corporate blog is operational. The matrix of 37 prompts and 111 publications over two months increased visibility across all tracked systems, cumulative brand visibility grew from 0.83% to 42.79%, the established KPI of 30% was exceeded ahead of schedule, and the brand became one of the category leaders.
