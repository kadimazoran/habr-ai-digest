How I Compared the Services

When I was looking for a service myself, I looked at other people's compilations. They often featured OpusClip, Repurpose.io, Runway, and Sora side-by-side, even though they solve different problems. It's likely that AI compiled them, and no one manually touched them. I wanted to try them out, and in some cases, even pay for the active plans.

For each option, I looked at five things:

  • Does the service independently find meaningful fragments in long videos?

  • Does it understand Russian speech and how does it format subtitles?

  • Is there an editor for manual refinement of the result (but ideally, it shouldn't be needed)?

  • Can it publish clips or at least prepare them without unnecessary transitions between services?

  • How much does a trial cost, and what difficulties await a user from Russia?

1. OpusClip — Automatic Cutting of Long Videos

OpusClip cuts long conversational videos into short clips. ClipAnything analyzes speech and image, then selects fragments with complete meaning. The service adds subtitles and keeps the speaker in the vertical frame. Virality Score helps compare options but doesn't predict views.

You can upload a file, paste a YouTube link, or import a video from Google Drive. The service supports 9:16, 1:1, and 16:9 formats. The project can be opened in Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve. Russian language support is weak and in beta: check subtitles manually.

Russia is not included in the official list of supported countries. Before starting, check if you can access your account and upload videos.

OpusClip workspace: timeline and additional footage (B-roll). Source: OpusClip help on layouts.

2. MnogoReels — Cutting and Publishing in One Service

I rank MnogoReels second: in one account, you can cut videos and publish clips. The service accepts files from your computer and links to YouTube or RuTube. The user sets the clip length from 10 seconds to three minutes. A text prompt helps find fragments on the desired topic. Import from VK is temporarily unavailable. Publishing can be scheduled for YouTube or RuTube.

After selecting the language, MnogoReels recognizes Russian speech and offers 23 subtitle styles. Additional footage (B-roll) can be taken from stock or generated during video cutting. In Agent mode, the algorithm re-evaluates the cuts and filters out weak fragments. For this, the Re-Act algorithm is used for cyclical quality improvement. The system assigns the clip a score from 0 to 100, considering the beginning, completeness, and tempo. The score helps sort clips but doesn't guarantee views. In terms of video quality, this turned out to be the strongest and most affordable algorithm due to reduced prices for LLM tokens through the Polza.ai service.

In the built-in MnogoCut editor, you can correct editing and subtitles, replace media files or effects. The editor is still in beta, designed for computers, and stores projects in the current browser. After edits, the user exports the clip and manually uploads it for publishing.

The "Do everything from one link" mode prepares clips, SRT subtitle files, covers, and texts for publication.

3. VEED — AI Cutting within a Browser Editor

AI Clips in VEED finds fragments with complete meaning, adds subtitles, and keeps the speaker in the center of the frame. Before starting, the user selects the language and video format; if necessary, they set the maximum length and describe the topic. VEED evaluates the results, after which the user opens the clip in the browser editor.

In the editor, you can correct the transcription, change clip boundaries, add graphics, or branding. AI Clips recognizes Russian speech, while Magic Cut only works with English.

AI Clips only accepts a new audio file with speech lasting from one minute to three hours. Projects from the VEED library and YouTube links are not suitable. There is no direct social media publishing: the result is downloaded in MP4.

AI Clips settings in VEED: video purpose and description of desired fragments. Source: screenshot from VEED help.

4. Descript — Editing by Text Transcript

In Descript, editing the transcript immediately changes the video — this is how you can edit English-language podcasts. Create Clips selects 1–20 fragments lasting from 10 seconds to five minutes and places each in a separate composition. The user sets the topic and chooses the 9:16 or 1:1 format.

Create Clips does not transcribe Russian speech. However, Descript translates finished subtitles and supports dubbing into Russian.

Descript Create Clips panel with settings for future clips. Source: Descript help on Create Clips.

5. Repurpose.io — Publishing Finished Clips

Repurpose.io works with finished videos: it connects social media accounts with cloud storage and publishes content on a schedule. A template changes the clip format for the platform. This reduces the number of manual uploads for teams with multiple channels.

In Create Clips, the user manually sets the start and duration of the fragment. The automatic scenario cuts video from a specified point or by timecodes from the description. The documentation does not mention semantic search. Repurpose.io does not create subtitles but accepts a finished track or an SRT file.

Repurpose.io workspace: active publishing scenarios. Source: Web Hive Digital review.

Additional Services for Scene Generation

Runway and Google Flow create short scenes or modify images. To find meaningful fragments in a long recording, a separate cutting service is needed.

6. Runway — Generating and Processing Short Scenes

Runway Agent transfers material to the Final Cut editing timeline and assembles a rough cut. Detect Shots divides the video by scene changes. You can also create B-roll there, change the background with a text prompt, and generate voiceovers and sound effects.

The user first selects an episode, then processes it in Runway. Edit Studio accepts source material shorter than 30 seconds and modifies a segment up to 10 seconds in one operation. Gen-4.5 creates videos lasting 2–10 seconds, including in 9:16 format. The documentation does not describe searching for speech by meaning or creating multiple Reels from an interview in one run.

The interface is in English. Runway accepts prompts in other languages, but the quality drops significantly.

Runway workspace: Agent analyzes clips on the Final Cut timeline. Source: Runway help on Studio.

7. Google Flow with Veo 3.1 — Generating Vertical Scenes

Veo 3.1 in Google Flow creates videos of 4, 6, or 8 seconds based on a text prompt or provided frames. It supports vertical format and can add sound. Scenebuilder combines fragments, and Video Resizer changes the aspect ratio.

Flow accepts source videos up to 1 GB in size and up to 60 seconds long. Material longer than 30 seconds needs to be trimmed before editing. Flow modifies a fragment up to 10 seconds long in one operation. It does not break down long interviews into meaningful clips with subtitles.

Russia is not included in the official list of supported countries. Google warns that VPNs do not grant access from unsupported regions.

Google Flow workspace with the Veo model: scenes and text-based generation. Source: Google Flow website.

Extra Slot — Sora, a Shut-Down Service

I included Sora in the list because the service still appears in compilations, and some people are still writing about it. Unfortunately, it is shut down. Users can only export previously created content. I also wanted to include Captions AI for creating dynamic stylish subtitles, but it lacks the full functionality for creating short videos from long formats.

Before it was shut down, Sora generated videos up to 20 seconds long in 1080p resolution, supported vertical format, Storyboard, and editing of finished scenes. It did not search for speech in long recordings and did not produce a series of Shorts.

Which Service to Choose

Choose a service based on your task:

  • OpusClip — automatically finds fragments, if the service is available in your region;

  • MnogoReels — works with Russian-language video and publishes clips from the same account;

  • VEED — combines automatic selection with manual editing in the browser;

  • Descript — edits English-language recordings based on transcription;

  • Repurpose.io — publishes finished clips on multiple platforms;

  • Runway or Flow — generate B-roll and short scenes.

For Russian-language interviews, test MnogoReels first.

Before publishing, review each video in its entirety: check fragment boundaries, subtitles (especially names), and whether the B-roll matches the content. AI speeds up fragment finding and rough editing, but it can interrupt the thought process.
