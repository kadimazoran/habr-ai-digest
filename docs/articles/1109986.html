Thirteen Years, One Miami, and Almost Five Thousand People

It all begins not with code, but with trips. In 2015, a group of Rockstar North developers went to Miami: nightclubs until 4 AM, a ride with the cops to South Beach, bars in the Florida Keys.

The working title of the game was Project Americas: they initially planned to cover two continents. They abandoned this idea, sticking with Florida. Later, Rockstar decided who the main characters would be. Only by early 2019 was the pair determined: Jason and Lucia, inspired by Bonnie and Clyde and couples from "True Romance" and "Natural Born Killers."

Then, everything happened at once:

  • September 18, 2022: Over 90 videos of early game versions leaked on GTAForums: a female protagonist, test maps, debug overlays;

  • December 4, 2023: Rockstar released the first trailer: 90 million views in 24 hours;

  • then the release date shifted from Fall 2025 to May 26, 2026, and May became November 19, 2026.

The team grew significantly. Nearly 5,000 people worked on the game, and the team quadrupled in size compared to GTA V. Rockstar hasn't disclosed the budget, but independent estimates start at $1 billion, with some sources citing up to $2 billion (including marketing and infrastructure for future online services).

An interesting detail that's easy to miss: Rockstar didn't just develop in Scotland. In Leeds, the studio grew from the Mobius Entertainment team, which Rockstar acquired, and was located in a former church in Stanningley. And now, the question I've been asking myself all these years: why thirteen years?

Nelson answers in an interview: previously, limitations were imposed by technology, but now they are human limitations. How much can you physically build? And Kolbe adds: "We could have made GTA 5.5 and released it faster," but they didn't want to.

RAGE 9: The Engine That Learned to Sweat

If GTA 6 has a heart, it's the engine. RAGE (Rockstar Advanced Game Engine) has been around since 2006. GTA V ran on RAGE 7, Red Dead Redemption 2 received RAGE 8 with weather, volumetric effects, and Vulkan/DX12. For GTA 6, there's talk of RAGE 9.

There's no official "RAGE 9 specification." But there's a report from Rockstar Mag from 2023, based on leaks and sources. It promised new water physics, better car deformation, physically based lighting, and real weather. Some of this has been confirmed over three years, while some remains unconfirmed.

The author claims that a "RAGE Technology Group" of about twenty engineers from the San Diego studio worked on the water: physically simulated water in real-time, and in an open world at that. Before this, NVIDIA's WaveWorks remained at the level of a beautiful tech demo.

⮴ The video Rockstar Mag used as a benchmark for future water in GTA 6

Rockstar, it turns out, experimented with surfing back in the days of Surfing H3O in 2000, when the hardware wasn't ready. Rumor has it that someone in the studio was reminded of this old debt.

The world is interconnected. Steven Donkin, Rockstar's VP of Design, explains that in the game, "everything is connected to everything else." Change one thing, and it has repercussions elsewhere. Tuning the simulation started to feel like collaborating with the game itself.

In-Game Simulations

  • Sweat. There's a separate exertion system that assesses how much a character is straining and adjusts their perspiration. In the gym and on the dance floor, sweat collects on the skin and reacts to lighting. A related Take-Two patent on fluid simulation on surfaces dates back to 2006, and Rockstar hasn't confirmed its use, so it shouldn't be called a "new invention."

  • Blankets. In most games, characters sleep on top of the bed because animating a sheet is difficult. In GTA 6, characters actually get under the covers. According to Nelson, this required considerable effort: Jason and Lucia share a home, and a person sleeping on top of a blanket at 3 AM would break the sense of home.

  • Sound. Snippets of pedestrian conversations travel further or closer depending on the wind. Appliances in a cheap store hum louder than refrigerators in an expensive one. Music plays from subwoofers and wireless speakers directly in the world.

Weather. Owen Shepherd, VP of Lighting and Rendering, says the team completely rethought the weather systems. Conditions are now local, persistent, and dynamic. One area has a downpour, while another is dry with fog over the bay. Rain alters materials, reduces visibility, and wets hair, which, incidentally, has its own physics.

Light That Unifies Everything

Ray tracing is enabled on all consoles: global illumination and reflections, and on PS5 Pro, ray-traced shadows are added. Some are calling it the best implementation of ray tracing in games released to date. Light from windows floods rooms, signs color nearby objects, and tinted glass provides colored transmission.

Police Who Don't Teleport

Previously, cops in GTA knew everything about you the moment you pulled the trigger. Now they have a dossier: witnesses, victims, cameras, clothing, weapons, car, and whether you were seen together. The HUD shows what information has already entered the case file.

And the player can work with this: change clothes, ditch an identified car, put on a mask before appearing on camera. Clothes can be kept in the trunk and changed on the fly. Police don't appear out of thin air: they drive to the location, must see the suspect, and when they lose sight of you, they search the last known area.

Life in Leonida: From Dogs to Six Stars

Now, about the world itself.

Ask Rob Nelson to show you something, and he'll stop at a passerby with a dog. A Guardian correspondent asked if they could pet the dog, and the GTA 6 character, Jason, directly asked the owner for permission. And after a question about a seagull on a pole, Jason took out binoculars and added the bird to his observation log (there are 62 bird species in the game).

The main change is in relationships. Jason and Lucia can make coffee for each other, watch TV, dance, go on dates, and rob stores together. There's even a dedicated button to hold hands, and it doesn't stick: release your finger, release your hand. Nelson calls this a profound moment because a simple physical action connects gameplay with the emotional core of the story.

It works like an "eternal" conversation: the couple discusses a recent mission, reminisces about the past, and you can't tell what's scripted for an event and what's accidental. After 50 hours, the game shows about 70 percent of the story.

Crime has become more cautious. Early on, you can't steal just any car or pick any lock: first, simple cars and basic locks, then more serious heists. You can't just pull out a machine gun on a crowded street: you need to scout the target, disable cameras or steal recordings, find a back entrance and the safe code. And those who cause a mess risk getting a sixth wanted star: then the entire state is after you until you're arrested or taken down.

Nelson admitted that he himself has never played for six stars after finalization: "I made it too long, so we made it even harder." There's no sadder tale in the world.

And the world is full of side stories. The game has already featured a police helicopter with a spotlight and a crowd around a person in a fox costume (being a 'furry' is illegal in Leonida).

To be fair, not everything is perfect. It's noted that there are so many side activities that the plot sometimes loses its edge, and some of the humor, typical of the 2000s, now sounds out of place.
