We've seen AI draw characters, buildings, and assets (even with sprites). But what about designing locations and levels?

For our test, we'll take ten locations created for an imaginary 3D fantasy RPG with a significant touch of low-poly aesthetics. Our designers will be Nano Banana Pro, Grok Image 2, the flagship Qwen Image 3, and the budget Seedream 5.0 Lite.

Nano Banana and Grok did better than the others, immediately understanding that they were asked for a concept of a game location with an approximate layout. Qwen, however, didn't quite grasp what was wanted in the first attempt and drew a piece of a dungeon that looked like it was stolen from Roblox. Seedream simply drew a corridor with skeletons – maybe it's suitable for a corridor shooter?

Grok and Nano Banana again designed something decent, and Qwen, having recovered, can also be added, as he also drew a reasonable location. Although this might just be a matter of taste.

#3. Alchemist's Laboratory

Seedream 5 Lite

Banana and his campmate Grok continue to create with passion. It's interesting that Grok seems to prefer a darker and more restrained color palette. While Nano Banana chooses brighter, richer, friendlier shades, even when specifically asked to draw something dark.

Qwen, having received positive reinforcement, now understands what is required. A good addition is its separate negative prompting window, where you can specify, for example, that the frame should not be cropped. Seedream, on the other hand, draws something too... childishly naive.

Grok is currently leading in stylistic consistency, although for some reason he added a rather... well-endowed female goblin behind the counter, which was an unexpected surprise. Nano Banana consistently develops the scene well. Qwen, while now drawing a reasonable prototype, creates locations that seem designed for claustrophobia – everything is too cramped and narrow in his version of the shop. Seedream draws quickly, but sparsely – its budget limitations are evident.

This is a more large-scale location with complex elements. Nano Banana interestingly interpreted the prompt, adding large blue circles in the air – apparently, they are needed for training the police griffin battalion. And here, his version is probably the best.

Grok also tried to deliver something large-scale, but the logic of the dashed arrows is not entirely clear – is it a flight path for a police griffin? Qwen's and Seedream's versions clearly lack detail.

All models coped with this task quite decently, even the ascetically drawing Seedream. Nano Banana has an interesting play of light and shadow, while Grok again decorated the scene with buxom women, only this time they are stone and in knightly armor. Qwen's location looks quite melancholic, and Seedream's version looks like a laminate liminal space, which is generally not bad.

Conclusion

Grok coped with this task best of all. He has more successful attempts and good stylistic consistency – based on his concepts, full-fledged locations can already be developed, adding elements as needed. Nano Banana once again proved that he can understand literally from half a word and draw high-quality, quite detailed assets.

Qwen clearly has potential, although for a flagship model, it takes too long to get on the right track. Seedream, on the other hand, makes rather infantile sketches. In terms of speed, it is comparable to Nano Banana, but the latter produces a much more luxurious result in the same amount of time.
