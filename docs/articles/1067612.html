Preamble

Familiar situation?: You go to a website you've used your whole life, and it has another update and "everything has moved everywhere"? And it's good if you're an IT specialist! - Because from complete beginners, you increasingly hear the familiar "I clicked somewhere - and everything is gone..." And even seasoned users don't have it easier - the menu section names have changed to unfamiliar and even counter-intuitive ones, especially for an "old-timer" user, and some menu sections can now only be found inside or outside where they originally were... Pain? Even idiITism? I understand...

Why is this happening and is "nobody does it better" a trend?

There's a thing called the "tragedy of the commons" problem. It's when everyone does what's convenient for themselves, and as a result, the whole mass as a whole becomes worse due to residual errors. John Nash's Game Theory is the opposite. No one is to blame for this - it's a property of society. However, Elinor Ostrom proved that it is possible to build certain social superstructures and conditions that facilitate special configurations of society in which this problem is elegantly bypassed (looking far ahead, we will address this in subsequent articles).

Our case is very similar. In the world of interfaces, I personally am used to describing this problem with the words "non-existent average user." There is a general mass, which on average, for some reason, in the last month, reaches for a button three clicks deep into the main menu more often than before. WHOOSH! - and this button is now "pulled out"... "You wanted it as usual, but it turned out as always.".

However, this is not the only reason for changes in the interface of websites and applications. Business is one of them... But here again, the principle of "the desires of the majority (or simply the more influential business owners) are more important than the desires of the minority."

The Project consists of several Macro-stages, and all of this has been thought through and rehashed so many times that it's scary to recall. But, looking a bit ahead, the stages sound something like this:

  1. A Turing-Complete programming language with the most dynamic, flexible, easily and quickly changeable and customizable syntax for the wishes of an individual programmer. Let's not argue here for now and throw around names of already existing, possibly equally flexible at first glance, programming languages. Let's leave that to the next articles, where I will elaborate on the nuances of our programming language and its fundamental difference from others. However, I can say in my defense now that usually (but not in our case), the more flexible a T-Complete language is, the fewer basic primitives it has, the harder it is to write simply, clearly, and concisely: here are Church's Lambdas and Numerals, and combinators, and, for God's sake, B**F... Nevertheless, our programming language has several mechanisms that give it an elegant way around this problem without losing completeness. The programming language is, in fact, "Zero" (the name reflects the essence!). Its Theory has been fully developed by me over 7 years of endless attempts and circling, and its Core interpreter is being developed right now (August 2026 - the moment of starting work on this article); the Theory was fully ready by mid-2023. I will publish it in its pure form when the time comes for it in my work on this series of articles in this unusual blog. I will also publish a preliminary assumed pseudocode dialect in Zero and clearly demonstrate how quickly and universally it can be customized and adapted by any programmer for themselves. The claim is precisely for the most flexible syntax (without loss of completeness).

  2. A dynamic interface constructor for various Systems (whatever they may be - not just in the OS sense)

  3. A dynamic constructor for layering API capabilities on top of each other, as flexibly as in our programming language, but as conveniently as possible for the user (STT & TTS technologies of voice assistants are relevant, but the main focus is still on the dynamic interface!)

  4. Launching a trend for the paradigm of dynamism, flexibility, complete, prompt, and convenient controllability for the end-user when working with electronics (from the computer system in Elon Musk's helicopter to an iron - anywhere with at least some interface)

  5. Solving some of the problems of modern AI by proposing a fundamentally different technology (the entire Theory is ready and deep code development has begun, the primary Core, so far only to confirm the hypothesis of operability, efficiency, and consistency - there are still nested stages, but more on that much later...)

  6. Setting a trend for applying the same philosophy, but now not to IT Systems - but to Society with a decentralized AI Auctioneer (AI should not become the "President of the Planet," like Skynet for the Cybernetics Era, presumably following the Latest Times).

There will be separate articles on points 5 and 6; they are already practically ready.
