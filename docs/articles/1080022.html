Like many software engineers, I'm certainly interested, and somewhat concerned, about the future of our profession. A recent Habr post about an AI adoption report caught my eye. I opened the reports and looked at the graphs. It turns out that adoption is not progressing as quickly as one might expect. And, unless new models offer a qualitative leap, the situation is unlikely to change.

Here are the links to the reports themselves (in English) for the first quarter and the second. Both are freely available and not too long, about fifty pages each. Incidentally, it turned out that the methodology in the reports changed. In the first quarter, they compared those who use AI with those who don't. In the second, this was no longer relevant—90% of programmers write code with the help of AI.

I wanted to compare this with my own experience and with our company (we fall into the "industrial development" category, 100–300 programmers, North America, not Silicon Valley). AI adoption began over a year ago when Copilot licenses started being distributed. But we only started using it actively last fall, with a gradual push from "above" towards AI-driven development, where the engineer "no longer writes code but commands agents.".

But I see that this adoption is proceeding with great difficulty. Management encourages its use at work and permits and welcomes experiments. And, generally speaking, developers are certainly using agents extensively. At least half the code is written by AI, and in some cases 100%, where small teams of 1–2 people use spec-driven development. But there are no serious breakthroughs, especially in complex projects, that are noticeable. Velocity has increased, but not by orders of magnitude.

I read the report and breathed a sigh of relief—it's not us, it's just how things are. Overall, as I understand it, our company's metrics fully align with those of the participants in the surveys. It's interesting to see what subsequent reports will show, but I suspect we shouldn't expect much difference from the second quarter yet.
