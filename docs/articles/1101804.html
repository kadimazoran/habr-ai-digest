I set up paid traffic for dental clinics (and not only). For good creatives, you always need photos of doctors: the real face of a doctor in the office works better than stock photos. But for a clinic, a doctor photoshoot turns into a separate project with significant time and financial costs. Doctors are busy with patients, offices are occupied, a photographer needs to be paid, and a good one needs to be paid a lot. In a month, new shots are needed because the old creatives have burned out. In general, for dental clinics, this is a long and expensive hassle. I understood this from the very beginning and decided to go a different way.

A clinic usually has at least one photograph of each doctor: a portrait from the website. From such a minimum, I wanted to get a series of shots: a doctor in loupes treating a patient, a doctor at a monitor with a CBCT scan, a surgeon during an operation, a pediatric dentist with a toy dinosaur. In the interior of this particular clinic, in their uniform, with their logo on the uniform/badge.

In a week, a conveyor belt was created: a link to the clinic's doctor page at the input → a folder of approved shots at the output. Now it includes several clinics, over 20 doctors, and 290 approved shots. GPU rental, model tests, and storage cost about $3.2.

How the combiner works as a whole:

Infrastructure: RunPod + ComfyUI via API

The requirements were simple: pay only for generation minutes, don't re-download models every time, and don't expose ComfyUI to the internet.

  • Secure Cloud + 60 GB network volume. Models and ComfyUI reside on the network drive; the pod is created and deleted around it. Community Cloud is cheaper but doesn't support network drives. The drive costs $0.07 per GB per month, which is $4.2 per month, and is charged even when the pod is stopped.

  • GPU by priority: RTX 4090 ($0.89/h), RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell 32 GB ($0.72/h), RTX PRO 4000 Blackwell 24 GB ($0.57/h). RunPod's availability is 'Low' everywhere and changes in minutes, so pod.sh up iterates through the cards in the list. If the old pod's host doesn't have the card, the script deletes the pod and creates a new one in the same data center, and the drive remains.

  • Only 22/tcp is exposed externally. The RunPod template launches FileBrowser and Jupyter with default passwords, which I do not publish. ComfyUI is accessible via an SSH tunnel from within on 127.0.0.1:8188. A separate SSH key is used for the pod; it is passed via the PUBLIC_KEY variable and is not added to the RunPod account.

  • API key only in .env. runpodctl is called via a wrapper that reads the key from .env. The runpodctl config and doctor commands are not used: they save the key to ~/.runpod.

scripts/pod.sh up        # start or recreate + tunnel :8188
scripts/pod.sh status
scripts/pod.sh down      # close tunnel and stop pod

down repeats stop until the pod transitions to EXITED. Once, the RunPod API responded with a timeout, and without retries, the pod would have remained active.

Project rule: before up, the agent writes the estimated cost (tariff × time + model loading); after the session, the pod is always stopped.

Model Selection: FLUX.2 klein 4B/9B → FLUX.3 klein

I proceeded incrementally, using the same prompts and one reference.

FLUX.2 klein 4B. Similarity to a single clean reference is good. Small instruments in hand are a gamble: in about half the shots, instead of a dental mirror, a probe appears. Instead of binocular loupes, a surgeon gets protective goggles from Splinter Cell. Screens are always sharp, with pseudo-interfaces and implausible X-rays.

FLUX.2 klein 9B. The mirror is real in 3 out of 3 shots, loupes are real. But the FLUX Non-Commercial license: commercial companies are only allowed tests and evaluations outside of production. For advertising, a paid BFL license is required, so 9B remained for comparison.

FLUX.3 Image Klein (released October 13). 5.6B transformer, Qwen3-VL-4B encoder, up to 10 references, distilled version at 16 steps without guidance. Of the three models, it follows the prompt best.

Graph: Upsampler is Mandatory

FLUX.3 expects a full scene description of 150–200 words: frame, person, clothing, pose, background, lighting. A short prompt yields a template result, and BFL explicitly recommends not working without their prompt upsampler. This is a separate language model: it looks at the references and rewrites the request into a detailed description.

The graph is assembled in Python and sent to ComfyUI via API:

LoadImage ×N → ImageScaleToTotalPixels (1 MP) → BatchImagesNode
      │
      ├─→ TextGenerate (upsampler, BFL system prompt for i2i, greedy decoding)
      │        └─→ CLIPTextEncode (Qwen3-VL) ─→ ConditioningZeroOut (negative)
      │
      └─→ ReferenceMultiLatent (resize, 1 MP) ─→ KSampler (16 steps, cfg 1, euler/simple)
                                                  └─→ VAEDecode ─→ SaveImage

How the prompt reaches the model can be seen in one frame: treatment in loupes, the last in the series at the beginning of the article.

1. Scene from the library. Template with placeholders, without the room, uniform, or lighting (fragment):

prompt: >-
  {Subject} as {role} treating a patient in {room}. {He} wears {uniform}, light blue nitrile gloves,
  ... dental TTL magnifying loupes built into slim eyeglasses, exactly like the loupes in {loupes} ...
  The hands and instruments are positioned exactly as in {hands}. ... {light}.

2. What the scheduler substituted. The choice is recorded in the slots field in .json next to the frame:

room_photo: assets/interior/treatment_2.jpg                 # → image 2
uniform: a mint-green medical tunic with a V-neck
light: soft daylight mixed with warm ceiling lights
eq_loupes: equipment/loupes/loupes_frame.jpg                 # → image 3
eq_mirror_probe: equipment/mirror_probe/dental_mirror.jpg    # → image 4
hands: hands/exam/mirror_probe_mouth.jpg                     # → image 5

The output is a prompt of 324 words and six references: face, office, loupes, mirror, hand grip, clinic logo.

3. What the upsampler wrote (beginning):

Create a photograph of the woman from the first image working as a dentist in the dental treatment
room from the second image, preserving its pale gray walls, gray tiled floor, white dental chair,
overhead examination lamp, window with beige blinds, radiator, desk, and all other furniture and
equipment. ... keeping her fair skin, long straight brown hair, and recognizable facial features.

The upsampler reduced 324 words to 235. It doesn't inflate the text but rewrites it for the model:

  • listed the furniture from the office photo;

  • described the appearance from the photo itself, although it shouldn't be described in our prompt;

  • removed the phrase about the hand grip reference and Documentary-style photo, 35mm lens;

  • re-numbered the images in its own way: the logo is from the fifth, although it's the sixth. The frame, however, turned out correct.

What's important about the upsampler:

  1. Description controls composition, photo controls the face. What's not in the description, the model will invent itself. What's in the photo and not forbidden (head tilt, clothing, wall sign) can transfer to the frame.

  2. Greedy decoding (sampling_mode: off, as in the reference BFL script): one prompt yields one description. The seed changes details, but not the pose or composition. Three variants of the same prompt turn out almost identical.

  3. The expanded description should be saved. It's written in .json next to the image. If the frame is wrong, you first read the description: the problem is either in it (fix the prompt) or in the execution (change the seed).

  4. The upsampler itself adds useful things: in other frames, it added "without logos and inscriptions on clothing" and "without signs.".

On an RTX PRO 4500, a frame is rendered in 12–18 seconds, with the upsampler, about 27 seconds per variant. The first frame of the session waits about two minutes while models are loaded from the network drive.

Prompts for FLUX.3: What Worked

The rules were gathered from BFL documentation and test runs, all verified on frames.

Do not describe appearance. The prompt starts with The woman from image 1 as a dentist … and not a word about age, hairstyle, or beard. The description contradicts the photo and distorts the face.

Specify torso and head rotation separately. With a single reference, the head pose is copied almost one-to-one from the photo. It helps like this:

body turned about 45 degrees to the right, arms crossed over her chest;
head turned three-quarters back toward the camera, chin level, looking into the lens

For different poses, create different setups, not more variants. Due to greedy upsampling, variants of the same prompt are similar.

Looking at the camera maintains similarity better. When the doctor looks down into the patient's mouth, the face comes out younger and thinner. For main creatives, a frontal or three-quarter view.

Medium shot framed from the waist up yields a waist-up shot. Just waist-up portrait turns into a head-and-shoulders portrait.

Dress the patient explicitly. Without a man in a casual light gray sweater, the model might dress the patient in the same uniform as the doctor.

Do not try to hide the patient's mouth. All attempts (mouth out of view, covered by his hands) led to the mirror being placed near the crown of the head, nose, or eye.

Walls without frames. plain white walls without pictures or frames, otherwise frames resembling diplomas appear. Fake diplomas in doctor advertising are unacceptable.

Syndrome of One Office

The first version stored ready-made setup prompts, and doctors from two different clinics ended up in almost identical offices.

I had to separate "what the doctor does" and "where and in what," and also significantly expand the number of scenes and link them to doctor specialties.

Now, a frame is assembled from three parts:

  • who: doctor's card (doctor.yaml: specialties, gender) and photo from the website;

  • what they do: scene (setups.yaml): action, pose, object, patient;

  • where and in what: clinic's look (looks.yaml): rooms, uniform, lighting, shot plan, logo.

Scenes and looks are stored at two levels: library (library/, common for all clinics) and project (projects/<clinic>/). The same file name means the same thing at both levels; the project file overrides the library one: dictionaries are merged by keys, lists are replaced entirely, scenes by id. Images (photos of rooms, screens, equipment, hand grips) are defined by folders: place a file in assets/equipment/loupes/ or assets/screens/cbct/, run gen.py plan, and no need to edit YAML.

Scene library (library/setups.yaml, 31 scenes) describes only the action, pose, object, and patient. The room, uniform, lighting, and shot plan are substituted with placeholders:

- id: implant-in-hand
  title: "Holding a real implant with an abutment"
  for: [implant, surgeon]
  priority: 4
  room: surgery
  uniform: coat
  screen: cbct
  equipment: [implant]
  patient: none
  prompt: >-
    {Subject} as a dental implantologist standing in {room} beside a monitor on a stand that shows
    the 3D CBCT scan from {screen}. {He} wears {uniform} and holds between {his} thumb and index finger
    a single small titanium dental implant with its abutment exactly like the one in {implant}, real size ...

Clinic look (projects/<clinic>/looks.yaml) overrides the default look: photos of rooms, uniform palette, logo.

rooms:
  treatment:
    photos: [assets/interior/treatment_1.jpg, assets/interior/treatment_2.jpg]
    text: a bright dental treatment room with white walls, light gray floor tiles, ...
uniform:
  scrubs: [a mint-green medical tunic with a V-neck, a white medical tunic with a stand-up collar, ...]
brand:
  emblem: assets/brand/logo_emblem.png
  emblem_desc: "a white tooth inside a green circle"   # emblem in words, example

The scheduler (gen.py plan <clinic>) selects five scenes for each doctor based on their specialties: a pediatric dentist doesn't extract teeth, an orthodontist doesn't sit in a children's room. It takes specialties in order, and within the order, scenes that were less frequently chosen by other doctors in this clinic come first. This way, ten doctors in one clinic don't end up with ten identical portraits at the chair.

For each variant, its own prompt is assembled: the uniform, lighting, shot plan, background detail, room photo, and image for the monitor are chosen randomly from the look lists. For example, default lighting and shot plan:

slots:
  light:
    - soft natural daylight from a large window
    - even, bright overhead clinic lighting
    - soft daylight mixed with warm ceiling lights
    - bright clinical light with gentle shadows
  framing:
    - medium shot framed from the waist up
    - medium close-up framed from the chest up
    - three-quarter shot from the knees up

Randomness is reproducible; the generator is tied to the clinic, doctor, scene, and variant number:

vr = random.Random(f"{project}|{ddir.name}|{sid}|{i}|{salt}")

This also fixes the uniformity issue due to greedy upsampling: three variants have different prompts, hence different descriptions. If a doctor's set of five isn't liked, change plan_salt in project.yaml, and the scheduler will reshuffle it.

References: Models Need to Be Shown, Not Described

FLUX.3 klein accepts up to 10 images. In the end, there are five or six in one frame, the order is fixed: doctor's face, room photo, screen image, equipment, hand grip, logo. The image N numbers in the text are placed by the scheduler itself.

Room photo. A photo of the clinic's interior works excellently as a reference: the window, the unit, the chair, the reception desk with a sign are recognizably transferred, the text on the sign is correct. If the room has a photo, a separate sign is not added; it's already in the photo.

Equipment

Without a reference, the model invents non-existent things. Binocular loupes consistently turned into a bulky device resembling night vision goggles on a strap around the head. This cannot be fixed with words: the model reads "slim TTL loupes built into eyeglasses" but draws what it has seen more often.

Therefore, for each noticeable object in the frame, there is a folder with photos of real equipment. Currently, there are 12 types: loupes, microscope, implant with abutment, color scale, aligner, mirror and probes, handpieces, ultrasonic scaler, intraoral scanner, surgical tray, plaster models, and a toy dinosaur for children's scenes.

Where I got them from:

  • Wikimedia Commons: loupes, microscope, implant, scale, aligners, mirror, handpieces.

  • Gemini: what couldn't be obtained from Wikimedia. Scaler, scanner, tray, plaster models, dinosaur.

Requirements for equipment references are:

  • one object in full, on a neutral background, in studio lighting;

  • no brands, inscriptions, numbers, or watermarks: everything on the reference will transfer to the frame;

  • no people or hands (separate references for hand grips, discussed below);

  • real size is understood from the shape: an implant the length of a phalanx of a finger, not a palm-sized model that the model draws by default.

The scheduler takes a random photo from the folder (reproducible, by variant seed), adds it to the reference list, and substitutes the string image 3 for {loupes}. Which file went into the frame is recorded in .json. After connecting the references, the loupes became real TTL on the frame in 6 out of 6 shots, and the doctor's eyes are visible above the eyepieces.

Screens

A monitor without a reference doesn't work on any model: fake X-rays with "photographic" teeth, almost readable interface labels, and sometimes a second instance of the doctor in the frame.

With a screen image as a separate reference, scenes with a monitor started to pass: the scanner scene yielded 3 out of 3. I generate screen images in Gemini and crop the bars with readable interface text.

Hands: The Longest Part

Portraits passed almost immediately. Scenes where the doctor works with their hands produced the most defects:

  • the mirror head is near the patient's nose or in the air, not in the mouth;

  • the doctor looks somewhere else, not into the mouth;

  • a third hand without a glove on the patient's chin;

Three things helped.

Describe the mechanics, not "holds the mirror near the patient."

The round head of the dental mirror is inside the open mouth, below the upper front teeth
and well below the nose, angled to reflect the inner surfaces of the teeth toward her eyes;
the tip of the probe touches one lower tooth. Her eyes look down into the patient's mouth,
at the mirror and the tooth being examined, not at the camera.

Specify hands explicitly and remove the assistant. The phrase "fingers resting on the patient's chin" drew a third hand. An assistant's hand at the edge of the frame also confused the model. Now, in each working scene:

Her LEFT hand holds a dental mirror and her RIGHT hand holds a probe.
Exactly two hands in the frame, both her own: her LEFT hand and her RIGHT hand,
never two left or two right hands; no assistant and no other hands near the patient.

Hand grip reference. A close-up of the correct grip: two gloved hands, the patient's mouth, the doctor's face cropped. I generated it in Gemini. Prompts for Gemini had to be rewritten with the same LEFT/RIGHT and with the requirement that the shoulders and forearms of one person be visible in the frame, otherwise Gemini also produced three hands. If there is no hand reference for a scene, the scheduler automatically removes the phrase "positioned exactly as in {hands}" to avoid referencing a non-existent image.

The result on one doctor: previously, the mirror was near the nose or in the air in half the shots; after corrections, 6 out of 9 shots are correct. Residual defects, the mirror near the upper lip, occur in about 1 out of 4 and are fixed by changing the seed.

Logo on Clothing

The clinic's logo is provided as a separate reference. Describing it in words is useless.

  • Sign on the wall (full logo with text) is correct in about 24 out of 27 frames.

  • Embroidery of the full logo with text on light fabric is correct in about two-thirds of the frames; on dark blue, it's almost always crooked: letters in the clinic name get mixed up and fall out.

  • Only the emblem without text on clothing yielded 24 clean frames out of 24 on any fabric.

Next problem: the complex emblem was simplified. Now, a verbal description (emblem_desc) has been added to the emblem; it's substituted into the phrase about embroidery.

Doctors from the Clinic Website

The doctor profiles are assembled by the universal parser fetch_doctors.py: the input is a link to the doctors' page. It searches for Full Name through JSON-LD → H1 → og-tags, and for position by classes like position/specialty and by Russian synonyms for specialties from the library. It collects photos from <img>, srcset, data-src, CSS backgrounds, and og:image, assigns a score to each, and penalizes certificates, diplomas, logos, and sliders. It identifies the list page by links with full names.

The title from the website is converted into specialties using Russian synonyms from the library:

# library/setups.yaml
specialties:
  general: {role: "a dentist", ru: [стоматолог общей практики, врач-стоматолог, стоматолог, зубной врач]}
  chief:   {role: "the head of the dental clinic", ru: [главный врач, главврач, руководитель клиники, директор]}
# doctors/<doctor>/doctor.yaml
title_site: Директор стоматологической клиники, врач стоматолог общей практики.
specialties: [general, chief]
gender: female

Because of chief, the director's scenes were included in this doctor's set of five: at the desk and at the reception (the first two frames in the series at the beginning of the article).

The second clinic had no doctor section at all: seven doctors were found in a carousel on the main page, without links to individual pages. The parser learned the "cards without links" mode and stopped harshly penalizing sliders if the alt text contained a surname.

After parsing, a human still reviews the photos: cropping logos on walls and name badges, checking gender. Inscriptions from references are transferred to the result in distorted form.

Verification: Checklist for Each Frame

The agent reviews each image against seven points:

  1. Facial similarity to the reference.

  2. Teeth and smile: normal number, natural color, no "Hollywood" smile.

  3. Hands, gloves, fingers.

  4. Plausibility of equipment and instruments.

  5. Skin without "plastic" look.

  6. Work mechanics: mirror in the mouth, probe at the tooth, exactly two doctor's hands (one left, one right), looking into the mouth.

  7. Text and logos: no readable text, only the clinic's logo or nothing on clothing, no diplomas, full names, titles, or "before/after" shots.

A failed frame is regenerated with a different seed. The prompt is changed only if the problem is clearly in it, and then I see the diff. For verification, the script compiles contact sheets: one sheet per doctor, rows are setups, columns are variants.

Results depend on the scene. The second clinic, portraits and consultations: 103 out of 105 frames approved on the first try. The first clinic, 9 doctors × 5 setups × 3 variants plus regeneration of 8 setups: 120 frames approved, 12 to 15 per doctor. The last test of working scenes with new references: 11 out of 15 approved.

What Didn't Work

  • Blurred background. FLUX.3 barely obeys shallow depth of field; the background is sharp and sometimes has an HDR "render" tint.

  • Uniformity with a single photo. With one reference, the head pose and expression are pulled from the photo. 2–3 photos of the doctor from different angles would provide significantly more variety, but clinics usually have only one.

  • Small embroidery. An emblem with a complex shape is sometimes simplified; text on fabric only appears on light colors.

  • Looking down. Similarity in working scenes is weaker than in portraits.

Conclusion

The entire pipeline now consists of about 1300 lines of Python, 170 lines of bash, and 700 lines of YAML, of which 500 are scene texts:

python3 fetch_doctors.py Clinic https://example-clinic.ru/doctors/   # doctors from the website
python3 gen.py plan Clinic                                           # sets of setups by doctor
scripts/pod.sh up
python3 gen.py batch Clinic                                          # generation
python3 scripts/contact_sheet.py projects/Clinic/out/2026-10-20      # sheets for review
python3 gen.py approve projects/Clinic/out/2026-10-20/<frame>.png     # to approved
scripts/pod.sh down

The main takeaway for me:

  1. In the task of "a real person in a real environment," the text prompt is less decisive than it seems.

  2. References work: photos of the room, real equipment, screen images, hand grips, logos as separate files. The prompt describes the mechanics: where the hand is, where the mirror is, where the doctor is looking.
