14 steps instead of four. This is how many actions, according to Bloomberg, a ChatGPT user now needs to allow a third-party tool to control a Mac in the macOS 27.3 beta. Simultaneously, it has become known that Apple itself is developing its own autonomous agent that can write code, browse websites, and control applications and devices on behalf of the user. We investigate below.

A vacancy instead of an announcement

A job posting for a manager for an "agentic AI assistant" (specifically: Senior Engineering Manager, Agentic AI – Proactive) has surfaced online. In it, Apple is looking for a leader to create an intelligent assistant that uses a computer like a human does: plans and executes multi-step tasks in applications, browsers, command line, code, and APIs, and coordinates specialized sub-agents. It is separately stated that such agents require "strong permission models, sandboxing, and transparency."

Judging by the candidate requirements, the project is being built at the intersection of three teams — Siri, UI Frameworks, and Apple Foundation Models (AFM). The manager is expected to have experience in optimizing models directly on the device (quantization and distillation), which suggests that the agent will operate locally on Apple chips, not just in the cloud. The job posting itself disappeared from Apple's website shortly after Bloomberg published it.

The project does not yet have a name or codename, nor is an external partner mentioned. Development is in its early stages, and Apple is considering a launch "as soon as possible, by the end of next year." If read from the report date, this refers to the end of 2027. It appears that the new CEO John Ternus is behind all these steps to integrate intelligent AI into Apple — it is he who is now pushing Craig Federighi, the head of software and Siri, to ensure the company finally stops lagging behind its competitors.

Moreover, the foundation for this is already being laid: in the current betas of iOS 27.3 and macOS 27.3, Apple is testing the first agentic Skills for Siri AI, allowing it to control third-party applications like taxi booking. The new project is intended to be the next, fully autonomous step.

What has changed in macOS 27.3

New warning screens have appeared in beta 27.3. The system now prompts the user to think twice before allowing third-party AI assistants to control the computer. For example, to grant such access to the ChatGPT application, you will now have to make 14 clicks instead of the previous four.

Apple has also split full disk access into several separate, stricter permissions and added warnings to them. The new rules apply only to programs you grant access to for the first time; old settings will not be reset.

Currently, these features are only available in the closed developer version of the system. The release date for everyone is unknown. The warning texts themselves have not yet leaked online. However, nothing is said about Accessibility or screen recording in the leaks, so rumors about strict prohibitions on screen viewing are just rumors for now.

Security or competition

Apple explains its actions as "concern for security." TechCrunch cites an October notification to developers where the company promised control, where only someone who consciously wants it will grant permission through a "very explicit user action." However, some AI companies and users see this as an attempt to slow down competitors. Especially since the US Department of Justice is already suing Apple for restricting third-party services' access to its features and devices.

One does not preclude the other. The Apple job posting also mentions strict permissions for AI — perhaps the company is building the same fence for others as for itself. The main question here is simple: will Apple's future assistant also have to go through all these complex approval circles?

Market and developers

Apple has many rivals in this race: Anthropic Duet, OpenAI Dots, Google Tasks, Meta Muse, and Grok Bot. The market reaction has been nervous: in evening trading after the report's publication, Meta shares fell by more than 2%, and investors began to re-evaluate the positions of independent agent developers.

What conclusion can be drawn? Installation and initial permission granting are turning into a funnel, and each extra screen will cost conversion. It is reasonable to rewrite the onboarding for narrow permissions instead of one broad one in advance, record the step counts, and test scenarios on beta 27.3.
