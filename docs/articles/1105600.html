Many site owners add Disallow for GPTBot in robots.txt, thinking they've blocked their texts from ChatGPT. Gary Illyes from Google reminded us: the file only determines if a crawler (a bot that crawls a site) can download a page, not what happens to the content afterward. Therefore, robots.txt for neural networks acts as a request to a specific bot, not a prohibition on training. The line about GPTBot does not remove the site from ChatGPT's responses and says nothing about pages that have already been downloaded. Therefore, you should first decide what to block, AI model training or appearance in their responses, and block it with separate rules for separate bots.

Disallow for GPTBot in robots.txt only affects subsequent site crawls

Disallow asks a bot not to request pages, and nothing more. In a Search Central video on October 21, 2026, Illyes referred to RFC 9309: robots.txt sets access preferences and does not define what the system does with data after crawling. The file works like a sign asking not to enter: a polite bot won't enter, but the sign doesn't say what to do with what has already been seen.

Therefore, the new line only concerns future crawls. robots.txt does not decide the fate of pages downloaded before it. There is no standard way to prohibit training while allowing crawling yet: it only exists in draft form.

There is also no line in the standard that would block all AI at once. Google only supports User-Agent, Allow, Disallow, and Sitemap in the file and ignores the rest, so a made-up directive like No-AI-Chicken-Training will be useless. A specific AI crawler is blocked only if its token, i.e., its name in the User-agent line, is explicitly specified. Illyes also mentioned this in the video (transcript on Search Engine Roundtable).

OpenAI writes that these settings are independent. You can allow OAI-SearchBot and remain in ChatGPT search, but disallow GPTBot so that texts are not used for training. ChatGPT-User visits upon user action, and according to OpenAI, robots.txt rules may not apply to it. The site is removed from ChatGPT search via OAI-SearchBot. Anthropic warns that if Claude-User is disabled, Claude will not be able to use your page in response to a user request, and site visibility may decrease.

Google has separated training from Search with the Google-Extended token. This token has no dedicated bot. It determines whether pages crawled by Google will be used for training future Gemini models and for grounding, i.e., providing the page to the AI model during a response. It does not affect inclusion in Search (Google documentation). Applebot-Extended seems to work similarly: it doesn't crawl pages and only decides whether AI models can be trained on Apple data. A site with this disallowed token remains in search (Apple Support). Common Crawl, an open archive of crawled pages, is crawled by the CCBot bot and stops if this token is blocked (Common Crawl FAQ).

In Russia, the options are more limited. To prevent pages from appearing in Yandex's Alice AI quick answers, Yandex Webmaster advises disallowing YandexAdditional or YandexAdditionalBot. Changes are reflected in 2–14 days. According to seodb's directory, Yandex does not have a separate token solely for training: AI answers use pages from the main index, so blocking YandexBot removes the site from both search and AI answers. Sber, according to Flowstat's observations as of October 2026, does not publish the name of the GigaChat search bot or rules for robots.txt. It is not yet possible to specify this bot in the file.

Which robots.txt lines block AI training, and which remove the site from responses

It is impossible to completely block a site from AI using robots.txt. If you try, you will likely lose your ranking in neural network responses, and already downloaded content will remain in the training data, i.e., the set of texts used to train the AI model. Training and responses must be separated: bots for training can be blocked if it's important for the business, while AI service search bots and bots that respond to user requests should be left open.

Do not be included in training and remain in responses:

``` User-agent: GPTBot User-agent: ClaudeBot User-agent: CCBot User-agent: Applebot-Extended Disallow: /

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot User-agent: ChatGPT-User User-agent: Claude-SearchBot User-agent: Claude-User Allow: / ```

The first group asks not to download pages for training bots from OpenAI, Anthropic, Common Crawl, and Apple. The second group is for clarity: without a disallow, these bots are already permitted. Google Search is not affected by this file, and the site is not removed from ChatGPT and Claude search. It does not affect pages that these bots have already downloaded, nor will it stop bots that do not read robots.txt.

I have not included Google-Extended in this list. It blocks not only Gemini training but also grounding, so the disallow might cost the site its place in Gemini's responses. It should only be used if blocking training is more important for the business than these responses.

Do not appear in responses:

`` User-agent: OAI-SearchBot User-agent: ChatGPT-User User-agent: Claude-SearchBot User-agent: Claude-User User-agent: YandexAdditional User-agent: YandexAdditionalBot Disallow: / ``

The price for this file is that the site will stop appearing in the responses of these services. The result with ChatGPT-User is not guaranteed: OpenAI states that rules may not apply to it.

Which AI bots bypass robots.txt and how to check this in server logs

Not all bots obey. In August 2025, Cloudflare showed that Perplexity changes its user agent and ASN (network number from which traffic originates) when blocked and sometimes doesn't even request robots.txt. The test was conducted on new domains with a complete block in robots.txt and rules against PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User. Perplexity still received content by impersonating a Chrome browser on macOS. According to Cloudflare's calculations, such stealth crawling occurred on tens of thousands of domains, millions of requests per day. Perplexity was removed from Cloudflare's list of verified bots. Amusingly, ChatGPT-User turned out to be the most compliant in the same test: it requested robots.txt and stopped crawling, even though OpenAI itself warns that rules may not apply to it.

You can check your bots using the access log. Select lines with tokens and see which pages they requested after the Disallow appeared:

`` grep -E "GPTBot|ClaudeBot|CCBot|OAI-SearchBot|ChatGPT-User" access.log ``

Each line contains the request time, path, server response code, and user-agent. A successful response to a request for a blocked section after Disallow appeared means the bot downloaded the page, while a denial or error means it did not receive the content. Check if the bot requested /robots.txt and continued to crawl blocked sections. You cannot detect spoofing by token, so look for requests with a regular browser user-agent that occur with the same frequency on the same pages. How GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and others appear in logs is explained in the article about which AI bots actually crawl websites and what they request.

What, besides robots.txt, blocks content from neural networks: server-side blocking, meta tags, and TDMRep

robots.txt and user-agent blocking are useless against a bot that pretends to be a browser. You need to instruct the developer to check for protection against automatic requests on the server or in the website delivery service (CDN, e.g., Cloudflare): it distinguishes a bot from a user with a regular browser by traffic characteristics, such as the network from which the request originates and the request rhythm. In Cloudflare's test, it worked: when the stealth crawler was also blocked, Perplexity's responses became less specific, without details from the original page. Blocking by IP instead of a file with Anthropic can be misleading: it prevents the bot from reading robots.txt, and the denial may not work.

• nosnippet meta tag. Apple offers this to prevent specific content from appearing in Siri and Apple Search responses to general queries.

• TDMRep. The W3C Community Group protocol (not a W3C standard) expresses reservations of rights for TDM, automated text and data analysis: the reservation is set by the parameter tdm-reservation with the value 1 (protocol description). It has legal weight in the EU: Article 4 of Directive 2019/790 permits TDM for commercial purposes unless the copyright holder has reserved rights, including in a machine-readable way. Outside the EU, this exception does not apply.

• Fee for crawling. Since July 2025, Cloudflare has had Pay Per Crawl in closed beta: a website can charge AI crawlers for each access to content.

General AI training prohibition in robots.txt is already considered by Anthropic, and Google promises it from 2027

Starting October 30, 2026, Anthropic will recognize the machine-readable training prohibition signal. The general prohibition looks like this:

`` User-agent: __usage_ai-training Disallow: / ``

Google plans to support the same AIPREF signal in early 2027. According to Lab Leaders, Google might consider such a prohibition as a ban on Google-Extended, and then the general line might also cost the site its Gemini responses. The IETF is preparing a standard: a draft, co-authored by Illyes, will add the Content-Usage rule and an HTTP header of the same name to robots.txt, but the dictionary has not yet been agreed upon. Until the standard is adopted, training and responses must be blocked separately, and each bot must be named individually.
